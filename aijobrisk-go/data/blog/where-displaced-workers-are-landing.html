Not every arrow points down. Alongside the layoff headlines, Forbes has reported that small businesses are actively hiring workers displaced by AI-driven cuts — and a LinkedIn study cited by CNBC finds younger workers landing fast-growing, well-paid AI-adjacent roles. The labour market is not just shedding jobs; it is reshuffling them.

The reshuffle follows exposure

Where the displaced workers land is not random. Two destinations stand out in the exposure data:

  • Low-exposure, hands-on work — the trades, in-person care, on-site services. These roles score low on generative-AI exposure because the bottleneck is a person in a place, and smaller firms that live on local, physical service are hiring for exactly that.
  • AI-adjacent roles — people who move toward the technology rather than away from it, supervising, integrating and selling AI systems rather than competing with them.

Exposure cuts both ways

The same data that flags which jobs are shrinking also points to where the slack is going. A worker cut from a high-exposure desk role has two rational moves: step sideways into a low-exposure occupation, or step up into the AI-adjacent layer. Both show up in the current hiring pattern.

A caution

This is a reshuffle, not a free lunch. Moving from an exposed office job into a trade or a care role is a real retraining cost, not a swap you make overnight. And "AI-adjacent" roles are still few relative to the roles under pressure. But the direction is clear enough to plan around.

Takeaway

The jobs opening up are, disproportionately, the ones AI cannot do — physical, local, trust-based — plus a thinner band of roles built on top of AI. If you are deciding where to move next, the low-exposure end of our data is a good place to start looking.
