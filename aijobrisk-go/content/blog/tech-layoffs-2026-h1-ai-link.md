The first half of 2026 brought another wave of tech layoffs. But how much of it is
genuinely driven by AI automation, and how much is the usual post-hype
restructuring? We looked at the numbers against our own exposure data.

## What the announcements say

Company statements increasingly cite "AI efficiency" as a reason for cuts. That
framing is convenient — it signals to investors that the firm is ahead of the
curve — but it does not tell us which roles are actually being automated.

## Cross-checking against exposure

When we line up the affected job functions against their generative-AI task
exposure, a clearer picture emerges:

- **High-exposure roles** (routine content, data entry, first-line support) show
  cuts consistent with automation.
- **Low-exposure roles** cut in the same rounds look more like budget-driven
  restructuring than AI displacement.

## Takeaway

"AI layoffs" is doing a lot of rhetorical work. The exposure data suggests the
truly automation-driven share is smaller than the headlines imply — but real, and
concentrated in a predictable set of occupations.
