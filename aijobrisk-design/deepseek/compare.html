Frequently asked questions

How does the comparison work?
We combine occupation-level data from national labour statistics (14 countries) with generative-AI exposure scores from ILO Working Paper 140 and Eloundou et al. (OpenAI). Each occupation is scored on a 1–10 scale based on task overlap with large language models.
Where does the data come from?
Official sources include the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, UK ONS, German BA, Australian Jobs and Skills Australia, and others. AI exposure research is drawn from peer-reviewed papers and the ILO’s global analysis.
What does the AI exposure score mean?
A score of 1 means minimal exposure (tasks unlikely to be automated or augmented by AI), while 10 means near-full exposure. Scores reflect the share of tasks that could be affected by current generative-AI capabilities, not job elimination.
Which countries are covered?
Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, Singapore, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, and the United States. More countries are added as data becomes available.
How often is the data updated?
Labour data is refreshed annually after each country’s official release. AI exposure scores are updated whenever new research with comparable methodology is published — at least once per year.
Can I trust the risk labels?
Risk labels (low/medium/high) always appear alongside the numeric score and a text description. Colour is never used as the sole indicator. Our methodology is fully documented in the Data section.
