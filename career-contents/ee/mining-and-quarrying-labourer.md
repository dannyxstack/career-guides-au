## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for mining and quarrying labourers in Estonia?**
A: According to Statistics Estonia, the average gross monthly salary for mining and quarrying labourers is approximately €1,200–€1,500. Pay varies by region and employer, with the oil shale sector in Ida-Virumaa typically offering higher wages. Overtime and shift work may increase earnings.

**Q: Can mining and quarrying labourers obtain an EU Blue Card for working in Estonia?**
A: The EU Blue Card is intended for highly qualified workers with a higher education degree or a high salary. Mining labourers usually do not meet these criteria. Instead, they can apply for a long-term (D) visa or a temporary residence permit for employment, which requires a valid job offer and approval from the Police and Border Guard Board.

**Q: Which companies are the main employers for mining and quarrying labourers in Estonia?**
A: Enefit, the state-owned energy company, operates large oil shale mines in Ida-Virumaa and is the primary employer. Other employers include private companies involved in limestone, sand, and gravel extraction, such as Kunda Nordic Tsement and Paekivitoodete Tehas. Most mining jobs are concentrated in northeastern Estonia.
