**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement
- AI analyzes corneal topography data to generate preliminary lens fitting plans.
- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Master the use of remote eye testing platforms
- Improve communication and customer management skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an optometrist in Estonia?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for optometrists in Estonia ranges from €1,500 to €2,500, depending on experience, location, and whether working in the public or private sector. According to Statistics Estonia, the median gross monthly wage for healthcare professionals was €2,068 in 2022. Private clinics and larger cities like Tallinn tend to offer higher pay.

**Q: What are the official visa routes for non-EU optometrists to work in Estonia?**
A: Non-EU optometrists can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a valid employment contract, higher education (or equivalent experience in optometry), and a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary in Estonia. Alternatively, a standard temporary residence permit for employment is available for contracts under specific conditions. Applications are processed by the Police and Border Guard Board.

**Q: What qualifications and licensing are required to practice optometry in Estonia?**
A: To practice optometry in Estonia, you need a recognized degree in optometry (e.g., from the University of Tartu) and registration with the Health Board (Terviseamet). Registration requires proof of education, language proficiency (usually Estonian), and completion of a professional exam. Continuing education credits are mandatory for license renewal every five years.
