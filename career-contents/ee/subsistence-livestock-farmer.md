## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Estonia?**
A: Income for subsistence livestock farmers in Estonia varies widely. According to Statistics Estonia, average monthly gross earnings in agriculture are around €1,200–1,500. However, subsistence farmers primarily produce for their own consumption, so cash income is often irregular. Many rely on EU Common Agricultural Policy subsidies, which for small farms can amount to a few thousand euros annually.

**Q: Can subsistence livestock farmers obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Estonia?**
A: Subsistence livestock farming generally does not qualify for an EU Blue Card, which requires a high-skilled job and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average. Third-country nationals may instead apply for a temporary residence permit for employment if they have a specific job offer meeting Estonia's salary criteria (€1,484 gross/month as of 2024) and the employer registers the vacancy with the unemployment insurance fund.

**Q: What are the main duties of subsistence livestock farmers in Estonia?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Estonia raise animals primarily for household consumption. Duties include feeding, herding, milking, and caring for cattle, sheep, pigs, or poultry. They also maintain animal health, clean shelters, and manage pastures. Many process milk, eggs, or meat for personal use or limited local sale. Farmers often combine farming with other work or EU subsidy schemes.
