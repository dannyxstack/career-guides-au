## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for forestry workers in Estonia?**
A: According to Statistics Estonia, the average gross monthly salary for forestry and related workers is around €1,300–€1,500 as of 2023. Earnings vary by experience, region, and employer. Overtime and piecework pay can increase income.

**Q: What are the visa options for non-EU forestry workers to work in Estonia?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work in Estonia as forestry workers via a temporary residence permit for employment, which requires a job offer and a labor market test unless the occupation is on Estonia's shortage list. The EU Blue Card is generally not applicable as forestry work is not considered high-skilled.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a forestry worker in Estonia?**
A: Formal vocational education in forestry or related fields is preferred. Many workers gain experience through apprenticeships or on-the-job training. Certification in chainsaw operation and safety is often required. Knowledge of local forestry practices and environmental regulations is beneficial.
