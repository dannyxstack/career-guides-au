## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for athletes and sports players in Estonia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sport and level. In top-tier football (Premium Liiga), players can earn €1,500–€5,000 per month. Lower-tier sports or amateur leagues may offer little to no pay. Many athletes receive additional income from bonuses, sponsorships, or state support. According to Statistics Estonia, the national average gross salary was €1,770 in 2024, but athlete earnings can be higher or lower depending on success and popularity.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card routes for athletes to work in Estonia?**
A: Non-EU professional athletes can apply for a temporary residence permit for employment under the Aliens Act. The employer must prove the athlete's professional skills and that the position cannot be filled locally. The EU Blue Card generally requires a higher education degree or equivalent and is not typically used for athletes. Instead, the standard work permit for athletes processed by the Police and Border Guard Board is the applicable route.

**Q: How competitive is it to become a professional athlete in Estonia?**
A: Competition is fierce due to Estonia's small population (1.3 million). Success often requires early specialization, rigorous training, and support from sports clubs or federations. Only a small fraction of athletes reach the professional level, mainly in popular sports like basketball, football, and track and field. Many athletes combine sports with other education or careers, as government grants and sponsorships may not provide full-time income.
