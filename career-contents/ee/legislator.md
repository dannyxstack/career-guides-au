**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Comparison and discrepancy analysis of legal provisions
- Automatic classification and draft reply to voter letters

- Preliminary data statistics and visualization for policy impact reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use NLP to analyze large-scale public opinion to grasp sentiment trends
- AI-assisted simulation of economic and social impacts of different legislation
- Real-time public opinion monitoring and early warning system enhances decision-making timeliness
- Optimize legislators' schedules and resource allocation through smart recommendation systems.

**Human moat:**

- Negotiation and compromise skills in complex conflicts of interest

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for legislators in Estonia?**
A: Members of the Estonian Parliament (Riigikogu) receive a monthly salary set by law, which as of 2023 is approximately €4,800 before taxes. Additional allowances may apply for leadership roles such as committee chairs or faction leaders. Salaries are publicly regulated and adjusted periodically.

**Q: Can foreign legislators work in Estonia via the EU Blue Card?**
A: No. Legislators are elected or appointed officials, typically requiring Estonian citizenship. The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled employment, not elected positions. Non-EU citizens cannot become legislators without citizenship. Official routes for political office are limited to citizens.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a legislator in Estonia?**
A: Candidates must be at least 21 years old, eligible to vote, and have permanent residence in Estonia. No specific educational degree is required. Typically, candidates are members of political parties and run in elections. Estonian citizenship is mandatory for parliamentary membership.
