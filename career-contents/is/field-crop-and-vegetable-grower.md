# Field Crop and Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Iceland manage the cultivation of cereals, potatoes, and vegetables, both outdoors and in greenhouses. With Iceland's short growing season and volcanic soils, growers often rely on geothermal heating for greenhouses to produce tomatoes, cucumbers, and leafy greens. The sector is small but vital for local food security, employing around 1,000 workers. Most farms are family-run, and the work is highly seasonal. Labour demand is stable, with occasional shortages for skilled greenhouse workers during peak seasons.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm labourer, advancing to farm manager or owner. Specialisation in organic farming or greenhouse management is possible. Some growers transition to agronomy consulting or agricultural research. Opportunities for further education include horticulture programmes at the Agricultural University of Iceland. The path is often informal, with many learning on the job, but formal qualifications can enhance prospects for larger operations or export-oriented farms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- Greenhouse vegetables
- Organic farming
- Sustainable agriculture
- Local food production

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a field crop and vegetable grower in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and farm size, but are generally based on collective agreements. Entry-level workers earn around 350,000 ISK per month, while experienced greenhouse managers can earn over 500,000 ISK. Overtime and seasonal bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a field crop and vegetable grower in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but a work permit is required. The employer must conduct a labour market test, and the job offer must meet minimum salary thresholds. Greenhouse specialists may have an easier route due to skills shortages.
