# Metal Polisher, Wheel Grinder and Tool Sharpener — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7224 (ISCO08)**

In Iceland, metal polishers, wheel grinders, and tool sharpeners (ISCO 7224) are skilled tradespeople who finish and maintain metal surfaces and tools. They work in manufacturing workshops, automotive repair, construction, and specialized tooling companies. The occupation is small but essential for precision work in sectors like fishing equipment, geothermal energy infrastructure, and metal fabrication. Due to Iceland's limited industrial base, most positions are concentrated in the Reykjavik capital area and a few regional industrial hubs. The role requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and competence with grinding and polishing machinery. Apprenticeship training through the Icelandic vocational education system is the standard entry route.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a 3-4 year apprenticeship combining on-the-job training and vocational school, leading to a journeyman's certificate. Experienced workers can advance to senior polisher or foreman roles, or specialize in areas like precision tool grinding or surface treatment. Some move into quality control or start their own small workshops. Further training in related fields such as welding or machining can broaden opportunities. The occupation offers a clear, structured path within Iceland's trades system, with wages increasing steadily after certification.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a metal polisher in Iceland?**
A: Salaries start around 350,000 ISK per month for apprentices and increase to 500,000–600,000 ISK for experienced journeymen. Overtime and shift differentials can add 20–30%.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit for this job in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not on the shortage list. Employers must prove no EEA worker is available, and permits are rarely approved for this trade.

**Q: Do I need formal education to work as a metal polisher in Iceland?**
A: Yes, a journeyman's certificate from the Icelandic vocational system is mandatory for employment. The apprenticeship program combines on-the-job training and school.
