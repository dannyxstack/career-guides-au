# Building Frame and Related Trades Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers in Iceland perform a variety of construction tasks including carpentry, bricklaying, and concrete work. They are essential for residential, commercial, and infrastructure projects across the country. The occupation covers skilled trades such as carpenters (trésmiðir), bricklayers (múrarar), and roofers (þakmenn), often working on new builds, renovations, and geothermal energy installations. Demand is steady due to ongoing housing shortages, tourism infrastructure, and government investments in transport and energy projects.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building frame trades worker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, specialization, and region. Entry-level workers earn around 450,000–500,000 ISK per month, while experienced journey-level tradespeople can earn 600,000–750,000 ISK. Overtime and project bonuses are common. Employers often provide benefits like pension contributions and paid holidays.

**Q: Can a non-EEA carpenter get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from an Icelandic employer and the position is on the shortage list (currently includes many construction trades). The employer must conduct a labour market test. You can then apply for a work and residence permit for qualified professionals. The permit is issued for one year and can be renewed.

**Q: Do I need formal training to work as a building frame trades worker in Iceland?**
A: While not always mandatory for basic tasks, formal vocational training (apprenticeship or diploma) is highly valued and often required for higher-paying positions and supervisory roles. Safety certification is mandatory. For trades like carpentry and bricklaying, a journeyman certificate (iðnréttindi) may be required to work independently.
