# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

Hunters and trappers in Iceland engage in the capture or killing of wild animals for food, fur, pest control, or wildlife management. The occupation is predominantly rural and seasonal, with activities including reindeer, seabird, and Arctic fox hunting. Employment is limited and often part-time or combined with other work such as farming or tourism guiding. Licenses from the Icelandic Environment Agency are mandatory, and the sector is closely regulated to conserve wildlife populations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

Career progression is minimal; experienced hunters may move into wildlife guiding, game management, or teach hunting safety courses. Formal education is not required, but completion of a certified hunting course and possession of a firearm license are essential. Diversification into related fields like pest control or nature tourism is common for sustained income.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a hunter in Iceland?**
A: Income varies greatly as many hunters are self-employed. Full-time hunters may earn between 3–5 million ISK annually, but most supplement their income with other work such as farming or tourism.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as hunters in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. Hunting is not classified as a skilled occupation, so work permits are rarely granted. Non-EEA nationals would need to meet the labour market test, and the occupation is typically reserved for local residents.

**Q: What licenses are required to hunt in Iceland?**
A: A hunting license from the Icelandic Environment Agency is mandatory for all hunting. If using a firearm, a separate firearm license from the police is also required.
