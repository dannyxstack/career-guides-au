# Rigger and Cable Splicer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7215 (ISCO08)**

Riggers and cable splicers in Iceland are essential for the assembly, installation, and maintenance of heavy lifting equipment and telecommunications cables. They work in sectors such as construction, energy (especially hydropower and geothermal), and telecommunications, often at heights and in challenging outdoor conditions. This occupation requires physical stamina, precision, and adherence to safety protocols.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression starts with a vocational apprenticeship (e.g., 3–4 years) leading to a journeyman's certificate. Experienced workers can advance to supervisor, project manager, or self-employment. Additional training in advanced cable splicing or heavy rigging can open higher-paid roles. Some professionals cross-train into related trades like electrical installation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Image recognition-based automatic detection of cable wear
- Use drones for overhead cable inspection
- Automatically generate rigging load calculations and configuration plans
- Installation path planning using digital twin simulation
- Automated report generation and compliance document filling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AR headset displays complex rigging connection steps and safety annotations

- machine learning-based defect identification and repair guidance for cable joints

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a rigger or cable splicer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector but generally range from 500,000 to 700,000 ISK per month pre-tax. Overtime and project bonuses can increase earnings. Entry-level apprentices earn less, while certified journeymen with several years of experience may exceed 800,000 ISK.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a rigger/cable splicer in Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer and meet the qualifications. The employer must apply for a work permit (D-tilvisun) after a labour market test. Since the occupation is often on the shortage list, the process can be streamlined. EEA/EFTA nationals do not need a permit.

**Q: Do I need formal education to become a rigger/cable splicer in Iceland?**
A: Yes, most employers require a vocational qualification (journeyman's certificate) obtained through an apprenticeship program. Alternatively, relevant experience and certification from abroad may be accepted after assessment by Iðnaðarmálastofnun.
