# Ship's Engineer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 3151 (ISCO08)**

Ships' engineers in Iceland are responsible for the operation, maintenance, and repair of marine engines and mechanical systems on vessels, including fishing trawlers, cargo ships, and passenger ferries. The maritime sector is a cornerstone of Iceland's economy, with a strong demand for qualified engineers due to the extensive fishing fleet and growing maritime tourism. Work often involves extended periods at sea, requiring technical expertise and adaptability. The occupation is regulated and typically requires STCW certification and engine watch certificates.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for ships' engineers typically starts with lower-level engine room positions, advancing through ranks such as second engineer, chief engineer, and marine superintendent. Many engineers obtain higher licences (e.g., unlimited chief engineer) to enhance prospects. Some transition to shore-based roles in ship management, classification societies, or maritime education. Continuous professional development is essential, especially in automation and environmental technologies.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine voyage data recording and report generation
- Basic meteorological route planning and optimization
- Marine machinery condition monitoring and early warning
- Cargo loading calculation and balance adjustment
- Communication relay and standard message forwarding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted navigation decisions to improve flight safety and fuel efficiency
- Real-time weather and sea condition forecasting, supporting dynamic adjustment of speed and route
- Automated fault diagnosis and predictive maintenance to reduce downtime
- Intelligent Cargo Monitoring Systems to Improve Stowage Accuracy and Safety
- Augmented reality (AR) assisted navigation at night and in adverse weather

**Human moat:**

- Ship handling and emergency response capabilities in crisis situations
- Team management and crew leadership (especially captain duties)
- Special cargo handling and complex port operations experience
- Regulatory compliance and maritime safety inspection responsibilities
- Flexible judgment in unpredictable marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Remote operation and automated system monitoring

- STCW certification renewal (including E-navigation module)
- Cross-cultural communication and team management

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a ship's engineer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and vessel type. Entry-level engineers earn around 500,000–700,000 ISK per month, while chief engineers can earn upwards of 1,200,000 ISK. Overtime and sea bonuses are common.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a ship's engineer in Iceland?**
A: EEA/EFTA nationals can work freely after registration. Non-EEA nationals require a work permit tied to a job offer; being on the shortage list helps but a labour market test still applies.

**Q: What education is required to become a ship's engineer in Iceland?**
A: Completion of a marine engineering diploma or degree from an accredited institution, plus STCW certification. On-the-job training is also critical.
