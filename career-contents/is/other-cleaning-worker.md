# Other Cleaning Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other cleaning workers in Iceland perform a variety of cleaning tasks in settings such as offices, hotels, hospitals, and public buildings. The role often involves sweeping, mopping, vacuuming, dusting, and waste disposal, and may require knowledge of cleaning chemicals and equipment. Many workers are employed by cleaning companies or directly by institutions, with shifts often outside standard hours. The occupation relies heavily on EEA labour due to limited local recruitment, but non-EEA hires face strict labour market tests.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited but possible through specialization (e.g., industrial cleaning, environmentally friendly cleaning methods) or moving into supervisory roles. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaner in Iceland?**
A: Cleaners in Iceland earn hourly wages, often around the national minimum wage or slightly above. Annual salaries vary by hours worked and sector, but exact figures depend on collective agreements and experience.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a cleaner?**
A: It is very difficult. Non-EEA nationals must have a job offer and the employer must prove no suitable EEA candidate is available. The occupation is not on the shortage list, and salary thresholds may apply. Permits are rarely granted for general cleaning.

**Q: What opportunities for advancement exist?**
A: With experience, cleaners can become team leaders, supervisors, or specialize in areas like industrial cleaning or green cleaning. Further education in cleaning technology can lead to higher positions.
