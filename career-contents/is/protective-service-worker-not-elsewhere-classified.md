# Protective Services Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective Services Workers in Iceland encompass a range of roles including security guards, private investigators, and loss prevention officers. They are employed across various sectors such as retail, hospitality, events, and government facilities. The demand for these professionals has grown with increased tourism and a focus on safety, though the occupation is not considered a shortage area. Most positions require licensing from the Icelandic Police and completion of a training course.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from entry-level security guard to supervisor or site manager. With additional training, workers can specialize in areas like cybersecurity, risk assessment, or executive protection. Some transition into law enforcement or private investigation roles. The Icelandic Police offer continuing education courses for license renewal and specialization.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for protective services workers in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by employer and sector. Entry-level security guards typically earn a monthly salary in the lower range of full-time wages, while experienced supervisors earn more. Exact figures change regularly; refer to Statistics Iceland for current data.

**Q: What visa or permit do I need to work as a protective services worker in Iceland?**
A: If you are an EEA/EFTA citizen, you can work freely upon registration. Non-EEA citizens require a work permit tied to a specific job offer; the employer must demonstrate that no EEA worker is available.

**Q: What training is required?**
A: A mandatory security guard course approved by the Icelandic Police is required. It covers legal aspects, emergency procedures, and customer service. The course typically takes a few months.
