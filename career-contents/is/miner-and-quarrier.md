# Miner and Quarrier — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers in Iceland extract minerals, stone, sand, gravel, and other natural resources from surface or underground mines. The sector is small due to limited mineral deposits, but quarrying for construction materials (e.g., basalt, gravel) is essential for infrastructure and building projects. Workers operate heavy machinery, drill, blast, and sort materials, often in remote locations. The industry employs around 200-300 people, with demand tied to construction cycles and geothermal energy projects. Most workers are Icelandic or EEA nationals, as the remote nature and physical demands require local labor mobility.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most workers remain in operational roles. Advancement to shift supervisor or quarry manager is possible with experience and additional safety certifications. Some transition into related fields like heavy machinery operation or construction site management. Continuing education in blasting techniques or environmental compliance can enhance prospects. The work is physically demanding and often seasonal, with opportunities for overtime during peak construction periods.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a miner or quarrier in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location, but generally range from 450,000 to 650,000 ISK per month before tax. Overtime and remote site allowances can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a miner or quarrier in Iceland?**
A: It is possible but difficult. The occupation is not on the shortage list, so a labour market test must show no EEA workers are available. Employers must advertise the job locally and prove insufficient applicants. Permits are rarely granted for this role.

**Q: Is there room for advancement in this field?**
A: Advancement is limited. With experience, workers can become shift supervisors, quarry managers, or safety officers. Additional certifications in blasting or environmental management can improve prospects, but most remain in operator roles.
