# Electrical Line Installer and Repairer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7413 (ISCO08)**

Electrical line installers and repairers construct, maintain, and repair overhead and underground electrical power transmission and distribution lines. In Iceland, they work for utility companies like Landsnet and RARIK, often in challenging weather and remote areas. The role is critical for the country's energy grid, which relies heavily on renewable sources such as hydropower and geothermal. The work involves climbing poles, splicing wires, and operating equipment. Icelandic employers typically require a journeyman's certificate (rafvirkjajafni) and on-the-job training due to the harsh environment and high safety standards.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an electrical line installer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location, but journeymen line installers earn competitive wages comparable to other skilled trades. Pay is generally higher for remote assignments and overtime. No specific figure can be given as it depends on collective agreements and individual contracts.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as an electrical line installer in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but a work permit is required. You must have a job offer from an Icelandic employer who has demonstrated that no suitable EEA worker is available. The permit is tied to the employer and is typically granted for one year with the possibility of renewal. Knowledge of Icelandic is often expected.

**Q: How do I become a licensed electrical line installer in Iceland?**
A: You must complete a four-year apprenticeship program, alternating between vocational school and paid work under a journeyman. After finishing, you take a trade test to earn your journeyman's certificate. Foreign qualifications may be recognized through an equivalency assessment.
