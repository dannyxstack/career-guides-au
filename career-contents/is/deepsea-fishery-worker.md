# Deep-sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6223 (ISCO08)**

Deep-sea fishery workers operate fishing vessels in Iceland's rich but demanding North Atlantic waters, hauling catches of cod, haddock, and other species. This occupation is central to Iceland's economy, employing many in coastal communities. Work involves long shifts at sea, handling heavy gear, and processing catch onboard. Iceland has a strong quota system ensuring sustainable stocks, but the work remains physically strenuous and seasonal. Foreign workers from the EEA are common, and the occupation is recognized as a skilled trade with opportunities for advancement.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a deckhand, advancing to boatswain, mate, and eventually skipper with experience and qualifications. Many workers also transition to onshore roles in fish processing, quality control, or fisheries management. Icelandic maritime schools offer specialized courses leading to certifications that boost career prospects. The path is straightforward for those committed to seafaring, but requires physical endurance and adaptability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing vessel navigation and route planning (AIS + autopilot)
- Fish catch classification and initial weighing (computer vision)
- Fish school detection and net deployment position optimization (AI sonar analysis)
- Predictive maintenance for fishing vessel equipment (IoT + machine learning)
- Catch tracking and compliance report generation (automated data entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fish school prediction (combining meteorological and oceanographic data)
- Real-time deck safety monitoring (AI alert wearable)
- Automation of catch processing (robot-assisted)
- Resource management and sustainable fishing strategies (AI models)
- Market sales data analysis (providing optimal auction timing)

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making in complex maritime environments (storms, currents)
- Manual net repair and deck emergency handling
- Judging fish behavior based on experience (limited AI data)
- Communication and coordination with fleet and shore teams
- Flexible operations to adapt to seasonal and regulatory changes

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Computer Vision Applications in Fisheries
- Data analysis and prediction tools (Python, R)
- Drone operation and maintenance
- Marine sensor and IoT device management
- Sustainable fisheries regulations and certifications
- Deck automation system operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fisheries
- Automated fishing gear
- Seafood processing
- Ocean monitoring

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deep-sea fishery worker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and catch shares, but the average annual gross income is around 6,000,000 to 8,000,000 ISK for a full-time worker. Earnings can be higher during peak seasons.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work visa for deep-sea fishing in Iceland?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the shortage list. You need a job offer and the employer must attempt to hire locally first. The permit is initially temporary but can lead to permanent residence.

**Q: What training is required to work as a deep-sea fishery worker?**
A: A mandatory sea safety certificate (STCW) is required. Basic fishing techniques are learned on the job. Some vocational schools offer pre-sea training courses.
