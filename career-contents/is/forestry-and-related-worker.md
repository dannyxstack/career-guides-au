# Forestry Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry workers in Iceland carry out manual and mechanical tasks related to the establishment, management, and harvesting of forests. Given Iceland's ambitious afforestation programs aimed at combating erosion and carbon sequestration, this occupation plays a key role in environmental restoration. Work includes planting, thinning, pruning, and felling trees, often in remote or challenging terrains. The sector is small but expanding, with a focus on sustainable practices and native species. Most workers are employed by the Icelandic Forest Service or private forestry companies.

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. —. Employment outlook for forestry workers is moderately positive, driven by government policies on afforestation and climate mitigation. Opportunities may arise in carbon offset projects and ecotourism-related forest maintenance. Automation is limited, so manual labor remains essential.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a general laborer, advancing to crew leader or supervisor with experience and additional training. Specialization in areas like forest health, machinery operation, or land reclamation is possible. Some workers transition to related fields such as landscaping or environmental monitoring. Formal vocational education (e.g., from the Icelandic Forest Service's school) can open paths to technical or managerial roles. The field offers stability for those committed to outdoor work.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- afforestation
- sustainable forestry
- carbon sequestration
- land reclamation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a forestry worker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are generally based on collective agreements. Entry-level workers earn around 350,000–400,000 ISK per month before tax, while experienced workers can earn 450,000–550,000 ISK. Overtime and remote location allowances may apply.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals get a work permit as a forestry worker?**
A: Yes, if they have a formal job offer and meet education/skill requirements (e.g., vocational training in forestry). A labor market test is usually required unless the occupation is on the shortage list (forestry worker is not). The process can take 2–4 months.
