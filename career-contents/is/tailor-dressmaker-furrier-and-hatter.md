# Tailor, Dressmaker, Furrier or Hatter — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors, dressmakers, furriers, and hatters in Iceland are skilled artisans who create, alter, and repair garments and headwear. The occupation is niche due to Iceland's small market and the prevalence of ready-to-wear clothing. However, there is steady demand for traditional Icelandic costumes (búningur), high-end custom clothing, and alterations in Reykjavik. Furriers and hatters are extremely rare, with most work concentrated in the capital region. The craft is taught through apprenticeships combined with vocational schooling, emphasising precision and creativity.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically follows the apprenticeship path from apprentice to journeyman (sveinn) and possibly master tailor (meistari). Many tailors eventually open their own shops or work freelance. Specialisation in traditional Icelandic clothing (búningur) or bridal wear can enhance career prospects. Some move into costume design for film and theatre. Due to the small market, advancement often requires entrepreneurial skills and a strong local reputation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tailor in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are moderate compared to other trades and depend on experience, specialization, and location. Journeymen typically earn a competitive hourly wage, while master tailors or business owners may have variable income. Income is generally sufficient for a modest living in Iceland.

**Q: Can a non-EEA tailor get a work permit for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. A job offer is required, and the employer must prove no qualified EEA/EFTA worker is available. Since tailoring is not a shortage occupation, approval may be difficult. Self-employment permits are also possible but require a detailed business plan and sufficient funds.

**Q: Is there demand for traditional Icelandic costume tailors?**
A: Yes, there is a steady niche demand for traditional costumes (búningur) for national celebrations and cultural events. Skilled tailors in this area can find work, especially if they can market to tourists and local buyers.
