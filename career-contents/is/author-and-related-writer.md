# Author and Related Writer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2641 (ISCO08)**

Authors and related writers create literary works, articles, scripts, and other written content. In Iceland, this occupation is part of the creative sector, often working freelance or on contract for publishers, media, and cultural institutions. The labour market is small but active, with opportunities in book publishing, journalism, advertising, and digital content creation. Icelandic language proficiency is essential for most roles, though some English-language writing exists in tourism and academic sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression for Icelandic authors often starts with freelance writing for magazines or online platforms. Advancing to published books or lead writing roles requires building a portfolio and reputation. Some move into editing, publishing management, or teaching creative writing. The market rewards versatility, including skills in multimedia and content marketing.

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for an author in Iceland?**
A: Income varies widely; many writers work part-time or combine writing with other jobs. Full-time authors may earn from book royalties, grants, and freelance assignments. The Icelandic government offers some artist salaries, but competition is high.

**Q: Can a non-EEA author get a work permit in Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer from an Icelandic employer and the position cannot be filled locally. A bachelor's degree is usually required. The employer must advertise the job in Iceland and the EEA first.

**Q: What are the main opportunities for writers in Iceland?**
A: Publishing houses, newspapers, online media, advertising agencies, and tourism content providers. Translators and copywriters are also in demand.
