# Street and Related Services Workers — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9510 (ISCO08)**

Street and related services workers in Iceland operate kiosks, food stalls, or souvenir stands in public areas, often in Reykjavík and tourist destinations. Due to Iceland's cold climate and strict regulations on street vending, employment is limited and highly seasonal, peaking in summer. Workers must obtain permits from local municipalities and may need a hygiene certificate for food handling. The occupation is typically informal and low-skilled, with minimal career progression.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is rare; workers may advance to supervisory roles in retail or food service after gaining experience. Many use this job as a stepping stone to indoor positions such as shop assistants or café workers. Self-employment (e.g., owning a food truck) is possible but requires capital and permits.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

**Key growth areas:**

- tourism
- seasonal
- street food
- souvenirs

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street services workers in Iceland?**
A: Hourly wages range from 2,500 to 3,500 ISK (approx. 2.5–3.5 million ISK annually for full-time), but work is often part-time or seasonal. Tipped income can vary.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a street vendor in Iceland?**
A: Generally no. Non-EEA nationals need a work permit, which requires a job offer for a skilled occupation (wage > ~450,000 ISK/month) or a labour market test. Street vending is low-skilled and not eligible.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities?**
A: Limited. Some move into indoor retail or food service roles, or become self-employed with a food truck. Further training in hospitality management can lead to supervisory positions.
