# Fruit, Vegetable and Related Preserver — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7514 (ISCO08)**

Fruit, vegetable and related preservers process and preserve food products such as jams, pickles, canned goods, and dried items. In Iceland, this niche occupation is primarily found in small-to-medium enterprises focusing on local and organic produce, with a growing emphasis on value-added products for tourism and export. The work involves cleaning, cutting, cooking, and packaging, often in facilities adhering to strict hygiene and quality standards. Due to Iceland's short growing season, imported raw materials are common, but there is a push for local sourcing.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fruit and vegetable preserver in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience and employer. Entry-level positions often pay around 350,000–400,000 ISK per month, while experienced preservers can earn 450,000–550,000 ISK. Overtime and shift differentials may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a fruit preserver in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but a work permit is required. The employer must prove that no suitable EEA/EFTA worker is available (labour market test). The occupation is not on the shortage list, so approval is not guaranteed. It is advisable to have specialized skills or experience.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for preservers?**
A: With experience, you can become a supervisor, quality assurance specialist, or production manager. Some start their own small businesses producing artisanal jams or pickles. Further education in food science or business management can open more doors.
