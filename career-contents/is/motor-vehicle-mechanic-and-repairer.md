# Motor Vehicle Mechanic and Repairer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7231 (ISCO08)**

Motor vehicle mechanics and repairers maintain, diagnose, and fix cars, trucks, and other vehicles in Iceland, where harsh weather and rough terrain cause high wear. The occupation is vital due to Iceland's high car ownership and dependence on road transport. Mechanics work in independent garages, dealerships, and fleet maintenance. The field is evolving with electric and hybrid vehicles, requiring continuous learning. Certification from the Icelandic Industry Training Board (Iðnmennt) is mandatory for professional practice.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a 4-year apprenticeship or vocational program leading to a Journeyman's Certificate. Mechanics can advance to master mechanic, specialize in areas like EV diagnostics or heavy vehicles, or open their own workshop. Further certifications from manufacturers (e.g., Tesla, Volvo) enhance prospects. Some move into fleet management or automotive teaching. Continuing education is crucial due to technological advances.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use diagnostic tools to read standard fault codes and provide common solutions

- Automatically generate routine maintenance reminder reports from vehicle sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

- Practice using AR-assisted maintenance equipment

**Key growth areas:**

- Electric Vehicle Repair

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a motor vehicle mechanic in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, certification, and employer. Entry-level mechanics with a Journeyman's Certificate typically earn around 5-6 million ISK annually, while experienced specialists can earn 7-9 million ISK or more. Overtime and bonuses are common.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a mechanic in Iceland?**
A: If you are an EEA/EFTA national, no visa or work permit is required. Non-EEA nationals must obtain a work and residence permit, which requires a job offer, a positive labour market test, and recognized qualifications. The process can take several months.

**Q: Can I train as a mechanic in Iceland if I come from abroad?**
A: Yes, but you must be proficient in Icelandic for vocational programs. The main vocational school is Tækniskólinn in Reykjavik. Apprenticeships are also possible, but require finding an employer willing to take you on. EEA nationals have easier access; non-EEA may need a residence permit for studies.
