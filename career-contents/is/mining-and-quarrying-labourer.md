# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers in Iceland support extraction of basalt, pumice, rhyolite, and other materials for construction and industry. The sector is small-scale due to Iceland's limited mineral resources, with activity concentrated in rural areas near deposits. Work is seasonal, physically demanding, and often combined with other manual labour. The occupation is not considered skilled and generally does not require formal education, with training provided on the job.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Progression typically involves moving from labourer to machine operator (e.g., excavator or crusher operator) with experience and additional training. Supervisory roles (foreman) are possible after several years. Due to the small sector, career advancement may require relocation or transition to related fields like construction or transport. Formal qualifications (e.g., heavy machinery licence) can accelerate upward mobility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- Basalt quarrying
- Pumice extraction
- Construction aggregate
- Rhyolite mining

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining and quarrying labourer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements in the sector and are comparable to other manual labour roles. Earnings can vary by employer, experience, and season, but are generally at the lower end of the pay scale for unskilled workers.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a mining and quarrying labourer in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. Non-EEA nationals must obtain a work permit, which requires a labour market test to show no available EEA worker. Due to the low-skilled nature, permits are rarely granted. EEA/EFTA citizens have free movement.

**Q: What career advancement is possible from this role?**
A: With experience, labourers can progress to machine operator (e.g., excavator, crusher) or foreman. Additional training (e.g., heavy machinery licence) improves prospects. However, the small sector means limited openings.
