# Sales and Marketing Manager — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 1221 (ISCO08)**

Sales and Marketing Managers in Iceland oversee sales strategies and marketing campaigns across key sectors such as tourism, retail, and exports. They blend creativity with data analysis to drive revenue and brand awareness. The role is critical in Iceland's small, open economy, where international markets and seasonal tourism demand adaptive, multilingual professionals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically begins in sales or marketing roles, advancing to management after several years of proven results. Many managers hold a bachelor's or master's degree in business, marketing, or related fields. Networking and continuous learning (e.g., digital marketing certifications) are key. Some professionals move into director-level positions or start their own agencies.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Handle initial customer inquiries through AI chatbots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Automating complex data visualization to support decision-making through AI tools

- Uses AI to simulate marketing campaign results, quickly iterating plans

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Sales and Marketing Manager in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and industry, but monthly earnings are competitive. Entry-level managers may earn around 600,000 ISK, while experienced managers can exceed 1,200,000 ISK per month, often including performance bonuses.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as Sales and Marketing Managers in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but they need a job offer from an Icelandic employer. The employer must prove that no qualified EEA/EFTA candidate is available (labour market test). If approved, a work and residence permit is granted for up to one year, renewable.

**Q: Is Icelandic language proficiency required?**
A: Yes, most employers require fluency in Icelandic, as the role involves local market strategies. However, some international companies may accept English-only candidates for senior positions focused on exports.
