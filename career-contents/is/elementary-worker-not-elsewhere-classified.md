# Elementary Worker Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9629 (ISCO08)**

This broad occupation category covers elementary manual tasks across various sectors in Iceland, including cleaning, maintenance, simple assembly, and basic service roles. Workers are often employed in hospitality, retail, construction, or cleaning companies. The work is typically entry-level and requires minimal formal education, though on-the-job training is common. Many positions are held by immigrants, both from EEA countries and beyond. Due to the low skill requirement, wages are generally at the lower end of the Icelandic labour market, and career progression opportunities are limited without additional training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression from elementary work is possible through vocational training or accumulating experience to move into semi-skilled roles. Many workers transition to more specialised trades (e.g., cleaning supervisor, construction assistant) after a few years. Language skills (Icelandic) and safety certifications enhance mobility. However, without further education, most remain in similar roles. The work is often seasonal or part-time, especially in hospitality.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple goods handling and stacking: replaced by autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)
- Basic cleaning (e.g., sweeping, mopping): replaced by automatic floor scrubbers or robotic vacuums
- Item classification and sorting: replaced by computer vision systems
- Mail or document distribution: replaced by digital notification systems
- Inventory counting assistance: replaced by RFID and drones

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Task scheduling and priority arrangement: AI optimizes daily work lists
- Equipment failure warning: AI analyzes sensor data to remind of maintenance
- Customer communication records: AI automatic transcription and classification of feedback
- Safety compliance checks: AI-assisted identification of hazardous areas
- Basic communication in multilingual environments: assisted by real-time translation tools

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability to unstructured environments (e.g., ad-hoc assembly tasks)
- Complex emotional communication and customer service (e.g., appeasing dissatisfied customers)
- Cross-task flexible decision-making (when AI recommendations are unclear)
- Fine manual operations (e.g., art packaging)
- Teamwork and on-site coordination skills

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (managing tasks using tablets/mobile phones)
- Operate semi-automated equipment (e.g., cleaning robots)
- Basic Data Analysis (Understanding Task Efficiency Reports)
- Interpersonal communication and customer service skills
- Basic English or a second language (to adapt to multicultural work environments)
- Safety and compliance basic training

**Key growth areas:**

- Cleaning services
- Maintenance
- Hospitality support
- Seasonal labor

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for elementary workers in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are modest, usually at or slightly above minimum wage (hourly). Average monthly earnings are significantly lower than skilled occupations, often between 300,000-400,000 ISK before tax depending on sector and experience. Many positions offer overtime during peak seasons.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit for an elementary job in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. Work permits for low-skilled occupations are rarely approved. The employer must prove no EEA worker is available, and the job must meet specific criteria. Most non-EEA workers in elementary jobs come through other channels, such as family reunification or study permits.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for an elementary worker?**
A: With experience and language skills, workers can become team leaders or supervisors. Vocational courses (e.g., cleaning management) can lead to higher positions. Some transition to trades like carpentry or plumbing after completing an apprenticeship.
