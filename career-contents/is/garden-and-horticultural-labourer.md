# Garden and Horticultural Labourer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9214 (ISCO08)**

Garden and horticultural labourers in Iceland perform manual tasks in plant cultivation, landscaping, and maintenance of green spaces. This includes planting, weeding, pruning, watering, and harvesting. The sector spans private gardens, public parks, greenhouses, and agricultural farms. Work is often seasonal, with peak demand in summer and during harvest. Many workers are employed by municipalities, landscaping companies, or greenhouse operations. The job requires physical stamina and the ability to work outdoors in Iceland's variable climate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from general labourer to specialized roles like greenhouse technician, landscape installer, or groundskeeper. With experience and vocational training, workers can advance to supervisory positions or start their own landscaping businesses. Formal education such as a certificate in horticulture from a agricultural college (e.g., Hvanneyri) can accelerate advancement. Some transition to farm management or park administration.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic lawnmowers for grass cutting.
- Automatically water plants based on soil moisture sensor readings.
- Using AI vision robots for weed identification and removal
- Conduct large-scale vegetation status monitoring using drones

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI plant identification apps to quickly diagnose pests and diseases
- Using smart irrigation systems to optimize water and fertilizer management
- Preview landscape design schemes with AR tools
- Predicts plant growth cycles using data analysis tools

**Human moat:**

- Creative Design and Plant Pairing for Complex Landscapes
- Client personalized needs communication and solution customization
- Fine care of rare or sensitive plants
- On-site emergency response (e.g., rescue after extreme weather)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate and maintain smart gardening equipment (e.g., automatic mowers, drones)
- Use gardening management software (e.g. landscape design CAD, plant databases)
- Basic data analysis: interpreting sensor data to optimize maintenance plans.
- Plant pathology and use of AI-assisted diagnostic tools
- Sustainable gardening practices (such as organic planting, water-saving techniques)
- Safety operation and emergency response skills

**Key growth areas:**

- landscaping
- organic farming
- greenhouse production
- public parks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garden labourer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary but average around 350,000–450,000 ISK per month for entry-level positions, with experienced workers earning up to 550,000 ISK. Seasonal work may offer hourly rates starting at 2,500–3,000 ISK.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals get a work permit for this occupation?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult due to the labour market test. Employers must prove the job cannot be filled locally. The permit is typically tied to a specific employer and job offer, with limited mobility.
