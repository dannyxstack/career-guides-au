# University and Higher Education Teachers — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2310 (ISCO08)**

University and higher education teachers in Iceland lecture and conduct research at universities and other tertiary institutions. The profession is concentrated in Reykjavík and Akureyri, with a strong link to research funding and international collaboration. Most positions require a PhD, and employment is typically stable but competitive. The sector has grown with increased university enrollment and emphasis on research output.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically follows the academic ladder: lecturer to senior lecturer to professor, with increasing research expectations. Many hold PhDs from abroad. Union representation (e.g., FHÍ) supports conditions. International collaboration is common, and a sabbatical system exists. Tenure is not automatic but possible after probation. Entrepreneurship and industry partnerships offer alternative paths.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making
- Creative integration in overall curriculum design

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI education tools (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning).
- Data analysis and statistical modeling (Python/R)
- Prompt engineering and large model fine-tuning
- Blended instructional design (MOOC/flipped classroom)
- Academic writing and AI-assisted polishing
- Data Privacy and AI Ethics

**Key growth areas:**

- Research

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a university teacher in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by rank (lecturer, senior lecturer, professor) and institution. Professors earn ISK 8-12 million annually, while lecturers start around ISK 5-7 million. Exact figures are available from Statistics Iceland or union contracts (FHÍ). Pre-tax salaries are competitive, but cost of living is high.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a university teacher in Iceland if I am from outside the EU/EEA?**
A: Yes, non-EEA nationals need a work and residence permit for qualified professionals. You must have a job offer from an Icelandic university, and the employer must conduct a labour market test to show no suitable EEA worker is available. Processing takes 1-3 months. EU/EEA nationals can work freely without a permit.

**Q: Is a PhD required to teach at Icelandic universities?**
A: Generally yes for professor and senior lecturer positions. Some lecturer positions may accept a Master's degree plus extensive professional experience. Teaching qualifications are not mandatory but enhance competitiveness.
