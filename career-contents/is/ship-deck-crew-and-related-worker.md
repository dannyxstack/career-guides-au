# Seafarer (Deck Crew) — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

In Iceland, ships' deck crews and related workers are essential to the maritime industry, which is a cornerstone of the economy due to fishing and shipping. Deck crew members, including able seamen and deckhands, perform tasks such as navigation watchkeeping, mooring, cargo handling, and maintenance. The job typically requires physical stamina, teamwork, and adaptability to harsh weather conditions. Many workers are employed on fishing vessels, cargo ships, and passenger ferries serving tourism routes. Formal vocational training is available, but on-the-job learning is common. The field offers a blend of traditional seafaring and modern maritime technology.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for deck crews in Iceland typically starts as a deckhand or ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman, then to deck officer (e.g., second officer, chief mate, and ultimately ship captain). Formal education at Iceland's Maritime School or equivalent is increasingly required for officer ranks. On-the-job experience and short courses enhance promotion prospects. Some seafarers transition to shore-based roles in maritime administration, port operations, or marine surveying. The career path rewards continuous learning and adaptability to new technologies.

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for deck crew in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by rank, experience, and vessel type. Deckhands earn a base pay plus overtime and sea-time bonuses. The industry standard is set by collective agreements, with entry-level pay starting around the minimum collective wage for seafarers. Higher ranks and longer sea time yield higher earnings.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as deck crew in Iceland?**
A: Non-EEA nationals can apply for a work and residence permit if they have a job offer and the position passes a labour market test. However, deck crew positions may not qualify as 'qualified professionals' because they do not require advanced education, making permit approval less likely. EEA nationals have easier access via free movement.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities for deck crew?**
A: Experienced deckhands can advance to able seaman, then to deck officer through formal education (e.g., navigation diploma). Officer ranks progress from third officer to chief mate and captain. Shore-side roles in maritime administration are also possible with further qualifications.
