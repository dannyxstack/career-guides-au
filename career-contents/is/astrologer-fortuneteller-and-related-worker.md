# Astrologer, Fortune-teller and Related Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 5161 (ISCO08)**

Astrologers, fortune-tellers and related workers provide personal guidance and entertainment through esoteric practices such as horoscope reading, tarot, palmistry, and clairvoyance. In Iceland, this is a very small, niche occupation, primarily operating in the private sector, often as self-employed individuals or small business owners. The market is limited due to the country's small population (around 376,000) and a cultural tendency toward pragmatism. Work is often seasonal, peaking during tourist seasons and around holidays. Income is highly variable and generally low, with most practitioners supplementing their earnings from other sources. The occupation is not regulated by the government, and there are no formal educational requirements. Most practitioners learn through apprenticeships, online courses, or personal study. The field is considered part of the personal services sector within the broader creative and wellness industries.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for an astrologer in Iceland?**
A: Income is highly variable and generally low. Most practitioners earn between 500,000 and 1,500,000 ISK per year, often part-time. Full-time income rarely exceeds 3,000,000 ISK annually. Many rely on additional jobs.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as an astrologer in Iceland?**
A: No. Astrologers do not qualify for a work and residence permit for qualified professionals, as it is not considered a skilled occupation requiring a degree or specialized training. Only EEA/EFTA nationals can work in this field in Iceland.

**Q: Is there any regulation or licensing for fortune-tellers in Iceland?**
A: No. The occupation is unregulated. However, if you operate as a business (self-employed), you must register with the tax authorities (RSK) and pay taxes. Some municipalities may require general business permits, but there is no specific license for esoteric services.
