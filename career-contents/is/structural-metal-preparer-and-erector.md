# Structural Metal Preparer and Erector — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7214 (ISCO08)**

Structural metal preparers and erectors in Iceland are skilled tradespeople who assemble, install, and repair metal frameworks for buildings, bridges, and industrial structures such as geothermal power plants and hydroelectric dams. With Iceland's ongoing infrastructure development and renewable energy projects, these workers are in high demand. The role typically requires vocational training and certification, and offers a direct route to permanent residence for non-EEA nationals due to the occupation's inclusion on the shortage list.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational diploma or apprenticeship (4 years). After certification, erectors can advance to lead erector, supervisor, or project manager roles. Some specialise in welding or crane operation. Cross-training in rigging and scaffolding is common. With experience, opportunities arise in offshore installations or heavy industrial projects. Self-employment as a subcontractor is also viable for those with a solid network.

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a structural metal erector in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and project, but entry-level erectors earn around 400,000-500,000 ISK per month, while experienced journeypersons can earn 600,000-800,000 ISK. Overtime and travel allowances (especially for remote projects) can increase earnings. Note: all figures are pre-tax and in line with collective bargaining agreements.

**Q: Do I need a job offer before applying for a work permit as a structural metal erector?**
A: Yes, non-EEA/EFTA nationals must have a job offer from an Icelandic employer before applying for a work permit. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA/EFTA worker is available, but this requirement is waived if the occupation is on the shortage list (which this one is). A signed contract and proof of qualifications are required.

**Q: Can I progress to a supervisor role without further formal education?**
A: Yes, with several years of experience and demonstrated leadership, you can become a foreman or site supervisor. Some employers require additional safety certifications (e.g., Vinnueftirlitið's supervisor course) but not a degree. Further vocational training in project management is available through the Icelandic Vocational Education Council.
