# Glaziers — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7125 (ISCO08)**

Glaziers in Iceland install, replace, and repair glass in windows, doors, facades, and interior partitions. Working primarily on commercial and residential construction sites, they handle various glass types (tempered, laminated, double-glazed) and use specialized tools and sealants. The job requires precision, physical stamina, and comfort with heights. Due to Iceland's harsh climate, glaziers also focus on thermal insulation and weatherproofing. The occupation is part of the broader construction sector, which has been active due to urban growth and tourism infrastructure.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for glaziers in Iceland typically starts with an apprenticeship leading to a journeyman's certificate. Experienced glaziers can advance to supervisor or project manager roles, or specialize in areas like historic restoration, safety glass, or energy-efficient installations. Self-employment is common, with many running small contracting businesses. Continuing education in new materials and techniques is important for advancement. The profession offers stability but requires ongoing adaptation to building standards.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a glazier in Iceland?**
A: Salaries for glaziers vary by experience and employer. Entry-level apprentices earn less, while experienced journeymen can expect competitive pay in line with other construction trades. Overtime and specialisation can increase earnings significantly.

**Q: Can a non-EEA glazier get a work permit for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is not straightforward. The employer must prove a labour shortage by advertising the job locally. The occupation may be on the shortage list, which facilitates the process. A relevant qualification and job offer are required.

**Q: What is the demand for glaziers in Iceland?**
A: There is moderate demand, especially during construction booms. Skilled glaziers are valued, but the market fluctuates. Specialisation in energy-efficient or historic glazing can improve prospects.
