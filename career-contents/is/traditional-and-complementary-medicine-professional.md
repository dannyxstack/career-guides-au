# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Iceland practice non-conventional healthcare modalities such as acupuncture, herbal medicine, homeopathy, and naturopathy. The field is small but growing, driven by increasing interest in holistic and preventive health. Practitioners often work in private clinics, wellness centers, or as independent contractors. The profession is not fully regulated in Iceland, meaning title protection varies; some modalities require certification from recognized institutions. Most practitioners have international training, as local educational programs are limited. The sector integrates with Iceland's thriving tourism industry, offering wellness retreats and spa services.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for traditional and complementary medicine professionals in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary greatly by modality, experience, and client volume. Many practitioners are self-employed. A full-time acupuncturist might earn 4-8 million ISK annually, while those with established practices can earn over 10 million ISK. Part-time practitioners often earn less.

**Q: Can a non-EEA citizen work as a complementary medicine professional in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The profession is not on the shortage list, so a labour market test is required. Self-employment is possible with a business plan. EEA/EFTA nationals have free movement. Non-EEA applicants need a job offer meeting minimum salary thresholds, which may be difficult due to the occupation's typically lower earnings.

**Q: Is a license required to practice complementary medicine in Iceland?**
A: Not uniformly. Acupuncture and certain modalities may be loosely regulated if they overlap with physiotherapy or nursing. Otherwise, there is no mandatory license, but training from a recognized school is strongly recommended for credibility and insurance purposes.
