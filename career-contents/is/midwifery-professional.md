# Midwifery Professional — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2222 (ISCO08)**

Midwifery professionals in Iceland provide comprehensive care to women during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period, including neonatal care. They work in hospitals, health centres, and sometimes in home birth settings. The profession is regulated by the Icelandic Directorate of Health, requiring a Bachelor of Science in Midwifery from the University of Iceland (the sole training programme) and registration with the Icelandic Nursing and Midwifery Association. Midwives play a crucial role in the national healthcare system, with an emphasis on physiological birth and continuity of care.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,480. The employment outlook for midwifery professionals in Iceland is positive, driven by the retirement of experienced midwives and a steady demand for maternity services. The government has recognized midwifery as a shortage profession, with initiatives to increase training capacity and recruit internationally. Opportunities exist in rural areas where coverage is thin. However, competition for positions in Reykjavík can be stronger. Overall, new graduates find employment relatively easily, and there is potential for career progression into specialist roles.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Many midwives advance to lead midwife positions in hospitals or become independent practitioners. Further education, such as a master's degree in midwifery or public health, can open doors to teaching, research, or leadership roles in the Directorate of Health or university. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a midwife in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary with experience and sector. Midwives in hospitals typically earn a base salary of around 500,000 to 700,000 ISK per month before overtime. Experienced midwives can earn more. Public sector wages are standardized.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a midwife in Iceland if I am from the EU?**
A: No, EU/EEA nationals can work in Iceland without a visa due to EEA free movement. However, you must register your residence and have your midwifery qualifications recognized by the Directorate of Health.

**Q: Can I specialize as a midwife in Iceland?**
A: Yes, midwives can specialize in areas such as ultrasound, lactation counselling, or high-risk obstetrics through postgraduate education. There are also opportunities to move into teaching or management.
