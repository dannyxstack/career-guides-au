# Film, Stage and Related Director or Producer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2654 (ISCO08)**

Iceland's film and stage production industry is small but vibrant, with a growing reputation for international co-productions, location filming, and original content. The sector employs around 1,500 people, concentrated in Reykjavík and nearby regions. Directors and producers oversee creative and logistical aspects of film, television, theater, and other live performances, often working on a freelance or project basis. The industry is supported by government tax incentives and cultural funds, attracting both local talent and foreign productions. Language skills in Icelandic are not always required for certain technical roles, but fluency is advantageous for stage and local projects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as an assistant director, production assistant, or in a specialized role (e.g., script supervisor) before moving into directing or producing. Many Icelandic directors and producers have formal education from the Icelandic Academy of the Arts or university film programs, though experience and industry connections are equally valued. Advancement often involves building a reputation through successful projects, winning grants, and collaborating with established production companies. Some directors transition to teaching, while producers may move into executive roles at media companies or start their own production firms.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a film director or producer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on experience, project scale, and role. According to Statistics Iceland, average monthly earnings for directors and producers (ISCO 2654) are around 600,000–800,000 ISK before tax, but top earners in major productions can exceed 1,500,000 ISK per month. Freelancers often earn less due to gaps between projects.

**Q: Can a non-EEA film director move to Iceland for work?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer and a work permit. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable candidate was available in Iceland or the EEA (labour market test). The permit is tied to that specific job. Alternatively, a short-term permit for artists (max 1 year) is available for invited professionals on specific projects, without a labour market test.

**Q: Do I need to speak Icelandic to work as a director or producer in Iceland?**
A: For many film and TV projects, especially international co-productions, English is often the working language, and Icelandic is not required. However, for theater and local-language productions, fluency in Icelandic is essential. Learning Icelandic is beneficial for networking and integrating into the local industry.
