# Well Driller and Borer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 8113 (ISCO08)**

Well drillers and borers in Iceland operate drilling rigs to create boreholes for geothermal energy, water supply, or environmental monitoring. The role demands technical skill, physical stamina, and adaptability to remote and harsh conditions, often working in teams on high-demand geothermal projects. This occupation is essential to Iceland's renewable energy sector, with consistent demand driven by expansion of geothermal plants and infrastructure.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training in drilling, leading to a tradesperson role. Experienced drillers can advance to site supervisor, drilling manager, or specialize in horizontal or deep geothermal drilling. Further qualifications in engineering (e.g., mechanical) open paths to senior technical roles. Some drillers transition to consulting or equipment sales. The field rewards hands-on experience and continuous learning.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual adjustment of drilling speed and pressure is taken over by AI adaptive control systems
- Experience-based judgment of strata changes is replaced by real-time data analysis
- Record drilling logs and parameters, replaced by automated sensors and IoT.
- Repetitive maintenance checks (e.g., lubrication, bolt tightening) performed by robots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes geological data to assist in optimizing drilling paths and depths
- Drone and camera AI identification of well site safety risks
- Machine learning predicts equipment failures, reducing unplanned downtime
- Augmented Reality (AR) remote guidance for complex operations

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency decision-making and troubleshooting ability
- Adaptability assessment for complex terrain and abnormal geology
- Team coordination and safety leadership responsibilities
- Fine manual skills in equipment repair

**Skills to build:**

- Geological data analysis software (e.g., Leapfrog)
- PLC and automation control system operation
- Drone operation and AI image analysis
- Equipment health monitoring and predictive maintenance
- Basic programming (Python/R) for customized reports
- Safety and compliance management (e.g., ISO 45001)

**Key growth areas:**

- geothermal energy
- infrastructure
- water supply
- renewables

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a well driller in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are competitive, with experienced drillers earning above the national average. Entry-level positions start around 400,000 ISK per month, while seasoned professionals can earn over 700,000 ISK monthly, often with bonuses and allowances for remote work.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as well drillers in Iceland?**
A: Yes, through a work and residence permit for qualified professionals. You need a job offer from an Icelandic employer and the position must pass a labour market test to prove no local workers are available. Drilling is on the shortage list, which can expedite the process.

**Q: What advancement opportunities exist for well drillers?**
A: Experienced drillers can become crew supervisors or drilling managers, specialize in deep geothermal drilling, or move into related fields like engineering or equipment sales. Further education can lead to roles in project management or consultancy.
