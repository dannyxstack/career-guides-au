# Plasterer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers in Iceland apply plaster, stucco, and other materials to interior and exterior walls and ceilings, finishing surfaces for insulation and aesthetics. The occupation is part of the construction sector, with demand driven by housing and commercial building projects, as well as renovation of older buildings. Plasterers often work for construction firms or as self-employed contractors, and must adhere to strict building standards in Iceland's climate. Most are trained through vocational education and apprenticeships, with mandatory certification to practice legally.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for plasterers typically starts with an apprenticeship leading to a journeyman's certification. Experienced plasterers can become foremen or start their own contracting businesses. Specializing in decorative plastering or energy-efficient systems may open advanced opportunities. Some progress into construction management or teaching at vocational schools. The field offers stable work but limited formal promotion pathways.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation works
- Energy-efficient retrofitting
- New residential construction
- Commercial projects

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a plasterer in Iceland?**
A: While exact figures vary by experience and employer, plasterers in Iceland earn an average pre-tax annual salary around 6,000,000 ISK to 8,000,000 ISK. Journeymen may earn more. Salaries are subject to collective bargaining agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EEA plasterer get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The employer must prove that no EEA worker is available for the job (labour market test). The applicant must hold a relevant journeyman's certificate. The permit is temporary and tied to the employer.

**Q: Do I need formal education to work as a plasterer in Iceland?**
A: Yes, a journeyman's certificate is mandatory. This is typically obtained through a vocational programme and apprenticeship. Recognition of foreign qualifications may be possible through the Icelandic Construction Authority.
