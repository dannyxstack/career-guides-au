# Inquiry Clerk — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 4225 (ISCO08)**

Inquiry clerks handle customer inquiries and provide information across various sectors. In Iceland, they are commonly found in tourist information centres, retail stores, and government offices, where they assist both locals and visitors with questions, directions, and services. The role requires strong communication skills and often multilingual abilities, given the high number of tourists.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically leads to supervisory roles in customer service or specialised information officer positions. Experience and multilingual skills enhance prospects, with some moving into tourism management or public relations. On-the-job training is common, and further education in hospitality or administration can open up higher-level opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Handle customer inquiries for general information about products or services
- Provides standardized process guidelines and frequently asked questions
- Records and transfers simple complaints to relevant departments
- Update and maintain customer information databases.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI real-time knowledge bases to quickly retrieve answers to complex questions.
- Using sentiment analysis tools to identify customer emotions and adjust communication strategies
- Optimize multilingual customer communication using AI-assisted scripts
- Use automatic summarization tools to quickly record call key points

**Human moat:**

- building long-term customer trust and personalised service

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an inquiry clerk in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Entry-level positions are often paid according to collective agreements, with average earnings reflecting the lower end of the services sector. For precise figures, refer to Statistics Iceland's salary data.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals become inquiry clerks in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not considered skilled. Non-EEA nationals must have a job offer and pass a labour market test, but employers must prioritise EEA/EFTA workers. Permits are rarely granted for this role.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for inquiry clerks in Iceland?**
A: With experience, inquiry clerks can move into supervisory roles, tourism information management, or switch to public relations and administration. Additional training in languages or tourism can accelerate progression.
