# Physicist and Astronomer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2111 (ISCO08)**

Physicists and astronomers in Iceland work primarily in academia, research institutes, and the renewable energy sector. The field is small but internationally connected, with key employers including the University of Iceland, Reykjavik University, and the Icelandic Meteorological Office. Research areas range from geothermal energy and climate science to space physics and aurora studies. Given Iceland's unique geology and location, there are niche opportunities in geophysics and atmospheric research. Most positions require advanced degrees (MSc or PhD), and the labor market is competitive due to limited openings. Collaboration with international projects is common, such as those at the Arctic region or in astronomy.

Some physicists transition to industry roles in geothermal energy companies (e.g., HS Orka, Landsvirkjun) or tech startups. A PhD is often required for permanent positions in academia. The field is intellectually rewarding but offers limited vertical mobility due to the small size of the sector. Networking and international collaboration are essential for advancement. Mid-career opportunities in management or policy (e.g., at the Ministry of Education) are rare but possible.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physicist in Iceland?**
A: Salaries for physicists and astronomers in Iceland are competitive and follow public sector collective agreements. Entry-level academic positions (e.g., postdoc) offer around 600,000 ISK per month, while senior researchers and professors earn significantly more. Industry roles in energy or tech may pay higher but require relevant specialization. Exact figures vary by employer and experience.

**Q: How can a non-EEA physicist obtain a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Non-EEA physicists generally need a job offer from an Icelandic employer. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA/EFTA candidate is available (labor market test). A work and residence permit for qualified professionals is then issued, usually valid for one year and renewable. Alternatively, researchers with a hosting agreement from an approved institution can apply for a researcher permit, which bypasses the labor market test.
