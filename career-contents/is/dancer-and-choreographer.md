# Dancer and Choreographer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

In Iceland, dancers and choreographers work primarily in the performing arts sector, including theaters, dance companies, and cultural events. The small domestic market means many professionals are self-employed or work on project-based contracts. Opportunities are concentrated in Reykjavik, with seasonal peaks during festivals and tourist seasons. The field is competitive and often combined with teaching or related arts work.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a dancer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and contract type. Full-time dancers in theaters may earn around the collective agreement minimum for performing artists, while freelancers' incomes depend on project volume. Teaching and additional gigs often supplement earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EEA dancer get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from an Icelandic employer and a labour market test to show no suitable EEA candidate is available. The visa is tied to that specific job and must be renewed if changing employers.

**Q: What career opportunities exist beyond performing?**
A: Experienced dancers often move into choreography, teaching at schools or studios, or arts administration. Some also work in fitness instruction or event planning.
