# Librarian and Related Information Professional — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2622 (ISCO08)**

Librarians and related information professionals manage collections of books, journals, digital resources, and provide information services to users. In Iceland, they work in public municipal libraries, academic libraries (e.g., at the University of Iceland and Reykjavík University), special libraries (government, corporate, health), and the National and University Library of Iceland. The role has evolved significantly with digitalization; librarians now curate digital archives, assist with research data management, and teach information literacy. Icelandic language proficiency is essential for most positions, as services are primarily in Icelandic, though English is common in academic settings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from library assistant or technician roles, often requiring a BA in library and information science. Advancing to librarian or senior librarian positions usually requires an MA/MLIS. Specialization paths include digital curation, data librarianship, and subject specialist roles (e.g., law, health). Some librarians move into records management, information architecture, or IT roles. Continuous professional development is encouraged, and membership in the Icelandic Library Association can aid networking. Leadership roles like library director are attainable with experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cataloging and metadata generation (AI can automatically extract keywords and classification numbers from full text)
- Basic reference inquiries (chatbots answer common questions like opening hours, borrowing rules)
- document delivery and reservation processing (AI scheduling system automatically allocates resources)
- Collection inventory and shelf checking (RFID + AI vision automatic identification of misplaced books)
- Overdue reminder notices and fine calculations (automated email/SMS system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- In-depth targeted search (AI analyzes user needs, selects high-relevance literature from multiple databases).
- Digital Literacy Education (AI-powered interactive learning scenarios teaching database search strategies)
- Digitization of special collections and knowledge graph construction (AI-assisted recognition of manuscript text and linking historical events)
- Reader behavior analysis (AI mining borrowing data to optimize collection purchasing and space layout)
- Multilingual resource recommendations (AI translation matching non-English users' needs)

**Human moat:**

- Critically evaluating information quality (judging authority and bias of AI-provided results)
- Complex research consultation (interdisciplinary issues requiring understanding of user true intent and context)
- Community service and reading promotion (organizing activities, building emotional connections with readers)
- Digital rights and privacy management (deciding which data can be public and how to use it compliantly)
- Cross-institutional collaboration and resource sharing (negotiating license agreements, promoting open access)

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool application (e.g., querying databases with natural language, training small classification models)
- Data ethics and privacy protection (understanding algorithmic bias, personal information protection regulations)
- User experience design (designing AI-assisted search interfaces and self-service processes)
- Digital curation (managing digital collection lifecycle, including metadata standards)
- Project Management and Change Management (coordinating teams and processes when introducing AI systems)
- Basic programming (Python/API calls, automating repetitive tasks)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital archiving
- Data management
- Information literacy
- Open access

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are the working hours like for librarians in Iceland?**
A: Most librarians work full-time, 40 hours per week, often including some evening or weekend shifts, especially in public libraries. Academic libraries may have more regular hours but also provide on-call services.

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a librarian in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Entry-level librarians may earn around 450,000 ISK per month, while experienced professionals in senior roles can earn 600,000-700,000 ISK. Public sector salaries are regulated by collective agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EEA librarian get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer that meets the minimum salary threshold (currently around 350,000 ISK per month) and the employer demonstrates that no qualified EEA candidate was available. Icelandic language skills are often required, which can be a barrier.
