# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Iceland are licensed healthcare professionals who assess, diagnose, and treat movement dysfunction and pain. They work in hospitals, rehabilitation centers, private clinics, and home care, addressing the needs of an aging population and a sporty nation. With a strong emphasis on evidence-based practice, they collaborate with doctors and nurses in multidisciplinary teams. The profession is regulated by the Directorate of Health, requiring a university degree and licensure. Demand is high due to demographic trends and Iceland's active lifestyle, making it a stable and rewarding career path.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Physiotherapists in Iceland can progress from clinical roles to senior therapist, team leader, or clinic manager. Specialization in areas like sports physiotherapy, neurology, or pediatrics is common and valued. Many pursue master's or PhD degrees to enter research, teaching, or advanced practice. Private practice ownership is a viable path, especially in underserved regions. Continuing education is mandatory for license renewal, encouraging lifelong learning. Networking through the Icelandic Physiotherapy Association can open doors to collaboration and leadership opportunities.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for physiotherapists in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience, sector, and location. Starting salaries for newly licensed physiotherapists in the public sector are around ISK 4.5-5.5 million annually, while experienced specialists can earn over ISK 7 million. Private practice incomes may differ. For precise data, consult Statistics Iceland or the Icelandic Physiotherapy Association.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a physiotherapist in Iceland if I am from the EU?**
A: No, if you are an EU/EEA/EFTA national. You have the right to work in Iceland under the EEA Agreement. However, you must register with the Directorate of Immigration if staying more than 3 months. Non-EEA nationals need a work permit and a license.
