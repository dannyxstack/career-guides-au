# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Medical imaging and therapeutic equipment technicians, commonly known as radiographers in Iceland, operate diagnostic imaging devices such as X-ray, MRI, CT, and ultrasound, as well as administer radiation therapy for cancer treatment. They work in hospitals, diagnostic centres, and private clinics across the country. In Iceland, this profession is regulated by the Directorate of Health and requires a university degree in radiography. The ageing population and steady demand for diagnostic imaging ensure stable employment opportunities, though the small labour market means competition can be moderate for specialised roles.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for radiographers in Iceland?**
A: Salaries for radiographers are competitive within the public healthcare sector, with entry-level pay starting around 5.5 million ISK per year and experienced specialists earning up to 8 million ISK. Exact figures depend on experience, location, and whether overtime is worked.

**Q: Can foreign-trained radiographers work in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but they must have their qualifications recognised by the Directorate of Health and demonstrate proficiency in Icelandic. EEA/EFTA nationals can work without a work permit but need recognition. Non-EEA nationals require a job offer and a work permit, subject to a labour market test.

**Q: What educational pathway is required to become a radiographer in Iceland?**
A: The standard pathway is a three-year Bachelor's degree in Radiography from the University of Iceland. The programme combines theoretical studies with clinical placements. Graduates must then apply for a license from the Directorate of Health. Other EU/EEA degrees may be accepted after assessment.
