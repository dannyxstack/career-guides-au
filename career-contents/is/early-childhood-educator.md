# Early Childhood Educator — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2342 (ISCO08)**

Early childhood educators in Iceland, known as leikskólakennarar, work with children aged 1-6 in preschools, focusing on play-based learning and development. The profession is regulated and requires a Bachelor's degree in Early Childhood Education from a recognized university. Iceland has a high demand for qualified preschool teachers due to a growing population and emphasis on early education. The working environment is collaborative, with a strong focus on Nordic pedagogical traditions, including outdoor activities and inclusive education. Employment is primarily in the public sector, but private preschools also exist. The role is highly respected and offers stability, with collective agreements governing salaries and conditions.

Career progression for early childhood educators in Iceland typically starts with a B.Ed. degree and a teaching license. Advancement includes roles such as head of department, assistant principal, or principal of a preschool. Experienced educators may specialise in inclusive education, curriculum development, or parental guidance. Further study can lead to roles in educational policy or teacher training. The sector values continuous professional development, and many educators pursue master's degrees to advance. There is also a path to becoming an independent consultant or opening a private preschool.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for early childhood educators in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements between the teachers' union (KÍ) and the government. Starting salary for a fully qualified preschool teacher is around 500,000 ISK per month, with increases based on experience and education. The exact amount depends on the municipality and seniority. For up-to-date figures, consult the union's pay scale.

**Q: What are the visa requirements for non-EEA early childhood educators?**
A: Non-EEA nationals need a job offer and a work permit. The employer must demonstrate that no qualified local candidate is available (labour market test). Since early childhood educators are on the shortage list, the process is faster. The permit is tied to the employer and is typically valid for one year, renewable. After four years, you may apply for permanent residence.

**Q: Can I work as an early childhood educator in Iceland with a foreign degree?**
A: Yes, but your qualifications must be assessed by the Directorate of Education. You may need to complete supplementary courses to meet Icelandic standards. A degree from an EU/EEA country is generally recognised. Once approved, you can apply for a teaching license.
