# Stonemason — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7113 (ISCO08)**

Stonemasons in Iceland work with natural stone in construction, restoration, and landscaping. The profession is vital for preserving Iceland's historic buildings and supporting modern infrastructure projects, particularly in geothermal energy and hydroelectric plants. Stonemasons cut, shape, and install stone for walls, façades, and decorative elements. The trade requires precision, physical stamina, and adaptability to Iceland's variable climate. With a strong tradition of craftsmanship, stonemasonry offers stable employment in both urban and rural areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Most stonemasons start as apprentices through the Icelandic vocational education system, progressing to journeyman after 4 years. Some specialize in restoration or monumental masonry. Experienced stonemasons can become supervisors, start their own firms, or teach in vocational schools. The industry values practical experience, and continued learning in new techniques (e.g., dry-stone construction) enhances career mobility.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a stonemason in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Journeymen earn competitive wages, typically in line with other skilled trades in the construction sector. Overtime and project bonuses can increase earnings.

**Q: Do non-EEA stonemasons need a job offer before applying for a work permit?**
A: Yes. A valid job offer from an Icelandic employer is mandatory. The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled locally. If the occupation is on the shortage list, the labour market test is simplified.

**Q: Can I learn stonemasonry in Iceland without prior experience?**
A: Yes. The vocational education system offers apprenticeships for beginners. You typically need to find an employer willing to take you on as an apprentice. Some schools offer pre-apprenticeship courses.
