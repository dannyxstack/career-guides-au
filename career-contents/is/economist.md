# Economist — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2631 (ISCO08)**

Economists in Iceland analyze data, forecast trends, and advise on fiscal and monetary policy, working mainly for the Central Bank, ministries, research institutes, banks, and consulting firms. The profession is shaped by Iceland's small open economy and its unique economic history, including the 2008 financial crisis and subsequent tourism boom. Demand is steady, especially in areas like macroeconomics, natural resource economics (fisheries, energy), and tourism analytics. A master's degree is the standard entry requirement, and many economists also study abroad. The labour market values quantitative rigor and policy relevance, with opportunities in both public and private sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior economist in government or research, moving to senior economist or policy advisor within 5-10 years. Specialists in energy, labour, or macroeconomics are particularly valued. A PhD can lead to roles at the Central Bank or academia. Experienced economists often advance to chief economist or director positions, or pivot into consulting or private sector management. International experience is highly regarded.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic data cleaning and sorting
- Create standard statistical charts and reports
- Preliminary regression analysis and model runs.
- Automated search and summarization of literature reviews
- Generate periodic economic forecast templates

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Rapid iteration and validation of complex economic models
- Real-time economic indicator monitoring and anomaly warning
- Policy impact simulation and scenario analysis
- Integration and insight extraction from multi-source heterogeneous data.
- Rapid generation of personalized economic advisory reports

**Human moat:**

- Understanding the political and social constraints behind economic policy
- Propose innovative theoretical frameworks and hypotheses
- Make value judgments and ethical considerations.
- Building trust and engaging in high-level communication with decision-makers
- Strategic decision-making in uncertain environments

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R Programming and Data Science Tools
- Machine learning and causal inference methods
- Data visualization and storytelling
- Natural language processing for text data analysis
- Explainability techniques for AI models.
- Interdisciplinary knowledge integration ability

**Key growth areas:**

- Green energy economics
- Tourism analytics
- Macroeconomic forecasting
- Data-driven policy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an economist in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level economists in government earn around 6-7 million ISK per year, while senior roles in banking can exceed 12 million ISK. The Central Bank offers competitive pay.

**Q: Can a non-EEA economist get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer as a qualified professional. Your employer must undergo a labour market test, and you must hold at least a master's degree. The permit is tied to that employer for the first 2 years.

**Q: What industries employ most economists in Iceland?**
A: The Central Bank, Ministry of Finance, Statistics Iceland, commercial banks, and energy companies are the largest employers. Research institutes and consulting firms also hire economists.
