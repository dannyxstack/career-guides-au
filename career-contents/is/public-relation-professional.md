# Public Relations Professional — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2432 (ISCO08)**

Public relations professionals in Iceland manage communication between organizations and the public, shaping corporate image, handling media relations, and crafting strategic messaging. Given Iceland's close-knit business environment and strong tourism sector, PR roles are vital in both private and public sectors. The field is growing with increasing demand for digital communication and crisis management expertise. Many PR professionals work in agencies, in-house for companies like Icelandair or Marel, or for government institutions. Fluency in Icelandic is typically required, though some international roles may use English. The occupation is recognized under ISCO-08 2432.

Many professionals specialize in areas like corporate communications, public affairs, or social media. Experience in multiple sectors (e.g., tourism, government) is common. ## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong writing and interpersonal skills who enjoy managing brand reputation and media relations.
- Professionals comfortable in a bilingual environment (Icelandic and English), with adaptability to a small but dynamic market.
- Those interested in strategic communication, digital content, and crisis management in sectors like tourism or energy.

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking a purely creative role without business strategy or metrics; PR in Iceland requires measurable outcomes.
- Individuals unwilling to learn Icelandic or with low tolerance for a tight-knit, network-driven labour market.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a public relations professional in Iceland?**
A: Entry-level PR assistants earn around 5–6 million ISK annually, while experienced PR managers or directors can earn 8–12 million ISK. Salaries vary by sector, with private companies often paying more than government. Digital and crisis specialists may command higher rates.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work in Iceland as a PR professional?**
A: Yes, but a work permit is required. You need a concrete job offer from an Icelandic employer, which must advertise the position to EEA/EFTA candidates first (labour market test). The job usually must meet minimum salary thresholds. The process can take months, and permits are tied to the employer.
