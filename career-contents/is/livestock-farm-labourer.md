# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers in Iceland perform daily tasks related to animal care and farm maintenance, including feeding, milking, cleaning barns, and monitoring animal health. They work on farms raising cattle, sheep, horses, or poultry, often outdoors in Iceland's variable climate. The role is essential to Iceland's agricultural sector, which focuses on self-sufficiency for dairy, meat, and wool. Labourers may work on family-run farms or larger commercial operations, and tasks vary seasonally from lambing and calving to haymaking and winter feeding.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a general labourer, advancing to specialised roles like milking technician or animal health assistant. With experience and training, workers can become herd managers or farm supervisors. Some pursue formal education in agriculture at Hvanneyri University to become farm managers or agricultural consultants. However, the career ladder is short, and many remain in the same role long-term.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic health data entry and case organization
- Translation and localisation of standard health education materials
- Appointment management and follow-up reminder calls
- Basic chronic disease knowledge Q&A (e.g., diabetes diet)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze community health data to identify high-risk groups and trends
- Use generative AI to create multilingual, culturally adapted educational videos and diagrams
- Preliminary health screening and triage via AI voice assistants
- Use AI-enabled remote monitoring tools to track chronic patient conditions
- AI-assisted report writing and policy proposal generation

**Human moat:**

- Cultural safety practices: deep understanding of Indigenous customs, history, and trust building
- Community Relationship Maintenance: Face-to-Face Emotional Support and Crisis Intervention
- Cross-cultural medical translation and advocacy: accurately convey medical information while preserving patient dignity
- Complex Ethical Decisions: Balancing Traditional and Modern Medicine
- On-site visits and home care: hands-on work that cannot be replaced by a screen

**Skills to build:**

- Master community health data analysis tools (e.g., Power BI, Tableau)
- Learn to create AI-driven health education content (e.g., Canva, Synthesia)
- Familiarity with telehealth platforms and electronic health record systems
- Basic natural language processing knowledge: for evaluating AI translation quality
- Trauma-informed care and mental health first aid certification
- Project management skills: coordinating multiple partners

**Key growth areas:**

- automation
- organic farming
- animal welfare
- sustainability

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by farm and experience, typically ranging from 300,000 to 450,000 ISK per month before taxes, with possible benefits like housing or meals on larger farms.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a livestock farm labourer in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. Work permits for low-skilled occupations are rarely issued because employers must prove no local or EEA worker is available. Most labourers are Icelandic or from EU/EEA countries.
