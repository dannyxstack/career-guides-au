# Building Caretaker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 5153 (ISCO08)**

Building caretakers in Iceland are responsible for the daily maintenance, cleaning, and security of residential and commercial properties. This role is essential in a country with harsh winters, involving snow removal, minor repairs, and systems monitoring. Demand is steady due to a growing housing stock and tourism-related facilities, though the occupation is often informal with on-the-job training. Caretakers work in apartment blocks, offices, hotels, and public buildings, providing a versatile entry point into property management.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building caretaker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer. Entry-level caretakers often earn around minimum wage (ISK 400,000–500,000 per month), while experienced caretakers in larger properties may earn ISK 600,000–700,000 monthly. Many positions include accommodation or a housing allowance, which increases total compensation.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national become a building caretaker in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult. Non-EEA nationals generally need a work permit, but building caretaker is not on the shortage list, so a labour market test is required. The Directorate of Labour must confirm no EEA worker is available. Permits are rarely issued for this occupation unless the employer can prove a genuine shortage.

**Q: What are the main duties of a building caretaker in Iceland?**
A: Duties include cleaning common areas, minor repairs (e.g., changing lightbulbs, fixing leaks), snow removal in winter, garbage disposal, monitoring heating and ventilation systems, and ensuring building security. Some caretakers also handle key management and basic administrative tasks.
