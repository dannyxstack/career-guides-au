# Athletes and Sports Players — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 3421 (ISCO08)**

Athletes and sports players in Iceland compete in various sports, often part-time due to the country's small professional sports market. Most pursue sports alongside other employment or education, with elite athletes receiving limited funding through national federations or sponsorships. The Icelandic sports system emphasizes amateur participation, though top performers in football, handball, and winter sports may achieve professional contracts abroad. Local opportunities for full-time athletes are scarce, leading many to train while working in unrelated fields.

Career progression in Icelandic sports typically moves from youth leagues to senior club teams, with top talents transitioning to professional clubs abroad, especially in Europe. For those who stay in Iceland, paths include becoming a coach, referee, or sports administrator. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a professional athlete in Iceland?**
A: Most Icelandic athletes earn modest incomes from sports; top footballers in the Icelandic league may earn 500,000–1,000,000 ISK per month, while others often receive little or no pay. Many rely on part-time jobs or sponsorship. International athletes playing abroad can earn significantly more.

**Q: Can a non-EEA athlete easily get a work permit to play in Iceland?**
A: No, it is difficult. The athlete must have a contract with an Icelandic club, and the job must pass a labour market test unless the sport is deemed to have a shortage. The permit is granted for up to two years and is tied to the employer. High-level athletes (e.g., national team members) have a better chance.

**Q: What career options exist for athletes after their sports career ends?**
A: Many transition to coaching, sports management, or fitness instruction. Others leverage their discipline to enter fields like physiotherapy, physical education teaching, or business. Some move into non-sports careers entirely, often supported by education undertaken during their athletic years.
