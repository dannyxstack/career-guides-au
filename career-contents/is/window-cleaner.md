# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners in Iceland are responsible for cleaning windows of commercial buildings, hotels, and private residences, often using water-fed poles, ladders, or harnesses for high-rise work. The occupation is part of the broader cleaning services sector, which includes both self-employed individuals and employees of cleaning companies. Demand is influenced by the tourism season, with increased work in hotels and restaurants, as well as by construction of new office and residential buildings. While considered low-skilled, it requires physical fitness, comfort with heights, and attention to detail.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from employed cleaner to team leader or supervisor within a cleaning company. With experience, one can start an independent business, contracting directly to property managers or hotels. Some window cleaners diversify into other cleaning services like pressure washing or facade restoration. Formal qualifications are not required, but certificates in working at height or handling chemicals can lead to higher-paying assignments. The occupation is often a stepping stone to other roles in facilities management or property maintenance.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Do I need certification to become a window cleaner?**
A: No formal certification is legally required. However, certificates in working at height, first aid, or handling cleaning chemicals are recommended and can improve job prospects.

**Q: What is the typical salary for window cleaners?**
A: Compensation is typically hourly, varies with experience and employer, and meets collective bargaining agreements. Rates are generally higher for high-rise or specialized work.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as window cleaners in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult as the occupation is not considered skilled. A work permit requires a labour market test and the salary usually does not meet minimum thresholds for skilled workers. EEA/EFTA nationals have free movement.
