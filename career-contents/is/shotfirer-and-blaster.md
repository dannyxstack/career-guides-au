# Shotfirer and Blaster — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters are specialized tradespeople responsible for safely handling and detonating explosives in mining, quarrying, construction, and infrastructure projects. In Iceland, they are essential for tunnel construction, geothermal energy development, and road building, often working in remote and challenging environments. The role requires rigorous safety training, precise execution, and certification from Icelandic authorities. Typically employed by mining companies, construction firms, or as independent contractors, shotfirers operate under strict regulations to ensure public and worker safety.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for shotfirers in Iceland typically begins with an apprenticeship or vocational training, followed by certification. With experience, they can advance to blasting supervisor, safety inspector, or operations manager. Some become self-employed contractors. Additional qualifications in rock mechanics or environmental safety open doors to consulting roles. Mobility is limited to similar roles in mining or heavy construction.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Geothermal energy
- Tunnel construction
- Mining
- Infrastructure development

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much can a shotfirer earn in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, employer, and project complexity. Due to the hazardous nature, shotfirers typically earn above average for trades, with experienced professionals often negotiating higher pay and overtime. Collective agreements in construction and mining set minimum rates.

**Q: Can a non-EEA shotfirer work in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but requires a work permit with a labour market test. The employer must prove no local or EEA candidate is available. A valid Icelandic blasting certificate is mandatory, and proficiency in English or Icelandic is expected.

**Q: What training is needed to become a shotfirer in Iceland?**
A: Typically a three-year apprenticeship in a relevant trade (e.g., mining, construction) plus a specialized blasting course. Alternatively, a vocational diploma in blasting technology. All must pass the national blasting certificate exam.
