# Health Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers allied health professionals such as physiotherapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, and speech therapists. In Iceland, they work in hospitals, primary care centres, rehabilitation clinics, and private practice. The healthcare system values interdisciplinary teams, and these roles are integral to patient recovery and chronic disease management. Demand is steady, driven by an aging population and increased focus on preventive and rehabilitative care. Licensing by the Directorate of Health is mandatory, and many professionals come from other EEA countries due to labour shortages.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong interpersonal skills and empathy who enjoy helping others achieve better health.
- Detail-oriented practitioners who can work in multidisciplinary teams and adapt to patient needs.
- Those comfortable with hands-on clinical work and physical activity, such as assisting with exercises.

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer routine administrative tasks or minimal patient contact.
- Individuals uncomfortable with bodily fluids, close physical proximity, or emotionally challenging situations.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What visa options exist for non-EEA health professionals?**
A: Non-EEA nationals need a work and residence permit tied to a specific job offer. The employer must prove no suitable EEA candidate is available (labour market test). If the occupation is on the shortage list, the process is expedited. The permit is initially temporary but can lead to permanent residence.

**Q: How do I get my foreign health qualification recognised in Iceland?**
A: Apply to the Directorate of Health (Embætti Landlæknis). They assess whether your education and training meet Icelandic standards. You may need to take supplementary courses or pass a language test. Recognition is required before you can obtain a license to practice.

**Q: Is this occupation in shortage in Iceland?**
A: Yes, many allied health professions (e.g., physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech therapists) are on the shortage list. This means easier work permit processing for non-EEA applicants and higher demand in the labour market.
