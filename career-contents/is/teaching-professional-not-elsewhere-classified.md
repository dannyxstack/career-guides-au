# Teaching Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2359 (ISCO08)**

Teaching professionals not elsewhere classified include a diverse group of educators such as adult education teachers, corporate trainers, language instructors, and special education specialists who work outside the traditional primary and secondary school systems. In Iceland, this occupation is growing due to increased demand for lifelong learning, professional development, and integration support for immigrants. These professionals are employed by universities, vocational schools, private training companies, and government agencies. The role typically requires strong subject knowledge, teaching skills, and often proficiency in Icelandic, though English-language instruction is common in certain niches.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 330. The outlook for these teaching professionals in Iceland is positive, driven by labor market needs for upskilling and reskilling, an aging population requiring adult education, and a steady influx of immigrants needing language and cultural training. The government has prioritized lifelong learning and digital education, creating opportunities in both public and private sectors. However, competition for permanent positions can be moderate, and many roles are project-based or part-time. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and sector. Public sector roles often follow collective agreements, while private sector pay is negotiable. Factors include experience, qualifications, and whether the role is full-time or part-time. It is best to consult current job listings for specific figures.

**Q: Do I need a work visa as an EU/EEA citizen to work as a teaching professional in Iceland?**
A: No. EU/EEA nationals (including Switzerland) have free movement and only need to register their residence if staying longer than three months. Non-EEA nationals require a work permit tied to a job offer and must undergo a labor market test.

**Q: Is this occupation on Iceland's shortage list?**
A: While specific subcategories may be in shortage (e.g., special education teachers, Icelandic as a second language instructors), the broad ISCO-2359 is not explicitly on the national shortage list. However, individual job offers for non-EEA nationals may be exempt from the labor market test if the role is deemed hard to fill.
