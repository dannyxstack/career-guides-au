# Handicraft Worker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7319 (ISCO08)**

Handicraft workers in Iceland create or repair handmade goods such as textiles, pottery, woodwork, or jewelry, often within the tourism and cultural sectors. Due to Iceland's strong focus on local heritage and sustainable products, these workers are valued in small workshops, artisan cooperatives, or as independent artists. The role requires manual dexterity, creativity, and knowledge of traditional techniques, with many craftspeople blending modern design with Nordic traditions. Employment is typically project-based or seasonal, with self-employment being common.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a handicraft worker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely by specialization and employment type. Apprentices may earn around 250,000 ISK per month, while experienced artisans can earn 350,000–500,000 ISK. Self-employed income depends on sales and can be lower or higher.

**Q: Can a non-EEA handicraft worker get a work permit for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but only if they have a job offer from an Icelandic employer and the position passes a labor market test. The job must meet the minimum salary threshold (around 400,000 ISK/month) and be in line with local wage standards. The permit is tied to that employer.

**Q: What are the main career paths for handicraft workers in Iceland?**
A: Common paths include becoming a master craftsperson and opening your own workshop, teaching at vocational schools or offering workshops to tourists, or moving into design and wholesale. Some collaborate with architects or interior designers for custom pieces.
