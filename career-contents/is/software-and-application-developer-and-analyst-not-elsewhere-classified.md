## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts with junior developer roles, advancing to senior positions, team lead, or architect. Many also move into management, project management, or entrepreneurship. Continuous learning and certifications in modern tech stacks are common for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Writing boilerplate code and repetitive CRUD operations
- Basic unit testing and debugging
- Data cleaning and simple report generation
- Standardised checks in code review
- Documentation writing and maintenance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI-assisted system architecture design and code optimization
- Use AI tools for rapid prototyping and idea validation
- Enhance data analysis and model iteration through AI
- AI-driven test generation and coverage analysis
- Leveraging AI to empower customer needs analysis and solution design

**Human moat:**

- Complex system architecture and domain modeling skills
- Cross-departmental collaboration and communication skills
- Creative problem solving and algorithmic innovation
- Deep understanding and translation of business logic
- Ethics, safety, and compliance decision-making

**Skills to build:**

- Proficient in using AI programming assistants such as GitHub Copilot and Cursor
- Mastering large language model (LLM) fine-tuning and prompt engineering
- Understanding MLOps and building AI model deployment pipelines
- Strengthening software architecture design and design patterns
- Enhance agile development and DevOps practices
- Learn data engineering and cloud-native technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- Cloud Computing
- Cybersecurity

- Artificial Intelligence

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are generally high relative to other occupations, reflecting strong demand. Starting salaries for junior developers are competitive, and experienced professionals earn well above the national average. Exact figures vary by experience and specialisation.

**Q: Do I need a work permit to work as a software developer in Iceland?**
A: EU/EEA/EFTA nationals do not need a work permit. Non-EEA nationals need a job offer and can apply for a work and residence permit for qualified professionals. The occupation is often on the shortage list, which may shorten processing and exempt from labour market test.

**Q: Is a university degree mandatory for this job?**
A: Not always, but most employers prefer a BSc in computer science or related field. However, proven experience and certifications can substitute for formal education, especially for senior roles.
