# Electrical and Electronic Equipment Assembler — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 8212 (ISCO08)**

Electrical and electronic equipment assemblers put together components such as circuit boards, wiring, and housings to create finished products like control panels, communication devices, and consumer electronics. In Iceland, these assemblers are primarily employed in specialized manufacturing plants, renewable energy equipment assembly, and maintenance workshops. The role is crucial for the production and repair of equipment used in the country's growing tech and green energy sectors. Due to Iceland's small population, the number of positions is limited, but skilled assemblers are valued for precision and reliability.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with entry-level assembly work, moving to senior assembler or team leader roles after gaining experience. Some assemblers transition into technician positions focusing on troubleshooting and repair, or into quality control. Further education can lead to engineering technician roles. The small size of the Icelandic labour market means advancement often requires versatility and willingness to take on additional responsibilities.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an electrical and electronic equipment assembler in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer, but entry-level assemblers typically earn around 400,000 to 500,000 ISK per month. Experienced assemblers may earn up to 600,000 ISK. Overtime and shift work can increase earnings.

**Q: Do non-EEA nationals need a job offer before applying for a work permit?**
A: Yes, a job offer from an Icelandic employer is required. The employer must prove the position could not be filled by a local or EEA candidate. The permit is tied to that specific employer.

**Q: What are the promotion opportunities for assemblers in Iceland?**
A: With experience, assemblers can move into team leader or supervisor roles, or specialize in quality control. Further training can lead to technician or maintenance positions.
