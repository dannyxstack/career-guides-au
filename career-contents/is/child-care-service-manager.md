# Child Care Services Manager — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 1341 (ISCO08)**

Child care services managers oversee daily operations, staff, and curriculum in daycare centers, preschools, and other early childhood facilities. In Iceland, this role is vital due to high demand for quality early education and the government's expansion of childcare services. Managers ensure compliance with national regulations, manage budgets, and foster a safe, nurturing environment for children. The position requires strong leadership, communication, and organizational skills, often combined with a background in education or management. With Iceland's emphasis on early childhood development, these managers play a key role in shaping the next generation.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 1,660. Employment outlook for child care services managers in Iceland is favorable. The population growth in urban areas, particularly in the Reykjavik capital region, drives demand for childcare facilities. Additionally, the government has committed to increasing the number of preschools and improving staff-to-child ratios, creating managerial positions. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for child care services managers typically begins as a preschool teacher or child care worker, moving into supervisory roles after gaining experience and additional education. Many pursue a master's degree in education or management to advance. In Iceland, opportunities exist in both public and private sectors, with public positions offering more stability and benefits. Experienced managers may become district supervisors or open their own daycare centers. The career is well-suited for those passionate about early childhood development and capable of multitasking. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a child care services manager in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, location, and whether the facility is public or private. Generally, managers earn higher than preschool teachers, with average annual salaries ranging from 5,500,000 to 7,500,000 ISK for experienced professionals in the private sector, while public sector positions may offer slightly lower starting pay but better benefits.

**Q: Does Iceland require a labor market test for child care services managers?**
A: For non-EEA nationals, a labor market test is usually required unless the occupation is on the shortage list. Child care services managers are considered a shortage occupation, so the test may be waived, streamlining the permit process. EEA nationals do not need a permit.

**Q: Do I need to speak Icelandic to work as a child care services manager?**
A: Yes, fluency in Icelandic is typically required as you interact with children, parents, and staff daily, and must comply with Icelandic regulations. However, some private international daycare centers may accept English-speaking managers if they have strong credentials.
