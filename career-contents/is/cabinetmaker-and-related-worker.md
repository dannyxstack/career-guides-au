# Cabinet-maker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7522 (ISCO08)**

Cabinet-makers and related workers in Iceland produce, assemble, and repair wooden furniture, cabinets, and other wood-based products. They work in workshops, construction sites, or run their own businesses. The occupation is part of the broader woodworking sector, which benefits from steady demand in housing construction, renovation, and custom furniture markets. While not a large occupation, it offers stable employment for skilled craftspeople. The role requires precision, creativity, and knowledge of woodworking techniques and materials. Many workers complete a formal apprenticeship and become certified journeymen before specializing. The industry is adapting to sustainable materials and modern design trends.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cabinet-makers in Iceland typically begins with a 3-4 year apprenticeship combining vocational school and on-the-job training. After passing the journeyman exam, workers can advance to master craftsman status (meistararéttindi), which allows them to hire apprentices and run a larger business. Some specialize in restoration, kitchen fitting, or custom furniture design. Others move into management, teaching, or start their own workshops. The field offers good prospects for self-employment, especially in niche markets like boat interior fitting or historic building restoration.

- Using CAD/CAM software to automatically generate cutting paths and material lists

- Automated inventory management and replenishment system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Project management platform automates scheduling and progress tracking.

**Human moat:**

- Hand carving and antique finishing techniques for high-end custom cabinets
- Creativity and experience judgment for solving non-standard installation issues on site
- Ability to communicate aesthetic needs with clients and translate them into unique designs
- Machining and assembly techniques for complex curved and irregularly shaped parts
- Touch-dependent skills such as wood property identification and defect repair

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cabinet-maker in Iceland?**
A: Entry-level cabinet-makers earn around 3,500,000 ISK per year, while experienced journeymen can earn 5,000,000–6,000,000 ISK. Master craftsmen or self-employed workers may earn more. Wages are generally higher in the Reykjavik capital area.

**Q: Can a non-EEA cabinet-maker get a work visa for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but a job offer from an Icelandic employer is required. The employer must demonstrate that no local or EEA worker is available (labour market test). The occupation is not on the shortage list, so approval may depend on regional demand. The permit is initially for one year and renewable.

**Q: Is there a demand for foreign cabinet-makers in Iceland?**
A: Demand is moderate. While not a shortage occupation, there are opportunities during construction booms. EEA nationals can enter freely; non-EEA workers face stricter rules. Speaking Icelandic is an advantage but not required in larger workshops.
