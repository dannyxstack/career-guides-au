# Subsistence Mixed Crop and Livestock Farmer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6330 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers farmers who grow crops and raise livestock primarily for their own consumption, with minimal surplus for sale. In Iceland, subsistence farming is rare and typically limited to remote rural areas. Most farmers operate on a larger commercial scale. Subsistence farming does not qualify for work permits, as it does not meet job offer or labour market test requirements. EU/EEA nationals may engage in subsistence farming under free movement rules, but income is generally very low.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**

There is no formal career progression for subsistence farming. Most farmers start with family land or purchase small farms, but they often diversify into tourism, organic certification, or value-added products to survive. Transition to commercial farming is possible but requires significant capital. Younger farmers typically pursue agricultural education and then work on larger farms or in related sectors.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence farmer in Iceland?**
A: Income from subsistence farming is negligible as production is mostly for own use. Many rely on other employment or social benefits. Average annual income is often below ISK 1,000,000.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national move to Iceland to work as a subsistence farmer?**
A: No. Subsistence farming does not meet the criteria for a work permit. Non-EEA nationals must have a job offer in a skilled occupation and pass a labour market test.

**Q: Is there a path from subsistence to commercial farming in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it requires substantial investment, land expansion, and often agritourism. Many succeed by diversifying into organic certification, farm stays, or local food markets.
