# Kitchen Helper — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9412 (ISCO08)**

Kitchen helpers in Iceland assist with basic food preparation, cleaning, and dishwashing in restaurants, hotels, and institutional kitchens, primarily within the tourism-driven hospitality sector. The occupation is dominated by immigrant labour, with many EEA nationals filling positions due to low barriers to entry.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; kitchen helpers can advance to line cook or chef with additional training and experience, but most remain in helper roles. The occupation serves as an entry point into the hospitality industry.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Weigh ingredients according to recipe
- Manual dishwashing
- Scrub kitchen surfaces
- Cutting standard vegetables
- Transporting ingredient inventory

**Where AI augments the role:**

- use automatic vegetable cutters to improve efficiency
- Uses intelligent inventory system to manage ingredients
- Learn new dishes through digital recipes.
- Operating automated dishwashing line
- Using cleaning robots to maintain floor hygiene

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability in emergency situations
- Sensory assessment of ingredient quality
- Collaboration with chefs and waitstaff.
- Personal responsibility for hygiene checks
- Flexibility for non-standard tasks

**Skills to build:**

- Basic automated equipment operation and maintenance
- Digital inventory management
- Food safety and hygiene monitoring system
- Basic cooking skills
- Multitasking coordination ability
- English communication (tourism and hospitality)

**Key growth areas:**

- tourism
- hospitality
- fast food
- seasonal work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a kitchen helper in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary but generally base on collective agreements. Entry-level pay starts around minimum wage, which is adjusted annually. Exact figures depend on location and employer.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a kitchen helper in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. The Directorate of Labour requires a labour market test proving no local or EEA worker is available. Permits are rarely issued for this low-skilled occupation. EEA nationals face no restrictions.
