# Research and Development Managers — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 1223 (ISCO08)**

Research and Development Managers in Iceland oversee innovation projects in key sectors such as renewable energy, biotechnology, fisheries, and software development. They lead teams of scientists and engineers, manage budgets, and align R&D strategies with business goals. Iceland's small but highly educated workforce and strong emphasis on sustainability make this role critical for maintaining competitive advantage. Employment is concentrated in Reykjavik and Akureyri, with opportunities in both private firms and public research institutes.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from senior researcher or engineer roles, followed by promotion to R&D manager after gaining project leadership experience. Many managers hold a PhD and have transitioned from academia to industry. Continuing education in management and innovation is valued. Lateral moves between sectors (e.g., from energy to biotech) are common. Senior positions may lead to directorial or executive roles, but such opportunities are rare due to Iceland's small economy.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an R&D Manager in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by sector and experience. According to Statistics Iceland, average monthly earnings for managers in professional, scientific and technical activities are around 1,200,000 ISK. Entry-level positions may start at 800,000 ISK, while senior roles can exceed 1,600,000 ISK.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national become an R&D Manager in Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they secure a job offer from an Icelandic employer. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA/EEA candidate is available (labour market test). The role qualifies as a skilled occupation, but the labour market test may be waived if the occupation is on the shortage list. After four years on a work permit, the individual can apply for permanent residence.

**Q: Is a PhD required to become an R&D Manager in Iceland?**
A: While not always mandatory, a PhD is highly preferred by employers, especially in technical fields like biotechnology or energy research. A Master's degree combined with significant experience may suffice in some sectors, but a PhD significantly improves prospects.
