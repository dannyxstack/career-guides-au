# Sales Worker Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 5249 (ISCO08)**

Sales workers in this category perform a variety of sales tasks not covered by more specific sales occupations, such as retail salespersons, telemarketers, or sales demonstrators. In Iceland, they are commonly employed in retail stores, tourism-related shops, and service stations. The role involves assisting customers, promoting products, and processing transactions. Due to Iceland's heavy reliance on tourism, many positions are seasonal, particularly in Reykjavík and popular tourist destinations. The job typically does not require formal education, but on-the-job training is provided.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sales worker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective bargaining agreements and vary by sector. Entry-level positions pay according to the minimum wage set by the relevant union, with experience and commission adding to earnings.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to work as a sales worker in Iceland?**
A: EEA/EFTA nationals can work freely. Non-EEA nationals typically require a work permit, which is subject to a labour market test and a minimum salary threshold. Sales worker positions often do not meet the skilled worker criteria, making it difficult for non-EEA nationals to obtain permits.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for sales workers?**
A: Common paths include moving into supervisory roles, specializing in high-demand product areas (e.g., luxury goods or outdoor equipment), or transitioning to customer service management. Further education in business or retail management can lead to store manager or regional positions.
