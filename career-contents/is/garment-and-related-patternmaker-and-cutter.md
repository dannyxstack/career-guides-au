# Garment and Related Patternmakers and Cutters — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7532 (ISCO08)**

Garment and Related Patternmakers and Cutters (ISCO-08 7532) in Iceland work in a niche sector focused on custom tailoring, traditional woolen garments (e.g., lopapeysa), and costume design. The industry is small due to high import reliance, but demand persists for skilled craftspersons in bespoke production, repair, and heritage clothing. Opportunities are concentrated in Reykjavik and tourist regions. The occupation requires precision, creativity, and knowledge of textiles. Most workers are self-employed or in micro-enterprises.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from apprentice to journeyman (sveinn) and eventually master (meistari). Some patternmakers advance to supervisory roles in larger workshops or start their own tailoring businesses. Cross-training in sewing, design, or textile restoration can broaden employability. The field lacks formal advancement hierarchies; experience and specialized skills in heritage techniques or haute couture provide competitive advantages.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom tailoring
- Traditional Icelandic wool
- Costume design
- Sustainable fashion

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garment patternmaker or cutter in Iceland?**
A: Pre-tax monthly salaries range from 350,000 to 500,000 ISK for experienced workers; entry-level or part-time roles may be lower. Self-employed workers have variable income.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a garment patternmaker in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not on the shortage list and the industry is small. Employers must prove the position cannot be filled locally, which is rare. EEA/EFTA nationals have free movement.

**Q: What training is required to become a garment patternmaker in Iceland?**
A: A 3-year vocational program at an upper secondary school (e.g., Iðnskólinn) combining coursework and apprenticeship, leading to a journeyman's certificate. Private short courses are available for specialisation.
