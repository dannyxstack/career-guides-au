# Pharmacist — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2262 (ISCO08)**

Pharmacists in Iceland dispense prescription medications, provide expert advice on drug interactions and dosages, and promote public health through patient education. They work in community pharmacies, hospitals, the pharmaceutical industry, and regulatory agencies. The profession is regulated by the Icelandic Medicines Agency (Lyfjastofnun) and requires a master's degree in pharmacy. With an aging population and increasing chronic disease prevalence, pharmacists play a vital role in Iceland's healthcare system.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for pharmacists in Iceland typically involves specialisation in clinical pharmacy, hospital pharmacy, or pharmaceutical research. Experienced pharmacists can advance to management roles in community or hospital pharmacies, or move into the pharmaceutical industry in regulatory affairs or drug development. Further academic pursuits (PhD) lead to university teaching and research positions. Lifelong learning is essential to keep up with new drugs and regulations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine prescription review and drug interaction checks
- Standardized drug dispensing and dose calculation
- Inventory management and automatic replenishment
- Drug information retrieval and simple medication consultation
- Automatic bill reconciliation with insurance systems

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to predict individual drug responses and adverse reaction risks
- Personalized medication regimen optimization based on clinical data
- Smart triage and remote patient medication follow-up
- Improved Vaccine Management and Promotion Efficiency
- Pharmacoeconomic analysis and real-world evidence generation

**Human moat:**

- Medication management and clinical decision-making for complex cases.
- Face-to-face patient consultation and medication adherence intervention
- Coordination and communication within multidisciplinary healthcare teams
- Ethical decision-making and pharmaceutical legal compliance judgment
- Patient education on emerging therapies (e.g., gene therapy)

**Skills to build:**

- Telemedicine and digital health tool application
- Statistical data analysis and real-world research skills

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a pharmacist in Iceland?**
A: The average monthly salary for a pharmacist in Iceland is approximately 700,000 ISK pre-tax, with variations based on experience, sector (hospital vs. community), and location. Entry-level positions may start around 550,000 ISK, while senior roles can exceed 900,000 ISK.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to immigrate as a pharmacist?**
A: Yes, non-EEA nationals must have a job offer from an Icelandic employer to apply for a work and residence permit. The employer often initiates the process. EEA nationals can move without a job offer but need to demonstrate sufficient resources and register after three months.

**Q: Is pharmacy considered a shortage occupation in Iceland?**
A: Yes, pharmacists are on Iceland's shortage list, which can expedite work permit processing for non-EEA applicants and sometimes waive the labour market test. This reflects the ongoing demand for pharmacists across the country.
