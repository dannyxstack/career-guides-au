# Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and aquaculture labourers perform manual tasks in Iceland's vital fishing and fish farming sectors, including net mending, fish handling, cleaning, and feeding. This occupation is the backbone of Iceland's economy, with many workers employed in fish processing plants and aquaculture facilities. The role requires physical stamina, tolerance for cold and wet conditions, and ability to work in shifts. Labourers are often migrants from EEA countries, as the work is hard and seasonal. Automation is gradually affecting some tasks, but demand remains steady due to high seafood exports.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for labourers is limited without further training. Experienced workers can become team leaders or shift supervisors in fish processing plants. Specialisation in net mending, diving (for aquaculture), or machine operation offers advancement. Some attend vocational courses to become skilled fish processors or technicians. With experience, workers may move into quality control or management roles, but most remain in labourer positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture expansion
- Fish processing automation
- Sustainable fishing
- Seafood export

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery and aquaculture labourer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective bargaining agreements and vary by experience and company. They generally start at the minimum wage for the sector and increase with overtime and seniority. Exact figures are not published here but are competitive within the industry.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as fishery labourers in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Non-EEA nationals must have a job offer and the employer must prove that no EEA/EFTA worker is available (labour market test). Work permits are typically temporary and tied to the employer, with limited pathways to permanent residence.

**Q: What career progression is possible for a fishery labourer?**
A: With experience, labourers can become shift supervisors, quality controllers, or specialize in net mending or aquaculture husbandry. Further vocational training can lead to roles like fish processing technician or aquaculture technician, offering higher pay and stability.
