# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers in Iceland perform manual tasks such as planting, thinning, and harvesting trees, as well as maintaining forest roads and clearing undergrowth. The sector has grown due to afforestation efforts to combat soil erosion and carbon sequestration, but it remains small-scale compared to other Nordic countries. Most forestry labourers are employed by the Icelandic Forest Service, municipalities, or private contractors. The work is seasonal, typically from spring to autumn, and requires physical stamina. Opportunities are concentrated in rural areas, especially in the east and north. The occupation does not typically require formal education, but on-the-job training is provided.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited for forestry labourers in Iceland. With experience, workers may advance to supervisory roles (skógarvinnustjóri) or specialised positions in tree nursery management or forest machinery operation. Additional training in forestry technology or ecology can lead to technician-level jobs. However, many labourers remain in the same role indefinitely. The small size of the sector means that advancement often requires moving to larger employers or combining forestry with other rural work, such as farming or sheep herding.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- Afforestation
- Carbon sequestration
- Rural development
- Land reclamation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry labourer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by employer and season, but the average pre-tax annual salary for forestry labourers is moderate compared to other manual trades. Pay is often hourly and may include benefits such as lodging in remote areas. Specific figures are not provided here, but wages are generally lower than in construction or manufacturing.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a forestry labourer in Iceland?**
A: It is difficult. The occupation is not on the shortage list, and a labour market test must show that no EEA/EFTA worker is available. Permits are rarely granted for low-skilled labour. Seasonal work permits may be possible for specific projects, but the process is restrictive.

**Q: Are there opportunities for advancement in forestry in Iceland?**
A: Advancement is limited but possible. With training, a labourer can become a machine operator or supervisor. Further education in forestry management can lead to technician or forester roles, but such positions are few and competitive.
