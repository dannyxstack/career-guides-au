# Securities and Finance Dealer and Broker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 3311 (ISCO08)**

Securities and finance dealers and brokers in Iceland facilitate the buying and selling of financial instruments, such as stocks, bonds, and derivatives, on behalf of clients or their own accounts. They work primarily in banks, investment firms, and brokerage houses, and are regulated by the Financial Supervisory Authority (FME). The Icelandic market is small but internationally connected, with a strong focus on transparency and digital innovation. Professionals in this field must hold a valid license and meet strict ethical standards.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior broker or analyst, advancing to senior broker, portfolio manager, or head of trading. Many professionals move into private banking, asset management, or advisory roles. Continuing education and certifications, such as the CFA or Icelandic Securities Dealers Association credentials, are highly valued. Networking and performance are key to advancement, and some pursue entrepreneurship in fintech startups.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically execute stock, bond, and commodity orders through algorithms, no manual order placement
- AI automatically generates market analysis reports and investment briefs
- AI-driven chatbots handle common customer inquiries (e.g., account inquiries, transaction confirmations)
- Rule-based system automatically identifies potential suspicious transactions and triggers alerts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes customer transaction history and risk preferences to assist in generating personalized investment portfolio recommendations
- Real-time market sentiment monitoring and event impact simulation to improve decision quality for advisors
- Automatically generate compliance checklists and regulatory reports to reduce paperwork time
- Predict upselling and cross-selling opportunities through customer behavior models

**Human moat:**

- Build deep trust relationships, understand clients' non-financial needs (e.g., family wealth inheritance, emotional needs)
- Provide qualitative judgment and real-time adjustments to investment strategies in complex market environments
- Take on personalized responsibility for one-on-one consulting and crisis communication with high-net-worth clients
- Ethical judgment and interpretation concerning regulatory grey areas and new financial products

**Skills to build:**

- Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and AI-enhanced customer interaction platforms
- Regulatory technology (RegTech) and compliance automation tools

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a securities dealer in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience and performance, but are generally above the national average. Entry-level positions start around 5–7 million ISK annually, while senior brokers can earn 12–20 million ISK plus bonuses. Bonuses can be a significant portion of total compensation.

**Q: Can a non-EEA citizen become a securities broker in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You need a job offer from an Icelandic employer, which must first advertise the position locally (labour market test). You also need to obtain the required FME license, which involves passing exams in Icelandic or English. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA candidate was found.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to work as a securities broker in Iceland?**
A: A bachelor's degree in finance, economics, or a related field is usually required. Additionally, you must obtain a license from the Financial Supervisory Authority (FME) by passing specific exams. Employers often prefer candidates with the Certified Securities Dealer credential from the Icelandic Securities Dealers Association.
