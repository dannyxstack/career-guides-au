# Legal Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2619 (ISCO08)**

Legal professionals in this category include advisors, consultants, and specialists in fields like corporate law, intellectual property, and regulatory compliance. In Iceland, they work in law firms, government agencies, and corporate legal departments, often advising on EEA and EU legal frameworks. The role requires strong analytical skills and knowledge of Icelandic law, with many professionals also fluent in English due to international business ties.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior legal advisor or associate in a law firm, advancing to senior advisor, partner, or in-house counsel. Specialization in niche areas such as energy law, fintech, or human rights can enhance prospects. Many professionals also pursue roles in the public sector, including regulatory authorities or the judiciary. Continuous education is common, with opportunities to obtain a master's degree (LL.M.) abroad or specialize through professional certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Template-based drafting of legal documents

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for legal professionals in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, sector, and specialization. Entry-level positions may start lower, while senior advisors and partners earn significantly more. For precise figures, consult Statistics Iceland (Hagstofa Íslands) pay surveys.

**Q: Can non-EEA legal professionals work in Iceland?**
A: Yes, but they need a work permit tied to a specific job. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA candidate is available (labour market test). Icelandic language proficiency is often a practical requirement.

**Q: Is Icelandic mandatory for legal professionals?**
A: Yes, for most roles. Legal documents, court proceedings, and client communication are in Icelandic. However, some international corporate firms may hire English-speaking specialists for niche areas like GDPR or cross-border transactions.
