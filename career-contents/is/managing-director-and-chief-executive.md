# Managing Director and Chief Executive — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 1120 (ISCO08)**

Managing directors and chief executives (ISCO-08 1120) are top-level managers who direct and coordinate the activities of private or public sector organizations. In Iceland's small but dynamic economy, they lead firms in key sectors such as tourism, fisheries, renewable energy, and technology. The role involves strategic planning, financial oversight, and stakeholder relations. Given Iceland's EU/EEA membership (non-EU), the position is subject to free movement for EEA nationals but requires work permits for others. Labour market context: with a population of ~380,000, executive roles are limited but critical for economic growth.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression to managing director typically requires extensive experience in senior management, often within the same company or industry. Many hold advanced degrees (MBA or equivalent) and have a background in business, finance, or engineering. Networking and a strong track record in strategic decision-making are essential. In Iceland, executives often progress through roles such as department head or vice president. International experience is valued, but local market knowledge is critical.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate routine operational reports and data analysis

- Automated schedule arrangement and meeting management
- Basic financial analysis and forecasting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Risk management: AI predicts potential operational or financial risks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a managing director in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and company size. In large corporations, total compensation including bonuses can exceed 15 million ISK annually, while SMEs may offer 8-12 million ISK. Exact figures depend on experience and industry.

**Q: Do non-EEA nationals face restrictions in obtaining a work permit for this role?**
A: Yes, the labour market test requires demonstration that no EEA candidate is available. Due to the limited executive positions, the process can be competitive. A job offer from an Icelandic company and proof of qualifications are essential.
