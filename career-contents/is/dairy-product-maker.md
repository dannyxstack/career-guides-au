# Dairy Products Maker — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7513 (ISCO08)**

Dairy products makers in Iceland are skilled workers who produce items such as cheese, butter, yogurt, and skyr—a traditional Icelandic dairy product. They work in processing plants, often for cooperatives like Mjólkursamsalan (MS) or smaller artisanal dairies. The role involves operating pasteurization equipment, culturing bacteria, separating curds, and packaging finished goods. Iceland's dairy sector is robust, driven by domestic consumption and growing export markets for high-quality products. Workers must adhere to strict hygiene and quality standards, often requiring vocational training in food science or dairy technology.

Career progression for dairy products makers typically starts as a production worker, advancing to team leader or shift supervisor with experience. Further specialization in cheese making, quality control, or product development is possible through additional vocational training. Many workers obtain a certification in food processing, which can lead to roles in management or food safety. The industry values practical skills and attention to detail, with opportunities for entrepreneurship in small-scale artisanal production.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a dairy products maker in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary with experience and employer. Entry-level workers generally earn a competitive wage in the food sector, with increases after training and specialization.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a dairy products maker?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA candidate is available. The occupation is not on the shortage list, so permits are not guaranteed. A job offer and positive labour market test are required.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist?**
A: With experience, dairy products makers can become supervisors, quality controllers, or production managers. Advanced training in food technology can lead to roles in product development or dairy consulting.
