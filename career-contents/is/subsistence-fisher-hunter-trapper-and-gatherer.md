# Subsistence Fishers, Hunters, Trappers and Gatherers — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 6340 (ISCO08)**

Subsistence Fishers, Hunters, Trappers and Gatherers in Iceland engage in traditional hunting, fishing, trapping, and gathering activities primarily for personal consumption or local barter. This occupation is deeply rooted in Icelandic culture but remains a very niche and informal sector. Practitioners often possess extensive knowledge of local ecosystems and sustainable resource use. While not a mainstream career, it appeals to those seeking self-sufficiency and connection to nature. Regulations on quotas and licenses ensure sustainability, and some individuals combine subsistence activities with tourism or seasonal work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression in subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, and gathering is typically flat. Individuals often work independently, passing down skills through family or apprenticeships. There is no formal career ladder, but some may transition to commercial fishing, wildlife guiding, or conservation work. Success depends on local knowledge and adaptability to environmental changes. The occupation offers minimal financial security but high personal fulfillment for those dedicated to the lifestyle.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments

- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence fisher/hunter/gatherer in Iceland?**
A: Income is highly variable and often non-monetary, depending on season, quotas, and personal consumption. Most combine subsistence with other work. No official salary data exists.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit as a subsistence fisher/hunter/gatherer in Iceland?**
A: This is extremely difficult. The occupation is not considered skilled, and the labour-market test would favor EEA nationals. Non-EEA applicants are unlikely to be granted a permit.

**Q: What licenses are needed for subsistence fishing or hunting in Iceland?**
A: Hunting requires a license from the Environment Agency. Fishing in rivers or lakes requires a permit from the Fisheries Directorate. Commercial scale may need business registration and additional permits.
