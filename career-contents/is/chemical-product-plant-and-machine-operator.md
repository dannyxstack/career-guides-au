# Chemical Products Plant and Machine Operator — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 8131 (ISCO08)**

Chemical products plant and machine operators in Iceland monitor and control equipment used in the production of chemical substances, including those for geothermal energy (e.g., hydrogen), fisheries processing (fishmeal and oil), pharmaceuticals, and basic industrial chemicals. They ensure safe and efficient operation of reactors, distillation columns, pumps, and control panels. The workforce is small but specialized, concentrated in the Reykjanes peninsula and eastern Iceland. Operators work in shifts and must adhere to strict safety protocols due to hazardous materials. Most positions require vocational training in chemical technology or process operations, though on-the-job training is also common.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for chemical products plant operators in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are competitive with other manufacturing industries. Entry-level operators earn around 4 million ISK annually, while experienced operators can earn 6–7 million ISK. Senior supervisors may earn more. Exact figures depend on experience, shift patterns, and plant location.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a chemical operator in Iceland if I am from outside the EU/EEA?**
A: Yes, non-EEA nationals require a work and residence permit. The employer must prove that no qualified EEA worker was available (labour market test). A valid job offer and relevant qualifications are necessary. The permit is job-specific and usually initially valid for one year, renewable.

**Q: Is it possible to progress to an engineering role from an operator position?**
A: Yes, some operators move into process engineering or management after gaining experience and completing further education, such as a diploma or degree in chemical engineering. Many plants offer tuition support for such advancement.
