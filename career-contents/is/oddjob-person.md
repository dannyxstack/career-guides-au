# Odd-Job Person — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons in Iceland perform a variety of manual tasks including cleaning, basic maintenance, moving goods, and assisting in construction or events. This occupation is common in sectors like hospitality, retail, and small trades, often on a temporary or part-time basis. Many workers are students, seasonal labour, or those seeking flexible hours. The role requires physical stamina and adaptability, with minimal formal training. In Iceland's tight labour market, odd-job workers fill gaps during peak seasons, though wages are relatively low and job security is limited.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; odd-job persons may transition to specialised roles like cleaner, construction labourer, or hospitality staff after gaining experience. Some rise to supervisory positions in cleaning or maintenance firms. Others use the role as a stepping stone while studying or seeking permanent employment. Upskilling through short courses (e.g., forklift, cleaning certification) can improve prospects, but formal education is rarely required.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical hourly wage for an odd-job person in Iceland?**
A: Hourly wages range from 2,000 to 3,500 ISK pre-tax, depending on employer and sector. The minimum wage in collective agreements is around 2,000 ISK for unskilled labour. Overtime or night shifts may increase pay.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work permit for an odd-job position in Iceland?**
A: It is very difficult. Non-EEA workers generally need a job offer with a salary at least equal to 330,000 ISK per month and the position must pass a labour market test. Odd-job roles rarely meet these criteria, and permits are usually denied.
