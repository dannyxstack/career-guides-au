# Traditional Chief and Head of Village — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 1113 (ISCO08)**

In Iceland, the occupation of traditional chief or head of village does not exist due to the country's modern democratic governance structure. Local leadership is exercised by elected municipal mayors (sveitarstjórar) who administer villages and towns under the Local Government Act. These mayors are not hereditary or ceremonial traditional chiefs; they are professional administrators. The role involves managing municipal services, representing the community, and implementing local policies. This is distinct from the ISCO-08 definition of traditional chiefs, which implies customary law and ceremonial functions. Therefore, this occupation has no direct equivalent in Iceland's labour market.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary for a traditional chief in Iceland?**
A: Salaries are not applicable as the occupation does not exist in Iceland. The nearest role, municipal mayor, typically earns between 800,000 and 1,500,000 ISK per month depending on municipality size.

**Q: Can a foreigner come to Iceland as a traditional chief for a visa?**
A: No. Iceland does not recognize this occupation for work permits. EU/EEA nationals can live in Iceland without a permit, but they cannot work as a traditional chief because the role is not recognized. Non-EEA nationals would need a job offer in a recognized skilled occupation.

**Q: Are there any job openings for traditional chiefs in Iceland?**
A: There are no job openings for this occupation. Community leadership in Iceland is performed by elected municipal mayors, which are political positions, not traditional chiefs.
