# Health Care Assistant — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 5321 (ISCO08)**

Health Care Assistants (sjúkraliðar) provide basic care and support to patients in hospitals, nursing homes, and home care settings across Iceland. Working under the supervision of registered nurses, they assist with daily activities such as bathing, feeding, and mobility, as well as monitoring vital signs. With an aging population and increasing demand for long-term care, this role is vital to Iceland's healthcare system. The occupation is regulated at the vocational level, requiring a specialized diploma from an upper-secondary vocational school. Job prospects are strong, particularly in the Reykjavik capital area and rural regions facing workforce shortages.

Shortages are reported in many municipalities, leading to active recruitment from abroad, especially within the EEA. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for Health Care Assistants in Iceland typically begins with vocational training and entry-level positions in hospitals or nursing homes. With experience, opportunities arise in specialized care units (e.g., dementia, rehabilitation) or home care teams. Some go on to become senior assistants or team leaders. Many use the role as a stepping stone to further studies in nursing (registered nurse) or other healthcare professions, supported by employer-sponsored training programs. Lifelong learning and upskilling in areas like palliative care are increasingly valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic monitoring and recording of patient vital signs.
- Basic health data entry and electronic medical record updates
- Use AI tools for medication reminders and simple checks
- Partial patient communication and information inquiry (e.g., AI chatbot)
- Scheduling and materials management (optimized by AI systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic tools help identify early symptoms
- Smart wearable devices remotely monitor patients, allowing assistants to focus on emergencies
- AI translation tools improve communication with multilingual patients
- Automated document generation reduces paperwork time, increasing direct care
- Virtual reality simulation training enhances nursing skills.

**Human moat:**

- Providing emotional support and human care
- Performing complex personal care tasks such as bathing and feeding
- Quick decision-making and flexible response in emergency situations
- Builds trust relationships and team collaboration
- Handling unstructured patient needs and discomfort

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in AI-powered nursing record and monitoring systems
- Learn basic data analysis and health information technology
- Enhancing Communication and Cross-Cultural Nursing Skills
- Participating in virtual reality simulation training
- Obtain certification in aged care or rehabilitation nursing
- Learn psychological support and crisis intervention skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Elderly care
- Home care
- Rehabilitation
- Mental health

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Health Care Assistant in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and shift differentials (e.g., nights, weekends). Entry-level positions start around 3.5-4 million ISK per year, while experienced assistants may earn up to 5 million ISK or more. Collective agreements set minimum rates.

**Q: Can a non-EEA Health Care Assistant get a work permit for Iceland?**
A: Yes, but a job offer must be secured first. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EEA applicant is available (labour market test). The role is on the shortage list, which facilitates the process. A work permit is granted for one year and renewable.
