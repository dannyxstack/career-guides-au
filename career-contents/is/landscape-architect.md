# Landscape Architect — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2162 (ISCO08)**

Landscape architects in Iceland plan, design, and manage outdoor spaces, balancing natural beauty with practical needs. They work on projects ranging from urban parks and geothermal swimming pools to sustainable housing developments and tourism infrastructure. The role is vital for harmonizing Iceland's unique landscape with human activity, often requiring collaboration with architects, engineers, and local authorities. Given Iceland's focus on sustainability and renewable energy, landscape architects are increasingly involved in environmental restoration and green space design.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for landscape architects in Iceland typically starts with a junior role at a planning or design firm, advancing to senior positions with project management responsibilities. Many professionals eventually specialize in areas like environmental restoration or large-scale tourism resort design. Some move into consulting or start their own practices. Public-sector roles offer stability but slower advancement. Further education, such as a master's, can accelerate career growth.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for landscape architects in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary with experience and sector. Entry-level positions may start around 500,000 ISK per month, while senior landscape architects can earn up to 800,000 ISK or more. Public-sector salaries are typically lower but offer job security.

**Q: Can non-EEA landscape architects get a work permit in Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer from an Icelandic employer and the position passes a labour market test. The employer must demonstrate that no qualified EEA worker is available. The permit is tied to the job and employer.

**Q: Is Icelandic language proficiency necessary for landscape architects?**
A: While not always mandatory in private firms, public-sector roles and client communication often require Icelandic fluency. Basic Icelandic is highly recommended for career advancement.
