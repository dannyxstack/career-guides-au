# Vocational Education Teacher — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 2320 (ISCO08)**

Vocational education teachers in Iceland are responsible for delivering practical and theoretical instruction in upper secondary schools, adult education centres, and vocational training programmes. They specialise in fields such as carpentry, healthcare, IT, or tourism, blending classroom teaching with hands-on workshops. Iceland's strong emphasis on skilled trades and lifelong learning drives demand for these professionals, particularly in rural areas where shortages persist. Teachers must hold a relevant vocational qualification and a teaching licence, often requiring a master's degree in education. The role is regulated by the Ministry of Education, and working conditions vary by region.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,840. The employment outlook for vocational education teachers in Iceland is positive, driven by an ageing teacher workforce and government initiatives to expand vocational training. The 2023–2027 national strategy prioritises vocational education to address labour shortages in construction, tourism, and healthcare. Digitalisation and green skills integration create new teaching niches, while adult retraining programmes boost demand. However, recruitment remains challenging in remote areas, and competition for permanent positions is moderate in Reykjavík.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with teaching licensure and a probationary period. Teachers may advance to senior teacher, department head, or curriculum coordinator roles, with further training in educational leadership. Experienced teachers often move into adult education or specialise in programme development. Some transition to policy roles at the Ministry of Education or pursue PhDs in vocational pedagogy. Salary increases with seniority and additional certifications, but advancement is primarily through administrative positions.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for vocational education teachers in Iceland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, but they are set by collective agreements. Entry-level teachers earn around 400,000–500,000 ISK per month, while experienced teachers with advanced degrees can earn up to 700,000 ISK. Additional allowances apply for remote areas.

**Q: Can a non-EEA vocational teacher get a work permit for Iceland?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer. The teacher must hold relevant vocational qualifications and a teaching licence. Icelandic language skills (B2 level) are usually required. The Directorate of Labour conducts a labour market test, but vocational teachers are on the shortage list, which speeds up approval.
