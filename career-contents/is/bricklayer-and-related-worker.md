# Bricklayer — career analysis · Iceland

**Occupation code: 7112 (ISCO08)**

Bricklayers and related workers in Iceland are skilled tradespeople who lay bricks, blocks, and stones to construct or repair walls, partitions, and other structures. The occupation is part of the construction sector, which has experienced steady demand due to housing shortages and infrastructure projects in the Reykjavik capital area and regional towns. Workers often specialize in traditional masonry or modern insulation systems. Many are self-employed or employed by small to medium construction firms. The industry is heavily reliant on EEA migrant workers, especially from Poland and Lithuania, who make up a large share of the workforce.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for bricklayers in Iceland often involves moving from employee to self-employed contractor, or into supervisory roles such as site manager. Specialization in heritage masonry or energy-efficient building techniques can boost earnings. Some obtain further vocational training in construction management to enter project coordination. The industry values experience and certifications, with older workers mentoring apprentices. However, the work is physically demanding, and many transition to related trades or inspection roles later in their careers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Residential construction
- Infrastructure projects
- Energy sector plants
- Renovation works

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a bricklayer in Iceland?**
A: Pre-tax annual salaries for bricklayers with a journeyman's certificate range from ISK 5.5 million to ISK 7.5 million, depending on experience and region. Overtime and bonus pay can add 20-30%. Self-employed bricklayers may earn higher but variable income.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a bricklayer in Iceland if I am from outside the EEA?**
A: Yes, non-EEA nationals require a work and residence permit tied to a specific job offer. The occupation is on the shortage list, which may facilitate approval, but the employer must first advertise the position locally and prove no suitable EEA candidate is available. The permit is initially valid for one year and can be renewed.

**Q: Is the Icelandic bricklaying qualification recognized automatically in other countries?**
A: Icelandic journeyman and master certificates are recognized within the EU/EEA through mutual recognition of professional qualifications. For non-EU countries, bilateral agreements or individual evaluation may be needed.
