## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for journalists in Belgium?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for journalists in Belgium ranges from €3,500 to €4,500, depending on experience, media type, and language region. Entry-level journalists may start around €2,800, while senior roles or specialized correspondents can earn up to €6,000. Salaries are often determined by collective bargaining agreements.

**Q: How can foreign journalists obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Belgium?**
A: Non-EU journalists need a single permit combining work and residence, obtained via a Belgian employer. The EU Blue Card is available for highly skilled roles requiring a bachelor's degree and a salary above a set annual threshold (e.g., €5,200/month in 2024). Applications are filed through regional employment authorities.

**Q: What language skills are required for journalists in Belgium?**
A: Belgium has three official languages: Dutch, French, and German. Most media outlets require fluency in at least one, preferably with strong knowledge of another. English is common in international news organizations. For national roles, bilingualism (Dutch/French) is a major asset, especially in Brussels.
