## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for heavy truck and lorry drivers in Belgium?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for heavy truck drivers in Belgium ranges from €2,500 to €3,500, depending on experience, company, and type of transport. Additional allowances for night shifts, weekends, or international routes can increase earnings. Overtime pay is common. Union-negotiated sectoral agreements often set minimum scales.

**Q: Can non-EU heavy truck drivers work in Belgium via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU drivers can work in Belgium through a single work permit (combined residence and work permit) or the EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers. However, the Blue Card typically requires a higher education degree or proven high-level skills, which may not apply to standard driving roles. Employers must demonstrate labor market shortages to obtain a work permit.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a heavy truck driver in Belgium?**
A: You need a valid category C or C+E driving licence and a Certificate of Professional Competence (CPC) for drivers, obtained through initial qualification training. A medical fitness certificate is required. Many employers also expect experience, a clean driving record, and basic knowledge of Dutch, French, or German depending on the region. ADR certification is needed for hazardous materials.
