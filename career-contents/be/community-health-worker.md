## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a community health worker in Belgium?**
A: Salaries for community health workers in Belgium typically range from €2,000 to €3,500 gross per month, depending on experience, sector (public, private, or NGO), and region. Public sector pay follows predefined scales, while private organizations may offer higher wages. Entry-level positions start around €2,000, with experienced workers earning up to €3,500.

**Q: What are the work visa options for a non-EU community health worker to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU community health workers can apply for an EU Blue Card if they hold a recognized higher education degree and meet the salary threshold (€45,000+ gross annually). Alternatively, the Single Permit allows work for up to 12 months with any employer, requiring a job offer and labor market check. Both routes require employer sponsorship and recognition of qualifications through NARIC.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a community health worker in Belgium?**
A: Becoming a community health worker in Belgium typically requires a bachelor’s degree in social work, public health, nursing, or a related field. Foreign diplomas must be recognized by NARIC (Dutch/Flemish community) or equivalency services (French community). Relevant experience and fluency in Dutch, French, or German (depending on region) are often required. Some roles may accept associate degrees with additional training.
