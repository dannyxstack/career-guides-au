**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)

- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building frame worker in Belgium?**
A: In Belgium, the gross monthly salary for building frame and related trades workers typically ranges from €1,900 to €2,800, depending on experience and region. Collective bargaining agreements set minimum wages, and additional benefits like holiday pay and end-of-year bonuses are common. Overtime and specialization can increase earnings.

**Q: What are the work visa options for non-EU building frame workers to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU workers can apply for a single permit (combined work and residence) if they have a job offer from a Belgian employer. For highly skilled roles, the EU Blue Card may be an option, but it requires a higher salary threshold (€50,622 in 2024) and a valid employment contract. Alternatively, the professional card for self-employment is available for independent workers.

**Q: What training is required to become a building frame worker in Belgium?**
A: Typically, a secondary education diploma in construction (e.g., from a technical school) or an apprenticeship is required. Many workers attain a Certificat d'Enseignement Secondaire Supérieur (CESS) with a specialization in building frame work. On-the-job training and experience are crucial. Continuous learning is available through sectoral training funds.
