## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Belgium admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a software developer in Belgium?**
A: For a mid-level software developer, gross monthly salaries typically range from €3,500 to €6,000, depending on experience and region. Senior positions can exceed €7,000. Benefits often include meal vouchers, insurance, and a company car. Note that salaries are subject to high social security contributions and income tax, so net pay is roughly half.

**Q: What is the work visa process for software developers coming to Belgium?**
A: Non-EU software developers can apply for a work permit (single permit) or the EU Blue Card. The Blue Card requires a binding job offer, a minimum salary threshold (currently about €45,000/year for non-managers), and a recognized diploma or five years' experience. The employer must prove the vacancy could not be filled locally.

**Q: What is the current job market for software developers in Belgium?**
A: Demand for software developers remains high, especially in Brussels, Antwerp, and Leuven. Key sectors include fintech, telecom, and IT services. Employers value experience with cloud, mobile, and web technologies. Fluency in Dutch or French is often required, but English-only roles exist in international companies.
