**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Painting standard signs using templates and paint

- Standard font-based design layout
- Simple color mixing and sample making
- Conventional sign installation and surface finishing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to quickly generate complex design sketches and refine them by hand
- Optimise colour schemes and material simulations using AI
- Assists cultural heritage restoration by analyzing historical restoration documents with AI
- Use AI to generate a preliminary draft of a personalized plan.
- Use an AI-driven laser engraving machine combined with hand finishing

**Human moat:**

- Deep knowledge of traditional craftsmanship and material properties
- Customisation ability to understand client culture and emotional needs
- Judgment of historical style and restoration authenticity
- Flexible adjustments for non-standard surfaces and on-site environments
- Uniqueness of personal brand and artistic reputation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for signwriters, decorative painters, engravers, and etchers in Belgium?**
A: Pay varies by experience and region. According to sectoral collective agreements, the gross monthly salary for a beginner ranges from €1,800 to €2,200. Experienced workers can earn up to €3,000. Overtime and self-employed rates are higher.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card routes for non-EU signwriters wanting to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for a single permit (combined work and residence) or the EU Blue Card for highly qualified work. This occupation typically requires proof of qualifications and a recognized employer. Regional labor market tests may apply. No specific quotas are set for this ISCO code.

**Q: What training or certification is required to work as a signwriter or decorative painter in Belgium?**
A: Typically, a secondary vocational diploma (e.g., in painting or signmaking) is required. Apprenticeships and recognized professional training programs offered by regional authorities or sectoral funds are common. Self-regulation is possible, but certification (e.g., VDAB or Forem) enhances job prospects.
