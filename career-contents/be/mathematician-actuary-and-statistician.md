## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Belgium?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Actuaries earn between €50,000 and €100,000 annually. Statisticians in the private sector average €45,000 to €75,000, while mathematicians in research typically earn €40,000 to €70,000. These figures are before taxes and include benefits.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU professionals can apply for an EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, requiring a valid job offer with salary at least €60,000 in 2024. Alternatively, a single permit combines work and residence. While the occupation may be on Belgium's shortage list, this does not guarantee a visa; check with the Belgian Immigration Office.

**Q: What qualifications are commonly required for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Belgium?**
A: A master's degree in mathematics, statistics, or actuarial science is standard. Actuaries often pursue professional certification from the Belgian Institute of Actuaries. Statisticians benefit from proficiency in data analysis tools. Language skills (Dutch, French, or English) are important depending on the region and employer.
