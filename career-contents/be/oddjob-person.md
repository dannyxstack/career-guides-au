## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for odd-job persons in Belgium?**
A: Pay varies widely by task and region, but most odd-job workers earn at least the Belgian minimum wage (€1,995.91/month gross for full-time in 2024, or about €12.50/hour). Many are paid per task; experienced general handypersons may charge €25–40/hour. Employers must also provide social security coverage.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as odd-job persons in Belgium via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is not available for odd-job persons as it requires a highly skilled job with a degree and a salary above €65,814/year (2024). For low-skilled work, non-EU nationals can apply for a single permit (work and residence) if the employer proves labour market shortage, but strict conditions apply. Self-employment is also possible with a professional card.

**Q: What are common tasks for odd-job persons in Belgium and are there advancement opportunities?**
A: Typical tasks include painting, minor plumbing, furniture assembly, gardening, and cleaning. Advancement is possible by building a reputation, obtaining certifications (e.g., VCA safety), or specializing in a trade. Some become self-employed handypersons or start small renovation businesses, but formal training often leads to better pay and job security.
