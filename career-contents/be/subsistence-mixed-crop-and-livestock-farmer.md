## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Belgium?**
A: Income from subsistence farming in Belgium is minimal, as production is mainly for own consumption. Many farmers rely on part-time employment or EU subsidies. Official data does not separately report subsistence farming income, but typical net farm income for smallholders is very low, often requiring supplemental support.

**Q: Can a foreign national work as a subsistence farmer in Belgium via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers with a university degree or equivalent experience in a high-level position, not subsistence farming. Third-country nationals wishing to farm in Belgium may apply for a self-employed visa (carte professionnelle) but must submit a viable business plan, prove sufficient means, and meet language requirements. Subsistence farming rarely meets the economic viability criteria.

**Q: What are the main challenges for subsistence farmers in Belgium?**
A: Subsistence farmers in Belgium face challenges including high land costs, strict environmental regulations, and limited market access. Many operate on small plots (<5 hectares) and rely on family labor. The aging farmer population and lack of successors are growing concerns. Changing climate patterns also affect crop yields and livestock health.
