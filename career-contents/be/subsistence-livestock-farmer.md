## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence livestock farmer in Belgium?**
A: Income is highly variable and generally low, as production is mainly for own consumption. Most subsistence farmers earn below the Belgian minimum wage (€1,954.53/month in 2025) and often supplement with other income sources. Earnings depend on farm size, livestock type, and direct sales.

**Q: What are the visa options for a non-EU subsistence livestock farmer to work in Belgium?**
A: The EU Blue Card is not applicable as it requires highly skilled employment. Non-EU nationals may apply for a single permit for self-employment, but must hold a professional card from the region (e.g., Brussels, Flanders, Wallonia). The application requires a detailed business plan, proof of sufficient funds, and compliance with zoning regulations. Approval is rare for subsistence-scale operations.

**Q: What are the main challenges faced by subsistence livestock farmers in Belgium?**
A: Key challenges include low profitability, high land prices, strict environmental regulations, and competition from industrial farms. Many struggle with administrative burdens and access to subsidies, which favor larger operations. Additionally, animal disease risks and fluctuating market prices for inputs like feed add to financial instability.
