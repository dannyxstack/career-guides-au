## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically replenish orders based on inventory data
- Regular product shelving and display
- Repeated counting and checking of goods
- Moving standard-size cargo boxes
- Print and attach price tags

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze high-demand areas and optimize product layout
- Quickly locate item positions through AR devices
- Adjust replenishment priorities based on sales forecasts
- Real-time synchronization and query using inventory management app
- Use AI assistance to plan efficient replenishment routes

**Human moat:**

- Fine handling of fragile, irregularly shaped, or fresh products
- resolving sudden issues such as shelf blockages and fallen merchandise
- Interacting with customers to provide product information and recommendations
- Coordinates non-standard processes such as returns and near-expiry items
- Maintain overall store environment and safety standards

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of retail automation systems
- Inventory data analysis and forecasting tools
- Operation of equipment such as forklifts and electric pallet jacks
- Knowledge of fresh food preservation and handling
- Basic Customer Service and Communication Skills
- Cross-functional team collaboration and coordination

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shelf filler in Belgium?**
A: The typical gross monthly salary for a shelf filler in Belgium ranges from €1,800 to €2,200, based on the national minimum wage and collective agreements. Pay may vary by employer, region, and experience. Part-time positions are common, with hourly rates around €11-13.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as shelf fillers in Belgium via the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers earning at least €45,000 annually (2024 threshold), which shelf fillers do not meet. Non-EU nationals can work as shelf fillers through a single permit (work visa), requiring employer sponsorship and a labor market test showing no suitable EU candidate available.

**Q: What qualifications or training are needed to become a shelf filler in Belgium?**
A: No formal qualifications are required. Employers typically provide on-the-job training. Knowledge of Dutch, French, or German (depending on region) is a strong advantage for customer interaction. Basic numeracy and physical stamina are essential. Some employers may require a secondary school diploma.
