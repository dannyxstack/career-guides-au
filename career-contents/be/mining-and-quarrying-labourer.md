- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts

- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).

- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for Mining and Quarrying Labourers in Belgium?**
A: The gross monthly salary for Mining and Quarrying Labourers in Belgium typically ranges between €2,000 and €2,500, depending on experience, employer, and region. Additional benefits may include shift allowances and bonuses. Wages are often regulated by collective bargaining agreements specific to the sector.

**Q: Can Mining and Quarrying Labourers from outside the EU obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Belgium?**
A: The EU Blue Card is reserved for highly qualified workers, typically requiring a higher education degree, so it is not applicable to labourers. However, non-EU workers may obtain a single work and residence permit (Permit B) if a Belgian employer sponsors them. The employer must prove that no suitable candidate is available in the EU.

**Q: What are the main duties and entry requirements for Mining and Quarrying Labourers in Belgium?**
A: Labourers assist with extracting minerals, operate machinery, and perform manual tasks like clearing debris and loading materials. Typically, no formal education is required, but on-the-job training is provided. A valid medical certificate and safety training (e.g., VCA certificate) are often mandatory. Physical fitness and a willingness to work in challenging conditions are essential.
