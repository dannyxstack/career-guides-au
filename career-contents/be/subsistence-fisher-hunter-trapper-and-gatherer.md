## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Belgium?**
A: Subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, and gathering are not common paid occupations in Belgium. Most such activities are recreational or part of the informal economy. For the few who engage as a primary livelihood, income is variable and generally low, often below minimum wage, as there is no structured employment market for these roles.

**Q: Can a non-EU national obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a subsistence fisher, hunter, trapper, or gatherer in Belgium?**
A: The EU Blue Card requires a highly skilled job with a higher education degree and a salary above a set threshold, which subsistence fishing/hunting does not meet. Regular work permits for non-EU nationals are only granted for occupations with demonstrable labor shortages; these activities have virtually no employer demand. Thus, no standard migration route exists for this occupation.

**Q: Is subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, or gathering a viable career path in Belgium?**
A: These activities are not recognized as formal career paths in Belgium. Fishing is largely recreational or commercial (regulated by quotas), while hunting is strictly licensed and seasonal. Gathering wild foods is minimal. There are no training programs or job vacancies; participation is primarily for personal consumption or as a cultural practice. Economic viability is extremely limited.
