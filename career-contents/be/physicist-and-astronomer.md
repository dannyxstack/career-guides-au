## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for physicists and astronomers in Belgium?**
A: Salaries for physicists and astronomers in Belgium vary by experience and sector. Entry-level positions in research or industry start around €45,000–€55,000 gross per year. Senior roles or positions in top research institutes can reach €70,000–€90,000. Benefits often include extra vacation days, meal vouchers, and a 13th month. Academic salaries are set by university collective agreements.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for physicists and astronomers moving to Belgium?**
A: Non-EU physicists and astronomers can work in Belgium via two main official routes: the EU Blue Card, for highly qualified workers with a job offer meeting salary thresholds (currently €65,499 gross/year, lower for shortage occupations), or the single permit, combining work and residence authorization. Both require employer sponsorship and recognition of your degree by authorities like NARIC.

**Q: What are the career prospects for physicists and astronomers in Belgium?**
A: Belgium offers diverse opportunities in research (e.g., universities, SCK•CEN, VITO, IMEC), aerospace, energy, and semiconductor sectors. With a PhD, roles in R&D, data science, or consultancy are common. The shortage of STEM professionals boosts demand. Bilingualism (French/Dutch) enhances prospects, especially in federal or Brussels-based institutions like the European Space Agency.
