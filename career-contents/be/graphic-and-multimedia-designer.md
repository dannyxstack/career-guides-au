## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch generation of social media templates and ad banners
- Basic image processing (cutout, color adjustment, filters)
- Automatically completing layout and formatting based on preset styles
- Generate simple logo and icon drafts
- Automated output of print-ready files (bleeds, crop marks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Generate creative sketches with AI to rapidly iterate design proposals
- Leverage AI to analyze user data and optimize visual strategies
- AI-assisted font matching and color matching to improve design efficiency
- Auto-generate design variants for A/B testing
- AI tools enable rapid prototyping of video and motion graphics

**Human moat:**

- Deep understanding of brand strategy and user psychology to define visual direction
- Original creativity and unique artistic expression
- Cross-media storytelling (print + digital + environment)
- Efficient communication and feedback integration with clients and teams
- Sharp insight into cultural trends and social contexts

**Skills to build:**

- AI design tools (Midjourney, DALL·E 3, Adobe Firefly)
- Motion design and video editing (After Effects, Premiere Pro)
- User Experience and Interface Design (Figma, Sketch)
- Data visualization and infographic design
- Project management and client communication (agile collaboration)
- 3D modeling and rendering basics (Blender, C4D)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for graphic and multimedia designers in Belgium?**
A: Starting salaries for junior graphic designers in Belgium range from €2,500 to €3,200 gross per month. Experienced designers earn between €3,500 and €5,000, while senior or art directors can reach €6,000 or more. Salaries vary by region (Brussels higher) and industry (e.g., advertising vs. tech). Benefits often include meal vouchers and transport.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU graphic designers to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU designers need a single permit combining work and residence. The EU Blue Card is another option for highly qualified workers, requiring a job offer and salary meeting the annual threshold set by Belgium (e.g., around €45,000 for non-regulated professions in 2024). Shortage occupation lists may lower the threshold. Employers must prove they couldn't find an EU candidate.

**Q: What career progression opportunities are available for graphic designers in Belgium?**
A: Designers typically advance from junior to senior roles, then to art director or creative director. Specialisation in UX/UI, motion design, or branding can lead to higher pay. Freelancing is common, with potential to build a client base. Belgian design agencies and in-house teams offer paths to team lead or managerial positions. Continuous learning is valued.
