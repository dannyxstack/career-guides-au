- Automatic comparison and recording of routine maintenance checklists (e.g., post-flight checks)
- AI image recognition initial screening for structural fatigue cracks

- Maintenance Schedule Optimization (Leveraging Fleet Usage Data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to overlay repair diagrams and real-time data to improve troubleshooting efficiency
- AI assists in analyzing historical repair case databases to quickly locate rare faults
- Digital twin simulates maintenance processes to optimize disassembly and assembly steps
- Use natural language processing to automatically generate maintenance logs and compliance documents

**Human moat:**

- Manual craftsmanship and experiential intuition in high-risk repair operations (e.g., engine disassembly, line repair)
- Creative troubleshooting for unexpected failures (involving cross-system logical reasoning)
- Legal responsibility and signing authority for airworthiness release (individual license holder bears liability)
- Communication with and compliance assessment of the regulator (CASA)
- On-site resource coordination and decision-making during emergency repairs

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an aircraft engine mechanic in Belgium?**
A: In Belgium, the gross monthly salary for an aircraft engine mechanic typically ranges from €2,500 to €3,500, depending on experience, certifications, and employer. Additional benefits like shift allowances and overtime pay are common. Salaries are often determined by sectoral collective bargaining agreements (PC 121 for the aviation sector) and may increase with seniority and specialization.

**Q: What are the work visa options for foreign aircraft engine mechanics to work in Belgium?**
A: Foreign aircraft engine mechanics can work in Belgium via the EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, or a single permit for employed work. The EU Blue Card requires a recognized higher education degree (or equivalent professional experience) and a high-salary threshold. The single permit combines work and residence authorization and is subject to labor market testing unless the occupation is on the shortage list.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become an aircraft engine mechanic in Belgium?**
A: To become an aircraft engine mechanic in Belgium, you typically need an EASA Part-66 aircraft maintenance license (B1 or B2) or a recognized diploma in aeronautical engineering. Apprenticeships or vocational training in aviation are common entry points. Continuing education and type ratings on specific engine models are essential for career advancement and higher pay.
