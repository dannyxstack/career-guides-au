## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Moving construction materials (replaced by AGVs/drones).
- Basic excavation and earthworks (replaced by unmanned excavators)
- On-site cleaning and debris sorting (replaced by cleaning robots)
- Simple bricklaying and plastering (replaced by automatic brick-laying machines/spraying machines)
- Repetitive rebar tying (replaced by tying robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to read construction drawings, improving accuracy.
- Operate semi-automated tools (e.g., electric screwdrivers, laser levels)
- Maintenance and debugging of automated equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Report progress and receive instructions via digital platforms
- Using BIM software for collaborative work and problem-solving

**Human moat:**

- Judgment in unstructured environments (e.g., complex terrain, unexpected situations)
- Fine hand-eye coordination (e.g., installing irregular components).
- Team on-site communication and emergency coordination
- Experience in safety supervision and accident prevention
- Mastery of traditional crafts (e.g., stonemasonry, special plastering)

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated construction equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Reading digital drawings and BIM basics
- Basic programming or equipment debugging skills
- Enhanced safety and quality management knowledge
- Knowledge of electrical/hydraulic system maintenance
- English or digital tools usage

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for building construction labourers in Belgium?**
A: The gross monthly salary for building construction labourers in Belgium typically ranges from €2,000 to €2,800, depending on experience, region, and employer. Under the joint committee for construction (PC 124), wages are set by collective agreements, and workers often receive additional benefits like meal vouchers and transport allowances.

**Q: What work visa or EU Blue Card routes are available for building construction labourers to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU workers can apply for a single permit (combined work and residence permit) if offered a job by a Belgian employer. The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers and generally not applicable for this occupation due to skill level requirements. Official routes include the single permit and, for certain third-country nationals, the professional card or work B permit.

**Q: What are the main duties of building construction labourers in Belgium?**
A: Building construction labourers in Belgium perform manual tasks such as digging, mixing concrete, carrying materials, cleaning sites, and assisting skilled tradespeople. They may operate simple machinery like compactors or concrete mixers. Work is physically demanding, often outdoors, and requires adherence to safety regulations under Belgian codex for well-being at work.
