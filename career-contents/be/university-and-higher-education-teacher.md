## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making
- Creative integration in overall curriculum design

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI education tools (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning).
- Data analysis and statistical modeling (Python/R)
- Prompt engineering and large model fine-tuning
- Blended instructional design (MOOC/flipped classroom)
- Academic writing and AI-assisted polishing
- Data Privacy and AI Ethics

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a university teacher in Belgium?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective bargaining agreements and vary by region (Flemish or French Community). A full professor earns between €5,500 and €9,000 gross per month, while assistant professors start around €3,500–€4,500. Exact figures depend on seniority, university, and academic rank.

**Q: What are the work visa options for non-EU teachers to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU teachers typically obtain an EU Blue Card or a single permit. The Blue Card requires a job offer with a gross annual salary above a threshold (adjusted yearly). The single permit combines work and residence; universities often sponsor the application. Both require a valid employment contract and qualifications recognition.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a university teacher in Belgium?**
A: A PhD is mandatory for most teaching and research positions. Postdoctoral experience is often required for professors. Proficiency in the language of instruction (Dutch, French, or German) is essential. International publications and teaching experience strengthen applications. Recognition of foreign degrees may be needed.
