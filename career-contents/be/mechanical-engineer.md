**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- CAD drafting and 2D drawing generation
- Standard finite element analysis (FEA) and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations
- Generate Standard BOMs and Technical Documentation
- Routine tolerance analysis and dimensional inspection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-driven generative design (e.g., topology optimization)
- Machine learning-based failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA)
- Digital twin real-time simulation and optimization
- Smart materials selection and supply chain optimization
- Write automated test and verification scripts

**Human moat:**

- Cross-system physical intuition and innovative design
- Complex nonlinear problem diagnosis and on-site debugging
- Ethical and responsible decision-making for safety-critical systems
- Unstructured communication with clients and suppliers
- Multidisciplinary integration and system architecture decision-making

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a mechanical engineer in Belgium?**
A: The average gross annual salary for a mechanical engineer in Belgium ranges from €45,000 to €65,000, depending on experience and sector. Entry-level positions start around €38,000, while senior engineers can earn over €80,000. Benefits often include meal vouchers, insurance, and a company car.

**Q: What are the work visa options for non-EU mechanical engineers to work in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU mechanical engineers can work in Belgium via the EU Blue Card or the Single Permit. The Blue Card requires a valid job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (approx. €60,000 in 2024). The Single Permit combines work and residence authorization, processed by the regional employment office.

**Q: Which industries employ the most mechanical engineers in Belgium?**
A: Belgian mechanical engineers are in high demand in automotive (e.g., Volvo Cars Gent), aerospace (e.g., Sabca, Sonaca), and machinery manufacturing. The chemical and pharmaceutical sectors also hire many, along with renewable energy companies focusing on wind and solar power. Brussels, Flanders, and Wallonia all have strong engineering hubs.
