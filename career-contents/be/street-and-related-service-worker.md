## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Belgium?**
A: Street and related services workers in Belgium typically earn around minimum wage, which as of 2024 is €1,994.81 gross per month for full-time work. Actual pay may vary by employer, location, and specific duties. Many workers in this category are self-employed or work part-time, so annual earnings can be lower.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as street and related services workers in Belgium via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is generally not available for this occupation due to the high salary threshold (€65,446 gross/year in 2024) and the requirement for a higher education degree. Non-EU nationals may instead apply for a single permit for low- or medium-skilled employment, but such opportunities are limited and employer sponsorship is required.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for street and related services workers in Belgium?**
A: Career progression may include moving into supervisory roles, such as team leader for a cleaning or vending company, or starting a small business (e.g., mobile food vending). Additional training in sales, customer service, or sanitation can improve prospects. Some workers transition to related fields like waste management or logistics.
