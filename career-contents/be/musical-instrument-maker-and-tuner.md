## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a musical instrument maker or tuner in Belgium?**
A: Pay varies by experience, employer, and instrument type. Full-time workers generally earn between €2,200 and €3,800 gross per month. Trainees or those in small workshops may start lower, while specialized tuners for pianos or string instruments with several years of experience can earn more. Benefits such as health insurance and pension contributions are usually included.

**Q: How can a non-EU national obtain a work visa as a musical instrument maker in Belgium?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work permit, typically via the EU Blue Card or a single permit. The Blue Card requires a binding job offer with a gross annual salary at least 1.5 times the average Belgian salary (approximately €60,000 in 2024). Alternatively, the single permit applies for lower-skilled roles; the employer must prove the position cannot be filled locally. Both require employer sponsorship.

**Q: What training or qualifications are needed to become a musical instrument maker in Belgium?**
A: Most are trained through apprenticeship programs (e.g., via VDAB in Flanders or FOREM in Wallonia) or full-time education at technical schools like the Institut des Arts et Métiers in Brussels. A certificate of competence (e.g., CQ6 in instrument making) is often required. Advanced roles may demand several years of apprenticeship or a specialized bachelor's degree from a recognized conservatory.
