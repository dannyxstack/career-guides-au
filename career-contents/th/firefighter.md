- Emergency dispatch and route planning (AI automatically assigns the nearest fire station and optimal route)
- Fire equipment inventory management and maintenance record generation
- Basic fire classification and resource matching report writing
- Some routine clerical work (e.g., training records, accident report drafts)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-enhanced fire scene situational awareness (infrared and gas sensor data fusion, real-time display of danger zones)
- AI-assisted rescue decision-making (generates optimal breaching/extinguishing plans based on historical cases)
- Drone and robot collaborative reconnaissance (AI-controlled drones automatically identify trapped persons)
- Smart monitoring of personal protective equipment (AI monitoring of heart rate, temperature, early warning of health risks)
- Training simulations and virtual reality (AI generates diverse fire scenarios to enhance practical skills)

**Human moat:**

- Physical fitness and adaptability under extreme conditions
- Cross-departmental field coordination and command authorization

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a firefighter in Thailand?**
A: The average monthly salary for a municipal firefighter in Thailand ranges from 15,000 to 30,000 THB, depending on experience, rank, and location. Additional allowances may apply for hazardous duty or overtime. Salaries are generally set by local government scales, with senior officers earning up to 50,000 THB.

**Q: What does a typical day look like for a firefighter in Thailand?**
A: Firefighters in Thailand work shifts, often 24 hours on duty followed by 24–48 hours off. Daily routines include equipment checks, physical training, and station maintenance. When responding to emergencies, they fight fires, perform rescues, and provide medical aid. Non-emergency duties may involve fire safety inspections and public education.

**Q: What skills and qualifications are required to become a firefighter in Thailand?**
A: Minimum requirements include a high school diploma, physical fitness, and passing a competency test. Many municipalities prefer vocational certificates in firefighting or emergency medical services. Key skills are teamwork, quick decision-making, and ability to work under extreme conditions. Continuous training in rescue techniques and hazardous materials handling is essential.
