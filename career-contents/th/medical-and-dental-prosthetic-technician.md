## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Digital design of standard dentures and crowns using CAD software
- Automated layout and grinding of restorations (e.g. CAM machine operation)
- Standardized model generation based on scan data
- Inventory management and order tracking.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software improves the efficiency and accuracy of complex restoration design
- Smart scanning and diagnostic analysis help technicians quickly obtain patient oral data.
- AI-driven material performance simulation optimization solution selection
- Enhanced communication with dentists and prosthetists via remote collaboration platforms

**Human moat:**

- Personalised aesthetic judgment and manual fine adjustment (e.g., biomimetic colouring)
- Handling complex maxillofacial defects, biomechanical adaptation
- Ability to communicate clinical needs directly with dentists/doctors
- Innovative Restoration Design (New Solutions Without AI Database Support)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM software operation (e.g., 3Shape, exocad)
- Knowledge of 3D printing and CNC precision machining
- Digital oral scanning and data analysis
- Materials science (bioceramics, composite resins, etc.)
- Interpersonal communication and clinical collaboration skills
- Basic programming and AI tool application

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for medical and dental prosthetic technicians in Thailand?**
A: In Thailand, entry-level prosthetic technicians earn around 15,000–25,000 THB per month. With 3–5 years of experience, salaries rise to 30,000–50,000 THB. Senior technicians in specialized labs or hospitals can earn 60,000–80,000 THB monthly. Pay varies by employer, location (Bangkok vs. provinces), and certification level.

**Q: What technical skills are essential for prosthetic technicians working in Thailand?**
A: Key skills include proficiency in CAD/CAM software for digital design, manual dexterity for crafting dental prostheses (crowns, bridges, dentures), and knowledge of materials like ceramics and polymers. Familiarity with Thai dental standards and laboratory equipment (3D printers, milling machines) is crucial, along with attention to detail and color matching.

**Q: How is AI impacting the career outlook for dental prosthetic technicians in Thailand?**
A: AI is automating routine tasks like digital scanning and basic crown design, but demand remains strong for skilled technicians who can handle complex cases, perform manual finishing, and ensure quality control. The outlook is positive for those who adapt to new technologies—employment is expected to grow steadily as Thailand's elderly population increases need for dental prosthetics.
