**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Underwater visual inspection and video recording: AI image recognition automatically labels defects.
- Routine structural measurement: ROV with sensors automatically collects data
- Underwater environment monitoring: AI analyzes parameters such as water temperature and turbidity
- Simple salvage operation: remote-controlled robotic arm performs standard operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making: real-time analysis of diver physiological data to warn of decompression sickness risk
- Augmented reality headset: overlays structural diagrams and repair instructions to improve work accuracy
- Predictive maintenance: AI analyzes historical data to optimize maintenance schedules.
- Remote specialist collaboration: AI translation and 3D reconstruction help land-based experts guide

**Human moat:**

- Manual operation and quick judgment in emergency situations
- Fine tactile sensation and force feedback (e.g., valve operation)
- Adaptability in harsh environments (current, visibility)
- Legal and safety responsibility attribution (divers must sign on-site)

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for underwater divers in Thailand?**
A: Entry-level divers earn around THB 20,000–30,000 monthly, while experienced commercial divers can make THB 50,000–100,000 per month. Pay varies by sector—oil and gas, construction, or tourism—with offshore diving offering higher rates due to risk and certification requirements.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to work as an underwater diver in Thailand?**
A: You need a recognized certification like PADI Open Water or SSI. For commercial diving, Thai Department of Fisheries or IMCA certifications are often required. Additional skills include underwater welding, inspection techniques, and proficiency in Thai language for local communication.

**Q: How is AI impacting underwater diving careers in Thailand?**
A: AI is automating data analysis from underwater sensors and ROVs, reducing demand for simple inspection dives. However, complex tasks like repair and salvage still rely on human divers. Divers skilled in ROV operation and data interpretation have better prospects as the industry evolves.
