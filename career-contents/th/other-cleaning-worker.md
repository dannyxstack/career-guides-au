**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for other cleaning workers in Thailand?**
A: Other cleaning workers in Thailand typically earn between 9,000 and 15,000 THB per month, depending on location and employer. Many are paid at or slightly above the provincial minimum wage. Overtime pay may be available, especially in hotels and large cleaning companies, which can increase monthly earnings.

**Q: What is the career outlook for cleaning workers in Thailand?**
A: The demand for cleaning workers in Thailand remains stable due to growth in tourism, residential services, and commercial cleaning. While automation is increasing for routine tasks, many specialized cleaning jobs require human workers. Advancement to supervisory roles is possible with experience, and job prospects are expected to remain steady.

**Q: Will AI affect cleaning jobs in Thailand?**
A: AI and automation are gradually being adopted in Thailand's cleaning industry, mainly for repetitive tasks like floor scrubbing in large facilities. However, many roles such as detailed manual cleaning, window washing, and waste collection still rely on human workers. The impact on job availability is currently limited, and demand for manual cleaners remains strong.
