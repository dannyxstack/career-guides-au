# Dancer and Choreographer — career analysis · Hungary

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

Dancers and choreographers in Hungary are primarily employed in state-funded theatres, independent dance companies, and cultural institutions, with a strong presence in Budapest. The profession is niche but valued in the creative sector, often requiring formal training from institutions like the Hungarian Dance Academy. Freelance work is common, with competition for permanent positions in classical ballet and contemporary dance. The labour market is small, and many dancers supplement income through teaching or choreographing for events.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with formal training at a dance academy or university, followed by joining a company or freelancing. Experienced dancers may become soloists or choreographers, and some move into teaching or artistic direction. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a dancer in Hungary?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on employer and experience. Government theatre corps members earn a base salary (approx. 3–6 million HUF pre-tax), while freelance dancers may earn less. Choreographers can earn more, especially on commercial projects. Check KSH data for updated averages.

**Q: Can a non-EU dancer work in Hungary?**
A: Yes, with a valid job offer and a Work and Residence Permit. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EU candidate is available. The EU Blue Card is an option for very high earners but is uncommon in dance. EU/EEA nationals have free movement.

**Q: Is formal dance education required to work as a dancer in Hungary?**
A: While not legally mandatory, most professional dancers in Hungary have completed a degree or vocational training. Major companies like the Hungarian National Ballet require a degree from a recognised academy. For freelance or commercial work, a certificate may suffice.
