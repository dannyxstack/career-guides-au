# Valuer and Loss Assessor — career analysis · Hungary

**Occupation code: 3315 (ISCO08)**

Valuers and loss assessors in Hungary estimate the value of property, goods, or assets and assess insurance claims, respectively. They are crucial for real estate transactions, mortgage lending, insurance underwriting, and legal proceedings. The profession requires strong analytical skills and impartial judgment, often supported by professional certification from the Hungarian Chamber of Valuers (MÉSZ). With a growing real estate market and robust insurance sector, demand for qualified valuers and loss assessors remains steady in urban areas, particularly Budapest.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an assistant to a certified valuer or loss adjuster, followed by obtaining professional certification (e.g., from MÉSZ or CEAR). Experienced professionals can become team leads, independent contractors, or open their own appraisal firms. Advanced specializations (e.g., heritage property, agricultural land, or large-scale claims) enhance earning potential. Digitalization (automated valuation models) is changing the field, requiring valuers to adopt new tools. Senior roles may involve court-appointed expert witness or advisory roles for banks and insurers.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a valuer in Hungary?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and specialization. Entry-level positions start around 350,000–450,000 HUF gross per month, while senior valuers can earn 700,000 HUF or more, with potential bonuses. Official statistics from KSH or professional surveys provide detailed figures.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a valuer in Hungary?**
A: Yes, but a work and residence permit is required, typically based on a job offer from a Hungarian employer. The EU Blue Card is an option for highly qualified professionals meeting the salary threshold. The occupation is not currently on the restricted lists, but immigration authorities assess each case individually.

**Q: Is professional certification mandatory to work as a valuer in Hungary?**
A: For independent valuation activities (e.g., for mortgage lending or court purposes), certification by the Hungarian Chamber of Valuers (MÉSZ) is mandatory. Loss assessors need a license from MABISZ. Working as an assistant may not require certification, but advancement typically does.
