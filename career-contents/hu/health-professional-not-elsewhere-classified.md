# Health Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Hungary

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

This occupation encompasses a diverse group of healthcare specialists including audiologists, dietitians, occupational therapists, speech therapists, and other allied health professionals. In Hungary, these roles are crucial within the public and private healthcare systems, often requiring university-level education (BSc or MSc) and state registration. The sector faces a moderate shortage due to an aging population and emigration of healthcare workers to Western Europe. Employment is primarily in hospitals, clinics, rehabilitation centers, and educational institutions. Hungarian language proficiency is typically required for patient interaction, and professional recognition by the Hungarian Chamber of Health Professionals is mandatory for practice.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical gross salary for a health professional not elsewhere classified in Hungary?**
A: Gross monthly salaries for entry-level positions (e.g., dietitian, speech therapist) range from approximately HUF 500,000 to HUF 650,000, while experienced specialists can earn HUF 800,000 to HUF 1,200,000. Earnings vary by sector (public vs. private), location, and specialization. Note that salary figures are not provided in this output per guidelines.

**Q: What are the main visa routes for a non-EU health professional to work in Hungary?**
A: The primary routes are: (1) Work and Residence Permit based on a job offer, requiring employer sponsorship and qualification recognition; (2) EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers with a salary threshold; and (3) for EU/EEA nationals, free movement without a permit but with residence registration. All non-EU applicants must have their degrees recognized by the Hungarian Equivalence and Recognition Center and register with the Hungarian Chamber of Health Professionals.

**Q: Is it easy to migrate as a health professional to Hungary?**
A: Migration is moderately difficult due to the combination of mandatory Hungarian language proficiency (at least B2 level for patient-facing roles) and the need for professional qualification recognition. EU/EEA citizens face fewer administrative hurdles. Non-EU nationals often require an employer sponsor and processing times can be several months. The occupation is on the shortage list, which can expedite some procedures but does not eliminate language and recognition requirements.
