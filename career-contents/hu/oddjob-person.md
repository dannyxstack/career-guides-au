# Odd-Job Person — career analysis · Hungary

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons perform a variety of simple, non-specialized tasks for private households or small businesses, including cleaning, gardening, minor repairs, and errands. In Hungary, this occupation is characterized by high flexibility, often informal or part-time arrangements, and low entry barriers. Many workers are employed in the grey economy, with limited social protection and variable earnings. The role is common among older workers, students, and newly arrived immigrants seeking immediate but temporary income.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,964. The outlook for odd-job persons in Hungary remains stable but stagnant. Demand is driven by household outsourcing and small business needs, but automation and self-service technologies are reducing some tasks. The informal nature of much of this work limits data visibility and growth potential. Immigration policies are very restrictive for non-EU workers in unskilled roles, so the labour pool is primarily domestic or EU/EEA. No significant expansion is expected aside from seasonal fluctuations in agriculture and tourism.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is minimal; most odd-job persons remain in this category throughout their working lives unless they acquire specific skills. Some may transition into specialized roles like professional cleaning, gardening, or construction assistance after gaining experience. The occupation often serves as a stopgap for those between jobs, new entrants, or retirees supplementing income. Upskilling is rare due to low earnings and time constraints.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- cleaning
- gardening
- gig economy
- household assistance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Hungary?**
A: Earnings vary widely; many are paid per task or hour, often at or below the minimum wage (currently around 232,000 HUF/month gross). Formal full-time employment is rare; part-time or informal work is the norm.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a visa to work as an odd-job person in Hungary?**
A: It is very challenging. Work permits for unskilled jobs are rarely granted due to strict labour market tests. EU Blue Cards are not applicable. The Guest Worker permit is limited to certain skilled trades, not odd-job roles. Only EU/EEA citizens have free access.
