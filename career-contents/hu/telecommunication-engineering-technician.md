# Telecommunications Engineering Technician — career analysis · Hungary

**Occupation code: 3522 (ISCO08)**

Telecommunications engineering technicians are responsible for installing, maintaining, and repairing telecommunications equipment and networks, including fiber optics, wireless systems, and IP-based infrastructure. In Hungary, demand is driven by the rapid expansion of 5G networks, fiber-to-the-home projects, and the digital transformation of industries. Technicians work for telecom operators, contractors, and increasingly for enterprise IT departments. The occupation is recognized as a skilled technical role, with opportunities in both urban and rural areas as the country aims for nationwide high-speed connectivity. The role requires practical skills in cabling, configuration, and troubleshooting, often supported by vocational training or specialized certifications.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a telecommunications engineering technician in Hungary?**
A: Entry-level technicians earn about 400,000–500,000 HUF gross per month. With 3–5 years of experience, salaries range from 550,000–700,000 HUF; senior technicians or team leads can earn 800,000 HUF or more. Overtime and on-call pay are common. Salaries are higher in Budapest and for those with specialized vendor certifications.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a telecommunications technician in Hungary?**
A: Yes, but they need a job offer and a work permit. The occupation is on the shortage list, which can simplify the process. EU Blue Card eligibility requires a higher salary threshold (around 1.5x national average). The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled by a Hungarian or EU worker.

**Q: What career advancement options exist for a technician in Hungary?**
A: Technicians can advance to senior technician, team leader, or network operations manager. Further education (e.g., BSc in telecom engineering) enables roles in network planning or project management. Specializing in cybersecurity or IP networking opens paths to higher-paying IT roles.
