- Automated fruit picking using AI vision and robotic arms

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze soil and meteorological data to optimize planting plans and variety selection
- Precisely identify early diseases through computer vision and recommend localized pesticide application.
- Uses predictive models to optimize harvest timing and market sales strategies
- Use digital twin technology to simulate the impact of different management strategies on production yields

**Human moat:**

- Ability to flexibly adjust operations in complex terrain or extreme weather
- Holistic management intuition and experience for multi-year tree growth cycles
- On-site coordination and negotiation with seasonal workers, cooperatives, and buyers.

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly salary for tree and shrub crop growers in Indonesia?**
A: In Indonesia, tree and shrub crop growers typically earn between IDR 2,500,000 and IDR 5,000,000 per month, varying by crop type (e.g., rubber, coffee, cocoa), experience, and region. Smallholder farmers may have irregular income based on harvest yields and market prices, while plantation workers often receive a base salary plus bonuses.

**Q: What key skills are required for tree and shrub crop growers in Indonesia?**
A: Essential skills include knowledge of crop-specific cultivation methods, pest and disease control, pruning, irrigation, and soil management. Physical stamina, attention to detail, and basic business skills for marketing are important. Familiarity with sustainable practices, such as organic farming or agroforestry, is increasingly valued due to market demand and environmental concerns.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence impacting tree and shrub crop farming in Indonesia?**
A: AI adoption in Indonesian tree and shrub crop farming is still emerging, with applications like drone-based pest detection, soil sensors for precision irrigation, and yield prediction models. However, high costs and limited digital literacy among smallholders constrain widespread use. Government programs promoting smart farming are gradually increasing awareness and adoption in larger plantations.
