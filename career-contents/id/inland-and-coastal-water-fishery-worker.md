## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly income for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Indonesia?**
A: Most earn between IDR 2-4 million per month, but income varies greatly by region, season, and catch volume. Self-employed fishers may earn more during peak seasons, while those employed on larger boats receive fixed wages plus bonuses. Prices for fish and operating costs also affect net earnings.

**Q: What are the career prospects for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Indonesia?**
A: Demand remains stable due to high domestic fish consumption and growing aquaculture. However, overfishing, climate change, and infrastructure gaps pose risks. The government promotes sustainable fisheries and training programs, offering some opportunities for skill upgrading. Overall, jobs are available but may become more competitive.

**Q: How is technology changing the work of inland and coastal fisheries in Indonesia?**
A: Tools like GPS, fish finders, and satellite data improve locating fish and navigation, boosting efficiency. Mobile apps help track weather and market prices. However, many fishers lack digital literacy or access, slowing adoption. AI is not widely used yet, but simple automation in processing may reduce demand for manual sorting in the future.
