**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical monthly income for street vendors and related workers in Indonesia?**
A: Monthly income varies widely by location, product, and hours worked. In major cities like Jakarta, street vendors can earn between IDR 1.5 million to IDR 5 million per month. Essential factors include daily sales volume, profit margins, and operational costs such as permits or stall rentals.

**Q: What skills are essential for street services workers in Indonesia?**
A: Key skills include basic numeracy for cash handling and change, customer service to retain regulars, knowledge of local regulations for permits, and resilience to work outdoors in all weather. For food vendors, hygiene practices and recipe consistency are crucial. Adaptability to digital payment trends is increasingly important.

**Q: How is AI affecting street services work in Indonesia?**
A: AI is not directly replacing street vendors but is reshaping their ecosystem. E-commerce platforms and delivery apps use AI to match consumers with nearby sellers, potentially increasing visibility for tech-savvy vendors. However, competition from AI-optimized online stores may reduce foot traffic. Basic digital literacy is becoming valuable to leverage these tools.
