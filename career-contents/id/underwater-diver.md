**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Underwater visual inspection and video recording: AI image recognition automatically labels defects.
- Routine structural measurement: ROV with sensors automatically collects data
- Underwater environment monitoring: AI analyzes parameters such as water temperature and turbidity
- Simple salvage operation: remote-controlled robotic arm performs standard operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making: real-time analysis of diver physiological data to warn of decompression sickness risk
- Augmented reality headset: overlays structural diagrams and repair instructions to improve work accuracy
- Predictive maintenance: AI analyzes historical data to optimize maintenance schedules.
- Remote specialist collaboration: AI translation and 3D reconstruction help land-based experts guide

**Human moat:**

- Manual operation and quick judgment in emergency situations
- Fine tactile sensation and force feedback (e.g., valve operation)
- Adaptability in harsh environments (current, visibility)
- Legal and safety responsibility attribution (divers must sign on-site)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for underwater divers in Indonesia?**
A: Entry-level divers earn IDR 8–15 million per month, while experienced commercial divers in offshore oil and gas can earn IDR 20–40 million. Salaries vary based on certifications, depth, and project location—higher in remote areas like Papua. Dive tourism roles in Bali or Lombok may pay less but include tips.

**Q: What skills and certifications are essential for underwater divers in Indonesia?**
A: Indonesian divers need certification from recognized bodies like ADAS (Australian Diving Accreditation Scheme) or IMCA (International Marine Contractors Association). Skills include underwater welding, inspection techniques, and emergency procedures. Strong swimming ability, physical fitness, and knowledge of local marine conditions—strong currents and low visibility—are critical.

**Q: How is the career outlook for underwater divers in Indonesia, and is AI impacting the profession?**
A: Demand remains steady in Indonesia due to offshore oil and gas fields (e.g., Natuna, Mahakam) and marine tourism. AI and ROVs (remotely operated vehicles) handle some inspection tasks, but human divers are still needed for complex repairs and salvage. Competition is moderate; divers with advanced commercial certifications have the best prospects.
