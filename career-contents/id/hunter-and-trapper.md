## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for hunters and trappers in Indonesia?**
A: Hunters and trappers in Indonesia typically earn between IDR 2–4 million per month, though income is highly seasonal and location-dependent. Earnings come from selling bushmeat, pest control services, or wildlife management contracts. Most work as independent operators, so pay fluctuates with demand. Salaried positions are rare, and many supplement income with farming or guiding.

**Q: What skills are essential for hunters and trappers in Indonesia?**
A: Essential skills include deep knowledge of local wildlife behavior, tracking, and trap-setting techniques, along with firearm safety if licensed. Physical fitness, stamina, and navigation in remote forests are crucial. Compliance with Indonesia’s conservation laws and ethical practices is increasingly important. Many learn through apprenticeship, and experience with traditional methods is highly valued.

**Q: How is technology affecting hunting and trapping in Indonesia?**
A: Technology like GPS, camera traps, and drones is used for wildlife monitoring and anti-poaching, reducing demand for traditional trapping in conservation areas. However, subsistence and pest control hunting persist. The occupation faces decline due to urbanization and stricter wildlife laws, but niche opportunities in eco-tourism guiding and wildlife management are emerging for those who adapt.
