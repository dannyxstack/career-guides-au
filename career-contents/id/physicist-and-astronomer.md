## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for physicists and astronomers in Indonesia?**
A: Salaries vary by sector. In academia, a fresh graduate with a master's earns around IDR 5-8 million per month, while experienced professors earn IDR 15-30 million. In oil/gas or mining, salaries are higher, ranging IDR 10-20 million for mid-career roles. Government research institutes pay according to civil servant scales. Note: top universities and private research labs in Jakarta or Bandung offer premiums.

**Q: What does a typical day look like for a physicist in Indonesia?**
A: Physicists in Indonesia often split time between research, teaching, and administrative tasks. In universities, they prepare lectures, supervise student projects, and conduct experiments or simulations. At research institutes like LAPAN or BATAN, they analyze data, maintain instruments, and publish papers. Those in industry (e.g., oil & gas) focus on modeling, data interpretation, and equipment calibration. Collaboration with international teams is common.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence impacting physicists and astronomers in Indonesia?**
A: AI is increasingly used for data analysis in astronomy (e.g., classifying galaxies from telescope data) and physics simulations. Indonesian researchers adopt machine learning to speed up model calibrations and pattern recognition. However, AI is a tool, not a replacement; physicists still design experiments and interpret results. Universities now offer AI courses tailored to physics students. The impact is positive, enhancing research efficiency without eliminating jobs.
