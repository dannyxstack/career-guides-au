## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised questionnaire surveys conducted by phone or online
- Data entry and basic cleaning
- Initial screening and recruitment of respondents
- Execution of interviews with preset scripts
- Summary statistics of simple opinion polls

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Designing more precise questionnaires and sampling plans
- Use AI analysis tools to mine deep insights from data
- Handling complex, semi-structured interviews and sensitive topics.
- Sentiment analysis using natural language processing techniques
- Provide training data and quality verification for AI systems

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and rapport
- Understand and manage complex, emotional respondent reactions
- Cross-cultural, multilingual communication skills
- Strategic thinking for project design and result interpretation.
- Data quality ethical judgment and compliance awareness

**Skills to build:**

- Data Analysis and Visualization (Python/R, Tableau)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT, SurveyMonkey AI)
- Questionnaire design and experimental methods
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
- Basic programming and automation scripts
- Domain knowledge (e.g., healthcare, finance)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for survey and market research interviewers in Indonesia?**
A: Entry-level interviewers earn around IDR 3-4 million per month, while senior interviewers or team leaders can earn IDR 5-7 million. Freelance interviewers are often paid per completed survey, ranging from IDR 20,000 to IDR 100,000 per successful response. Pay varies by location, company, and project complexity.

**Q: What skills are essential for becoming a successful survey interviewer in Indonesia?**
A: Strong communication and interpersonal skills are crucial, especially in Bahasa Indonesia and local dialects. Interviewers need patience, active listening, and the ability to handle rejection. Basic numeracy and familiarity with digital tools like tablets or survey apps are increasingly important. Adaptability to different respondent types and cultural sensitivity are also key.

**Q: How is the job outlook for survey and market research interviewers in Indonesia, particularly with the rise of online surveys?**
A: The demand for face-to-face interviewers remains steady, especially for in-depth research in rural areas and specific demographics. However, online and phone surveys are growing. The role is evolving to include data quality checks and interviewer-assisted digital data collection. Automation of simple surveys may reduce entry-level opportunities, but skilled interviewers who can engage respondents and ensure data quality are still valued.
