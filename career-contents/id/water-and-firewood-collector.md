## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do water and firewood collectors typically earn in Indonesia?**
A: Pay varies widely by region and season. In rural areas, daily earnings range from IDR 30,000 to 70,000 (about USD 2–5). Payment may be in cash or kind, such as food or shelter. Income is irregular and often below official minimum wages, reflecting the informal nature of this labor.

**Q: What does a typical workday look like for a water and firewood collector?**
A: Work starts before dawn. Collectors travel to forests or water sources with axes, ropes, and containers. They chop firewood or fetch water, carrying heavy loads back home or to selling points. The job is physically demanding, taking 6–8 hours daily in remote areas with limited access to modern amenities.

**Q: Is the job of water and firewood collector threatened by AI or automation in Indonesia?**
A: AI and automation have minimal impact because the work relies entirely on manual labor in off-grid areas. However, gradual expansion of electricity, gas, or piped water may reduce demand. Still, for many remote communities, this occupation remains essential and is unlikely to be automated in the near future.
