## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence livestock farmer in Portugal?**
A: Subsistence livestock farming in Portugal generally yields low and variable income, often below the national minimum wage (€886 per month in 2025). Earnings depend on herd size, product sales (milk, meat), and access to subsidies from the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP). Most farmers supplement income via other work or family labor.

**Q: Can a non-EU subsistence livestock farmer obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Portugal?**
A: A subsistence livestock farmer is unlikely to qualify for an EU Blue Card, which requires a highly skilled job with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average. A standard work visa for non-EU nationals is possible if a Portuguese employer offers a contract meeting minimum wage and labor market test conditions. Self-employment via the D2 visa (entrepreneur) may apply, but proof of viable business plan and resources is needed.

**Q: What are the main challenges for subsistence livestock farmers in Portugal today?**
A: Portuguese subsistence farmers face challenges including climate change-induced droughts, limited access to veterinary services, and competition from larger agribusinesses. CAP subsidies often require administrative paperwork that smallholders find burdensome. Land fragmentation and an aging farmer population also hinder viability. Many rely on local markets and traditional breeds to maintain their livelihood.
