## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for dancers and choreographers in Portugal?**
A: Salaries vary widely. Entry-level dancers may earn the national minimum wage (around €760/month) or work on a per-project basis. Experienced choreographers employed by companies or productions can earn between €1,500 and €3,000 per month. Freelancers' income fluctuates depending on contracts and reputation.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card routes for dancers and choreographers to work in Portugal?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a higher education degree and a job offer meeting the salary threshold (adjusted annually). Alternatively, a regular work visa (Autorização de Residência para Trabalho Subordinado) requires a job offer and a labor market test. Self-employment is also possible through a residence permit for independent workers.

**Q: What qualifications or experience are typically required to become a dancer or choreographer in Portugal?**
A: A degree in dance or professional training from accredited schools is highly valued. For dancers, experience in companies or notable productions is essential. Choreographers often need a strong portfolio of original work, teaching experience, or additional training in dance pedagogy. Networking and participation in festivals also help build a career.
