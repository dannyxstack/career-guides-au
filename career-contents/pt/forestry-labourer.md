- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring

- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for forestry labourers in Portugal?**
A: As of recent data, forestry labourers in Portugal earn approximately €700 to €900 per month, depending on region and employer. The national minimum wage is €760 per month in 2024. Some seasonal variations may affect pay, with higher rates during peak forestry activities. Overtime and additional allowances can supplement income.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign forestry labourers to work in Portugal?**
A: Foreign forestry labourers can apply for a D1 residence visa for employed work if they have a valid job offer from a Portuguese employer. The employer must justify the need for a non-EU worker. The EU Blue Card is intended for highly qualified professionals requiring a higher education degree and higher salary threshold, so it typically does not apply to forestry labourers. All processes are managed by SEF.

**Q: What are the main duties of a forestry labourer in Portugal?**
A: Forestry labourers in Portugal perform tasks such as planting, pruning, thinning, and harvesting trees. They also clear undergrowth, maintain trails, and assist with fire prevention measures. Work occurs in forests, plantations, and natural reserves. Physical stamina and familiarity with tools like chainsaws and axes are essential. Duties vary seasonally, with peak activity in planting and harvesting periods.
