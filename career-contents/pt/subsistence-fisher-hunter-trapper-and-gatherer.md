## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers in Portugal?**
A: Income is irregular and depends on catch and local market prices. Most subsistence fishers earn near the national minimum wage or below, often supplemented by other work. Average annual earnings range from €6,000 to €10,000, but can vary widely by region and season.

**Q: What is the work visa route for subsistence fishers in Portugal?**
A: Non-EU workers need a work visa (Autorização de Residência para Exercício de Atividade Profissional Subordinada) sponsored by a Portuguese employer. The EU Blue Card is typically for high-skilled roles, so subsistence fishing usually does not qualify. Self-employment may require a D2 visa for entrepreneurs or independent workers.

**Q: What are the main activities of a subsistence fisher in Portugal?**
A: Subsistence fishers catch fish, shellfish, and other seafood using small boats or shore-based methods for personal consumption or local sale. They also gather seaweed, crabs, and mollusks along the coast. Work is seasonal, often combined with agriculture or tourism, and relies on traditional knowledge of tides and marine habitats.
