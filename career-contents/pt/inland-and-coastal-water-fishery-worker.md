**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Portugal?**
A: Entry-level workers earn around €800–€1,000 per month, while experienced workers can earn €1,200–€1,500. Many are self-employed or paid per catch. The national minimum wage is €820 (2024), but fishery work often exceeds this due to seasonal demand and cooperative shares.

**Q: What are the official visa routes for non-EU nationals to work as a fishery worker in Portugal?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work visa sponsored by a Portuguese employer. The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified roles and typically does not apply to fishery workers. Other routes include the D7 visa (for passive income) or job seeker visa, but work authorization requires employer sponsorship and compliance with national quotas.

**Q: What are typical working conditions for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Portugal?**
A: Workers face long hours, often before dawn, in variable weather. Tasks include netting, sorting, and gear maintenance. Employment can be seasonal, especially for coastal fishing. Safety training is mandatory. Regions like Algarve, Setúbal, and Aveiro are key fishing areas, with many workers part of cooperatives.
