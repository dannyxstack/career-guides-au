**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement

- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Improve communication and customer management skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an optometrist in Portugal?**
A: Entry-level optometrists earn around the national minimum wage (€1,035 per month), while experienced professionals in private practice can earn up to €2,500 per month. Salaries vary based on location, experience, and sector (public vs. private).

**Q: What work visa options exist for non-EU optometrists in Portugal?**
A: Non-EU optometrists can apply for a residence permit for employment with a valid job offer and work contract. The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified professionals with a recognized degree and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross annual salary.

**Q: What qualifications are required to practice optometry in Portugal?**
A: You need a recognized degree in optometry from a Portuguese university or an equivalent foreign degree approved by the Ministry of Science. Registration with the Portuguese Association of Optometrists (APO) is also required. Continuing education may be necessary to maintain registration.
