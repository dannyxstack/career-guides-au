## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Portugal?**
A: Earnings vary widely as many are self-employed or tip-based. Salaried workers often earn around Portugal's national minimum wage (€823 per month in 2024) plus tips. Average monthly income can range from €800 to €1,200, depending on location, hours, and customer volume.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens obtain a work visa for street and related services work in Portugal?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens need a work visa sponsored by a Portuguese employer. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EU worker is available. The EU Blue Card is not applicable for this occupation as it requires a higher skill level. Alternatively, the D7 passive income visa may be an option if eligible.

**Q: What are the main duties of street and related services workers in Portugal?**
A: Duties include selling goods or services in public spaces, such as food, crafts, or tickets; assisting with street parking; or providing information. Workers must often obtain a municipal license. Working conditions involve long hours outdoors, and language skills in Portuguese are typically required.
