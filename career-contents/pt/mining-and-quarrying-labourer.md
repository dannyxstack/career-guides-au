## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for mining and quarrying labourers in Portugal?**
A: Mining and quarrying labourers in Portugal earn between €700 and €1,200 per month, depending on experience, region, and employer. The national minimum wage is €820 per month (2025), but wages may vary with shift work or hazardous conditions. Collective bargaining agreements can also set higher rates.

**Q: What are the work visa requirements for non-EU mining and quarrying labourers in Portugal?**
A: Non-EU citizens need a job offer to apply for a work visa (D1 Visa). The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers with a degree or 5+ years experience; mining labourers typically do not qualify. The employer must prove the vacancy could not be filled locally. After 5 years, permanent residency may be possible.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to work as a mining or quarrying labourer in Portugal?**
A: No formal qualifications are required, but employers prefer basic literacy and numeracy. On-the-job training is common. Safety certifications (e.g., handling explosives or operating machinery) may be needed. Physical fitness and ability to work outdoors in all weather are essential. Experience in construction or manual labour is advantageous.
