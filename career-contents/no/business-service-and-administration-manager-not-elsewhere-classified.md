# Business Services and Administration Managers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1219 (ISCO08)**

Business services and administration managers oversee operations, strategy, and personnel in companies providing consulting, administrative support, or other business services. In Norway, these professionals are vital for organizational efficiency and are employed across industries such as management consulting, facilities management, and corporate services. The role requires strong leadership, analytical skills, and knowledge of Norwegian business practices. Demand is stable, with opportunities in both private and public sectors, particularly in Oslo and other urban centers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts in specialist roles (e.g., junior consultant, administrative coordinator) before advancing to manager, senior manager, and director positions. Many professionals hold a master's degree in business administration (MBA) or specialized certifications (e.g., PMP, Lean). Networking and experience in international environments are advantageous. The path offers high earning potential but requires continuous adaptation to regulatory changes and digital tools.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Administrative scheduling such as arranging meetings, managing calendars, and booking travel
- Enter, organize, and archive documents and data
- Handle routine customer inquiries and complaints (email/phone)
- Monitor office inventory and generate purchase orders
- Generate standard reports (e.g., budgets, attendance)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze facility usage data to optimize spatial layouts and energy efficiency
- Use natural language processing tools to quickly generate business proposals and contracts
- Developing customer service and supplier management strategies based on AI prediction models
- Integrate cross-departmental data using RPA tools to support strategic decisions
- Use AI to monitor compliance risks and automatically generate audit logs

**Human moat:**

- Complex stakeholder coordination and conflict resolution
- Corporate strategic direction setting and crisis management
- Compliance decisions involving ethical judgment
- Cross-cultural team leadership and humanized motivation
- Unconventional innovation and business intuition

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool integration with RPA (e.g., UiPath, Power Automate)
- Data Analysis and Visualization (Python, Tableau)
- Digital project management (Jira, Asana)
- AI ethics and compliance knowledge
- Change management and guiding team AI transformation
- Advanced Excel and business process modeling

**Key growth areas:**

- Digitalization
- Consulting Services
- Sustainability
- Organizational Efficiency

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a business services and administration manager in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and sector, but mid-range annual earnings are typically between NOK 700,000 and 1,200,000, with top managers earning significantly more. Refer to SSB table 11418 for current statistics.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as a business services manager in Norway?**
A: Non-EEA nationals need a Skilled Worker residence permit, requiring a job offer, a completed bachelor's degree or higher, and a salary meeting the threshold (approx. NOK 500,000 per year). EU/EEA nationals can work under free movement rules.
