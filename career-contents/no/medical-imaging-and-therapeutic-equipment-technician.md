# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Medical imaging and therapeutic equipment technicians, commonly known as radiographers, operate advanced imaging and radiation therapy equipment in Norwegian hospitals and clinics. They play a crucial role in diagnosing and treating illnesses such as cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and fractures. The role requires a combination of technical expertise, patient care skills, and adherence to strict radiation safety protocols. Norway has a high standard of healthcare, and radiographers are integral to modern medicine, working closely with radiologists and other specialists. The profession is regulated, and practitioners must obtain authorization from the Norwegian Directorate of Health to practice independently.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a radiographer in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and region. Public sector radiographers follow collective agreements, with starting salaries around NOK 450,000 per year and experienced professionals earning upwards of NOK 600,000. Private clinics may offer higher pay. Overtime and shift differentials can increase total compensation.

**Q: How can a non-EEA radiographer obtain a work visa for Norway?**
A: Non-EEA radiographers need a Skilled Worker Residence Permit. Requirements include a written job offer meeting the salary threshold (currently approx. NOK 500,000), a recognized bachelor's degree in radiography, and authorization from the Norwegian Directorate of Health. The shortage designation can speed up processing.

**Q: What is the typical education path to become a radiographer in Norway?**
A: The standard route is a 3-year bachelor's degree in radiography at an accredited university or university college. The program includes both theoretical courses and supervised clinical placements. Graduates must then apply for authorization from the Norwegian Directorate of Health to practice.
