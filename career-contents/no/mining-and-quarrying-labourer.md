# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers perform manual tasks in the extraction of minerals, stone, and gravel from open-pit and underground mines. In Norway, this occupation is vital for supplying raw materials to the construction and metallurgical industries, with significant operations in Nordland, Finnmark, and Trøndelag. The sector employs around 2,000 workers, often in remote areas with shift work. Labourers assist in drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials, adhering to strict safety regulations enforced by the Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves advancement to machine operator or blaster after certification, or to supervisory roles like shift leader with experience. Many workers start as labourers and pursue vocational training (agrifag) to become skilled operators. Some move into quarry management or mineral processing. The Norwegian apprenticeship system (fagbrev) offers structured paths, though labourers often rely on on-the-job training and safety certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- mineral extraction
- quarry operations
- mining technology
- renewable energy minerals

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much do mining and quarrying labourers earn in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and collective agreements. According to SSB and industry sources, typical annual earnings range from 450,000 to 550,000 NOK. Overtime and remote location bonuses can significantly increase pay.

**Q: Can non-EEA citizens work as mining labourers in Norway?**
A: Non-EEA citizens generally cannot obtain a residence permit for this low-skilled occupation unless they have a completed vocational training (fagbrev) within mining. EU/EEA/EFTA nationals can work freely. There is no jobseeker permit for non-EEA low-skilled workers.

**Q: Is mining experience from abroad recognized in Norway?**
A: Foreign experience is valued but safety certifications (HMS-kort) and Norwegian language skills often required for permanent roles. Vocational qualifications may need approval from NOKUT.
