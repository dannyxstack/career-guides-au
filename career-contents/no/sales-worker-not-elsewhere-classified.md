# Sales Worker Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 5249 (ISCO08)**

Sales Workers Not Elsewhere Classified (ISCO-08 5249) encompass a wide range of roles in retail, telemarketing, and direct sales across Norway. These positions often involve selling products or services, handling customer inquiries, and processing transactions. The sector is diverse, with opportunities in small shops, large chains, and online sales. While many positions require no formal education beyond high school, employers often seek strong communication skills and customer service experience. Part-time and seasonal work is common, particularly in tourism-heavy regions. The role serves as an entry point for many into the Norwegian labour market, but career progression may be limited without further training or specialization.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression from general sales worker often involves moving into supervisory, specialist, or management roles, such as store manager, department manager, or key account representative. Some advance into procurement, marketing, or business development after gaining experience and completing relevant vocational training. Sales roles also serve as a stepping stone to entrepreneurship. In Norway, formal vocational certificates in retail (e.g., 'Salgsmedarbeider' vocational training) can enhance promotion prospects. However, many workers remain in sales long-term without upward mobility.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sales worker in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience. Entry-level positions often start around NOK 300,000–350,000 per year, while experienced workers in specialized sales can earn NOK 400,000–500,000. Commission-based roles may yield higher income. Pay is regulated by collective agreements in many retail sectors.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa for a sales position in Norway?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult for basic sales roles because the Skilled Worker permit requires a completed degree or vocational training and a salary above the threshold (NOK 456,500 in 2024). Most sales jobs do not meet these criteria. EU/EEA nationals can freely work in sales without a permit.

**Q: Is formal education required for sales jobs in Norway?**
A: No, many sales positions require only high school education. However, vocational training in sales can improve job prospects and lead to management roles. Employers often value experience and language skills over formal qualifications.
