# Clerical Support Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 4419 (ISCO08)**

Clerical support workers not elsewhere classified (ISCO-08 4419) encompass a wide variety of administrative roles in Norway, including office clerks, data entry operators, and general assistants. These positions are found across all sectors, from private companies to public administration. The work typically involves routine tasks such as filing, data processing, and customer correspondence. While many roles require only secondary education, some may demand vocational training or short courses. The Norwegian labour market offers stable employment in this field, with opportunities in both urban and rural areas. EU/EEA nationals can work freely, while non-EEA applicants need a job offer and meet salary thresholds for a skilled worker permit.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for clerical support workers often involves moving into more specialised administrative roles, such as executive assistant, office manager, or project coordinator. Further education, such as a bachelor's degree in business administration or a vocational certificate in office management, can accelerate advancement. Many use these roles as a stepping stone into other fields like HR, accounting, or customer service. In Norway, employers value experience and language skills (Norwegian and English). Continuous learning and adaptability to digital tools are key to long-term career growth.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual data entry and copy-paste operations
- Document Classification, Filing, and Retrieval
- Standardized email and notification template generation
- Schedule planning and meeting coordination
- Simple tables and report generation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze document content and extract key information
- Dictation and transcription of documents using voice assistants
- Use smart tools for multilingual translation and proofreading
- Automate repetitive approval processes using RPA
- Using AI to assist in generating complex report drafts

**Human moat:**

- Flexible judgment in handling ambiguous, non-standard requests
- Cross-departmental communication and complex interpersonal coordination
- Understanding and interpretation of policies and regulations
- Discretion and ethical judgment when handling sensitive or confidential information
- Integrated decision-making combining outputs from different AI tools

**Skills to build:**

- RPA tool usage (e.g., UiPath, Blue Prism)
- AI document processing tools (e.g., Azure Cognitive Services)
- Basic data analysis and advanced Excel functions
- Project management and collaboration software (e.g., Jira, Trello)
- Basic knowledge of digital ethics and data privacy
- Basic programming or scripting (Python basics)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digitalisation adaptation

- Public sector
- Remote work support

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for clerical support workers in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience, but typical annual pre-tax earnings range from 350,000 to 450,000 NOK for entry-level positions, and up to 550,000 NOK for experienced roles in sectors like oil and gas or finance.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a skilled worker visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes, if the position requires at least upper secondary education (which is typical for clerical roles) and the salary meets the threshold (NOK 477,900 in 2024). A job offer and proof of qualifications are required.

**Q: Do I need a university degree to work as a clerical support worker in Norway?**
A: No, a university degree is not mandatory. Upper secondary education or vocational training in office administration is often sufficient. However, some employers may prefer candidates with a bachelor's degree for more advanced roles.
