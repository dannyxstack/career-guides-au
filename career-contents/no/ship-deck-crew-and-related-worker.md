# Ships’ Deck Crew and Related Workers — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ships’ deck crews and related workers perform various tasks on vessels, including cargo handling, mooring, and maintenance. In Norway, this occupation is vital for the maritime industry, which includes shipping, offshore oil & gas, and fishing. Many workers are EU/EEA nationals benefiting from free movement. Jobs often require physical stamina, shift work, and basic seafarer certifications. The sector faces moderate demand, with opportunities in coastal shipping and emerging offshore industries.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a deckhand, advancing to able seafarer, boatswain, and then deck officer through maritime college. Apprenticeships are common, combining work and study. Experienced crew can become captains with further licensing. The Nordic model supports continuous training and union representation, ensuring good working conditions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and AI tool usage (e.g., route optimization software)
- Data analysis and dashboard interpretation skills
- Skills in remotely operating drones or robots.
- Advanced maritime regulations and certification updates
- Soft skills: cross-cultural communication and crisis management
- Digital forensics and cybersecurity fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Coastal shipping
- Aquaculture
- Cruise tourism

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deck hand in Norway?**
A: Salaries are market-driven and vary by ship type, experience, and collective agreements. Entry-level deck hands often earn according to the Norwegian shipping industry's minimum wage rates, while experienced crew earn more. For exact figures, consult NAV or industry reports.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as deck crew in Norway?**
A: Yes, but they need a Skilled Worker Residence Permit if the job requires vocational training and meets the salary threshold. However, many deck hand positions may not qualify as 'skilled' under UDI rules, making it harder for non-EEA workers. EU/EEA nationals have free movement.

**Q: What certifications are required to work on ships in Norway?**
A: All seafarers must hold a Basic Safety Training (BST) certificate and a valid Seafarer Medical Certificate. For deck officers, a Deck Watchkeeping Certificate is mandatory. These are issued by approved training centres and the Norwegian Maritime Authority.
