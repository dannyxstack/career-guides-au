# House Builder — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7111 (ISCO08)**

House builders (ISCO 7111) construct, renovate, and maintain residential buildings using traditional and modern techniques. In Norway, they work mainly in the private housing market, with increasing demand for sustainable and energy-efficient homes. The occupation requires vocational training (fagbrev) and often a certificate of apprenticeship. Employment is sensitive to housing cycles, and the ongoing housing shortage in urban areas supports steady demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a 2-4 year apprenticeship leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced builders can become foremen, site managers, or start their own construction firms. Further specialisation in areas like passive house construction or sustainable building can enhance opportunities. Many builders also move into real estate development or building inspection.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of bill of materials based on CAD drawings
- Basic building structure calculations and optimization (e.g., load-bearing analysis)
- Construction site safety monitoring and anomaly alerts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to generate multiple design blueprint options for client selection
- Collision detection and construction simulation optimization through BIM+AI
- Using drones and AI for rooftop inspections and damage identification
- Use machine learning to predict material waste and project delays

**Human moat:**

- On-site fine operations such as manual bricklaying, plastering, and carpentry
- Understanding of individual client needs and designing tailored solutions
- Emergency response to unexpected issues on complex construction sites

**Skills to build:**

- BIM software (e.g., Revit) operation.
- Drone operation and image analysis.
- Basic Programming & AI Collaboration Tools (e.g., ChatGPT)
- Knowledge of green building and energy-saving technologies.
- Project management and communication coordination skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable Housing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a house builder in Norway?**
A: If you are from an EU/EEA/EFTA country, you can work under free movement. Non-EEA nationals usually need a Skilled Worker Permit, which requires a completed vocational training (minimum 3 years) and a job offer meeting the salary threshold. The occupation is on the shortage list for skilled trades, which may facilitate immigration.

**Q: What is the typical salary for a house builder in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. Entry-level apprentices earn around 300,000–400,000 NOK annually, while fully qualified (fagbrev) house builders typically earn between 450,000 and 600,000 NOK. Experienced foremen can earn 600,000–700,000 NOK. Overtime and allowances can increase earnings.

**Q: How long does it take to become a certified house builder in Norway?**
A: The typical path is a 2-year vocational programme in upper secondary school (or adult education) followed by a 2-year apprenticeship. After passing the trade test, you receive the fagbrev. Total time is about 4 years, though prior experience can shorten it.
