# Managing Directors and Chief Executives — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1120 (ISCO08)**

Managing directors and chief executives lead organisations, setting overall strategy and direction. In Norway, these roles are concentrated in Oslo and other major business hubs, spanning private, public, and non-profit sectors. The Norwegian labour market demands strong leadership in industries like oil and gas, maritime, technology, and renewable energy. Due to the country's flat organisational culture, CEOs often work closely with boards and employees. International experience is valued, and many top executives hold advanced degrees from Norwegian or foreign institutions. The role carries significant responsibility for financial performance, compliance, and stakeholder relations.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a managing director in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector, company size, and location. In 2024, the median salary for top executives is around 1.2 million NOK, with many earning above 2 million NOK. The skilled worker permit requires a minimum salary of 447,900 NOK, but actual offers are much higher.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national become a CEO in Norway without prior residence?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker permit if they have a concrete job offer, a completed degree, and a salary meeting the threshold. The employer must document recruitment efforts. A jobseeker visa is also possible but requires a master's degree.

**Q: What is the typical career path to a CEO role in Norway?**
A: Most start with a relevant bachelor's or master's degree, then progress through management roles in finance, operations, or strategy. Many gain experience abroad or in large Norwegian companies. Networking and board work are crucial. The average age of first-time CEOs is around 50.
