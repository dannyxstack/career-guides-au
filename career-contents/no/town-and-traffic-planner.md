# Town and Traffic Planner — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2164 (ISCO08)**

Town and traffic planners in Norway shape the development of cities, towns, and transport systems, balancing growth with sustainability. They work for municipalities, county authorities, the Norwegian Public Roads Administration, and private consultancies. Their role involves land-use planning, transport infrastructure design, environmental impact assessments, and public consultation. With Norway's focus on climate-friendly urbanisation and the national transport plan, demand for these professionals remains steady. The occupation is considered skilled, requiring a relevant degree, and is open to EU/EEA nationals through free movement, offering a clear migration route for qualified non-EEA applicants via the skilled worker permit.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,444. The employment outlook for town and traffic planners in Norway is positive, driven by ongoing urban densification, major infrastructure projects (e.g., new roads, railways, cycle networks), and climate adaptation initiatives. Municipalities increasingly require planners to manage growth in a sustainable manner. The Norwegian Public Roads Administration and private consultancies also seek specialists in transport modelling and environmental planning. However, competition may be high for central Oslo positions, while regional areas face shortages. Technological shifts toward digital planning (GIS, BIM) are boosting demand for tech-savvy professionals. Overall, the occupation is expected to grow in line with the construction and public administration sectors.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression for town and traffic planners typically starts with junior roles in municipal planning departments or consultancies, advancing to senior planner, team leader, or project manager. Specialisation in transport, environmental planning, or urban design opens doors to higher-level strategic positions. Many planners also move into management roles within government agencies or become self-employed consultants. Further education (e.g., PhD) or certifications (GIS, project management) can enhance prospects. With experience, planners may lead large-scale development projects or influence national policy through roles at directorate level. The profession offers a stable career path with increasing responsibility and impact on Norway's built environment.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a town planner in Norway?**
A: Entry-level planners earn around NOK 500,000–600,000 annually; senior planners in consultancy or government can earn NOK 700,000–900,000. Public sector salaries are regulated, while private sector may offer higher pay.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as town planners in Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker permit. You need a job offer, a completed relevant degree (typically master's), and a salary meeting the threshold (NOK 461,200 in 2024). Employer must show efforts to recruit within EEA.

**Q: What are the main specializations within town planning in Norway?**
A: Common specializations include transport planning, urban design, environmental planning, and regional development. Many planners also work in GIS, data analysis, or sustainability consulting.
