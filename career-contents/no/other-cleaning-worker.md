# Other Cleaning Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other cleaning workers in Norway perform general cleaning duties in various settings such as hotels, offices, hospitals, and private homes. This occupation is primarily entry-level, requiring no formal education beyond compulsory schooling, and is dominated by part-time and shift work. The labour market shows steady demand due to high turnover, but wages remain low, typically below the skilled worker threshold. Many workers are EU/EEA nationals using free movement rights, while non-EEA migrants rarely access this role through skilled permits.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is very limited; most cleaning workers remain in the role long-term. Advancement typically requires moving into supervisory or specialised cleaning roles (e.g., industrial or hospital cleaning), which may offer slightly higher pay. Some workers combine cleaning with other part-time jobs. Further education in facility management or entrepreneurship may lead to owning a cleaning business, but this is uncommon.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to operate cleaning robots or smart cleaning equipment
- Basic computer operation and simple programming (e.g., setting cleaning routes)
- Understanding the Internet of Things (IoT) technology to monitor the status of cleaning equipment
- Knowledge of Disinfection and Chemical Safety in Learning Environments
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn data analysis fundamentals to optimize cleaning efficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- hotel cleaning
- office cleaning
- industrial cleaning
- green cleaning

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What can a cleaning worker expect to earn in Norway?**
A: Pre-tax annual salaries typically range from 300,000 to 400,000 NOK for full-time work, but part-time roles are common. The skilled worker permit threshold (around 500,000 NOK in 2024) is usually not met.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa as a cleaning worker?**
A: Generally no, because the occupation does not require higher education and the salary is below the skilled worker threshold. Only EU/EEA nationals can freely take such jobs.

**Q: How do I find cleaning jobs in Norway?**
A: Mainly via NAV (Norwegian Labour and Welfare Administration) website, private agencies, and direct applications to hotels, office cleaning companies, or private households.
