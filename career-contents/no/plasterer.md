# Plasterer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers in Norway apply plaster, stucco, and other finishes to buildings, working on both new construction and renovation projects. The profession is part of the construction sector, a key industry in Norway. Due to the focus on maintaining older buildings and energy-efficient renovation, plasterers are in steady demand. The apprenticeship system ensures a supply of qualified workers, but shortages persist in some regions. This occupation suits those who enjoy practical, hands-on work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with an apprenticeship leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced plasterers can become foremen, project managers, or start their own business. Further training allows specialization in decorative plasterwork, acoustic plastering, or restoration. Master craftsman status (svennebrev) can lead to higher wages and authority. Many plasterers also work abroad or on offshore projects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation projects
- Energy-efficient buildings
- Restoration
- Commercial construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical earnings potential for plasterers in Norway?**
A: Earnings depend on experience, project type, and location. With a trade certificate, plasterers can expect a stable income. Many become self-employed to increase earnings. Check current tariff agreements for hourly rates.

**Q: Can non-EU plasterers obtain a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker Residence Permit if you have a completed trade certificate and a job offer meeting the salary threshold. Also, a Jobseeker Permit allows you to search for work for up to 6 months.

**Q: Is plastering a shortage occupation in Norway?**
A: Yes, plasterers are in demand, particularly for renovation and restoration projects. The occupation is listed on NAV's shortage list for several regions.
