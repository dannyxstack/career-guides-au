# Domestic Housekeeper — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 5152 (ISCO08)**

Domestic housekeepers perform cleaning, tidying, and household duties in private homes, hotels, hospitals, and other establishments. In Norway, many are employed in the hospitality industry or directly by households, often through part-time or contract arrangements. The occupation is dominated by women and a significant share of workers are immigrants from the EEA. Though formal qualifications are not mandatory, employers increasingly value certification in cleaning or first aid. The work is physically demanding and typically offers limited career progression without further training.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 213. Employment of domestic housekeepers in Norway is expected to remain stable due to consistent demand in hotels, hospitals, and private homes. However, growth is limited as automation (e.g., robotic vacuums) and outsourcing may reduce some roles. The aging population drives demand in elderly care facilities. Competition for permanent positions is moderate, but part-time and seasonal work is common.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression is possible by moving into supervisory roles (e.g., head housekeeper) or specializing in areas like industrial cleaning or environmentally friendly methods. Some housekeepers transition to facility management or start their own cleaning businesses. Vocational training in cleaning services can enhance mobility, but most advancement depends on experience and language skills (Norwegian).

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Vacuuming/mopping floors (replaced by robotic vacuums/mops)
- Dishwashing (replaced by dishwashers)
- Bed making, clothing folding (replaced by smart bed-making robots/folding machines)
- Regular surface dust removal (replaced by automated dusting robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling tools to plan cleaning routes and task priorities
- Monitoring appliance status and scheduling repairs using smart home systems
- Remote control of cleaning equipment via voice assistant or APP
- Use data analytics to optimize cleaning supplies inventory and procurement

**Human moat:**

- Handle complex cleaning issues such as accidental damage and special material stains
- Communicate with employers about customized service needs and build trust
- Manage multiple smart devices and coordinate troubleshooting.
- Engaging in fine manual work (e.g., antique furniture maintenance)

**Skills to build:**

- Smart home device operation and basic troubleshooting
- Basic data analysis (supply inventory management)
- Customer Service and Communication Skills
- Time management and multitasking coordination
- Knowledge of eco-friendly cleaning technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- Elderly care
- Hotel sector
- Cleaning services
- Private households

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a domestic housekeeper in Norway?**
A: The typical annual salary for a domestic housekeeper ranges from 320,000 to 380,000 NOK, depending on experience, employer (e.g., hotel vs. private home), and region. Overtime and night shifts may increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a domestic housekeeper in Norway?**
A: It is very difficult because the Skilled Worker permit requires a salary above about 450,000 NOK, which is rarely met. Non-EEA nationals typically need family immigration (e.g., spouse visa) or a different skilled job to obtain a work permit.

**Q: What career progression is possible as a domestic housekeeper in Norway?**
A: With experience and further training (e.g., supervision courses), you can advance to head housekeeper, cleaner supervisor, or facility manager. Starting your own cleaning company is also an option.
