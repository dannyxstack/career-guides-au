# Draughtsperson — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3118 (ISCO08)**

Draughtspersons (tegnere) in Norway prepare technical drawings and plans for engineering, construction, and manufacturing projects. They work closely with engineers and architects to convert designs into precise blueprints using CAD software. The Norwegian labour market offers steady demand, particularly in oil and gas, maritime, construction, and renewable energy sectors. Many positions are in engineering consultancy firms, shipyards, and construction companies in major cities like Oslo, Bergen, Stavanger, and Trondheim. Proficiency in Norwegian is often required, and knowledge of Norwegian standards (e.g., NS 3420) is beneficial.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,138. Employment of draughtspersons in Norway is expected to remain stable, with moderate growth driven by infrastructure projects, energy transition, and digitalisation. The shift towards Building Information Modelling (BIM) is reshaping the role, requiring continuous upskilling. Opportunities are good for those specialising in 3D modelling and sustainable design. However, automation of routine drafting may reduce demand for basic roles. The oil and gas sector, a major employer, is in structural decline, but renewable energy (offshore wind, hydropower) and public infrastructure investments provide new opportunities.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for draughtspersons in Norway typically involves moving from junior drafter to senior drafter, then to team leader or project manager. Specialisation in areas like BIM coordination, structural detailing, or MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) drafting can lead to higher pay. Many draughtspersons upgrade to engineering roles by completing a bachelor's degree. Continuing education in CAD software (AutoCAD, Revit, Tekla) and BIM courses is common. Networking through organisations like Tekna or NITO can aid advancement. Some become independent consultants.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD software to create standard 2D technical drawings
- Convert hand-drawn sketches or scanned drawings into digital drawings.
- Check drawing dimensions, annotations, etc., for compliance with industry standards
- Generating repetitive design variants with standard parts libraries
- Basic 3D model to 2D projection views

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design assistants to quickly generate preliminary plans, focusing on creative adjustments
- Use AI simulation analysis to identify structural conflicts in drawings early
- Quickly Modify Drawing Parameters Using Natural Language Commands
- Integrate Building Information Modeling (BIM) with AI to check collision issues
- Automatically generate multiple design options for client or engineer evaluation

**Human moat:**

- Understand design intent and engineering constraints, making creative trade-offs
- Interpersonal communication and negotiation skills when collaborating with engineers, architects, etc.
- Handling non-standard, highly customized special structures.
- Perform quality audit and reliability assessment on AI output
- Tasks requiring physical presence, such as site inspections and coordination with construction parties.

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in BIM software (e.g., Revit) and AI design plugins
- Grasp basic programming (Python, Dynamo) to customize automated workflows
- Learning AI-assisted generative design (Generative Design)
- Strengthen engineering mechanics and materials knowledge to review AI solutions
- Enhance cross-departmental communication and project management skills
- Familiar with point cloud scanning and reverse engineering tools

**Key growth areas:**

- BIM
- CAD
- Renewable Energy
- Infrastructure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a draughtsperson in Norway?**
A: Entry-level draughtspersons earn around 450,000–500,000 NOK per year, while experienced draughtspersons can earn 600,000–700,000 NOK. Specialists with BIM expertise may earn up to 800,000 NOK. Salaries vary by sector and region.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to get a Skilled Worker permit as a draughtsperson?**
A: Yes, the Skilled Worker permit requires a concrete job offer from a Norwegian employer. The position must be classified as skilled (requiring higher education or vocational training). The salary must meet or exceed the annual threshold (500,800 NOK in 2024).

**Q: Is Norwegian language proficiency required for draughtsperson jobs?**
A: Many employers require at least B1-level Norwegian, especially for roles involving communication with Norwegian-speaking colleagues or clients. Exceptions exist in international companies or for short-term contracts. Learning Norwegian is strongly recommended for career progression.
