# Stonemason, Stone Cutter, Splitter or Carver — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7113 (ISCO08)**

Stonemasons, stone cutters, splitters and carvers shape and install natural and artificial stone for buildings, monuments, and decorative projects. In Norway, this craft is essential for preserving heritage structures and for high-end construction. The occupation requires precision and physical stamina, with work often outdoors or in workshops. Norway's strong construction sector and focus on restoration of historical buildings provide steady demand, though the trade is niche and specialized.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 701. Employment for stonemasons in Norway is expected to remain stable, driven by ongoing restoration of medieval churches, fortresses, and urban heritage projects. New construction of luxury homes and commercial buildings using natural stone also supports demand. However, automation in stone cutting may reduce need for manual labour in production. Overall, the outlook is moderate, with opportunities for skilled craftspeople in niche markets.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational apprenticeship (lærling) leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced stonemasons can advance to supervisor, project manager, or start their own business. Specialization in restoration or natural stone carving offers higher earning potential and recognition. Continuous learning of new techniques and materials is valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for stone cutting and layout design
- Operating automatic stone cutting machines and CNC engraving machines
- Material quantity calculation and layout using BIM models
- Use robots for repetitive stone handling and laying

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using 3D scanning and digital twins for precise stone structure installation
- Use AR glasses to overlay design drawings on construction sites, reducing errors
- Using AI to optimize stone splicing plans, improving aesthetics and strength
- Conduct drone inspections of large stone structures and remotely monitor maintenance needs.

**Human moat:**

- Artistic judgment for complex stone carving and hand modeling
- Adaptive skills for integrated on-site stone and structure installation
- Empirical judgment of stone texture, color, and physical properties
- Ability to communicate customized design solutions with clients

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/BIM software operation (e.g., AutoCAD, Revit)
- CNC stone processing equipment programming and operation
- 3D scanning and digital modeling skills
- Engineering surveying and precision installation technology
- Drone operation and image analysis basics

**Key growth areas:**

- Restoration
- Natural stone
- Custom fabrication
- Infrastructure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a stonemason in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and project type. For specific figures, consult Statistics Norway's salary statistics or NAV's wage data. Generally, experienced stonemasons earn above the median for trades.

**Q: Can I get a Skilled Worker visa as a stonemason without a degree?**
A: Yes, if you have a relevant trade certificate (or equivalent) and a job offer meeting the salary threshold. Your certificate must be recognized as equivalent to Norwegian fagbrev.

**Q: Is there demand for stonemasons in Norway?**
A: Demand is steady but niche. Restoration projects and high-end construction provide opportunities, but the occupation is not on the official shortage list.
