# Mining and Metallurgical Technician — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3117 (ISCO08)**

Mining and metallurgical technicians in Norway support the extraction and processing of minerals and metals, including critical raw materials for the green transition. They work in mines, processing plants, and laboratories, ensuring efficient and safe operations. With Norway's focus on sustainable mining and circular economy, these technicians are increasingly involved in automation, environmental monitoring, and recycling technologies. The role typically requires vocational training or a bachelor's degree, with strong demand in northern and central regions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression often starts with a vocational qualification (fagbrev) followed by roles as senior technician or shift supervisor. Further education (e.g., bachelor's in mining engineering) opens paths to management or specialization in environmental compliance, mine planning, or mineral processing. Continuous upskilling in automation and digital tools is essential. Some technicians advance to roles with HSE responsibilities or move into consulting.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual drafting of mine design and construction drawings
- Resource reserve estimation based on experience
- Manually write routine mining plans and progress reports
- Manual monitoring of equipment status and energy consumption
- On-site Inspections for Hazard Identification

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI for 3D modeling of ore deposits and resource assessment
- Optimizing autonomous truck routes and blasting plans using AI
- Using machine learning to predict rock mass stability and support requirements
- Integrating digital twin technology for real-time monitoring and adjustment of mining operations
- Quickly parse geological reports and compliance documents using natural language processing

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making under complex geological conditions
- Communication and coordination with government/community/Indigenous peoples
- Safety regulation enforcement and emergency incident command
- Multidisciplinary team collaboration integrating AI recommendations
- Comprehensive assessment of ethics and environmental impact

**Skills to build:**

- Geostatistics and machine learning applied to resource modeling
- Operation and configuration of automated scheduling systems (e.g., Minestar)
- Python/R data analysis and digital twin platform usage
- Mine simulation software (e.g., Vulcan, Surpac) with AI plugins
- Project Management and Cross-Departmental AI Solution Integration
- Data visualization and report automation tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Green transition minerals
- Automation and digitalization
- Mineral exploration
- Process optimization

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining and metallurgical technician in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location, but they are competitive and generally meet the skilled worker threshold for visa purposes. Entry-level technicians earn around 450,000-500,000 NOK annually, while senior roles can exceed 700,000 NOK.

**Q: Do I need a job offer before applying for a skilled worker visa?**
A: Yes, you must have a concrete job offer from a Norwegian employer for the skilled worker permit. The offer must meet salary requirements and be in a relevant occupation. The jobseeker permit allows you to search for work without an offer, but it is limited to 6 months.

**Q: Can I work as a mining technician with only a vocational diploma?**
A: Yes, a vocational qualification (fagbrev) in mining or quarrying is often sufficient. Many technicians start with this and gain experience. For specialized roles or advancement, a bachelor's degree may be preferred.
