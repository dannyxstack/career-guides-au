# Stationary Plant and Machine Operator — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 8189 (ISCO08)**

Stationary plant and machine operators in Norway run and monitor machinery used in industrial production, processing, and recycling. They work in factories, refineries, waste treatment plants, and similar settings, controlling equipment such as crushers, mixers, packing machines, and conveyor systems. The role requires technical aptitude, safety awareness, and ability to follow procedures. With Norway's strong industrial base and focus on automation, these operators are in demand across sectors like manufacturing, energy, and environmental management.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to operator roles. Experienced operators can advance to team leader, shift supervisor, or technician positions. Further education, such as a fagbrev (trade certificate) in industrial mechanics or automation, opens doors to maintenance and engineering roles. Some operators specialize in high-demand areas like CNC machinery or waste-to-energy plants, enhancing job security and salary potential.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated production quality inspection using sensors and AI vision
- Predictive maintenance scheduling for equipment based on historical data
- Standardized material handling and packaging processes.
- Adaptive adjustment and optimization of production parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted predictive maintenance improves equipment reliability
- Real-time production data dashboards assist operational decisions
- Smart scheduling system optimizes multi-machine coordination efficiency
- Remote expert collaboration system enables rapid fault diagnosis

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of non-standard mechanical faults.
- Ability to flexibly switch between multiple varieties and small batch production
- Perception of subtle anomalies like unusual equipment noises and vibrations.
- Safety management and emergency shutdown procedures

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and PLC debugging.
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) data analysis
- Collaborative robot (KUKA/Universal Robots) operation
- Lean production and Six Sigma methods
- Understanding of AI tools in industrial applications

**Key growth areas:**

- Green transition
- Automation
- Recycling
- Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a stationary plant and machine operator in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by industry and experience. Entry-level operators can expect around 450,000-500,000 NOK annually, while experienced operators often earn 550,000-650,000 NOK. Shift work, overtime, and specialization can increase earnings.

**Q: Can I get a work visa for this job without a degree?**
A: Yes, for non-EU/EEA nationals, a job offer plus a completed vocational training or trade certificate (e.g., fagbrev) can qualify for a Skilled Worker permit. The occupation is on the shortage list, which facilitates the process.

**Q: Is language proficiency required in Norwegian?**
A: Basic knowledge of Norwegian (A2-B1) is often required for safety and communication. Many employers provide language courses if needed.
