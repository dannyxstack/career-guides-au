# Sweeper and Related Labourer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9613 (ISCO08)**

Sweepers and related labourers in Norway perform manual cleaning and tidying of streets, public spaces, buildings, and worksites. This occupation is characterised by low skill requirements and high turnover, with many workers from EU/EEA countries taking these jobs. Employment is often seasonal or part-time, and wages are typically below the national average. The role involves using brooms, shovels, and basic machinery to remove debris, snow, and waste. Despite automation, demand remains steady in urban areas and for seasonal tasks like leaf and snow removal.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 74. The outlook for sweepers in Norway is stable but with limited growth. Municipal budgets for public cleaning are under pressure, but there is constant need for street and building maintenance. Automation of cleaning tasks is growing slowly, especially in indoor environments. The main opportunities lie in waste management and industrial cleaning. However, competition from EU/EEA labour keeps wages low. Non-EEA workers face significant barriers due to strict immigration rules for low-skilled work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for sweepers in Norway is limited. With experience, workers can move to supervisory roles (e.g., team leader) or specialise in areas like industrial cleaning or waste sorting. Some transition to driving cleaning vehicles (e.g., street sweepers) which require a driver's license. Additional training in safety or machine operation may open doors to maintenance roles. However, most remain in entry-level positions without formal advancement. The job is often a stepping stone for new immigrants or temporary workers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Daily floor cleaning and garbage collection
- Surface wiping with fixed cleaning agents
- Repeatedly patrol and clean along predetermined routes
- Simple waste sorting and disposal

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling system to optimize cleaning routes
- Identify high-pollution areas using sensor data
- Using robots to assist in large-area cleaning
- Report facility issues using a mobile app

**Human moat:**

- Cleaning strategies for complex stains such as grease and bloodstains
- Safe handling of hazardous waste (e.g., needles)
- Etiquette and adaptability for interacting with the public
- Careful work maintaining precision equipment surfaces
- Flexible maneuvering in narrow or confined spaces

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI scheduling and monitoring tools
- Automated cleaning equipment maintenance
- Basic Chemistry and Disinfectant Knowledge
- Data recording and analysis skills
- Communication and customer service skills
- Compliance and safety regulations training

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban cleaning
- Waste management
- Industrial cleaning
- Seasonal demand

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sweeper in Norway?**
A: Sweepers in Norway earn an average annual salary of around 350,000–400,000 NOK before tax. However, wages can be lower for part-time or seasonal workers, with many earning close to the minimum in collective agreements (approx. 180–200 NOK per hour).

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a sweeper in Norway?**
A: Generally no, because sweepers are considered low-skilled workers. The Skilled Worker permit requires a job offer that meets a salary threshold (currently around 500,000 NOK) and either a completed degree or trade certificate. Since sweeper roles rarely meet these criteria, non-EEA workers have very limited options.
