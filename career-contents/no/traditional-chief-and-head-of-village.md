# Traditional Chief and Head of Village — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1113 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, this role is primarily found in indigenous Sámi communities and remote rural areas, where leaders oversee local governance, cultural preservation, and conflict resolution. It is not a regulated profession but carries significant social responsibility. The position is often hereditary or elected by community consensus, with no formal educational requirements. Labour market data is scarce due to the occupation's niche nature, and it is not tracked in standard Norwegian statistics. Employment opportunities are extremely limited and tied to specific geographical and cultural contexts, making it relevant only for those deeply rooted in such communities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is non-standard; typically, individuals are selected by the community based on lineage, wisdom, or experience. There are no formal training programmes. Some may transition into modern local government roles or work for the Sámi Parliament, but the traditional chief role itself is terminal. Younger generations often pursue mainstream careers, making succession challenging. The role may evolve into a symbolic or advisory capacity as formal democratic structures take precedence.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a traditional chief in Norway?**
A: There is no standard salary; compensation is often in the form of community support, housing, or symbolic gifts. Some may receive a small honorarium from the Sámi Parliament or local municipality, but it is not a salaried profession.

**Q: Can a non-Norwegian become a traditional chief in Norway?**
A: Extremely unlikely. The role requires deep integration into a specific community, usually with cultural and linguistic ties. Non-EU/EEA nationals would have no pathway, and even EU/EEA nationals would face practical barriers.

**Q: Is this occupation recognised by Norwegian authorities?**
A: Not as a standard occupation. The Sámi Parliament has its own governance structures, but 'traditional chief' is not a formal title in Norwegian law. It may be acknowledged informally within Sámi communities.
