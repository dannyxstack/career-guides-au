# Personal Care Worker in Health Services Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 5329 (ISCO08)**

Personal care workers in health services (NEC) provide essential daily living support to individuals with physical or mental health needs, assisting with personal hygiene, mobility, meal preparation, and social activities. In Norway, these roles are primarily employed by municipalities (home care, nursing homes) or private care agencies. The occupation is in high demand due to the country's aging population and policy of enabling people to live at home as long as possible, leading to a stable and growing need for compassionate, trained care workers.

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 72,032. The employment outlook for personal care workers in Norway is very positive, with sustained demand driven by demographic trends. Municipalities are struggling to fill positions, especially in rural areas. The Norwegian Labour and Welfare Administration (NAV) forecasts a continued shortage, particularly for workers with formal vocational qualifications. Digitization and new technology in home care are changing tasks but not reducing the need for human interaction. Overall, job prospects are excellent for qualified candidates.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves specialization (e.g., dementia care, palliative care, or mental health support) or moving into supervisory roles (team leader, assistant manager). Many workers use the role as a stepping stone to further education, such as nursing (bachelor's degree in nursing), occupational therapy, or social work, often with employer-sponsored study schemes. With experience and additional courses, personal care workers can become authorized health care assistants or specialize in areas like rehabilitation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use digital tools to record care data (such as vital signs, medication times), automatically uploaded to electronic health record systems.
- Remotely monitor patient activities via smart sensors and cameras, automatically issuing fall or abnormality alerts.
- Generate and submit daily nursing reports, shift schedules, and inventory lists, all auto-organized by AI
- Use AI voice assistants to answer routine patient questions (e.g., meal times, activity schedules)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted action planning: intelligently recommends optimal movement routes or transfer techniques based on patient activity data
- Real-time translation/speech-to-text: assist in communicating with patients or families who have language barriers
- Non-verbal emotion recognition: using facial expressions and voice analysis to assist in assessing patient emotional state
- Smart reminder system: automatically reminds patients to take medication, drink water, change position, etc. based on their schedule.

**Human moat:**

- micro-observation and emergency response during physical intimacy (e.g., identifying skin conditions when turning over)
- Building trust to calm anxious or resistant patients
- Complex emotional support (e.g., psychological companionship in hospice care)
- Tailor personalized care plans combining home environment and individual preferences

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis: interpreting patient activity trend reports to optimize care plans
- Electronic health record system operation and data entry.
- Use of assistive technology devices (e.g., smart mattresses, fall detection sensors)
- Empathy and communication skills: managing emotions for patients with cognitive impairments
- Basic infection control and safe operating procedures
- Cross-cultural care ability (language, religious dietary restrictions)

**Key growth areas:**

- Elderly care
- Home-based care
- Mental health support
- Assisted living

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a personal care worker in Norway?**
A: Annual salaries typically range from 430,000 to 480,000 NOK before tax for entry-level positions, with higher pay for evening/weekend shifts and experience. Municipal pay scales are regulated by collective agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a personal care worker in Norway?**
A: Yes, provided they have a completed vocational training (trade certificate in health and care) and a job offer meeting the skilled worker salary threshold (approx. 450,000 NOK). The occupation is on Norway's shortage list, which can simplify the process.

**Q: What education is needed to become a personal care worker?**
A: The most common path is a 4-year vocational programme in Health and Care (2 years school + 2 years apprenticeship), leading to a trade certificate. Adults can also take shorter courses (1-2 years) through adult education centres.
