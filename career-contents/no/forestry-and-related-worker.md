# Forestry and Related Workers — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, forestry and related workers manage and harvest forests, performing tasks such as planting, thinning, logging, and maintaining forest stands, an occupation integral to the country's sustainable bioeconomy and wood products industry.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with general forestry labor, advancing to specialized machine operation (e.g., harvester, forwarder) or supervisory roles. Further vocational training or education (e.g., forestry technician) can lead to positions in forest management, planning, or consulting.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable forestry
- Bioeconomy
- Wood construction
- Mechanization

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI)](https://www.udi.no/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for forestry workers in Norway?**
A: Salaries are competitive and increase with experience, specialization, and responsibility, typically aligned with collective agreements in the forestry sector.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as forestry workers in Norway?**
A: Yes, provided they have a job offer and meet the requirements for a Skilled Worker residence permit, including completed vocational training or a relevant degree. The occupation is often on shortage lists, which may facilitate the process.

**Q: What education is needed to become a forestry worker?**
A: Most employers require a vocational qualification in forestry (fagbrev) obtained through upper secondary school or apprenticeship. Higher education in forestry or natural resource management can lead to advanced roles.
