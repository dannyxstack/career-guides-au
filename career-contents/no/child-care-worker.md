# Child Care Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 5311 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, child care workers (barne- og ungdomsarbeidere or barnepleiere) are essential in kindergartens (barnehager) and after-school programs (SFO). They support children's development through play, learning activities, and care routines, following the national curriculum (Rammeplan). The sector is predominantly municipal but includes private operators. With near-universal kindergarten coverage and expanding, demand remains steady, though working conditions vary. Many positions require a trade certificate (fagbrev) in child and youth work, but assistants with less formal education are also employed. Immigration from the EU/EEA is common, though non-EEA workers face salary threshold challenges for work permits.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 95,199. Norway's kindergarten system is well-funded, ensuring stable employment for child care workers. However, budget pressures in some municipalities may limit hiring to replacements. The ongoing shortage of qualified staff, especially in rural and multilingual settings, creates opportunities for those with relevant credentials. The government's focus on early childhood education quality may raise training requirements, potentially increasing demand for skilled workers. Overall, job prospects are favorable for certified candidates, while assistants might experience more competition.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an assistant, with on-the-job training. Completing an apprenticeship and obtaining the trade certificate (fagbrev i barne- og ungdomsarbeiderfag) opens doors to higher pay and responsibility. Further education—such as a bachelor's in early childhood education (førskolelærer)—leads to leadership roles like pedagogical leader (pedagogisk leder) or kindergarten manager (styrer). Specialization in areas like outdoor pedagogy, multilingual support, or special needs education can enhance career prospects.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a child care worker in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and education. As of 2024, the average annual salary for a child care assistant (uten fagbrev) is around 350,000–400,000 NOK, while those with a trade certificate earn about 400,000–450,000 NOK. Kindergarten teachers (bachelor's degree) earn higher, but that is a different occupation (ISCO 2342).

**Q: Can a non-EU national become a child care worker in Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker residence permit if you have a completed vocational education (fagbrev) in child and youth work or equivalent. You need a job offer with salary meeting the threshold (currently 484,700 NOK/year). However, reaching this salary level is challenging for entry-level positions, so many start as assistants under other permits or consider alternative routes.

**Q: Is child care worker a shortage occupation in Norway?**
A: Yes, there is a persistent shortage of qualified child care workers, especially in rural areas and for roles requiring specialized skills like bilingual support or special needs care. The NAV Business Survey frequently lists this occupation as having recruitment difficulties, and many municipalities report unfilled positions.
