# Precision-instrument Maker and Repairer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7311 (ISCO08)**

Precision-instrument makers and repairers manufacture and repair instruments such as gauges, measuring devices, optical instruments, and surgical instruments. In Norway, this occupation is found in industrial manufacturing, aerospace, medical equipment, and maritime sectors. Workers typically hold a vocational trade certificate (fagbrev) in instrumentmaking or a related field. The role demands meticulous attention to detail, manual dexterity, and knowledge of mechanical and electronic systems.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression includes specialising in certain instrument types (optical, electrical, mechanical) or advancing to technician, supervisor, or quality assurance roles. Further education can lead to engineering degrees. Apprenticeship is the main entry path, with opportunities for lifelong learning and certification in new technologies like 3D printing and laser measurement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repeated part dimension measurement and recording (replaced by AI vision and automated instruments)
- Calibration process for standard instruments (taken over by AI automatic calibration algorithms)
- Remote diagnosis of common faults (AI knowledge base provides solutions)
- Inventory management and material procurement (optimised by AI prediction systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis (AI-generated fault trees and historical cases)
- Fine-tuning decisions in precision adjustment (AI provides real-time compensation suggestions)
- Customized instrument design (AI-assisted 3D modeling and tolerance analysis)
- Repair plan optimization (AI compares efficiency and cost of multiple approaches)
- Multi-domain knowledge quick retrieval (AI integrating optics, mechanics, electronics materials)

**Human moat:**

- Manual finishing and tactile feedback (e.g., micron-level polishing, balance tuning)
- Repair of non-standard/antique instruments (no blueprints, no spare parts)
- Communicate with clients to understand custom requirements and translate them into technical solutions
- On-site emergency repairs (decision-making in uncontrollable environments)

**Skills to build:**

- Using AI-assisted fault diagnosis tools (e.g., image-based inspection systems)
- CNC machine and 3D printing operation (for manufacturing replacement parts)
- Digital measurement and reverse engineering software (e.g., CAD, laser scanning).
- IoT sensor and smart instrument debugging.
- Python scripts (automating repetitive data collection)
- Client communication and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- precision manufacturing
- offshore maintenance
- medical instruments
- automation calibration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a precision-instrument maker in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector, but the average annual salary is around 550,000–650,000 NOK for skilled workers, with higher pay in offshore and specialised industries.

**Q: Can I get a work visa as a precision-instrument maker without a university degree?**
A: Yes, if you have a completed vocational trade certificate (fagbrev) equivalent to Norwegian standards and a job offer meeting the salary threshold, you can apply for a Skilled Worker permit.
