# Subsistence Fisher, Hunter, Trapper or Gatherer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6340 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, subsistence fishing, hunting, trappers and gathering are traditional livelihoods, particularly in rural and remote areas such as Finnmark and along the coast. This occupation involves harvesting wild fish, game, berries, and other natural resources primarily for personal or local community consumption, with limited commercial sale. The work is seasonal and deeply connected to Sami culture and outdoor life. Employment is informal or self-employed, with few formal positions. The sector is small and declining due to urbanization and stricter regulations, but remains culturally significant.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is rare in this occupation, as it is generally a traditional or lifestyle choice rather than a structured career. Most practitioners are self-employed or part of family traditions. There is no formal career ladder. Some may diversify into guiding, tourism, or small-scale commercial sales to supplement income. Formal qualifications are not required, but safety and sustainability courses are beneficial. The occupation is expected to continue its decline as a primary livelihood, but will persist as a cultural heritage activity.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What can I expect to earn as a subsistence fisher or hunter in Norway?**
A: Income is highly variable and generally low, often supplemented by other work or social benefits. Most practitioners have no guaranteed salary; earnings depend on harvest success and occasional small-scale sales. Average annual income may range from 0 to 200,000 NOK, but many do not report taxable income.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a visa to work as a subsistence fisher or hunter in Norway?**
A: No, this occupation does not qualify for a Skilled Worker permit, which requires a job offer with a salary above a threshold (currently 449,900 NOK) and relevant qualifications. The informal nature of subsistence work means it is not a viable migration route.

**Q: Is there any formal training for this occupation in Norway?**
A: Formal training is not required, but voluntary courses on hunting safety, fishing regulations, and sustainable harvest are available through organizations like the Norwegian Association of Hunters and Anglers (NJFF). A hunting license is mandatory, obtained by passing a theoretical and practical exam.
