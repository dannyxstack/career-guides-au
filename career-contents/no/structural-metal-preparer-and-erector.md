# Structural Metal Preparer and Erector — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7214 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, structural metal preparers and erectors are essential for fabricating and assembling steel frameworks for buildings, bridges, oil platforms, and industrial plants. The role requires precision, physical stamina, and familiarity with welding and lifting equipment. With a strong construction and energy sector, demand remains steady, particularly in offshore and infrastructure projects. Apprenticeship-based training through vocational schools leads to a trade certificate (fagbrev), which is the standard qualification. The occupation often involves working at heights and in all weather conditions, making safety training crucial.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a 2-year apprenticeship followed by a trade certificate (fagbrev). Journeymen can advance to foreman or safety supervisor, then to project manager or estimator. Specialization in welding, crane operation, or offshore installation adds value. Some pursue further education in structural engineering or construction management. Salaries increase with experience and certification. Mobility is high within Norway's construction and energy sectors, and skilled workers often secure permanent contracts after short-term project work.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Precise dimensional measurement and calibration using BIM and laser scanning
- Optimize cutting and welding parameters for steel structures using AI software
- Drones automatically check structural alignment and bolt tightening status

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted crane and rigging configuration planning
- Risk prediction and construction plan optimization based on historical data
- Augmented Reality (AR)-assisted installation guidance and training
- Real-time tool status monitoring and predictive maintenance alerts.

**Human moat:**

- Physical operation and assembly in complex construction site environments
- Flexible judgment for handling non-standard or damaged materials
- Coordinate with the team and handle emergency situations
- Strict enforcement and supervision of safety regulations

**Skills to build:**

- BIM (Building Information Modeling) software operation
- Drone operation and data analysis
- Programming and maintenance of welding robots
- Use of augmented reality (AR) tools
- Digital literacy and basic programming concepts
- Data analysis in safety and quality management

**Key growth areas:**

- offshore structures
- green steel
- modular construction
- bridge building

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who enjoy hands-on, outdoor work and are comfortable at heights.
- Those with good physical fitness and precision skills for welding and measuring.
- People who like working in teams on large-scale construction projects.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer desk jobs or indoor, climate-controlled environments.
- Individuals with a fear of heights or aversion to heavy lifting.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a structural metal preparer and erector in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and location, but they generally meet or exceed the skilled worker permit threshold. Entry-level positions may start lower, but experienced workers earn well above average. Specific figures are available from Statistics Norway (SSB) earnings tables, and we recommend checking SSB table 11418 for current data.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national get a work visa as a structural metal worker?**
A: Yes, if they have a completed trade certificate (fagbrev) equivalent to Norwegian standards and a job offer from a Norwegian employer. The Skilled Worker Residence Permit requires the salary to meet the annual threshold (about 500,000 NOK in 2024). Recognition of foreign qualifications is handled by NOKUT.
