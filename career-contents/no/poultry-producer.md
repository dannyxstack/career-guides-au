# Poultry Producer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6122 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, poultry producers manage the day-to-day operations of farms raising chickens, turkeys, and other fowl for meat or egg production. The industry is highly automated and regulated, with a strong focus on animal welfare, biosecurity, and environmental sustainability. Poultry producers oversee feeding, health care, breeding, and facility maintenance, often working alongside veterinarians and agronomists. The sector is vital to Norway's food self-sufficiency, with growing consumer demand for organic and free-range products. Employment is concentrated in rural areas, particularly in the south and east. The role requires practical skills, attention to detail, and adherence to strict quality and safety standards.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression in poultry production typically starts as an assistant or worker, advancing to herd manager, then farm manager, and potentially farm owner. Formal education in agriculture (vocational certificate or bachelor's) accelerates advancement. Opportunities exist in large commercial operations, family farms, or advisory roles. Specialization in areas like breeding, nutrition, or animal welfare is possible. Some producers move into agribusiness, sales, or consulting. The trend toward larger, more automated farms means fewer but higher-skilled jobs, with greater responsibility for technology and data management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Environmental parameter monitoring and recording (temperature, humidity, light)
- Basic feed delivery and water supply management
- Mechanized operation of poultry house cleaning and waste disposal
- Preliminary statistics and report generation for production data
- Collection of vital signs data in routine health checks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize feed ratios and feeding times through AI analysis
- Early detection of abnormal behavior in sick poultry using computer vision
- Predicting egg production/slaughter volume based on production data to plan sales
- Efficiency improved in inspecting large poultry houses with drones or ground robots
- AI-assisted epidemic early warning and biosecurity decision support

**Human moat:**

- Acute observation and immediate handling of abnormal behavior in individual poultry.
- veterinary-level disease diagnosis and medication decisions
- Emergency management for sudden weather or equipment failure
- Animal welfare operations in special production systems like organic and free-range
- Regulatory compliance responsibilities related to government quarantine and food certification

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and basic troubleshooting of agricultural IoT devices
- Data analysis and visualization tools (e.g., Excel, Tableau)
- Understanding the application of computer vision systems in farming
- Deepened knowledge of animal behavior and biosecurity
- IT fundamentals and automated systems management skills
- Food safety regulations and sustainable farming practices

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Sustainability
- Organic production
- Animal welfare

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a poultry producer in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, farm size, and region; entry-level workers earn less while experienced managers earn more. For current average figures, refer to SSB's earnings tables (table 11418) or NAV statistics.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a poultry producer in Norway?**
A: Yes, non-EEA nationals can apply for a Skilled Worker residence permit if they have a job offer and relevant vocational training or a degree in agriculture. The position must meet the salary threshold set by the government.

**Q: Do I need formal education to become a poultry producer?**
A: While not legally mandatory, most employers prefer candidates with at least a vocational certificate in agriculture or relevant experience. A bachelor's degree in animal science or agriculture can lead to higher-level positions.
