# Landscape Architect — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2162 (ISCO08)**

Landscape architects in Norway plan and design outdoor spaces such as parks, gardens, urban squares, and infrastructure corridors, integrating ecological, social, and aesthetic considerations. They work for municipalities, private consultancies, and government agencies on projects ranging from coastal protection to recreation areas. With growing urbanization and climate adaptation needs, the demand for landscape architects is steady, especially in cities like Oslo, Bergen, and Stavanger. The profession is regulated: the title 'landskapsarkitekt' is protected and requires authorization from the Norwegian Association of Landscape Architects (NLA).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 931. Employment outlook for landscape architects in Norway is positive, driven by increased focus on green infrastructure, climate resilience, and sustainable urban development. Major infrastructure projects, such as the new National Museum and urban regeneration in Oslo's waterfront, require landscape architects. The aging workforce and few graduates entering the field create opportunities. However, competition for positions in private firms can be moderate. Overall, the sector is expected to grow slower than average but with stable demand for specialists in ecological design and coastal planning.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior in a consultancy or municipality, advancing to project manager or senior designer with 5–10 years of experience. Some specialize in GIS, visualisation, or environmental impact assessment. Many landscape architects eventually lead own firms or move into academic research. The Norwegian system encourages continuous professional development through courses and conferences. International experience is valued, and many professionals transition between public and private sectors.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a landscape architect in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level landscape architects earn around 450,000–500,000 NOK annually, while senior roles can exceed 700,000 NOK. Public sector salaries are typically lower than private consultancies. The Skilled Worker permit threshold is currently about 468,000 NOK.

**Q: Do I need Norwegian language skills to get a work visa as a landscape architect?**
A: No, but most employers require at least conversational Norwegian (B1/B2) for client communication and project documentation. For the Skilled Worker permit, language is not a formal requirement, but it strengthens your application.

**Q: How do I get my foreign landscape architecture qualifications recognized in Norway?**
A: You must apply to the Norwegian Association of Landscape Architects (NLA) for authorization. Your degree will be assessed for equivalence to Norwegian standards. If accepted, you may need to complete additional coursework or supervised practice. The process typically takes 6–12 months.
