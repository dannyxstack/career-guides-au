# Physiotherapy Technician and Assistant — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3255 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapy technicians and assistants support licensed physiotherapists in delivering rehabilitation, exercise therapy, and manual treatments. They work in hospitals, nursing homes, rehabilitation centres, and private clinics across Norway. With an aging population and increased focus on preventive healthcare, demand for these professionals is steady. The role involves preparing treatment areas, guiding patients through exercises, and documenting progress. Most positions require a vocational trade certificate (fagbrev) in health and social care with a physiotherapy specialisation. Salaries typically range from 360,000 to 420,000 NOK annually, depending on experience and sector. The occupation offers a direct entry point into healthcare for those without a university degree.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves further education to become a fully qualified physiotherapist, often after gaining practical experience. Many assistants pursue a bachelor's degree in physiotherapy, which requires additional schooling. Others specialise in areas such as geriatrics, sports rehabilitation, or paediatrics through vocational courses. Advancement to supervisory roles like team leader in rehabilitation units is possible with experience. The trade certificate also allows mobility to other healthcare support roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient treatment progress recording and report writing
- Basic vital signs monitoring and data entry
- Setup and calibration of routine treatment equipment
- Appointment scheduling and patient file management.
- Simple Rehabilitation Exercise Guidance (Standardized Movements)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze patient movement data and optimize personalized treatment plans
- Balance and coordination training assisted by virtual reality (VR)
- Use wearable devices to monitor patient progress in real time and adjust intensity
- AI-assisted generation of treatment plan documents and patient education materials
- Smart voice assistant for patient communication and follow-up

**Human moat:**

- Manual therapy techniques (e.g., massage, joint mobilization)
- Empathy to assess patient mental state and pain
- Clinical judgment in adjusting treatment based on nonverbal cues
- Build trust with patients and motivate them to adhere to rehabilitation
- Handling emergencies (e.g., patient falls or discomfort)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis and use of AI tools
- Electronic Health Record (EHR) system operation.
- Virtual reality rehabilitation technology applications
- Patient communication and behavior change coaching
- Wearable device data interpretation.
- Basic programming (e.g., Python scripts for data processing)

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- home-based rehabilitation
- sports medicine
- preventive care

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapy assistant in Norway?**
A: The annual pre-tax salary for physiotherapy assistants in Norway ranges from 360,000 to 420,000 NOK, depending on experience, sector (public vs. private), and region. Entry-level positions may start around 350,000 NOK, while experienced assistants in hospitals can earn up to 450,000 NOK.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a skilled worker visa as a physiotherapy assistant?**
A: Yes, if you have a completed vocational training (fagbrev) or equivalent, a job offer, and a salary meeting the threshold (401,000 NOK in 2024). However, the typical salary for this role may be borderline, so it is important to ensure the offer meets the requirement.

**Q: Do I need to speak Norwegian to work as a physiotherapy assistant?**
A: Yes, proficiency in Norwegian (B1-B2 level) is usually required, as the role involves direct patient communication and documentation. Some employers may accept other Scandinavian languages.
