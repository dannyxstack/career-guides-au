# Electrical Line Installer and Repairer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7413 (ISCO08)**

Electrical line installers and repairers build, maintain, and repair high-voltage power lines and cables, ensuring reliable electricity supply across Norway. This skilled trade is critical for the country's energy transition, including connections to wind farms and hydropower plants. Work often involves outdoor conditions, heights, and travel to remote areas. The occupation requires a trade certificate (fagbrev) and authorization from the Norwegian Electrical Safety Authority (DSB).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Typical progression starts with a two-year vocational education in electric power systems, followed by a two-year apprenticeship. With experience, workers can become team leaders, project supervisors, or specialize in areas like high-voltage underground cables or offshore installations. Some advance to engineering roles with further education. The Norwegian Labour and Welfare Administration (NAV) supports retraining programs for this trade.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Uses drones or AI vision systems for line inspection and preliminary fault location
- Automatic generation of predictive maintenance plans based on historical data
- Remote monitoring system automatic alarms and status reports
- AI-optimised decision-making for load dispatch and grid balancing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fault diagnosis and repair plan recommendations to improve on-site efficiency
- Smart wearable devices provide real-time safety alerts and construction guidance
- Digital twin simulation helps plan complex line replacement or emergency repair solutions
- Collaborative robots assist with heavy lifting and precise operations

**Human moat:**

- Safety judgment and emergency response in live-line work and high-voltage environments.
- Field operations and decision-making in complex terrain (e.g., mountainous areas, adverse weather)
- Direct communication and coordination with communities, governments, and grid customers
- Team Trust and Leadership in High-Risk Environments

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- Data analysis fundamentals and AI tool usage (e.g., predictive maintenance software)
- Remote monitoring and digital twin platform operation
- Collaborative robot operation and programming
- Upgrade safety management and emergency rescue skills

**Key growth areas:**

- renewable energy grid
- subsea cables
- smart grid
- maintenance contracts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an electrical line installer in Norway?**
A: Entry-level salaries start around NOK 500,000 per year, while experienced workers can earn NOK 600,000–700,000. Specialized offshore roles may exceed NOK 800,000. Salaries vary by region and employer.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA electrician get a work permit for this job?**
A: Yes, if you have a relevant trade certificate (fagbrev or equivalent) and a job offer. The occupation is on Norway's shortage list, which can expedite processing. You must meet the salary threshold of around NOK 452,000 per year.

**Q: What education is needed to become a line installer in Norway?**
A: You need a two-year vocational upper secondary program (electric power systems) followed by a two-year apprenticeship, leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Some workers qualify through foreign credentials assessed by NOKUT.
