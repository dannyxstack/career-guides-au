# Physical and Engineering Science Technician Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3119 (ISCO08)**

Physical and Engineering Science Technicians not elsewhere classified in Norway support engineers and scientists across diverse industries, including oil and gas, renewable energy, manufacturing, construction, and R&D. They perform technical tasks such as calibrating equipment, conducting tests, analyzing data, and assisting in design and development. With Norway's focus on green transition and digitalization, demand is steady for technicians who can adapt to new technologies. Many are employed by engineering firms, research institutes, and public agencies. The role often requires vocational training or a bachelor's degree, offering a practical pathway into the engineering sector.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression from technician can lead to senior technician, team leader, or specialist roles in areas like quality control or project management. Further education (e.g., a bachelor's in engineering) can open pathways to become an engineer. Norway's system of fagbrev (trade certificate) allows for formal advancement. Continuous learning in automation, data analysis, or sustainability is valued. Some technicians move into technical sales or consulting.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically record experimental data and generate standardized reports
- Use AI algorithms for preliminary data analysis and anomaly detection
- Automatically calibrate and adjust experimental parameters based on historical data
- Classify samples and perform basic inspections using computer vision
- Automatically organize literature and preliminary experimental design

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using machine learning models to optimise experimental design and improve efficiency
- AI-assisted multi-variable data analysis to discover hidden patterns
- Using digital twins to simulate experimental processes and predict outcomes.
- Quick retrieval of technical documents and specifications using natural language processing
- AI-driven predictive maintenance reduces equipment downtime.

**Human moat:**

- On-site equipment fault diagnosis and repair decision-making
- Innovative experimental design and non-standard testing.
- Interdisciplinary collaboration, communication, and explaining technical details
- Safety management and compliance assessment
- Customer requirement analysis and customized solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Basic knowledge of Python or R programming
- Introduction to Statistics and Machine Learning
- Digital lab management software (e.g., LabVantage).
- PLC or Embedded Systems basics
- Communication and project management certifications (e.g., PMP)
- Industry-specific regulations (e.g., ISO 17025)

**Key growth areas:**

- Renewable Energy
- Oil and Gas Technology
- Automation
- Research and Development

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Physical and Engineering Science Technician in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by sector, experience, and location. Typically, entry-level positions start around 450,000 NOK annually, while experienced technicians can earn 550,000-650,000 NOK. Senior roles or those in oil and gas may reach 700,000 NOK or more.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a technician in Norway under the Skilled Worker permit?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer meeting the salary threshold (around 500,000 NOK) and possess a relevant bachelor's degree or fagbrev (trade certificate). The employer must be registered and the role genuine.
