# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers in Norway perform manual tasks on farms raising animals such as cattle, sheep, pigs, and poultry. They feed and water animals, clean barns, assist with milking, and help maintain farm equipment. This occupation is essential to Norway's agricultural sector, which is characterized by small to medium-sized family farms and a strong focus on animal welfare and sustainable practices. Labour demand fluctuates seasonally, with peaks during calving and lambing periods. Many positions are part-time or seasonal, and wages are often negotiated through collective agreements. The workforce includes both local workers and migrant labourers from the EU/EEA, particularly from Eastern Europe.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 824. Employment outlook for livestock farm labourers in Norway is stable but with limited growth due to ongoing mechanization and consolidation of farms. The number of farms is declining, but those remaining often require hired help, especially during peak seasons. Climate change may affect grazing seasons and indoor housing requirements. EU/EEA free movement ensures a steady supply of labour, but stricter immigration rules for non-EEA workers may create shortages in remote areas. The Norwegian government supports agricultural subsidies to maintain rural employment, but overall demand is expected to remain flat over the next decade.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for livestock farm labourers is limited without additional training. Experienced workers can advance to lead hand or farm manager roles, often requiring a vocational certificate (fagbrev) in agriculture. Some labourers pursue specialized courses in animal husbandry or organic farming. Others transition to related fields like agricultural machinery operation or meat processing. The occupation is physically demanding and often leads to burnout or early exit. However, those who gain permanent positions on larger farms may enjoy stable employment and opportunities to acquire skills in modern farming technologies.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by region and farm size, but according to collective agreements, the minimum hourly wage for agricultural workers is around NOK 170-190 per hour. Annual full-time earnings are typically between NOK 350,000 and 400,000 before tax, though many positions are seasonal or part-time.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a visa to work as a livestock farm labourer in Norway?**
A: Yes, through a seasonal work permit for up to 6 months. You must have a job offer from a Norwegian employer, and the employer must prove that no suitable EEA labour is available. The permit does not lead to permanent residency. EU/EEA citizens can work freely.

**Q: Do I need to speak Norwegian to work as a livestock farm labourer?**
A: Basic Norwegian is often beneficial for communication with colleagues and understanding safety instructions, but many farms employ workers who speak English. However, for vocational certificates or advancement, Norwegian proficiency is required.
