# Heavy Truck and Lorry Driver — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 8332 (ISCO08)**

Heavy truck and lorry drivers operate large vehicles to transport goods across Norway. With a vast geography and reliance on road freight, drivers are essential for supply chains. The occupation is in high demand due to an aging workforce and e-commerce growth, but requires a valid C/CE license and often a vocational trade certificate.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 26,686. Employment outlook for heavy truck drivers in Norway is positive, with steady demand driven by logistics and retail. Shortages persist, especially for long-haul and specialized transport. Automation may shift roles but is not expected to drastically reduce jobs in the short term. The aging workforce creates opportunities for new entrants.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression includes upgrading licenses for specialized cargo (e.g., hazardous materials, refrigerated goods) or moving into fleet management, dispatching, or owning a small transport company. Cross-border transport to Europe offers higher pay but longer absences. Continuing education in logistics can lead to senior roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

- Autonomous driving system operation and monitoring
- Use of logistics scheduling software and multimodal transport platforms
- Basic Maintenance of New Energy Trucks (Electric/Hydrogen)
- Safety compliance and digital document management
- Customer communication and on-site problem solving
- Basic data analysis (fuel efficiency, route optimization)

**Key growth areas:**

- E-commerce logistics
- Refrigerated transport
- Hazardous materials
- Cross-border transport

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals get a work permit as a heavy truck driver?**
A: Yes, if they have a completed vocational training certificate (fagbrev) as a professional driver and a job offer meeting the salary threshold (currently 505,000 NOK).

**Q: Is heavy truck driving on the shortage list?**
A: Yes, it is listed as a shortage occupation by NAV, which can streamline the permit process.

**Q: What training is needed to become a heavy truck driver in Norway?**
A: You need a C/CE driving license, and typically a vocational training (fagbrev) in transport and logistics, which includes 2 years of school and 2 years of apprenticeship.
