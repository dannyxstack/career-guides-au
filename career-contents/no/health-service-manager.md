# Health Services Manager — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1342 (ISCO08)**

Health services managers in Norway oversee the planning, coordination, and administration of healthcare facilities, departments, or specific services. They work within the public healthcare system (e.g., regional health authorities, hospitals) or private clinics. The role requires strong leadership, financial management, and knowledge of Norwegian health regulations. With an aging population and ongoing healthcare reforms, there is a consistent demand for experienced managers who can improve efficiency and quality of care.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from clinical roles (e.g., nurse, doctor) into managerial positions, or via dedicated health management studies. Many advance to higher executive roles within regional health authorities or the Ministry of Health. Continuing education in leadership and healthcare economics is common. The role offers stable employment and high job satisfaction for those passionate about improving health systems.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI)](https://www.udi.no/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Experienced healthcare professionals (nurses, doctors) transitioning into leadership

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a health services manager in Norway?**
A: Salaries are competitive and among the highest in the public sector. They vary with experience and the size of the facility, but generally reflect the responsibility of the role. Specific figures can be found in Statistics Norway's salary statistics.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to apply for a Skilled Worker permit as a health services manager?**
A: Yes, a job offer from a Norwegian employer is mandatory. Additionally, your salary must meet the threshold set by the Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI), which is updated annually.

**Q: Is a master's degree mandatory to become a health services manager in Norway?**
A: Not mandatory, but highly preferred. Many employers require a master's in health administration or a related field for senior positions.
