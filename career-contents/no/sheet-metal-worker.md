# Sheet Metal Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7213 (ISCO08)**

Sheet metal workers in Norway fabricate, assemble, install, and repair sheet metal products used in construction, ventilation, and industrial sectors. The occupation is part of the trades and construction industry, with steady demand driven by infrastructure projects, building maintenance, and the growing focus on energy-efficient ventilation systems. Training follows a vocational pathway combining school-based education with a paid apprenticeship, leading to a recognized craft certificate (fagbrev). Employment conditions are regulated by collective agreements, and the role offers stable work in both urban and rural areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship, advancing to a skilled worker (fagarbeider) upon achieving the fagbrev. Experienced workers can specialize in areas like ventilation, roofing, or industrial sheet metal, or move into supervisory roles, project management, or self-employment. Continuing education and upskilling in digital fabrication and green technologies can enhance career prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD/CAM software to generate CNC cutting paths for automatic metal sheet cutting
- Repetitive bending and punching operations done by robots
- Automated welding systems take over standard joint welding
- AI vision inspection systems replace manual surface defect inspection
- Nesting optimization algorithms automatically calculate the most material-efficient cutting layout.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complete complex installations with remote technical expert guidance using AR headsets
- AI-assisted design tools quickly generate ventilation duct layout plans
- Digital twin simulates installation outcomes to reduce onsite rework
- Real-time feedback on fastening torque and angle accuracy via smart handheld tools
- Predictive maintenance systems based on historical data optimize repair schedules.

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability to handle non-standard, irregular structures during on-site installation
- Expertise in diagnosing and manually repairing complex system faults
- Ability to communicate with clients to clarify needs and provide customized solutions
- Physical skills for flexible operation in confined or hazardous environments
- Intuitive understanding of material properties (e.g., ductility of different metals)

**Skills to build:**

- CNC machine programming (e.g., CNC press brakes, laser cutters)
- 3D CAD/CAM software (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD)
- Industrial robot operation and maintenance basics
- Post-processing Techniques for Additive Manufacturing (Metal 3D Printing)
- Data analysis and digital twin tool application.
- Green building standards (e.g., LEED) and knowledge of energy-efficient materials

**Key growth areas:**

- Ventilation systems
- Sustainable construction
- Industrial maintenance
- Roofing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sheet metal worker in Norway?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by experience, region, and sector. Skilled workers with fagbrev typically earn a competitive salary that meets the threshold for skilled worker permits. For current figures, consult SSB's earnings statistics or NAV data.

**Q: Can a non-EEA sheet metal worker obtain a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, if you have a valid job offer and completed vocational training (e.g., fagbrev or equivalent), you can apply for a Skilled Worker Residence Permit. You must meet the salary threshold and document your qualifications. EU/EEA nationals do not need a permit.

**Q: How can I become a certified sheet metal worker in Norway?**
A: Complete upper secondary vocational education (Vg1 and Vg2 in construction or technical subjects), then a two-year apprenticeship with an approved company. After passing the trade examination (fagprøve), you receive your fagbrev. Public education is free.
