# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, mixed crop and livestock farm labourers perform a variety of tasks on farms that combine crop cultivation and animal husbandry. These workers are essential for day-to-day operations, including feeding and tending livestock, planting and harvesting crops, maintaining fences and buildings, and operating farm machinery. The role is physically demanding and often seasonal, with peaks during planting and harvest times. Most farms in Norway are small to medium-sized family operations, though larger industrial farms exist. Labourers often work outdoors in varying weather conditions. The occupation attracts primarily local and EU/EEA nationals due to free movement, as non-EEA labour faces significant barriers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 551. Employment for farm labourers in Norway is projected to remain stable but slightly decline due to mechanization and structural changes in agriculture. Technological advances like automated milking and precision farming reduce demand for manual labour. However, there is steady demand for seasonal workers, particularly in fruit and vegetable picking and livestock care. The Norwegian government supports rural employment through subsidies, but cost pressures encourage consolidation. Organic farming and local food trends may create niche opportunities. Overall, the occupation is not expected to grow significantly, and competition for year-round positions is moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for farm labourers is limited but possible. With experience, one can become a specialist in animal husbandry, crop management, or machinery operation. Some workers advance to farm supervisor or foreman, overseeing teams. Further education in agriculture at vocational schools or universities can lead to farm management or advisory roles. However, many labourers remain in the same role for years due to the physical nature and lack of formal requirements. Seasonal work often leads to temporary contracts, but loyal employees may secure permanent positions on larger farms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using automated tractors for plowing, sowing, and harvesting
- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots
- Use drones to inspect farms and produce yield maps
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans based on historical data and weather forecasts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Collaborate with AI for precision agriculture management
- Train new employees in using digital tools

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a mixed crop and livestock farm labourer in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by region, employer, and experience, but are generally below the national median. Full-time year-round workers can expect around 350,000 to 450,000 NOK per year, while seasonal workers earn hourly wages. Note that many farm labourers work part-time or seasonally, affecting annual figures.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as farm labourers in Norway?**
A: It is difficult for non-EEA nationals to obtain a work permit for this occupation. The Skilled Worker permit requires higher qualifications and salary, while the Seasonal Work permit is limited to specific short-term needs (e.g., berry picking) and has strict quotas. Most farm labourers are EU/EEA citizens or have family ties.

**Q: Is prior experience required to get hired as a farm labourer?**
A: While not mandatory, experience with farm work, animals, or machinery is highly valued. Employers often prefer candidates with a tractor licence and basic agricultural knowledge. On-the-job training is common for motivated workers.
