# Cabinet-maker and Related Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7522 (ISCO08)**

Cabinet-makers and related workers craft, assemble, and repair wooden furniture, cabinets, and other fixtures, often using traditional hand tools as well as modern power tools and CNC machinery. In Norway, demand is driven by custom furniture trends, kitchen renovations, and a strong DIY culture, with many employed in small to medium-sized workshops, construction companies, or self-employed. Vocational education and apprenticeship are the primary entry routes, and the occupation is open to EU/EEA nationals without a permit. For non-EEA applicants, a skilled worker permit requires a completed trade certificate and a job offer meeting the salary threshold.

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,042. Employment outlook for cabinet-makers in Norway is stable, with moderate replacement demand due to retirements. Digitalization and automation of mass production reduce opportunities in large-scale furniture manufacturing, but custom and high-end craftsmanship remain in demand, especially in urban areas and regions with vibrant construction activity. NAV does not currently list the occupation as a shortage sector, so competition can be moderate, but skilled cabinet-makers with CNC and design skills may have an edge. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a 2–4 year vocational education (VGS) followed by a 2–3 year apprenticeship to obtain a trade certificate (fagbrev). Many move on to become master craftsmen, start their own firms, or specialize in areas like kitchen fitting, antique restoration, or sustainable furniture. Further training in CAD/CAM, project management, or interior design opens pathways to supervisory roles or independent contracting. With experience, income rises as reputation and client base grow.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD/CAM software to automatically generate cutting paths and material lists
- Batch assembly and cutting of standardized cabinets (done by CNC robots)
- Finished product quality inspection via AI vision detection system
- BIM-based automatic measurement and adjustment planning for on-site installation dimensions
- Automated inventory management and replenishment system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI-driven custom design software to quickly generate client proposals (e.g. space fitting and style recommendations)
- AR/VR-assisted customer preview of finished product results, improving communication efficiency
- AI optimizes material usage and reduces waste
- Smart tools (e.g., electric screwdrivers with automatic torque adjustment) improve precision in manual installation
- Project management platform automates scheduling and progress tracking.

**Human moat:**

- Hand carving and antique finishing techniques for high-end custom cabinets
- Creativity and experience judgment for solving non-standard installation issues on site
- Ability to communicate aesthetic needs with clients and translate them into unique designs
- Machining and assembly techniques for complex curved and irregularly shaped parts
- Touch-dependent skills such as wood property identification and defect repair

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in CAD/CAM software (e.g., TopSolid, WoodWop)
- Learn AI-assisted design tools (e.g., Autodesk Fusion 360 generative design)
- Improve client communication and project management skills
- Operate CNC machining centers and basic robot maintenance.
- Learn BIM modeling (e.g., Revit) for building integration
- Digital manufacturing (3D printing molds, laser cutting)

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom furniture
- Kitchen renovation
- Sustainable materials
- CNC machining

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cabinet-maker in Norway?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from 350,000 to 400,000 NOK per year, while experienced cabinet-makers earn 450,000 to 500,000 NOK. Median pay according to SSB is around 420,000 NOK, varying by region and employer.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a cabinet-maker in Norway?**
A: Yes, if you have a recognized trade certificate (fagbrev) and a job offer that meets the skilled worker salary threshold (around 450,000 NOK/year). You can apply for a skilled worker residence permit. A jobseeker permit is also possible for up to 6 months.

**Q: Is cabinet-making a regulated trade in Norway?**
A: Yes, it is a regulated trade. You must have a trade certificate (fagbrev) to work legally as a cabinet-maker in most contexts, especially for permanent positions. Without it, you may only work under supervision or in non-certified roles.
