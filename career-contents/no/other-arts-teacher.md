# Other Arts Teacher — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2355 (ISCO08)**

Other Arts Teachers in Norway provide specialized instruction in visual arts, music, dance, drama, or other creative disciplines outside the formal school system, such as in cultural schools, private studios, or community organizations. The role often combines artistic expertise with pedagogical skills, adapting lessons to diverse age groups and skill levels. Many work part-time or on a freelance basis, especially in urban areas like Oslo and Bergen. The occupation is characterized by a mix of permanent positions in municipal culture schools and self-employment. Demand is steady, driven by public funding for cultural activities and private interest in hobby education, though competition for full-time roles is moderate.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 351. The employment outlook for Other Arts Teachers in Norway is stable, with slight growth in digital and online arts education. Municipal culture schools remain the largest employer, but budget constraints may limit new hires. Private tutoring and adult education are expanding, particularly in music and visual arts. The Norwegian government supports cultural participation, which sustains demand. However, many positions are part-time, and full-time opportunities are limited. Overall, competition is moderate, and job seekers with digital teaching skills have an advantage.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an Other Arts Teacher in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and experience. Employed teachers in municipal culture schools earn between 450,000 and 550,000 NOK annually pre-tax, while private tutors often charge 300–600 NOK per hour. Full-time positions are less common.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national work as an Other Arts Teacher in Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker residence permit. You need a job offer, a completed bachelor's degree or higher, and a salary meeting the threshold (around 500,000 NOK/year). A 6-month jobseeker visa is also available for skilled workers to look for work.

**Q: Is teaching certification mandatory to work as an arts teacher in Norway?**
A: Not always. For private lessons and some freelance positions, no certification is required. However, for permanent jobs in public culture schools, a Practical Pedagogical Education (PPU) is often expected. It is highly recommended for career stability.
