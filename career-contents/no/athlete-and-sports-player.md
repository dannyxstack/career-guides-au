# Athlete and Sports Player — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 3421 (ISCO08)**

Athletes and sports players in Norway participate in competitive sports at various levels, from amateur to professional. The country has a strong tradition in winter sports like skiing and skating, as well as popular team sports such as football, handball, and ice hockey. The labour market for athletes is highly competitive, with few full-time positions. Many athletes combine training with education or part-time work. Norway offers good public support for elite athletes through sports federations and funding, but career longevity is short, and most transition to other roles after their athletic peak.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for professional athletes in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely: top football or handball players can earn several million NOK annually, but many athletes earn modest incomes or rely on part-time jobs. There is no official average due to the diverse nature of the profession.

**Q: Can non-EU/EEA athletes obtain a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker residence permit if they have a concrete job offer from a Norwegian sports club and meet the salary threshold (usually around NOK 500,000 per year). Documentation of qualifications or experience is required.

**Q: What do athletes typically do after their sports career ends?**
A: Many transition to coaching, sports management, personal training, or pursue higher education (e.g., in sports science or business). Some retrain for entirely different fields. Networking and early career planning are important.
