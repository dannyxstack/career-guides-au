# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers in Norway construct, repair, and maintain building frames and structures, working with materials such as concrete, steel, and timber. They are essential to the country's booming construction sector, driven by urbanisation and infrastructure investments. These workers often specialise in formwork, steel fixing, or timber framing, and are employed by construction firms or as self-employed contractors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a two-year apprenticeship (lærling) leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced workers can advance to foreman, site manager, or specialised contractor roles. Specialisations include sustainable construction, prefabrication, and heritage building restoration. Many workers also become self-employed, offering flexibility and higher earning potential.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable construction
- off-site prefabrication
- infrastructure renewal
- housing construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for building frame and related trades workers in Norway?**
A: Salaries in Norway for this occupation are competitive and vary by experience, location, and specialisation. They generally rank among the higher-paid trades, with increases from seniority and certifications.

**Q: Can non-EEA nationals work as a building frame worker in Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker Permit if you have a job offer, a recognised trade certificate or degree, and meet the salary threshold. The employer must comply with Norwegian working conditions.

**Q: Is there a shortage of building frame workers in Norway?**
A: Yes, many construction trades are in shortage, especially in urban areas and for specialised skills like timber framing and sustainable construction, making it relatively easier to find employment.
