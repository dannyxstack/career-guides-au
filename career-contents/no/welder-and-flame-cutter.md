# Welder and Flame Cutter — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7212 (ISCO08)**

Welders and flame cutters are in high demand in Norway, driven by the oil and gas industry, shipbuilding, construction, and renewable energy projects. The occupation requires precision and adherence to strict safety standards. Many welders obtain a vocational trade certificate (fagbrev) through apprenticeships or technical schools, and specialised certifications such as ISO 9606 are often required by employers. While EEA/EFTA nationals can work freely, non-EEA skilled workers must meet permit requirements including a job offer and relevant qualifications. The Norwegian labour market offers stable employment and opportunities for advancement to supervisory or specialist roles.

The employment outlook for welders in Norway remains positive due to an ageing workforce, ongoing infrastructure projects, and sustained activity in offshore oil, gas, and renewable energy sectors. Skilled welders with certifications in advanced techniques (e.g., TIG, MIG) and safety training will have the best prospects. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Welders typically start as apprentices under a certified mentor, completing a two-to-three-year vocational programme leading to a fagbrev. With experience, they can progress to specialised roles like underwater welding or robotic welding programming. Many advance to team leader or quality inspector positions. Additional certifications in advanced welding methods or non-destructive testing open further career paths. Some welders move into teaching or start their own contracting businesses. The career path is flexible, with strong demand across multiple industries.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch standardised metal parts automatic welding (e.g., car body welding, pipe joints)
- Robot programming and path planning based on CAD models replace manual teaching
- Online weld quality inspection (visual inspection + AI defect detection)
- Automatic adjustment of welding parameters and welding process monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted welding parameter optimization (real-time recommendation of current/voltage based on material thickness and ambient temperature)
- Augmented reality (AR) welding guidance system improves novice training efficiency
- Drones/crawler robots equipped with welding modules for high-altitude or hazardous welding
- Digital twin welding simulation reduces material waste from test pieces
- AI-driven multi-robot collaborative welding group control

**Human moat:**

- On-site manual welding of non-standard workpieces and confined spaces (e.g., on ships, inside pipes)
- Welding of pressure vessels and load-bearing structures with high stress and fatigue life requirements
- Welding process development for dissimilar metals and special alloys (titanium, aluminum-magnesium alloys)
- Welding quality responsibility determination and repair decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Programming and debugging of welding robots (e.g., FANUC, KUKA, ABB)
- Welding process simulation software (e.g., Simufact Welding, SYSWELD)
- Automated welding quality control and testing methods
- Digital manufacturing basics (CAD/CAM, BOM management)
- Advanced certifications such as International Welding Engineer (IWE)
- Operation of AR/VR welding simulation training systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore Oil & Gas
- Shipbuilding
- Renewable Energy
- Infrastructure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a welder in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by industry, experience, and certifications. Entry-level welders may earn around 450,000–500,000 NOK annually, while experienced specialists can exceed 650,000 NOK. Offshore oil and gas positions often pay higher due to demanding conditions.

**Q: How can a non-EEA welder get a work permit for Norway?**
A: A non-EEA welder needs a job offer from a Norwegian employer and must possess a completed trade certificate (fagbrev) or equivalent, plus meet the salary threshold for skilled workers (approx. 457,350 NOK/year). The Skilled Worker Permit is the main route. A Jobseeker Permit can be used to enter Norway to look for work, but a job offer is eventually required.

**Q: Do I need Norwegian language skills to work as a welder?**
A: While not always mandatory, many employers require at least basic Norwegian (A2–B1 level) for safety communication and teamwork. Vocational training programmes also include Norwegian language instruction.
