# Livestock and Dairy Producer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6121 (ISCO08)**

Livestock and dairy producers manage the breeding, feeding, and health of animals for meat, milk, and other products, often operating family farms in rural Norway. The role involves daily animal care, pasture management, handling of milking equipment, and ensuring compliance with strict animal welfare and food safety regulations. Many farms are transitioning towards organic production and adopting precision farming technologies to improve efficiency and sustainability.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 3,234. Employment in livestock and dairy production is relatively stable but faces consolidation pressures as smaller farms merge or close. The sector offers opportunities for skilled workers with expertise in organic farming, animal health, and modern technologies. However, overall workforce numbers are slowly declining, and recruitment of young farmers remains a challenge. Automation and digital tools are increasingly adopted to offset labor shortages.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm hand or apprentice, moving to senior herdsman or farm manager, and eventually to farm ownership. Specialization in organic livestock, dairy technology, or animal nutrition opens further opportunities. Some experienced producers transition into advisory roles with agricultural extension services or supply cooperatives. Continuous learning in animal welfare and sustainable practices is valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding systems replace manual feeding and water management.
- Smart sensors automatically monitor animal health data and send alerts
- Milking robots replace manual milking operations.
- Automation of data recording and analysis replaces manual ledgers
- Driverless tractors replace some field labor

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted early disease warning system improves veterinary diagnostic accuracy.
- IoT optimizes pasture environment control and feed ratios on farms
- Drone inspection of large-scale pastures to improve management efficiency
- Data analysis tools assist breeding plans and yield prediction
- Blockchain technology tracks dairy supply chains to enhance trust

**Human moat:**

- On-site judgment of animal welfare and emergency health conditions
- Unstructured problem-solving ability in complex environments
- Interpersonal communication and team management with veterinarians, staff, and others
- Trade-off decisions on macro factors such as ecology, market, and policy.
- Intuition and feel gained from long-term farming experience

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and sensor technology application
- Automated equipment operation and maintenance
- Use of AI diagnostic tools for animal health
- Sustainable agriculture and precision farming practices
- Digital marketing and supply chain tracking
- Change management and team digital leadership

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- precision livestock farming
- local food production
- sustainable agriculture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical starting salary for livestock and dairy producers in Norway?**
A: Starting salaries for farm workers or apprentices are lower, while experienced farm managers can earn 400,000–550,000 NOK annually. Wages vary by region, farm size, and production type.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a livestock and dairy producer in Norway?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. You need a relevant trade certificate and a job offer that meets the skilled worker salary threshold (over 468,000 NOK). Agricultural wages are often below this, so approval is not guaranteed.

**Q: What advancement opportunities exist in this field?**
A: Experienced workers can become farm managers, start their own farm, or specialize in organic production, animal health, or farm technology. Some move into advisory roles with agricultural cooperatives or government agencies.
