# Supply, Distribution and Related Managers — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1324 (ISCO08)**

Supply, distribution and related managers in Norway oversee logistics, supply chains, inventory, and distribution networks across sectors such as retail, manufacturing, oil and gas, and maritime industries. With a strong export-oriented economy and growing e-commerce, efficient logistics are critical. The role requires strategic planning, coordination with suppliers and customers, and optimization of transportation and warehousing. Norway's focus on sustainability and digitalization is reshaping the field, demanding skills in green logistics and advanced supply chain technologies.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts from logistics coordinator or supply chain analyst, advancing to manager, then director. Relevant experience and certifications (e.g., APICS CSCP, Lean Six Sigma) enhance prospects. In Norway, many professionals hold a bachelor's or master's degree in logistics or business. The field values digital literacy and cross-functional skills. Some managers move into consultancy or entrepreneurship, while others specialize in sectors like offshore logistics or cold chain distribution.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Daily inventory data entry and updates
- Manual planning and optimization of transport routes
- Order tracking and status updates
- Automatic generation of supplier performance reports
- Basic statistical calculations in demand forecasting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI for demand forecasting and inventory optimization
- Optimizing transport routes through real-time data analysis
- Use automated tools to monitor supply chain risks and issue warnings
- Utilize natural language processing to analyze supplier communications and contracts
- Use digital twin simulation of supply chain scenarios for decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Cross-departmental coordination and negotiation skills
- Emergency decision-making ability to handle sudden disruptions
- Maintenance of supplier relationships and long-term strategy
- Understand business objectives and set supply chain strategies.
- Ethical and compliance judgement (e.g., labour law, environmental protection)

**Skills to build:**

- Supply chain data analysis and visualization (Python/SQL/BI tools)
- AI and Machine Learning in Logistics Optimization
- Digital supply chain management systems (e.g., SAP, Oracle SCM)
- Project Management and Agile Methodologies
- Automated process design (RPA basics)
- Soft skills: leadership, cross-cultural communication

**Key growth areas:**

- E-commerce logistics
- Green supply chain
- Digitalization
- Automation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary level for supply and distribution managers in Norway?**
A: Salaries are competitive and above the national average, varying by experience, sector, and location. Official statistics from SSB (table 11418) provide detailed annual figures. For precise data, refer to the latest SSB earnings tables.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign supply chain managers?**
A: EEA/EFTA nationals have free movement rights. Non-EEA skilled workers can apply for a Skilled Worker Residence Permit with a job offer meeting the salary threshold (500,000 NOK/year) and relevant qualifications. A 6-month Job Seeker Visa is also available for highly skilled individuals.

**Q: Is supply and distribution management on Norway's shortage list?**
A: Yes, logistics and supply chain managers are currently listed as in shortage by NAV, reflecting demand in e-commerce, manufacturing, and export industries. This may facilitate work permit processing for qualified applicants.
