# Odd-Job Person — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons, known as allroundmedarbeidere in Norway, perform a variety of temporary or low-skilled tasks across industries such as construction, cleaning, warehousing, and maintenance. They are often hired on short-term contracts or through staffing agencies, filling gaps in the labour market where specific skills are not required. The role demands flexibility, physical stamina, and basic Norwegian language skills for safety and communication.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,314. Employment outlook for odd-job persons in Norway is stable but not growing significantly. Demand is driven by seasonal fluctuations in construction, tourism, and agriculture. Automation and digitalisation may reduce some manual tasks, but there will always be a need for flexible, on-demand labour. The position rarely leads to permanent employment; most workers cycle between assignments.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for odd-job persons is limited. With experience and on-the-job training, some may move into more specialised roles like warehouse operator, cleaner, or construction helper. Vocational courses or certificates (e.g., forklift license) can improve job prospects. However, the occupation is often a stepping stone for immigrants or temporary workers rather than a long-term career path.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- flexible labour
- seasonal work
- staffing agencies
- temp jobs

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

**Who is suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and location, but typically range from 180,000 to 280,000 NOK per year before tax. Hourly rates are common, around 150-200 NOK. No specific salary threshold applies for visa purposes.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa as an odd-job person?**
A: No. The Skilled Worker residence permit requires at least a bachelor's degree or completed vocational training. Odd-job persons do not qualify. Only EU/EEA nationals can work in this role without a permit.
