# Metal Working Machine Tool Setter and Operator — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7223 (ISCO08)**

Metal working machine tool setters and operators in Norway set up, operate, and maintain CNC and conventional machine tools used to cut, shape, and finish metal parts. They work primarily in manufacturing industries such as oil and gas, maritime, machinery, and renewable energy. The role requires precision, technical understanding, and adherence to safety standards. Many positions involve shift work, and the occupation is integral to Norway's industrial base, supporting both domestic production and export-oriented sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice, leading to a skilled operator with a trade certificate (fagbrev). Operators can advance to CNC programmer, shift supervisor, or technician. Further education at a fagskole can lead to engineering technician roles. Specializations in multi-axis machining, robotics, or additive manufacturing can increase earning potential. Some operators move into sales or service of machine tools.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- CNC machine programming (G-code/M-code) automatically generated by AI
- Real-time monitoring of processing and tool life prediction
- Automatic measurement and feedback compensation of workpiece dimensions
- Optimization of batch processing paths for standard parts.
- AI-assisted equipment fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Simulation and optimization of complex part machining processes using AI.
- Using augmented reality (AR) for equipment debugging and maintenance
- AI-driven quality prediction system improves yield rate
- Digital twin technology enables virtual commissioning and training
- Smart scheduling system improves efficiency in high-mix, low-volume production

**Human moat:**

- Experience in debugging non-standard, high-precision parts
- Understand drawing design intent and propose process improvement suggestions
- Hands-on ability to troubleshoot on-site sudden failures
- Programming and operating combined machining (turning, milling, drilling) on a single workpiece
- Cross-departmental collaboration and communication with designers, quality engineers, etc.

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM software applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX)
- Robot loading/unloading and automation cell integration
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) data acquisition and analysis
- Python/MATLAB basics for processing parameter optimization
- CNC multi-axis (5-axis) programming
- Technical English reading (equipment manuals, international standards)

**Key growth areas:**

- CNC programming
- Precision engineering
- Renewable energy manufacturing
- Industrial maintenance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a metal working machine operator in Norway?**
A: Annual salaries typically range from 450,000 to 600,000 NOK, with experienced CNC operators earning up to 700,000 NOK. Shift work and overtime can add 10-20%.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a metal working machine operator in Norway?**
A: Yes, if you have a recognized vocational qualification (trade certificate) and a job offer meeting the minimum salary threshold (450,000 NOK in 2024). You must apply for a Skilled Worker residence permit.

**Q: How do I become a certified machine operator in Norway?**
A: Complete upper secondary vocational education in industrial mechanics, then a 2-year apprenticeship leading to a fagbrev (trade certificate). Alternatively, attend a fagskole program in CNC technology for advanced skills.
