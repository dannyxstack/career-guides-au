# Field Crop and Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Norway manage the cultivation of cereals, potatoes, and vegetables suited to the Nordic climate. With an aging workforce and many small-to-medium farms, the sector relies on subsidies and trade protections. Increasing demand for organic and locally sourced produce is driving innovation in sustainable practices, greenhouse production, and precision agriculture. Digitalization is gradually transforming farm management, but traditional methods still dominate. The role requires adaptability to weather conditions and a commitment to year-round planning.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 134. Employment in traditional field crop and vegetable growing is stable but faces long-term challenges from climate change and import competition. Organic farming and greenhouse vegetable production are growth areas, supported by government policy and consumer trends. Automation and data-driven tools are slowly being adopted, but the sector remains labor-intensive. Opportunities for new entrants are limited due to high land and capital costs, and many farms are family-owned. Overall, job openings are few, with the majority of growers being self-employed or working within family operations.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker, advancing to farm manager or self-employed grower. Succession planning is critical in family farms. Specialization in organic farming, greenhouse management, or niche crops (e.g., heritage varieties) can offer better prospects. Some growers diversify into farm-to-table initiatives, agritourism, or on-farm processing. Formal education from Norwegian agricultural schools or universities (e.g., NMBU) is common, and a trade certificate (fagbrev) in agriculture enhances mobility. Digital skills are increasingly valued for precision farming.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Greenhouse technology
- Sustainable agriculture
- Precision farming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is a typical salary for a field crop and vegetable grower in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely; self-employed growers' income depends on farm size and yields. For employed growers, annual pre-tax earnings typically range from 450,000 to 550,000 NOK. Larger farms and organic certification may offer higher pay.

**Q: Can I work as a field crop and vegetable grower in Norway with a Skilled Worker permit?**
A: It is possible if you have a concrete job offer, relevant education or trade certificate, and the salary meets the threshold (approx. 500,000 NOK). However, many agricultural jobs pay below this threshold, so this route is restricted and not common.

**Q: Is there demand for technology skills in this occupation in Norway?**
A: Yes, increasingly. Precision farming tools, GPS-guided tractors, and data analytics are being adopted to boost yields and sustainability. Growers with digital skills are more competitive, and some farms use drones for crop monitoring.
