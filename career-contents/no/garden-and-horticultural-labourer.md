# Garden and Horticultural Labourer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 9214 (ISCO08)**

Garden and horticultural labourers perform manual tasks such as planting, watering, weeding, pruning, and harvesting in nurseries, parks, gardens, and agricultural settings. In Norway, this occupation is concentrated in the agricultural sector, especially during the growing season, and draws many seasonal workers from EU/EEA countries. The work is often physically demanding and may involve outdoor work in varying weather conditions. Labourers are employed by private gardens, municipal parks, landscape contractors, and greenhouse operations. The role typically requires no formal education, though some employers value practical experience or a relevant vocational certificate.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 1,242. Employment for garden and horticultural labourers in Norway is stable but seasonal. Demand peaks in spring and summer for planting and maintenance, and in autumn for harvesting. The sector relies heavily on seasonal migrant labour, primarily from EU/EEA countries. Climate change may extend growing seasons, potentially increasing demand. Automation is limited in many tasks, but technology like robotic mowers may reduce demand for some routine work. Overall, opportunities exist but with limited career progression and lower wages compared to skilled trades. Non-EEA workers face strict visa restrictions, as this occupation does not meet the skilled worker threshold.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for garden and horticultural labourers in Norway is limited without further education or training. Many workers remain in seasonal or temporary positions. Advancement opportunities include becoming a team leader, foreman, or moving into specialized roles like tree surgery or landscaping design, which require vocational qualifications (e.g., a trade certificate in horticulture). Some may start their own small gardening businesses. With experience and additional certification (e.g., pesticide application), workers can access higher-paid positions. However, without formal education, the scope for career mobility remains modest.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garden and horticultural labourer in Norway?**
A: Salaries for garden and horticultural labourers vary by employer and region, but are generally in the lower range of Norwegian wages. Typical hourly rates are between 180-220 NOK per hour, resulting in an annual income of around 350,000-420,000 NOK for full-time work. Seasonal workers may earn less. Pay is often higher in the Oslo area or for specialized roles.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa as a garden labourer in Norway?**
A: Non-EU/EEA nationals have very limited options. The Skilled Worker permit is not available for this occupation because it does not require a higher education or meet the salary threshold. The only route is a seasonal work permit for up to 6 months, but this requires a concrete job offer, employer sponsorship, and is subject to annual quotas. Most garden labourers in Norway are EU/EEA citizens using free movement rights.

**Q: Are there many job opportunities for garden labourers in winter?**
A: Job opportunities decrease significantly in winter due to the seasonal nature of most horticultural work. Some indoor greenhouse operations or municipal snow clearance tasks may be available, but many labourers are laid off or work reduced hours. Workers are often advised to seek alternative employment or save income from the peak season.
