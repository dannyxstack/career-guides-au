# Floor Layer and Tile Setter — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7122 (ISCO08)**

Floor layers and tile setters install and repair flooring materials such as wood, laminate, vinyl, carpet, and tiles in residential and commercial buildings across Norway. They work for specialized flooring firms, construction companies, or as self-employed contractors. The role demands precision, physical stamina, and knowledge of different materials and techniques. With a strong construction and renovation market, demand for these skilled tradespeople remains consistent, particularly in urban growth areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Typical career progression starts with a two-year vocational school program followed by two years of apprenticeship, earning a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced workers can become foremen, self-employed, or specialize in areas like underfloor heating, large-scale commercial projects, or historic restoration. Further education in construction management or civil engineering opens pathways to higher-level positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic measurement of room dimensions and material quantity calculation, AI can quickly generate optimal layout plans
- Automatic cutting of tiles/stone, CNC waterjet or laser cutting replaces manual cutting
- Automatic detection and adjustment solution generation for ground flatness, reducing manual trial laying
- Inventory management and material ordering processes, AI analyzes project requirements to automatically place orders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to view tiling blueprints and level indicators in real time, improving accuracy
- AI-assisted design of complex patterns and parquet, generating diverse options for clients
- Work collaboratively with automatic mortar mixing and plastering robots to improve efficiency
- AI-driven project progress prediction and scheduling to optimize multi-trade collaboration

**Human moat:**

- Manual cutting and fine placement in irregular areas and corners
- Removal of old flooring, base treatment, and leveling judgment for uneven floors.
- Communicate design preferences with clients and provide personalized advice
- Flexible problem-solving ability for on-site unexpected issues.

**Skills to build:**

- Digital Measurement Tools and BIM Software Operations
- CNC cutting machine programming and maintenance
- Use of augmented reality (AR) tools for construction assistance.
- Project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and environmentally friendly construction methods
- Basic electrical and mechanical troubleshooting

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Eco-friendly materials
- Underfloor heating
- Commercial projects

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a floor layer or tile setter in Norway?**
A: Salaries for floor layers and tile setters in Norway vary by experience, region, and employer. Generally, entry-level workers earn around 400,000–500,000 NOK per year, while experienced professionals can earn 500,000–650,000 NOK or more. Self-employed workers have higher earning potential but less predictability.

**Q: Can non-EEA floor layers get a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, if you have a completed vocational training (trade certificate) equivalent to Norwegian fagbrev and a concrete job offer from a Norwegian employer meeting the salary threshold (approx. 506,700 NOK in 2025), you can apply for a Skilled Worker Residence Permit. The occupation is considered skilled, so it is a viable migration route.

**Q: Is there a shortage of floor layers in Norway?**
A: While not on the official shortage list, many employers report difficulties recruiting qualified floor layers, especially in rural areas and for specialized work like underfloor heating or large commercial projects. The demand is expected to remain solid due to ongoing renovation and maintenance needs.
