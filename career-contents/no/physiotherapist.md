# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Norway assess, diagnose, and treat patients with movement disorders resulting from injury, illness, or ageing. They work in hospitals, rehabilitation centres, municipal health services, and private clinics. The profession is regulated by the Norwegian Directorate of Health, requiring authorization. Due to an ageing population and increasing focus on preventive care and rehabilitation, demand is high and steady, with physiotherapists frequently appearing on NAV's shortage list. The role involves developing tailored exercise programs, manual therapy, and patient education, often collaborating with other healthcare professionals.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 6,058. The employment outlook for physiotherapists in Norway is strong. An ageing population, rising chronic disease prevalence, and a focus on outpatient and primary care are driving sustained demand. Municipalities and hospitals report recruitment difficulties, and the profession is included on the national shortage list. Job opportunities are particularly good in rural and northern regions. The trend toward multidisciplinary rehabilitation teams and digital health solutions also opens new roles. Overall, physiotherapy remains a stable, growing field with low unemployment and good prospects for new graduates.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression in Norwegian physiotherapy often begins in municipal health services or hospitals. With experience, physiotherapists can specialise in areas such as sports, paediatrics, neurology, or geriatrics, often through further education (Master's or specialist training). Many move into private practice, either solo or in partnership, which can offer higher earnings but requires business acumen. Senior roles include clinical specialist, department head, or research positions. Some physiotherapists transition to teaching or management in healthcare institutions. The profession encourages lifelong learning, and specialisation is rewarded with higher pay and responsibilities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient record organization and electronic health record entry
- Scoring and data analysis of standardized assessment scales.
- Generate routine reports on treatment progress
- Administrative tasks such as appointment scheduling and patient reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical starting salary for a physiotherapist in Norway?**
A: A newly qualified physiotherapist in the public sector (municipal or hospital) typically earns between 500,000 and 550,000 NOK per year. With experience and specialisation, salaries can rise to around 600,000–700,000 NOK. Private practice earnings vary widely based on client volume and clinic overhead.

**Q: Can a non-EEA physiotherapist get a work visa without a job offer?**
A: Generally no. For a Skilled Worker permit, a specific job offer as a physiotherapist is mandatory. The job must meet the salary threshold (approx. 509,600 NOK in 2024) and be deemed skilled. If you have a completed degree and job offer, you can apply from abroad. However, Norway also offers a jobseeker permit for six months, which does not require a job offer, but you would then need to find a job and change to a skilled worker permit.
