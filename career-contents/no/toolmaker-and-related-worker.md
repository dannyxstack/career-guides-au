# Toolmaker and Related Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7222 (ISCO08)**

Toolmakers and related workers in Norway produce and repair precision tools, dies, jigs, and fixtures used in manufacturing, primarily in the oil and gas, maritime, and renewable energy sectors. The occupation is highly skilled and typically requires a completed vocational apprenticeship (fagbrev) as a verktøymaker. Employment is concentrated in industrial areas along the coast and Eastern Norway. The Norwegian labour market values certified tradespeople, and there is a consistent demand for precision machining skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for toolmakers typically starts with a two-year vocational school program followed by a two-year apprenticeship that leads to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced toolmakers can advance to specialist roles in CNC programming, quality control, or become supervisors. Further education (fagskole) in mechanical engineering or industrial automation opens opportunities for technical leadership or teaching. Some toolmakers transition to larger industries or start their own workshops.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual CNC programming and tool path generation
- Simple repetitive machine operations (e.g., loading/unloading workpieces, starting machining cycles)
- Routine dimensional measurement and quality recording (manual measurement with calipers and micrometers)
- Paper-based drawing management and document archiving
- Basic troubleshooting (based on fixed diagnostic procedures)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted tool path optimization (automatic collision avoidance, improved machining efficiency)
- Machine learning-based online quality inspection (real-time prediction of dimensional deviations)
- Digital twin simulation of machining processes (pre-validate fixture designs)
- Smart production scheduling system integration (automatic assignment of machine tasks)
- Augmented reality-assisted assembly and debugging (overlaying virtual guidance)

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment in complex fixture design and debugging
- On-site diagnosis and repair of multi-process integration issues.
- Ability to communicate non-standard requirements with clients/engineers
- Safety responsibility and process innovation decisions
- Precision manual finishing and assembly touch

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX machining modules)
- Robot-assisted loading/unloading programming and integration
- Python or MATLAB for machining data analysis
- Basics of additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and smart tool management
- Quality control data visualization (e.g., Power BI)

**Key growth areas:**

- Manufacturing
- Automation
- Precision Engineering
- Renewable Energy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical career progression for a toolmaker in Norway?**
A: After completing vocational training and a 2-year apprenticeship, you obtain a trade certificate. You can then work as a journeyman, later become a specialist in CNC machining, or pursue further education at a technical college to become a technician or supervisor.

**Q: Can non-EU toolmakers get a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Norwegian employer and hold a trade certificate (fagbrev) or equivalent qualification recognized by the authorities. The salary must meet the skilled worker threshold. EU/EEA citizens do not need a visa.

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a toolmaker in Norway?**
A: Toolmakers earn a competitive wage that meets the skilled worker permit requirements. Salaries increase with experience and specialization.
