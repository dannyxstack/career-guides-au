# Tree and Shrub Crop Grower — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6112 (ISCO08)**

Tree and shrub crop growers cultivate fruit, berries, and ornamental trees and shrubs in Norway, often in small to medium-sized farms. The sector is small but vital for domestic fruit and berry production, with a focus on apples, plums, cherries, and currants. Growers manage orchards and nurseries, perform pruning, pest control, and harvesting. Norway's challenging climate requires robust varieties and protective measures. The industry is seeing a shift towards organic and sustainable practices, with growing demand for locally sourced produce. Employment is largely seasonal, but full-time positions exist on larger estates or in cooperatives.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or apprentice, advancing to orchard manager or farm owner. With experience, growers may specialize in organic cultivation, nursery management, or agricultural consulting. Further education in horticulture or agronomy can lead to roles in research or agricultural advisory services. Many growers are self-employed and may expand into farm shops or agritourism to supplement income.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated fruit picking using AI vision and robotic arms
- Automated irrigation and fertilization scheduling based on sensor data
- Automatically monitor crop health and pests using drones and satellite imagery
- Automated pruning and shaping operations (e.g., grapevines)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze soil and meteorological data to optimize planting plans and variety selection
- Precisely identify early diseases through computer vision and recommend localized pesticide application.
- Uses predictive models to optimize harvest timing and market sales strategies
- Use digital twin technology to simulate the impact of different management strategies on production yields

**Human moat:**

- Ability to flexibly adjust operations in complex terrain or extreme weather
- Holistic management intuition and experience for multi-year tree growth cycles
- On-site coordination and negotiation with seasonal workers, cooperatives, and buyers.
- Emergency decision-making for handling unexpected events (e.g., frost, pest outbreaks)

**Skills to build:**

- Precision agriculture technologies (e.g., variable rate irrigation, drone operation and image analysis)
- Agricultural data analysis and basic machine learning applications
- Smart sensor network maintenance and troubleshooting
- Sustainable agriculture practices (carbon accounting, soil health management)
- Digital tool integration (farm management software, ERP systems)
- Knowledge of crop physiology and new breeding techniques

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- agroforestry
- berry production
- sustainable land management

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tree and shrub crop grower in Norway?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from 350,000 to 450,000 NOK per year, while experienced growers can earn 450,000 to 550,000 NOK. Self-employed incomes vary significantly.

**Q: Can non-EU/EEA nationals work as tree and shrub crop growers in Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker residence permit if they have a job offer and relevant vocational training or a trade certificate. The salary must meet the threshold (currently around 450,000 NOK). Seasonal work permits are also available for shorter periods.
