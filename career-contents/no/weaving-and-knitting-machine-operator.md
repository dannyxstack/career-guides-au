# Weaving and Knitting Machine Operator — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 8152 (ISCO08)**

Weaving and Knitting Machine Operators set up, operate, and monitor machines that weave or knit textile products such as fabrics, carpets, and garments. In Norway, the textile industry is relatively small and has faced long-term decline due to low-cost imports, but niche segments like technical textiles, sustainable fabrics, and specialty products offer limited opportunities. Employment is primarily in the southeastern region, with most workers in manufacturing plants. The occupation requires precision, mechanical aptitude, and the ability to detect defects. Automation and digitalization are gradually influencing job roles, emphasizing maintenance and quality control skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for Weaving and Knitting Machine Operators typically involves moving from operator to senior operator, shift supervisor, or production technician. Further vocational training (e.g., fagskole) in textile technology or industrial engineering can lead to roles in production planning, quality assurance, or sales. Some operators transition into machine maintenance or setup specialist positions. The path is often linear within the same company, and opportunities for advancement are limited without additional education. Apprenticeship completion (fagbrev) is advantageous for mobility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically detects fabric defects and adjusts tension
- Automatically switch patterns and parameters based on production orders
- Remote diagnosis and automatic repair prompts for machine faults
- Automatic recording of production data and generation of summary reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-Assisted Optimization of Weaving Process Parameters to Improve Yield and Quality
- Using digital twin simulations to predict equipment wear and maintenance cycles
- Predictive maintenance using smart sensors and AI
- Use machine learning to analyze historical data and improve production planning

**Human moat:**

- Manual diagnosis and on-site repair of complex faults.
- Development and trial production of new fabrics or patterns
- Flexible scheduling for small-batch production of multiple varieties
- Communication with designers and clients, and understanding of custom requirements

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of industrial robots
- Basic programming (PLC, Python, C#)
- Data analysis and visualization tools (Tableau, Power BI)
- Digital twin and simulation software (AnyLogic, Simul8)
- Debugging smart sensors and IoT devices
- Basic English or Italian to read technical documentation

**Key growth areas:**

- Technical Textiles
- Sustainable Textiles
- Automation
- Niche Production

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a Weaving and Knitting Machine Operator in Norway?**
A: The average annual salary is approximately 350,000–400,000 NOK pre-tax, though it varies by experience, location, and employer. Entry-level positions start lower, while senior operators or those in technical niches may earn up to 450,000 NOK.

**Q: Can non-EEA citizens get a work visa as a Weaving and Knitting Machine Operator?**
A: It is very difficult because the Skilled Worker Permit requires a salary above the threshold (currently about 449,900 NOK), which is rarely offered for this occupation. Additionally, the job must be considered skilled, which may not apply. EEA/EFTA citizens have free movement rights.

**Q: Is there a shortage of Weaving and Knitting Machine Operators in Norway?**
A: No. The occupation is not on any official shortage list (e.g., NAV's shortage list). The industry is small and declining, so there is ample supply of workers.
