# Environmental and Occupational Health and Hygiene Professional — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2263 (ISCO08)**

Environmental and occupational health and hygiene professionals in Norway identify, assess, and control environmental and workplace hazards to protect public health and worker safety. They work across industries, including oil and gas, construction, manufacturing, healthcare, and public administration, ensuring compliance with strict Norwegian HSE regulations. The role involves monitoring air quality, noise, chemical exposure, and ergonomic factors, as well as developing preventive measures. With Norway's strong emphasis on sustainable working environments and climate adaptation, these professionals are increasingly sought after by companies and municipalities to mitigate risks and promote health.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression often starts with a bachelor's degree in environmental health, occupational hygiene, or related fields, followed by practical experience as an HSE advisor. Senior roles include head of HSE department, environmental manager, or consultant. Many professionals pursue certification from the Norwegian Occupational Hygiene Association (Norsk Yrkeshygienisk Forening) to enhance credibility. Opportunities exist in multinational corporations, government agencies (e.g., Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority), and consulting firms. Advancement to management or specialised roles in climate risk assessment or industrial hygiene is common.

- Preliminary analysis of standardized occupational health screening questionnaires

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an environmental and occupational health professional in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level positions start around NOK 500,000–600,000 annually, while experienced professionals (5–10 years) earn between NOK 600,000 and 800,000. Senior roles or specialists can exceed NOK 900,000. Public sector salaries are often lower but come with good benefits.

**Q: How can a non-EEA national secure a job and visa as an environmental and occupational health professional in Norway?**
A: You need a job offer from a Norwegian employer. The employer typically sponsors your Skilled Worker permit. You must have a relevant bachelor's degree or higher and meet the salary threshold (currently around NOK 500,000). Alternatively, you can enter on a Jobseeker visa if you have a degree and sufficient funds, then look for work within 6 months.

**Q: What is the demand like for this profession in Norway?**
A: Demand is steady, with shortage in specialized areas like chemical safety and ergonomics. Many employers report difficulties finding qualified candidates. The occupation is on the NAV shortage list for some regions, making it attractive for skilled migration.
