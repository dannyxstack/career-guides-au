# Aged Care Services Manager — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 1343 (ISCO08)**

In Norway, aged care services managers oversee nursing homes, home care services, and other elderly care facilities, ensuring high-quality care, regulatory compliance, and efficient operations. With a rapidly aging population, demand is strong, particularly in municipalities with large elderly cohorts. Managers typically have a health professional background (e.g., nursing) and leadership training. The role combines administrative duties with person-centered care planning and staff management.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression often begins as a registered nurse or social educator in elderly care, then moves into team leader or department head roles, and finally to facility or service manager. Many complete a Master's in Health Management or similar. Further advancement includes regional director or municipal health administration roles. Continuous professional development and leadership courses are encouraged. For non-healthcare professionals, a relevant management degree combined with experience in elder care can be a pathway.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate shift schedules and personnel assignments
- Automated nursing documentation and review
- Budget and Resource Allocation Report Based on Data Analysis
- Compliance monitoring and automatic alarm system
- Standardized customer satisfaction and service quality statistics

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to predict occupancy and care needs, optimizing bed and staff allocation
- Use analytical tools to improve care quality monitoring and improvement decisions
- Conduct risk management and emergency drills through digital twin simulations
- AI-assisted personalized care plan creation and dynamic adjustment
- Automatically generate compliance reports, freeing up human resources to focus on strategic planning.

**Human moat:**

- Handling complex interpersonal interactions and empathetic communication such as sudden behaviors in dementia patients
- Lead teams and mediate conflicts between staff and families
- Making human judgments and assuming responsibility in ethical dilemmas
- Cross-departmental coordination (medical, government, family) negotiation skills.

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and visualization (e.g., Power BI, Tableau)
- Application of AI tools in nursing management (e.g., predictive modeling)
- Digital project management and remote collaboration tools
- Basic programming skills (Python for data cleaning)
- Ethical and compliance knowledge updates (data privacy regulations)
- Change management and digital training skills for employees

**Key growth areas:**

- Elder care digitalization
- Dementia care
- Home care coordination
- Leadership in welfare technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an aged care services manager in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by municipality and experience, but typically range from 600,000 to 800,000 NOK per year. Public sector managers have fixed pay scales; private sector may vary. The Norwegian Nurses Organisation (NSF) provides collective agreements.

**Q: Do I need to speak Norwegian to get a skilled worker visa?**
A: While not an explicit visa requirement, most employers require fluent Norwegian (B2 level or higher) for direct patient contact and team leadership. Some documentation and care coordination tasks also demand Norwegian proficiency.

**Q: Is a Norwegian nursing license mandatory to work as an aged care services manager?**
A: Yes, typically a background as a licensed health professional (nurse or social educator) is mandatory for clinical management. Non-clinical managers may be considered with relevant management degrees, but this is rare.
