# Engineering Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2149 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers a broad range of engineering specialties not listed elsewhere, such as biomedical, environmental, aerospace, and industrial engineers. In Norway, these professionals are highly sought after in the oil & gas, renewable energy, maritime, and technology sectors. The country's focus on green transition and digitalization drives demand for specialized engineering skills. EU/EEA nationals enjoy free movement, while non-EEA workers typically require a skilled worker residence permit with a job offer and salary threshold.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Many professionals pursue further education (e.g., master's or PhD) to specialize. Norwegian work culture emphasizes flat hierarchies, teamwork, and continuous learning. ## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI)](https://www.udi.no/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for engineering professionals in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by specialty, but experienced engineers typically earn between 600,000 and 800,000 NOK per year. Entry-level positions may start around 500,000 NOK. Senior roles can exceed 1,000,000 NOK.

**Q: What visa options do non-EEA engineering professionals have?**
A: The most common route is the Skilled Worker Residence Permit, requiring a job offer, relevant degree, and salary above the threshold (approx. 450,000 NOK). Alternatively, a Job Seeker Visa allows a six-month stay for those with a master's degree.

**Q: Are engineering professionals in shortage in Norway?**
A: Yes, there is a general shortage of engineers, particularly in renewable energy, automation, and digitalization. NAV lists many engineering roles as shortage occupations, making it easier for employers to sponsor work permits.
