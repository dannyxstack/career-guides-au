# Other Craft Workers — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

This group covers a diverse range of specialized craft and related occupations in Norway not classified elsewhere, such as musical instrument makers, glass artists, jewellery makers, and restorers. These workers typically combine traditional manual skills with creativity, and many are self-employed or work in small workshops. The Norwegian labour market for such crafts is niche but valued, particularly in sectors like cultural heritage, high-end manufacturing, and bespoke production. Employment opportunities are often tied to tourism, interior design, and restoration projects, with a strong emphasis on quality over quantity.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often involves specialisation or starting one's own business. Many artisans begin with a vocational apprenticeship (fagbrev) and later pursue master craftsman status (mesterbrev). Some transition into teaching, museum work, or custom manufacturing. Self-employment is common, offering flexibility but requiring entrepreneurial skills. Networking and a strong portfolio are key to advancement.

- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments

**Human moat:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for craft workers in this group in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by trade and experience, but annual pre-tax earnings typically range from 400,000 to 600,000 NOK for employed workers. Self-employed incomes fluctuate more and can be lower or higher depending on reputation and demand. The average for all craft workers is around 500,000 NOK, but this group is small and diverse.

**Q: Can a non-EU craftsperson get a visa to work in Norway?**
A: Yes, if they have a concrete job offer and a trade certificate (fagbrev) or equivalent qualification, they may apply for a Skilled Worker residence permit. The job must meet the salary threshold (currently ~500,000 NOK/year) and be in an occupation that requires formal skills. Self-employment is also possible but requires a different permit (self-employed person). EU/EEA nationals do not need a permit.

**Q: Are craft workers in this category in high demand in Norway?**
A: No, they are not on the official shortage list. Demand is moderate and niche, with more opportunities in heritage restoration, custom manufacturing, and tourism-related crafts. Employment is often project-based, and competition can be low due to specialized skill requirements.
