# Butcher, Fishmonger and Related Food Preparer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7511 (ISCO08)**

Butchers, fishmongers and related food preparers in Norway work in slaughterhouses, fish processing plants, retail butchery counters, and food manufacturing facilities. With a strong seafood industry and a tradition of meat production, Norway demands skilled cutters who can produce high-quality products for domestic and export markets. The role requires precision, physical stamina, and knowledge of food safety regulations. Many positions are in rural areas near fishing ports or agricultural regions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a two-to-four-year apprenticeship leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). From there, workers can advance to supervisory roles, specialise in organic or niche products, or move into management in processing plants. Some pursue further education in food technology or start their own businesses. The occupation offers a clear path from apprentice to skilled master.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch cutting and segmentation of standard parts
- Repetitive motions in deboning and skinning processes
- Basic processing like meat grinding and sausage stuffing
- Weighing, labeling, and packaging line operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI vision systems for meat grading and defect detection
- Optimizing cutting plans through data analysis to reduce waste
- Manage meat preservation and turnover using smart inventory systems
- Using augmented reality (AR) to assist training new employees in cutting techniques

**Human moat:**

- Hand-finishing special parts and custom cutting according to client requirements
- Sensory judgment of meat freshness, texture, and abnormalities
- Flexibility in handling unexpected orders and personalized needs.
- Provide cooking advice and demonstrate cutting techniques to customers

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of automated cutting equipment
- Food quality and safety management systems (e.g., HACCP)
- Basic data analysis and inventory management software
- Customer service and communication skills
- Fine hand cutting (for high-end customisation)
- Food safety regulations and traceability knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- artisan butchery
- seafood processing
- organic meat
- specialty cuts

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy hands-on, manual work and take pride in craftsmanship.
- Individuals comfortable working in cold, wet environments (e.g., refrigerated rooms, fish plants).
- Those who value food quality and safety, and are detail-oriented.

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike repetitive tasks or standing for long hours.
- Vegetarians, vegans, or those sensitive to handling raw meat or fish.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a butcher in Norway?**
A: Entry-level butchers earn around 350,000–400,000 NOK per year, while experienced skilled workers can earn 450,000–550,000 NOK. Wages vary by region and employer, with higher pay in industrial processing than in retail.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a butcher in Norway?**
A: Yes, but via the Skilled Worker Permit. You need a job offer and a completed vocational training (trade certificate) in butchery or a related field. The salary must meet the current threshold (approx. 449,900 NOK). Many employers sponsor permits if there is a documented shortage.

**Q: Do I need formal education to work as a butcher in Norway?**
A: While not legally required, most employers prefer a trade certificate (fagbrev). Apprenticeships are the common path. Without formal training, you may work as an unskilled assistant, but progression and higher wages are limited.
