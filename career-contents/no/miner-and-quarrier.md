# Miner and Quarry Worker — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers in Norway extract minerals, metals, and stone from underground mines and surface quarries. The industry is concentrated in regions like Finnmark, Nordland, and Trøndelag, with key resources including olivine, quartz, limestone, and metals like copper and zinc. Work often involves drilling, blasting, loading, and operating heavy machinery. Safety is paramount due to hazards like rock falls and dust. Employment is stable but niche, with most workers having vocational training. The sector is vital for domestic construction material and export minerals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an operator after completing a vocational qualification (fagbrev) in mining or quarrying. Experienced workers can become team leaders, shift supervisors, or safety delegates. Specialisation in drilling and blasting or heavy machinery operation offers higher pay. Some advance to mine planning or management with further education. Mobility between companies is common, and skilled workers may find opportunities in related industries like construction or civil engineering.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Operating mining equipment such as drills and excavators (automated remote control replaces manual driving)
- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)
- Daily equipment inspection and simple maintenance (predictive maintenance system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)
- Production scheduling and resource allocation (AI optimization algorithm)

**Human moat:**

- On-site assessment and handling of emergencies (e.g., collapses, gas leaks)
- Adaptive operation for abnormal geological conditions (unstructured environment)
- Team safety management and communication coordination
- Mining regulation compliance and government reporting responsibilities
- Complex repairs and equipment modifications (requiring physical hands-on skills)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated mining systems and remote-control equipment
- Data analysis and use of mining software (e.g., Surpac, Datamine)
- Skills in drone and sensor maintenance
- Basic programming and automation scripting (Python)
- Safety regulations and emergency response training
- Hazardous environment work skills (e.g., confined space entry)

**Key growth areas:**

- Mineral extraction
- Quarrying
- Blasting
- Safety

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for miners in Norway?**
A: Salaries are generally above the national average due to demanding conditions and shift work. Miners with a trade certificate typically earn between 480,000 and 600,000 NOK per year, with experienced workers and those in remote locations earning more.

**Q: Can a non-EU miner get a work visa for Norway?**
A: Yes, through the Skilled Worker permit. You need a job offer from a Norwegian employer, a completed vocational training or degree in mining or a related field, and a salary that meets the current threshold (around 500,000 NOK). The employer must show that the position cannot be filled locally or within the EEA.

**Q: Do I need a Norwegian certification to work as a miner?**
A: Yes, a trade certificate (fagbrev) in Mining and Quarrying is mandatory. Foreign qualifications may be assessed by the Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training. Additionally, a blasting certificate (sprengkort) is required if handling explosives.
