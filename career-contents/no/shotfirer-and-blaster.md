# Shotfirer and Blaster — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters in Norway detonate explosives in mining, quarrying, tunnelling, and construction projects, often in mountainous terrain. The occupation is regulated under the Norwegian Explosives Act, requiring certification from the Directorate for Civil Protection (DSB). With Norway's extensive infrastructure investments and active mining sector, demand is steady for skilled blasters, though strict safety and environmental regulations limit supply.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Entry is via vocational training (apprenticeship) leading to a trade certificate, then DSB certification. Experienced blasters can become specialists in underwater or large-scale blasting, supervisory roles, or safety inspectors. The workforce is aging, creating replacement demand. Mobility between construction and mining is common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Tunnel Construction
- Mining
- Infrastructure Projects
- Hydropower

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shotfirer in Norway?**
A: Typical annual salaries range from 500,000 to 700,000 NOK depending on experience, location, and sector (mining tends to pay higher). Entry-level apprentices earn a percentage of full salary during training.

**Q: Do non-EEA shotfirers need a visa to work in Norway?**
A: Yes, they require a Skilled Worker Residence Permit. You must have a job offer in writing, a completed vocational qualification (Fagbrev) or equivalent, and meet the salary threshold (currently about 500,000 NOK/year). The employer must also show the position could not be filled locally.

**Q: Is a Norwegian blasting license mandatory for foreigners?**
A: Yes, all shotfirers must hold a DSB blasting license (Sprengningssertifikat) to handle explosives. EEA qualifications may be recognised, but non-EEA certificates often require additional assessment or courses in Norway.
