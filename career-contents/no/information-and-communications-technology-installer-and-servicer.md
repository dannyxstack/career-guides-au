# ICT Installer and Servicer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7422 (ISCO08)**

Information and communications technology (ICT) installers and servicers in Norway set up, maintain, and repair a wide range of equipment including computers, network infrastructure, telecommunications systems, and broadband connections. They work across industries such as telecom, IT services, and the public sector, often on-site at businesses and homes. With Norway's high digital adoption and ongoing investments in fiber-optic and 5G networks, demand for these technicians is strong. The role typically requires vocational training or an apprenticeship, and many technicians are members of the Norwegian Labour and Welfare Administration (NAV) shortage list, making it a viable path for skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for ICT installers and servicers in Norway typically starts with vocational training (fagbrev) or an apprenticeship. After gaining experience, technicians can move into senior roles, team leadership, or specialized areas such as network security, cloud infrastructure, or optical fiber technology. Further education, such as a bachelor's degree in ICT engineering, opens doors to higher-level positions like project manager or systems consultant. Some technicians become independent contractors. Continuing professional development through vendor certifications (e.g., Cisco, CompTIA) is valued and can accelerate advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automate hardware troubleshooting steps using remote diagnostic scripts
- Automatically deploy and configure network devices using AI-driven configuration tools
- AI predicts hardware failures based on historical data and triggers preventive maintenance
- Using AI chatbots to handle common user installation/configuration issues
- Automated firmware upgrade and patch management processes

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted augmented reality (AR) tools to guide complex equipment installation and wiring
- AI-driven inventory and scheduling systems optimize field technician routes and parts management
- Analyze log data with AI to quickly identify network performance bottlenecks
- AI-powered knowledge base accelerates fault diagnosis and solution recommendations
- Reduce on-site risks by pre-simulating upgrade plans in AI simulation environments

**Human moat:**

- Physical operations: cabling in confined spaces, installing fiber optics, replacing hardware
- Client communication: explain technical issues, soothe emotions, gather vague requirements
- Non-standard faults: handling non-standard configurations, legacy system compatibility
- Safety responsibilities: ensure compliant installation (e.g., telecom regulations, grounding requirements).
- On-site decision-making: adjust plans dynamically based on environment (e.g., temporary cable routing detours)

**Skills to build:**

- AI/ML basics: using AI tools for log analysis, predictive maintenance
- Network certifications: advance to CCNA, CCNP, etc., master automated network configuration (SDN/Ansible)
- Augmented Reality (AR) maintenance: proficient in using AR devices like HoloLens for repair assistance
- Cybersecurity basics: ensure installations and configurations meet security baselines
- Cloud computing and virtualisation: familiarity with remote management platforms (e.g. VMware, AWS Outposts)
- Project Management and Customer Service: Enhancing End-to-End Delivery and Communication Skills

**Key growth areas:**

- 5G deployment
- fiber optics
- smart home
- cybersecurity infrastructure

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EEA country, Norway grants skilled-worker residence permits to qualified professionals who hold a concrete job offer meeting local pay and qualification rules; long-term residence can follow. Requirements are set nationally and change over time.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Practical, hands-on individuals who enjoy working with technology and solving problems on-site
- People who thrive in varied work environments, from offices to homes and construction sites
- Tech-savvy workers who are comfortable with continuous learning as technology evolves

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer desk-based, routine tasks or have limited patience for troubleshooting
- Individuals uncomfortable with physical work, including climbing ladders or working in confined spaces

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an ICT installer and servicer in Norway?**
A: Salaries are competitive and meet the skilled worker threshold. Exact figures vary by experience, region, and employer; consult Statistics Norway (SSB) for the latest averages.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as an ICT installer and servicer in Norway?**
A: EEA/EFTA nationals have free movement. Non-EEA nationals need a Skilled Worker Residence Permit, requiring a job offer, completed vocational training or degree, and a salary above the threshold. A 6-month Jobseeker Permit is also available for qualified candidates.

**Q: Is this occupation on the shortage list in Norway?**
A: Yes, ICT installers and servicers appear on NAV's shortage lists, indicating strong demand across many regions.
