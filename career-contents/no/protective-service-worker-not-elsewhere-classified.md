# Protective Services Worker Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective Services Workers Not Elsewhere Classified in Norway encompass a diverse group of professionals including security guards, fire inspectors, bailiffs, traffic wardens, and private investigators. They work in various settings such as public buildings, private companies, airports, and government institutions. The role requires vigilance, physical fitness, and often shift work. Licensing requirements vary by specialty, with security guards needing a police-issued security certificate. This occupation is sensitive to economic cycles and security concerns, with steady demand from both public and private sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with entry-level roles such as security guard or traffic warden. With experience and additional certifications (e.g., fire safety, first aid, security management), workers can advance to supervisor, site manager, or specialist roles like K9 handler or loss prevention. Further education in security management or criminal justice can lead to positions in the police or private investigation. However, upward mobility is limited without formal qualifications, and many workers remain in operational roles due to the hierarchical nature of the field.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a protective services worker in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely by role and sector. Entry-level security guards often earn below 400,000 NOK, while experienced fire inspectors or prison officers can earn over 500,000 NOK. Specialization and overtime can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a protective services worker in Norway?**
A: Typically only EEA nationals due to low salary thresholds and licensing requirements. Non-EEA nationals may qualify for specialized roles (e.g., airport security) if they meet the skilled worker permit conditions, including a job offer above the salary threshold.
