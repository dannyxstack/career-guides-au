# Mixed Crop and Animal Producer — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 6130 (ISCO08)**

Mixed crop and animal producers in Norway manage farms that integrate both crop cultivation and livestock production, contributing to the country's goal of self-sufficiency in food. Norwegian agriculture is characterized by small-scale, family-run farms, often located in challenging climatic and topographical conditions. Producers must navigate a heavily regulated sector with subsidies, quotas, and strict animal welfare standards. The role requires practical skills in animal husbandry, crop management, and machinery operation, as well as business acumen to manage finances and comply with environmental regulations. Organic farming and sustainable practices are growing trends, influencing production methods.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or apprentice, advancing to farm manager or owner. Many producers undergo formal vocational training (agri-opplæring) leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Further education, such as a bachelor's in agricultural science, can open paths to advisory roles or specialized farming. Diversification into agro-entrepreneurship, including farm shops, tourism, or renewable energy production, is common for increasing income. Some producers transition to roles in agricultural policy, education, or technology development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated collection and report generation of crop growth data
- Irrigation and fertilisation scheduling based on sensors and satellite imagery
- Basic financial records and inventory management
- Path planning for automated farm machinery (e.g., autonomous tractors)
- Automatic tracking and alerts for weather forecasts and market prices

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyse soil, meteorological, and historical data to optimise crop rotation and planting decisions
- Using drones and computer vision to quickly detect pests and diseases and apply precise treatments
- Financial forecasting for farm expansion or transition using AI simulation tools
- Using intelligent scheduling systems to optimize labor, equipment, and transportation arrangements
- Automatically generate compliance reports and subsidy applications through natural language processing.

**Human moat:**

- Dynamic response capabilities for handling sudden natural disasters (e.g., drought, floods, fires)
- Managing interpersonal relationships in coordinating cross-cultural temporary labor (e.g., backpackers, migrants)
- Subtle judgments based on local experience and intuition (e.g., irrigation timing adjustments)
- Build long-term trust with supply chain partners
- On-site oversight of animal welfare and ethical standards

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and visualization (Python/R, Power BI)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming (automation scripts, API integration)
- Project management and digital transformation management
- Agricultural economics and risk assessment

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Farm diversification
- Sustainable agriculture
- Agricultural technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mixed crop and animal producer in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on farm size, ownership, and profitability. According to Statistics Norway, average annual earnings for agricultural producers range from 400,000 to 600,000 NOK, but many are self-employed with variable income. For exact figures, refer to SSB's earnings tables.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a mixed crop and animal producer in Norway under the Skilled Worker permit?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer from a Norwegian farm with a contract meeting the salary threshold (currently 500,000 NOK/year) and hold a relevant degree or trade certificate. However, the permit is for employees; self-employed farmers must apply under different rules. Practical farming experience alone is not sufficient.

**Q: Is there a shortage of mixed crop and animal producers in Norway?**
A: Currently, this occupation is not listed on Norway's shortage list (NAV's 'mangel på arbeidskraft'). The sector is stable with a gradual decline in overall employment, though niche areas like organic farming may have unmet demand.
