# Garment and Related Patternmakers and Cutters — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 7532 (ISCO08)**

This occupation involves creating patterns and cutting fabrics for garments, primarily in Norway's small but specialized textile and fashion industry. Employment is concentrated in high-end fashion, workwear, and traditional bunad production, with hubs in Oslo and a few regional clusters. Demand has been stable to declining due to competition from offshore production and automation, but niche markets and replacement needs offer limited opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev). Experienced workers may advance to supervisor, product developer, or start their own workshop. Some transition into fashion design or teaching. Specialization in areas like costume design or industrial cutting can enhance mobility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- High-end fashion
- Workwear
- Costume design
- Textile recycling

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garment patternmaker or cutter in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary depending on experience, sector, and region, but are generally below the national average for skilled trades. Entry-level positions may start around 350,000–400,000 NOK annually, while experienced workers in specialised niches can earn 450,000–500,000 NOK.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national work as a garment patternmaker in Norway?**
A: Non-EU/EEA nationals can apply for a Skilled Worker residence permit if they have a job offer, a recognised trade certificate, and a salary meeting the threshold (about 450,000 NOK). However, the occupation is not on any shortage list, and typical salaries may be below the threshold, making it difficult unless the employer offers a high wage.

**Q: What are the main employers for this occupation in Norway?**
A: Key employers include small and medium-sized garment manufacturers, bespoke tailoring shops, workwear and protective clothing companies, and costume rental houses for theatre and film. A few larger firms exist in the sportswear and outdoor apparel sector.
