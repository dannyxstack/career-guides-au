# Philosopher, Historian and Political Scientist — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2633 (ISCO08)**

Philosophers, historians and political scientists in Norway are primarily employed in academia, research institutes, think tanks, and government agencies. They analyze societal issues, policy, and historical contexts, often contributing to public debate and decision-making. The labor market for these specialists is small but stable, with opportunities concentrated in universities, the public sector, and some private research organizations. Competition is strong, especially for permanent academic positions, and most roles require a master's or doctoral degree. Non-Norwegian speakers may face language barriers in policy-oriented roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically follows an academic path: from PhD to postdoctoral fellow, to associate professor, and eventually full professor. In government, progression involves moving into senior advisory or leadership roles. Some professionals transition to private sector consultancy or media. Norwegian language proficiency is crucial for many non-academic policy roles. Networking and publications are key for advancement.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a political scientist in Norway?**
A: Standard annual salary for a political scientist in research or government is around 600,000–700,000 NOK. Professors earn 800,000–1,000,000 NOK. Salaries vary by sector and experience.

**Q: Can a non-EU philosopher get a Skilled Worker visa?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer as a philosopher or researcher, a completed master's degree, and salary meets the threshold (508,800 NOK in 2025). Academic positions often qualify.

**Q: Is this occupation on Norway's shortage list?**
A: No, philosophers, historians and political scientists are not on any national shortage list. The job market is competitive and not considered in short supply.
