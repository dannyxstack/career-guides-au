# Pawnbroker and Money-lender — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 4213 (ISCO08)**

Pawnbrokers and money-lenders in Norway operate under strict regulation by the Financial Supervisory Authority (Finanstilsynet). They provide secured loans against personal property or small unsecured loans. The sector is small but stable, with most businesses concentrated in urban areas. Licensing requires a clean criminal record and approval from the authorities. The role involves asset valuation, customer service, and compliance with anti-money laundering rules.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from junior appraiser to senior pawnbroker or loan officer. With experience, one may open an independent pawnshop or move into related fields like asset management or compliance. Further education in finance or law can open doors to supervisory roles. The field is small, so advancement opportunities are limited without entrepreneurship.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Collateral initial valuation: AI image recognition and market data comparison can replace human appraisers.
- Loan application data entry and compliance checks: automated systems handle standard forms
- Customer credit scoring: algorithm automatically generates scores based on historical data
- Periodic report generation: AI extracts data from transaction systems to produce reports
- Standardized contract generation: auto-fill template documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- High-Risk Loan Decisions: AI provides real-time risk models to assist manual judgment
- Customer Insight & Cross-Selling: AI analyzes transaction behavior to recommend loans or pawn products
- Fraud detection: Machine learning identifies abnormal patterns, enhancing manual verification efficiency
- Inventory management: AI predicts item turnover rate, optimizing capital allocation
- Compliance monitoring: NLP scanning for regulatory updates, automatic policy change alerts

**Human moat:**

- Complex/non-standard item identification (e.g., antiques, artwork) relies on human professional experience
- Client trust and negotiation: human touch in handling sensitive financial situations.
- Local regulations and moral judgment: grey-area decisions cannot be fully algorithmized
- Humane and flexible handling in emergency/special situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Data Analysis (Python/R) and Visualization (Tableau)
- Operation of fintech tools (e.g. automated valuation platforms, CRM systems)
- Risk management and compliance knowledge (local financial regulatory framework)
- Digital communication and customer relationship management (virtual channel services)
- Continuous learning ability to track AI tool advancements

**Key growth areas:**

- second-hand goods
- online lending
- asset valuation
- regulatory compliance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a pawnbroker in Norway?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location but are generally moderate. Entry-level positions may start around 350,000–400,000 NOK annually, while experienced pawnbrokers can earn 450,000–550,000 NOK. Owners may earn more based on business performance.

**Q: Can a non-EEA national work as a pawnbroker in Norway?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer and a skilled worker residence permit. The position must meet skill requirements, typically needing a relevant degree or vocational training. Given the low skill level, it may be challenging to secure a permit unless the role involves higher-level finance tasks.
