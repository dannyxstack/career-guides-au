# Health Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Norway

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

Health professionals not elsewhere classified encompass a diverse group of specialized practitioners such as audiologists, dietitians, occupational therapists, physiotherapists, and speech therapists. In Norway, these professionals are integral to the healthcare system, addressing specific therapeutic, rehabilitative, and preventive needs. They typically work in hospitals, clinics, municipal health services, or private practice. As part of the broader healthcare sector, they are subject to Norwegian regulations, including mandatory authorization for clinical work. The demand for these roles is consistently high, driven by an aging population, increasing focus on mental health, and advancements in rehabilitation and digital health solutions. Proficiency in Norwegian is essential for effective communication with patients and for meeting licensing requirements.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for health professionals not elsewhere classified in Norway?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by profession, experience, and region. Therapists and other specialists typically earn between the national median and higher percentiles, with municipal positions offering lower starting pay but good benefits. Private practice can yield higher income but with less stability. Exact figures are not provided here, but the job market generally offers good compensation.

**Q: Can I work as a physiotherapist in Norway with a non-EU degree?**
A: Yes, but you must obtain authorization from the Norwegian Directorate of Health. This involves having your education assessed for equivalency, completing any missing courses, passing a language test (B2 Norwegian), and completing a period of supervised practice. Once authorized, you can apply for a Skilled Worker residence permit if you have a job offer.

**Q: Do I need a master's degree to work as a health professional in Norway?**
A: Not necessarily. Many professions, such as physiotherapy and occupational therapy, require only a bachelor's degree for entry. However, a master's degree can open doors to advanced clinical roles, teaching, or research. Requirements vary by profession, so check with the Norwegian Directorate of Health for specific education standards.
