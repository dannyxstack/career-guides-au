# Security Guard — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 5414 (ISCO08)**

Security guards in Finland are responsible for monitoring and protecting property, assets, and people in various settings such as shopping centers, airports, office buildings, and events. They control access, patrol premises, respond to alarms, and handle incidents. The profession is regulated by the Finnish Police, requiring a license and background check. Guards often work shifts, including nights and weekends. The role is essential for public safety and crime prevention, with increasing demand due to security concerns and the growth of private security services.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for security guards in Finland typically involves moving into supervisory or management positions, such as shift supervisor or security manager. Specialization in areas like airport security, close protection, or technical security systems can lead to higher pay and responsibility. Additional vocational qualifications (e.g., 'Turvallisuusalan ammattitutkinto') open doors to advanced roles. The field values experience and continuous training, with opportunities in both private security firms and in-house security departments.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically detect intrusions and abnormal behavior via video analysis, replacing long-term human monitoring.
- Automatic access control system (facial recognition/QR code) replaces manual identity verification and registration
- Robots patrol electronically along preset routes, replacing some manual patrols.
- Automatically generate patrol logs and incident reports, replacing manual records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted analysis of surveillance video, quick event playback, key frame identification, improving investigation efficiency
- Smart scheduling system optimizes patrol routes based on real-time risk, enhancing manual patrol accuracy
- AI voice assistants help handle visitor inquiries, improving service response speed and professionalism.
- Receive AI alerts on mobile terminals, guiding security personnel to prioritize high-risk areas and improve emergency response.

**Human moat:**

- Handling physical conflicts, violent incidents, and other high-risk interventions requires human judgment and physical strength
- Situational understanding and flexible communication for suspicious behavior (e.g., dispute mediation)
- Humanized reassurance and emotional interaction, such as calming public sentiment.
- Legal compliance and ethical judgment, such as reasonable use of force boundaries.

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart security systems (video analysis, access control, alarm platforms)
- Fundamental data analysis and report interpretation (e.g., event statistics, risk heat maps)
- Human-machine collaboration communication skills (coordinating with AI scheduling and robots)
- Emergency Management and Crisis Intervention (Conflict Resolution, First Aid)
- Service Awareness in Digital Transformation (Visitor Self-Service System Guidance)
- Continuous learning and tool adaptability

**Key growth areas:**

- event security
- corporate security
- airport security
- surveillance technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a security guard in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by employer, shift, and experience, but the median gross monthly salary is around €2,400–€2,600, with extra pay for nights and weekends.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a residence permit as a security guard in Finland?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You need a job offer from a Finnish employer and the salary must meet the collective agreement threshold. The employer must also prove that no suitable EU/EEA worker is available.

**Q: Is experience in security required to start training?**
A: No, basic training courses accept beginners. However, clean criminal record and good physical condition are essential.
