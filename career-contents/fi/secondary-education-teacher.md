# Secondary Education Teacher — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2330 (ISCO08)**

Secondary education teachers in Finland work in upper secondary general schools (lukio) and vocational schools (ammatillinen), teaching students aged 16-19. They are required to hold a master's degree in their subject area along with pedagogical studies. The profession is highly respected, with good working conditions and moderate salaries. Demand is steady due to retirements, with specific shortages in mathematics, science, languages, and special education. Language proficiency in Finnish or Swedish is essential, and teachers typically enjoy summer breaks and professional autonomy.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 24,007. The employment outlook for secondary education teachers in Finland is positive overall, with an aging workforce leading to replacement needs. The government has initiatives to improve teacher retention and attract new teachers to shortage subjects. However, budget constraints in municipalities can affect hiring. For well-qualified teachers in STEM or language subjects, opportunities are good. The sector also benefits from Finland's focus on high-quality education.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression for secondary teachers includes advancement to senior teacher, head of department, or principal roles. Many pursue further education in educational leadership or specialized pedagogy. Teachers also move into curriculum development, teacher training, or administrative positions within education boards. Continuous professional development is encouraged and often mandatory.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated grading of standardized assignments and multiple-choice questions
- Generate basic lesson plans and course draft materials
- handling student attendance and grade records
- Provide standardized FAQ services

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Analyze student learning data with AI to achieve personalized teaching
- Using AI to generate differentiated practice problems and extension materials
- Enhance classroom interaction and lab simulation with AI virtual assistants
- Using natural language processing tools to quickly search teaching materials
- Use AI to monitor student participation and adjust teaching strategies in a timely manner.

**Human moat:**

- Classroom management and student behavior guidance
- Emotional support and psychological counseling
- Complex teaching decisions and curriculum design
- In-depth communication with parents and the community
- Cultivate students' critical thinking and creativity

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and learning situation diagnosis.
- AI teaching tool applications (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning)
- Digital content creation and multimedia teaching
- Programming and STEM education basics
- Online teaching and blended learning design
- AI ethics and data privacy protection

**Key growth areas:**

- Multilingual education
- Digital pedagogy
- Special needs education
- STEM education

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a secondary education teacher in Finland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and depend on experience, qualifications, and municipality. Generally, starting salaries are moderate but increase with years of service and additional responsibilities.

**Q: Can non-EU teachers get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, non-EU teachers can apply for a Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) after securing a job offer. The employer must demonstrate that the position cannot be filled locally. Language proficiency in Finnish or Swedish is typically required.

**Q: Do I need to speak Finnish to teach in Finland?**
A: Yes, proficiency in Finnish or Swedish is mandatory for teaching positions, as classes are taught in these languages. Some international schools may use English, but these are limited.
