# Floor Layer and Tile Setter — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7122 (ISCO08)**

Floor layers and tile setters install, repair, and maintain floor coverings such as tiles, hardwood, laminate, carpet, and linoleum in residential, commercial, and industrial settings. In Finland, the construction industry drives steady demand, particularly in renovation and new building projects. The occupation requires precision, physical stamina, and knowledge of materials and adhesives. Workers often specialize in either tiles (laatoittaja) or other floor coverings (lattianpäällystäjä), but many combine both skills. The role is hands-on and typically involves working indoors on construction sites or in existing buildings.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,890. Finland's construction sector faces cyclical fluctuations, but long-term demand for floor layers and tile setters remains strong due to aging building stock requiring renovation and a growing focus on sustainable materials. The labour market for this trade is tight, especially in the Helsinki-Uusimaa region and other growth centres. Skilled workers are often hired through subcontractors or own businesses. While automation and new materials may change some tasks, manual expertise remains essential. The outlook is positive for experienced professionals, with moderate job growth expected over the next decade.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational qualification (ammattitutkinto) and on-the-job training. Experienced floor layers can advance to master craftsperson (mestari) status, become site supervisors, or start their own contracting business. Specialization in high-end finishes, waterproofing, or eco-friendly materials can boost earnings. Continuing education through short courses (e.g., on new adhesive technologies or underfloor heating systems) is common. The trade offers stable employment, though income varies with project work. Many workers are members of the Trade Union for Construction Workers (Rakennusliitto).

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic measurement of room dimensions and material quantity calculation, AI can quickly generate optimal layout plans
- Automatic cutting of tiles/stone, CNC waterjet or laser cutting replaces manual cutting
- Automatic detection and adjustment solution generation for ground flatness, reducing manual trial laying
- Inventory management and material ordering processes, AI analyzes project requirements to automatically place orders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to view tiling blueprints and level indicators in real time, improving accuracy
- AI-assisted design of complex patterns and parquet, generating diverse options for clients
- Work collaboratively with automatic mortar mixing and plastering robots to improve efficiency
- AI-driven project progress prediction and scheduling to optimize multi-trade collaboration

**Human moat:**

- Manual cutting and fine placement in irregular areas and corners
- Removal of old flooring, base treatment, and leveling judgment for uneven floors.
- Communicate design preferences with clients and provide personalized advice
- Flexible problem-solving ability for on-site unexpected issues.

**Skills to build:**

- Digital Measurement Tools and BIM Software Operations
- CNC cutting machine programming and maintenance
- Use of augmented reality (AR) tools for construction assistance.
- Project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and environmentally friendly construction methods
- Basic electrical and mechanical troubleshooting

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Green building
- Waterproofing
- Smart flooring

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a floor layer/tile setter in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. A typical monthly gross salary for a skilled floor layer is around €2,500–€3,500. Piecework or own-account work can yield higher earnings. The collective agreement sets minimums, e.g., around €1,800–€2,000 for entry-level.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen easily get a work permit as a floor layer in Finland?**
A: Yes, if there is a labour shortage. The occupation is on the shortage list, and employers can sponsor a Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL). The process requires a job offer, salary meeting thresholds, and a positive labour availability assessment. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: Do I need a license to work as a floor layer in Finland?**
A: No formal license, but a vocational qualification is highly recommended and often required by employers. Certain tasks like hot work require a permit (tulityökortti). Most sites also require an occupational safety card.
