# Buyer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 3323 (ISCO08)**

Buyers in Finland, classified under ISCO-08 3323, are responsible for purchasing goods and services for organizations across industries such as manufacturing, retail, construction, and the public sector. The role requires strong negotiation skills, market knowledge, and an understanding of supply chain management. Finland's stable economy and export-oriented industries, particularly in technology and forest products, create steady demand for buyers who can source cost-effectively and sustainably. The profession is evolving with digital procurement tools and a growing emphasis on ethical sourcing and circular economy principles, aligning with Finland's sustainability goals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for buyers in Finland typically follows a path from junior buyer to senior buyer, procurement manager, and eventually to head of procurement or supply chain director. Many professionals gain experience in specific industries such as technology, retail, or public procurement. Advanced roles require expertise in contract law, international trade, and strategic sourcing. Finnish employers value language skills (Finnish and Swedish often necessary), and a master's degree in business or supply chain management can accelerate advancement. Cross-functional moves into logistics or operations are common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically Analyze Public Property Data, Generate Market Reports and Comparable Sales Analysis
- Automatically filtering property listings that match client criteria.
- Basic clerical work, such as contract drafting and document filing
- Initial customer inquiries and communication via chatbots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI tools for in-depth data analysis to quickly identify investment opportunities and market trends
- Using AI to generate personalized property valuation models and investment reports
- Enhance client communication efficiency and follow-up automation through CRM systems and AI chatbots
- Virtual property tours and remote assessments to expand service coverage

**Human moat:**

- Build long-term client trust and reputation through relationship-based referral business
- Complex negotiation strategies and psychological tactics, especially in multi-party bidding or difficult deals
- On-site property inspection and detail insights (structure, community factors)
- Experience-based judgment for non-standard properties (such as inherited properties, auction properties)

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and visualisation (Python, Tableau, Power BI)
- AI tool applications (AI-driven valuation models, chatbots)
- Customer relationship management (CRM systems and automated marketing)
- Advanced negotiation skills and conflict resolution
- Legal and compliance knowledge (state conveyancing regulations)
- Digital marketing and social media customer acquisition skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable sourcing
- Digital procurement
- Supply chain resilience
- Strategic sourcing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a buyer in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, industry, and location, but the range is generally between €35,000 and €50,000 per year for mid-level positions. Senior buyers and managers can earn significantly more.

**Q: Is there a salary requirement for the residence permit as a buyer?**
A: Yes, for the Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL), the salary must meet the minimum threshold set by the Finnish government, which is updated annually. As of 2024, this is approximately €40,000 per year for general applicants. The EU Blue Card requires a higher threshold, typically around €60,000 per year.

**Q: Do I need to speak Finnish to work as a buyer in Finland?**
A: For many positions, especially in domestic companies or public procurement, Finnish language skills are essential. However, international companies and roles focused on English-speaking markets may accept English-only. Swedish may also be required in some regions.
