# Protective Services Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective services workers in Finland, including security guards, patrol officers, and surveillance staff, ensure safety and order in various settings such as shopping malls, corporate offices, and public events. This occupation falls under ISCO-08 category 5419 and covers roles not elsewhere classified in protective services. In Finland, the sector employs thousands, with major employers including private security companies, municipalities, and transport hubs. Demand is steady, driven by security needs in retail, infrastructure, and events.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for protective services workers in Finland typically starts with a security guard license and basic vocational training. With experience, one can become a supervisor, shift manager, or security manager in larger companies. Specializations like event security or airport security may require additional certifications. Some transition to roles in private investigation, cybersecurity, or public law enforcement. Further education, such as a bachelor's in security management, opens doors to higher-level positions in safety planning and consultancy.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI video surveillance automatically detects abnormal events and alerts
- Automated visitor registration and identity verification systems replace manual registration
- AI-driven patrol robots replacing fixed-route patrols

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI video analysis assisting real-time threat assessment and decision-making
- AI report generation tools quickly summarize event logs
- AI voice assistant supports emergency response communication and coordination
- AI training simulators improve exercise effectiveness

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency judgment and crisis intervention ability
- Interpersonal communication and persuasion with clients, visitors, and law enforcement
- Legal compliance and licensing liability (e.g., use-of-force boundaries)
- Risk assessment intuition in complex physical environments

**Skills to build:**

- AI monitoring system operation and data analysis
- Basic cybersecurity knowledge (integration of physical security and IT)
- Emergency communication and coordination software (e.g., Duress system).
- Advanced training in conflict resolution and counter-terrorism awareness.
- Data privacy regulations and compliance (Australian Privacy Act)
- Digital reporting and visualization tools (e.g., Tableau)

**Key growth areas:**

- Security technology
- Event security
- Corporate security
- Airport security

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for protective services workers in Finland?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by experience, employer, and sector. They generally reflect the local labour market standards for protective services.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a protective services worker in Finland?**
A: Yes, with a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). However, the occupation is not on the shortage list, so the job offer must meet the normal permit requirements, including a valid reason for hiring from outside the EU/EEA.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for protective services workers?**
A: With experience and additional training, one can progress to supervisor roles, security manager, or specialize in areas like event security or emergency response. Further education in security management can lead to higher-level positions.
