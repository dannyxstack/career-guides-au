# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers in Finland perform manual tasks on farms raising cattle, pigs, sheep, poultry, or other animals. Duties include feeding, watering, cleaning animal housing, assisting with births, and basic health monitoring. Most work is seasonal or on family farms, with a significant share of foreign seasonal workers. The sector is concentrated in rural areas like Pohjanmaa, Satakunta, and Itä-Suomi. Labour demand fluctuates with agricultural cycles and EU subsidies.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 22. Employment outlook for livestock farm labourers is stable but declining slightly due to farm consolidation and automation. The number of active farms shrinks by about 2% annually, but large farms still need manual labour for tasks robots cannot fully replace. Seasonal peaks (spring calving, autumn housing) create temporary shortages. Climate change may affect pasture seasons. Overall, demand is low and competitive for non-EU workers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically limited; labourers may advance to herd manager or farm supervisor after several years, especially on large dairy or pig farms. Further education (agriculture vocational degree) can lead to farm entrepreneurship or agricultural technician roles. Digital skills (robot milking systems, herd management software) are increasingly valued. The path is stable but slow, with most remaining as general labourers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic health data entry and case organization
- Translation and localisation of standard health education materials
- Appointment management and follow-up reminder calls
- Basic chronic disease knowledge Q&A (e.g., diabetes diet)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze community health data to identify high-risk groups and trends
- Use generative AI to create multilingual, culturally adapted educational videos and diagrams
- Preliminary health screening and triage via AI voice assistants
- Use AI-enabled remote monitoring tools to track chronic patient conditions
- AI-assisted report writing and policy proposal generation

**Human moat:**

- Cultural safety practices: deep understanding of Indigenous customs, history, and trust building
- Community Relationship Maintenance: Face-to-Face Emotional Support and Crisis Intervention
- Cross-cultural medical translation and advocacy: accurately convey medical information while preserving patient dignity
- Complex Ethical Decisions: Balancing Traditional and Modern Medicine
- On-site visits and home care: hands-on work that cannot be replaced by a screen

**Skills to build:**

- Master community health data analysis tools (e.g., Power BI, Tableau)
- Learn to create AI-driven health education content (e.g., Canva, Synthesia)
- Familiarity with telehealth platforms and electronic health record systems
- Basic natural language processing knowledge: for evaluating AI translation quality
- Trauma-informed care and mental health first aid certification
- Project management skills: coordinating multiple partners

**Key growth areas:**

- Animal welfare specialization
- Robotic milking systems
- Organic livestock
- Precision feeding

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Physically fit individuals comfortable with outdoor work in all weather
- People with animal-handling experience and willingness to work early mornings/weekends
- Rural-minded workers open to living in remote areas on or near the farm

**Who is not suited**
- Urban-oriented people seeking office or structured 9-to-5 schedules
- Those averse to manual labour, odours, or working with large animals

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Finland?**
A: Salaries range from about 1,500 to 2,000 EUR per month before tax, depending on experience, farm size, and region. Overtime and accommodation benefits may be provided.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa as a livestock farm labourer?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The job must meet the income threshold (≈1,300 EUR/month in 2025) and the employer must prove no suitable EU/EEA candidate exists. Seasonal work (D-visa) possible for short stays, but permanent residence is difficult.

**Q: Is livestock farm labour on Finland's shortage list?**
A: No, it is not on the current shortage list (2025). The occupation is considered low-skilled, and recruitment preference goes to EU/EEA workers.
