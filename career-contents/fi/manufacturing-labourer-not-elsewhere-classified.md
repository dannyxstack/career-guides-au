# Manufacturing Labourer Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9329 (ISCO08)**

Manufacturing labourers perform simple manual tasks in factories and workshops, such as moving materials, cleaning work areas, packing products, and assisting machine operators. In Finland, these roles are common in sectors like electronics, metal, food, and wood processing. The labour market relies partly on temporary agency workers and EU/EEA migrants, especially for seasonal peaks. Finnish language skills are not always required, but basic safety training is often mandatory. The work can be physically demanding and shift work is typical.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,881. The employment outlook for manufacturing labourers is stable but influenced by automation and offshoring. While some routine tasks are replaced by machines, demand remains for flexible, manual workers in assembly, packaging, and logistics support. The number of jobs is expected to decline slightly over the next decade, but turnover creates entry opportunities. Regional variation exists: growth in Southern Finland, slower in remote areas. Digitalisation may shift tasks rather than eliminate them.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with on-the-job training and can lead to roles such as machine operator, team leader, or quality inspector. Many workers gain vocational qualifications (e.g., in production technology) to move into more skilled positions. Finnish language proficiency and additional certificates (e.g., fork-lift licence, safety card) improve advancement prospects. Some workers transition into logistics or maintenance. The path is not automatic but achievable with initiative.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive material handling and palletizing
- Basic cleaning and tidying work
- Simple packaging and box sealing operation
- Manual quality visual inspection and counting
- fixed route material delivery

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use handheld terminals or AR glasses to receive digital instructions, improving accuracy
- Operate semi-automated equipment, such as collaborative robot-assisted assembly
- Monitor production line anomalies through data dashboards and report promptly
- Use AI-driven predictive maintenance tools to provide equipment status feedback
- Learn basic programming, adjust automation workstation parameters

**Human moat:**

- Flexible physical ability for handling unstructured scenarios (e.g., irregular workpiece handling)
- Ability to quickly adapt to task switching in high-mix, low-volume production
- Communication and adaptability to collaborate with colleagues on complex assembly
- Rapid diagnosis and manual intervention for on-site anomalies (e.g., equipment jams, quality deviations)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy: scanning, touchscreen operation, digital records
- Basic operation and safety protocols for collaborative robots.
- Simple sensor identification and alarm processing
- Lean production and 5S management
- Automated logistics system (AGV/conveyor) anomaly handling
- Basic English or technical document reading

**Key growth areas:**

- Logistics support
- Assembly line
- Packaging
- Material handling

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a manufacturing labourer in Finland?**
A: The typical annual gross salary ranges from €24,000 to €30,000, depending on experience, sector, and shift bonuses. Entry-level may start lower, around €22,000.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes, if the employer cannot find suitable labour within Finland or EU/EEA. The job must meet the minimum salary threshold (approx. €1,200/month) for a Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL). However, it is not on the shortage list, so processing may take longer and require labour market testing.

**Q: What are the career progression opportunities?**
A: With experience and training, workers can become machine operators, team leaders, or move into quality control. Finnish language skills and vocational qualifications (e.g., in production) greatly enhance advancement prospects.
