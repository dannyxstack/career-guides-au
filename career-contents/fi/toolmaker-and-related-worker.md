# Toolmaker and Related Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7222 (ISCO08)**

Toolmakers and related workers in Finland manufacture, repair, and maintain precision tools, dies, jigs, and fixtures used in metalworking and other industries. This occupation is part of the country's strong manufacturing sector, especially in machinery, electronics, and automotive subcontracting. Finnish toolmakers often work in small to medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) or industrial workshops, relying on both traditional machining skills and modern CNC programming. The field values manual dexterity, problem-solving, and familiarity with computer-aided manufacturing (CAM).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 4,956. Employment outlook for toolmakers is stable but modestly declining due to automation and offshoring. However, niche demand persists for high-precision work in electronics, medical devices, and mold making. The aging workforce in Finland's manufacturing sector creates replacement needs, but growth is limited. Prospects are best for those combining manual skills with digital competencies like CNC programming and 3D printing.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from vocational school or apprenticeship, advancing through journeyman to master craftsperson (mestari). Many toolmakers transition into specialist roles in CNC programming, quality assurance, or production management. With experience, self-employment as a contractor or workshop owner is possible. Continuous upskilling in digital fabrication is essential for staying relevant.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual CNC programming and tool path generation
- Simple repetitive machine operations (e.g., loading/unloading workpieces, starting machining cycles)
- Routine dimensional measurement and quality recording (manual measurement with calipers and micrometers)
- Paper-based drawing management and document archiving
- Basic troubleshooting (based on fixed diagnostic procedures)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted tool path optimization (automatic collision avoidance, improved machining efficiency)
- Machine learning-based online quality inspection (real-time prediction of dimensional deviations)
- Digital twin simulation of machining processes (pre-validate fixture designs)
- Smart production scheduling system integration (automatic assignment of machine tasks)
- Augmented reality-assisted assembly and debugging (overlaying virtual guidance)

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment in complex fixture design and debugging
- On-site diagnosis and repair of multi-process integration issues.
- Ability to communicate non-standard requirements with clients/engineers
- Safety responsibility and process innovation decisions
- Precision manual finishing and assembly touch

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX machining modules)
- Robot-assisted loading/unloading programming and integration
- Python or MATLAB for machining data analysis
- Basics of additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and smart tool management
- Quality control data visualization (e.g., Power BI)

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Machining
- CNC Programming
- Mold Making
- Additive Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a toolmaker in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, employer, and region. They are determined by collective agreements and typically increase with competence and responsibility. Experienced toolmakers can expect higher pay than entry-level workers.

**Q: Can a non-EU toolmaker get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, through the residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). You need a job offer where the salary meets the threshold set by the Finnish government. The employer must demonstrate that no suitable EU/EEA candidate is available.

**Q: Is toolmaker considered a shortage occupation in Finland?**
A: Yes, toolmakers appear on regional shortage lists, especially for precision machining and mold making. This may facilitate work-based immigration, but each application is assessed individually.
