# Ships’ Deck Officer and Pilot — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 3152 (ISCO08)**

Finland, with its extensive coastline and bustling ports, relies heavily on skilled ships' deck officers and pilots to navigate its maritime industry. These professionals are responsible for the safe operation of vessels, from cargo ships and passenger ferries to icebreakers in Arctic waters. The occupation demands strong leadership, navigational expertise, and adherence to international maritime regulations. Finland's maritime sector is a cornerstone of its economy, facing a generational shift as experienced officers retire, creating steady demand for new recruits. The role offers a dynamic career at sea, with opportunities for advancement and specialization in areas such as pilotage, where local knowledge of Finnish archipelagos is critical.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,328. Employment prospects for ships' deck officers and pilots in Finland are positive, driven by an aging workforce and sustained demand in shipping, offshore wind, and Arctic operations. The Finnish Transport and Communications Agency projects retirements will outpace new entrants, maintaining a shortage of qualified officers. Automation and digitalization are reshaping navigation but require upskilling, not replacement. Pilotage, state-regulated and vital for safe port access, remains stable. EU/EEA free movement ensures a steady flow of workers, but non-EU recruitment is limited by licensing and language requirements. Overall, the sector offers long-term career stability for those willing to meet the demands of seafaring.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a deck cadet, advancing to third, second, and chief officer, then captain. Many officers also become pilots after extensive local experience and licensing. Shore-side roles in maritime administration, port operations, or shipping company management are common later in careers. Specialization in ice navigation, offshore energy, or environmental compliance is growing. Continuous professional development through STCW refresher courses and higher education (e.g., maritime technology degree) enhances promotion prospects. The field values loyalty and sea time, with senior positions requiring 10–15 years of experience. Leadership skills and fluency in Finnish or Swedish boost employability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data integration and collision avoidance computation
- Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS) automatic route planning and updates
- Repetitive data collection and analysis in weather route optimization
- Automated recording and report generation of routine navigation logs
- Automatic interpretation of sensor data in ship performance monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Combine AI weather models and real-time ocean data to make more accurate route adjustment decisions
- Use digital twins and predictive maintenance systems to identify equipment issues early
- Optimizes workforce and fatigue management through AI-assisted crew scheduling systems
- Computer vision-based berthing and anchoring operation assistance
- AI-generated emergency scenario simulations for training and crisis decision-making rehearsals

**Human moat:**

- Take ultimate decision-making responsibility in emergencies (e.g., engine failure, man overboard)
- Statutory qualifications and experience required by international maritime regulations (SOLAS, STCW)
- Cross-cultural crew management and interpersonal communication (multi-national team collaboration on board)
- Manual control and situational judgment in complex port environments (e.g., tugboat coordination)
- Long-term experience (intuitive judgment) with hulls, machinery, and marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced configuration and troubleshooting of electronic charts and autopilot systems
- Basic data analysis (Python/R) for interpreting ship sensor data
- Digital twin and predictive maintenance software operation
- Maritime cybersecurity knowledge (to counter onboard system attacks)
- Advanced English Communication and Cross-Cultural Leadership
- Use of fatigue management and compliance software (e.g., rest time monitoring)

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Arctic shipping
- Automation
- Maritime digitalization

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a ships’ deck officer in Finland?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries range from about 50,000 EUR for junior officers to 80,000 EUR for experienced captains; pilots may earn 70,000–100,000 EUR depending on seniority.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as a deck officer in Finland?**
A: Yes, but they need a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL), a valid STCW certificate, and often Finnish language skills. The occupation is on the shortage list, which may facilitate the process, but licensing requirements are strict.

**Q: Is there a shortage of ships’ deck officers in Finland?**
A: Yes, the sector faces a notable shortage, especially of experienced officers and pilots, due to an aging workforce and high retirement rates. The Maritime Administration has identified this as a recruitment challenge, with good long-term prospects for new entrants.
