# Miner and Quarrier — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers extract minerals, ores, stone, and other solid materials from open-pit or underground mines and quarries. In Finland, this occupation is concentrated in northern and eastern regions, where mining operations focus on metals like copper, nickel, gold, and zinc, as well as industrial minerals and aggregates. The sector employs about 7,000 workers, with major companies like Outokumpu, Boliden, and Agnico Eagle. Work involves drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials, requiring physical stamina and adherence to strict safety protocols. The industry is capital-intensive and subject to commodity price cycles, but Finland's strong mining regulation and technological advancement create a stable, though niche, labour market.

Employment is projected to decline slightly over the next decade due to productivity gains, while skilled workers remain needed for specialized tasks. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a trainee or assistant, advancing to machine operator, driller, or blaster after gaining experience and certifications. With further training, workers can become shift supervisors, mining engineers, or safety inspectors. Many workers obtain a vocational qualification in mining or quarrying, which can lead to specialized roles in rock mechanics, environmental management, or automation. Mobility between mines is possible, but limited diversity in employers means advancement often requires relocation to remote areas. Some workers transition to related fields like civil engineering or construction.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Operating mining equipment such as drills and excavators (automated remote control replaces manual driving)
- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)
- Daily equipment inspection and simple maintenance (predictive maintenance system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)
- Production scheduling and resource allocation (AI optimization algorithm)

**Human moat:**

- On-site assessment and handling of emergencies (e.g., collapses, gas leaks)
- Adaptive operation for abnormal geological conditions (unstructured environment)
- Team safety management and communication coordination
- Mining regulation compliance and government reporting responsibilities
- Complex repairs and equipment modifications (requiring physical hands-on skills)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated mining systems and remote-control equipment
- Data analysis and use of mining software (e.g., Surpac, Datamine)
- Skills in drone and sensor maintenance
- Basic programming and automation scripting (Python)
- Safety regulations and emergency response training
- Hazardous environment work skills (e.g., confined space entry)

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Sustainable mining
- Mineral exploration
- Digitalization

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for miners in Finland?**
A: The median monthly salary for miners and quarriers in Finland is around €2,800–€3,500 before tax, depending on experience, shift bonuses, and location. Entry-level workers start at about €2,400, while experienced operators can earn up to €4,000 or more with overtime.

**Q: Can a non-EU miner get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, but it is not straightforward. You need a job offer from a Finnish employer, and the employer must demonstrate that no suitable EU/EEA worker is available (labour market test). The occupation is not on the shortage list, so processing can take longer. A residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) is the main route; the EU Blue Card is unlikely due to salary thresholds.

**Q: How can I advance my career as a miner in Finland?**
A: Gain experience and additional certifications in blasting, drilling, or automation. Pursuing a higher vocational degree (e.g., technician) or an engineering degree can lead to supervisory or specialist roles. Some workers transition to mining inspector or safety consultant positions.
