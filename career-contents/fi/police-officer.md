# Police Officer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 5412 (ISCO08)**

Police officers in Finland are responsible for maintaining public order, preventing and investigating crime, and ensuring traffic safety. They work under the Ministry of the Interior, organized into local police departments and national units. The profession requires Finnish citizenship, a clean criminal record, and completion of a three-year Bachelor's program at the Police University College. The field is competitive, with emphasis on integrity, physical fitness, and community engagement.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a constable, advancing to senior constable, detective, sergeant, and inspector. Specializations include criminal investigation, traffic, K9 units, and SWAT. Further education can lead to leadership roles in the police administration or national agencies like the National Bureau of Investigation. The path is structured and seniority-based.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How does the police pay scale work in Finland?**
A: Police salaries are determined by collective agreements with step increases based on years of service and performance. Special skills and assignments can lead to pay supplements. No specific salary figures are provided here.

**Q: Can non-citizens become police officers in Finland?**
A: No, police officers must be Finnish citizens. There are no visa routes for foreign nationals to work as police officers in Finland.

**Q: What education is required to become a police officer?**
A: Applicants must complete a three-year Bachelor's degree at the Police University College, which requires a secondary education certificate and passing entrance exams including fitness and psychological tests.
