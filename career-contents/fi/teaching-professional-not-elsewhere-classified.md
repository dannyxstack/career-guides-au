# Teaching Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2359 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers a wide range of teaching professionals working outside formal primary and secondary education, including adult education teachers, language instructors, vocational trainers, and tutors in folk high schools, study centres, and private training institutes. In Finland, these roles are essential for lifelong learning, integration training for immigrants, and upskilling programmes. Employment is often part-time or project-based, with strong demand in language teaching and digital skills training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving into specialised roles such as curriculum design, educational coordination, or management of adult education centres. Many professionals combine teaching with freelance work or consulting. Further qualifications, such as a Master's degree in education or a vocational teacher certificate, can open doors to higher-level positions in educational administration or teacher training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardized language drills and oral scoring
- Basic computer skills training
- Generates standardised lesson plans and practice exercises
- Recording and distribution of online courses.
- Simple Q&A and homework grading

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to generate custom teaching content and practice questions
- Analyze student learning data through AI to adjust teaching strategies
- Uses AI to assist in personalized tutoring and feedback
- Teach multiple languages using AI translation and voice recognition tools
- Use AI-simulated scenarios for vocational training

**Human moat:**

- Complex interpersonal communication and emotional interaction
- Cross-Cultural Understanding and Sensitivity
- Course design and innovative thinking
- Ethical judgment and value guidance
- Differentiated instruction for students with special needs

**Skills to build:**

- AI teaching tool integration and use
- Data analysis and learning analytics
- Curriculum design and learning experience design
- Interdisciplinary knowledge integration
- Soft skills such as communication, emotional intelligence, and leadership
- Remote teaching and digital collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- adult education
- language training

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals passionate about lifelong learning and teaching adults from diverse backgrounds
- Those comfortable with flexible, often non-standard working hours and project-based contracts
- Professionals with strong language skills in Finnish, Swedish, or English and ability to adapt curricula to learner needs

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking a traditional school environment with fixed schedules and full-year contracts
- Individuals uncomfortable with administrative duties and documentation required in publicly funded programmes

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. In public adult education centres, monthly gross pay typically ranges from €2,500 to €4,000. Private language schools often pay less, while specialised vocational trainers may earn more. Collective agreements set minimums.

**Q: What visa do I need as a non-EU citizen to work as a teaching professional in Finland?**
A: You generally need a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) based on a job offer. If your field is in shortage (e.g., language teaching for migrants), you may benefit from faster processing through the D-visa route. EU/EEA citizens can work freely.
