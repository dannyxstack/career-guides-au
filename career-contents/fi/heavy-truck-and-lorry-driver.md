# Heavy Truck and Lorry Driver — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 8332 (ISCO08)**

Heavy truck and lorry drivers are vital to Finland's logistics sector, moving goods across long distances and challenging terrain. With the country's heavy reliance on road transport for industries like forestry, mining, and e-commerce, these professionals handle large vehicles (over 12 tonnes) and often operate in harsh winter conditions. The occupation is characterized by a mix of long-haul and local routes, requiring physical stamina and strict adherence to safety and driving regulations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression includes specializing in hazardous materials (ADR), oversized loads, or long-distance international routes. Experienced drivers can become owner-operators, fleet supervisors, or logistics coordinators. Further training can lead to passenger transport or machinery operation. Many drivers also transition into logistics management roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

- Autonomous driving system operation and monitoring
- Use of logistics scheduling software and multimodal transport platforms
- Basic Maintenance of New Energy Trucks (Electric/Hydrogen)
- Safety compliance and digital document management
- Customer communication and on-site problem solving
- Basic data analysis (fuel efficiency, route optimization)

**Key growth areas:**

- E-commerce
- Forest industry
- Construction
- International transit

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a heavy truck driver in Finland?**
A: Salaries are regulated by collective agreements, varying by employer, experience, and route type. In general, drivers earn a competitive monthly wage with supplements for night work, weekends, and long distances.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for heavy truck driving in Finland?**
A: Yes, through a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). You need a job offer from a Finnish employer. The employer must confirm that the position could not be filled by an EU/EEA candidate. The salary must meet the applicable collective agreement.

**Q: Is heavy truck driving a stable career in Finland?**
A: Yes, it offers stability due to consistent demand in key industries. With workforce aging, there are good opportunities for new entrants. Career progression includes specialization or moving into logistics management.
