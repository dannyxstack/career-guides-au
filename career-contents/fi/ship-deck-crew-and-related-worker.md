# Ship’s Deck Crew — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ships’ deck crews perform navigation watch duties, mooring, cargo handling, and maintenance on vessels in Finland’s extensive maritime sector. Finland’s position as a major exporter (forest products, machinery) and importer (raw materials) drives steady demand for deckhands and able seamen on cargo ships, tankers, and passenger ferries. The industry operates round the Baltic Sea and Arctic routes, with a significant portion employed through Finnish shipping companies and crewing agencies. Jobs are mostly seasonal or voyage-based, with a strong presence of unionized labour and mandatory STCW certification.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,312. Employment outlook for deck crews in Finland is stable but with gradual replacement demand as many seafarers near retirement. Newbuilding orders and growth in Arctic shipping may sustain openings. However, automation (e.g., autonomous ferries) could reduce crew sizes over the long term. The sector faces competition from low-cost flag states, but Finnish-flagged vessels require local crew for safety/regulatory reasons. Overall, about 200–400 annual job openings are expected, mainly for experienced able seamen.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as deckhand or ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman (AB) after sea service and STCW exams. Further steps include boatswain or deck officer (watchkeeping) via maritime academy. Many experienced crew move to shore-based roles in port operations, ship management, or maritime training. Continuous upgrading of STCW endorsements (e.g., advanced firefighting, medical care) is required for promotion. Senior positions (chief mate) often demand higher education and unlimited certification.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and AI tool usage (e.g., route optimization software)
- Data analysis and dashboard interpretation skills
- Skills in remotely operating drones or robots.
- Advanced maritime regulations and certification updates
- Soft skills: cross-cultural communication and crisis management
- Digital forensics and cybersecurity fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

- Baltic Sea shipping
- Arctic maritime
- Winter navigation
- Autonomous vessel operations

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pre-tax salary range for a ship’s deck crew member in Finland?**
A: According to collective agreements, an able seaman (AB) earns a monthly basic wage of €2,400–€3,200, plus allowances for overtime, night work, and special voyages. Annual pre-tax earnings (including allowances) range approximately €33,000–€45,000.

**Q: Do I need a residence permit to work as a deck crew on a Finnish ship as a non-EU/EEA citizen?**
A: Yes, unless you are a EU/EEA citizen. Non-EU/EEA seafarers require a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) based on a job offer from a Finnish shipping company. The permit is usually tied to the employer and vessel. Alternatively, crew on short stops may use a visa-free transit (up to 90 days) but employment requires a permit.

**Q: Can I advance to a deck officer position without a maritime academy degree?**
A: It is uncommon but possible through the “rating to officer” route, requiring additional STCW certificates, sea service, and passing Traficom exams. However, formal education (maritime academy degree) is strongly preferred for higher ranks (e.g., second officer, chief mate).
