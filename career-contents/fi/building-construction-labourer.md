# Building Construction Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9313 (ISCO08)**

Building construction labourers perform a variety of manual tasks on construction sites, including loading and unloading materials, digging trenches, operating hand and power tools, and assisting skilled tradespeople. In Finland, this occupation is a common entry point into the construction sector, with demand driven by ongoing urban development and infrastructure projects. The work is physically demanding and often seasonal, with many positions filled through temporary contracts. While formal education is not always required, vocational qualifications and safety certifications enhance employability. The role is subject to collective agreements ensuring minimum wages and working conditions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for building construction labourers often involves gaining experience and moving into more specialised roles such as carpenter, bricklayer, or concrete worker. Many proceed to obtain a vocational qualification (e.g., from a vocational school) to become skilled tradespeople. With further training, some advance to site supervisor or foreman positions. The construction industry in Finland values on-the-job learning, and employers often support upskilling through apprenticeships. The path from labourer to skilled trade is well-established, but mobility is limited for those without Finnish language skills or formal qualifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Moving construction materials (replaced by AGVs/drones).
- Basic excavation and earthworks (replaced by unmanned excavators)
- On-site cleaning and debris sorting (replaced by cleaning robots)
- Simple bricklaying and plastering (replaced by automatic brick-laying machines/spraying machines)
- Repetitive rebar tying (replaced by tying robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to read construction drawings, improving accuracy.
- Operate semi-automated tools (e.g., electric screwdrivers, laser levels)
- Maintenance and debugging of automated equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Report progress and receive instructions via digital platforms
- Using BIM software for collaborative work and problem-solving

**Human moat:**

- Judgment in unstructured environments (e.g., complex terrain, unexpected situations)
- Fine hand-eye coordination (e.g., installing irregular components).
- Team on-site communication and emergency coordination
- Experience in safety supervision and accident prevention
- Mastery of traditional crafts (e.g., stonemasonry, special plastering)

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated construction equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Reading digital drawings and BIM basics
- Basic programming or equipment debugging skills
- Enhanced safety and quality management knowledge
- Knowledge of electrical/hydraulic system maintenance
- English or digital tools usage

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Infrastructure
- Green building
- Residential construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building construction labourer in Finland?**
A: The monthly gross salary typically ranges from €2,000 to €2,800 for full-time work, with variation by experience, region, and employer. Overtime and shift allowances may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EU national obtain a visa to work as a building construction labourer in Finland?**
A: Yes, a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) is possible if you have a job offer from a Finnish employer. However, the income threshold (€1,400-€1,600 per month) must be met, and employers often prioritise local or EU workers. Labour market testing may be required.

**Q: Do building construction labourers need any safety certifications?**
A: Yes, the Construction Safety Card (Työturvallisuuskortti) is mandatory for most construction sites. Additional certifications for specific tasks (e.g., asbestos, scaffolding) may be required.
