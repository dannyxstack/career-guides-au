# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers in Finland construct and maintain the structural components of buildings, such as foundations, walls, roofs, and floors. This occupation covers roles like bricklayers, concrete finishers, carpenters, and roofers, who work primarily on residential, commercial, and industrial construction sites. The Finnish construction industry is experiencing steady demand due to urbanization, renovation needs, and infrastructure investments. Most workers are employed by private construction firms, with opportunities for self-employment. The profession requires vocational training, physical fitness, and adherence to strict safety standards. Seasonal fluctuations affect employment, but overall, the sector offers stable job prospects for skilled workers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,904. Finland's construction sector faces a moderate labour shortage for skilled building frame workers, driven by an aging workforce and increased activity in renovation (korjausrakentaminen) and sustainable building projects. The outlook is positive, with demand expected to remain steady through 2025–2030, especially in southern growth centres like Helsinki, Tampere, and Turku. However, economic cycles impact hiring, and competition may increase during downturns. Workers with expertise in energy-efficient construction and modular techniques have better prospects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Building frame trades workers in Finland can progress to specialized roles such as site supervisor (työnjohtaja) or foreman, or become self-employed contractors. Further vocational qualifications enable movement into technical design, occupational safety, or energy consulting. Many experienced workers also teach at vocational institutions. Career advancement is achievable through continuous learning, with government-funded training support available. The typical path is from apprentice to journeyman, then to master-level certification (mestari), which allows taking on larger projects and managing teams.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Sustainable construction
- Modular construction
- Infrastructure projects

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building frame trades worker in Finland?**
A: The median monthly gross salary for building frame trades workers is approximately 2,500–3,000 EUR, depending on experience, employer, and region. Overtime and special skills can increase earnings.

**Q: Can I get a residence permit as a building frame trades worker from outside the EU?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer meeting the collective agreement salary threshold (around 2,000–2,500 EUR/month), you can apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The occupation is on the shortage list, which may expedite processing.

**Q: What language skills are required?**
A: Basic Finnish (or Swedish in some regions) is usually required for workplace communication and safety instructions. Some larger companies may accept English, but Finnish is strongly recommended for long-term employment and career progression.
