# Audiologist or Speech Therapist — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2266 (ISCO08)**

Audiologists and speech therapists in Finland diagnose, treat, and prevent communication and hearing disorders across all ages. They work in public healthcare, private clinics, schools, and rehabilitation centers. The profession requires a master's degree, Finnish language proficiency, and a license from Valvira. Demand is steady due to an aging population, increased awareness of developmental disorders, and integration of speech therapy into multidisciplinary care.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression includes specialization in areas like pediatric, neurological, or hearing rehabilitation, moving into management of therapy units, or pursuing research and academia. Experienced therapists can open private practices. Continuous professional development is required to maintain licensure, and advanced degrees (e.g., PhD) open doors to university teaching or clinical research roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening and automated report generation for standard hearing tests
- Preliminary analysis of voice recordings and marking of abnormal patterns
- Automatic scoring and preliminary suggestions for dysphagia screening questionnaires.
- Standardized speech recognition and feedback for speech therapy exercises
- Basic content generation and customization of patient education materials

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: using deep learning models to analyze audiograms, speech spectra, and images to improve diagnostic accuracy
- Enhanced remote therapy: AI-driven real-time voice feedback system improves patient self-training at home
- Personalized treatment plan design: generate customized rehabilitation plans based on patient data (e.g., voice samples, hearing loss patterns)
- Data-Driven Prognostic Assessment: Analyze large clinical datasets to predict treatment responses and optimize treatment pathways
- Multilingual support: AI real-time translation and accent adaptation expand coverage to multicultural populations

**Human moat:**

- Clinical judgment and human empathy for complex communication barriers.
- Empathy for building trust with patients and their families
- Collaborative decision-making and case management in multidisciplinary teams
- Clinical reasoning skills for rare or comorbid conditions
- Ethical decision-making and privacy protection (involving sensitive health data)

**Skills to build:**

- AI/machine learning basics (how to explain model outputs for clinical diagnosis)
- Remote health assessment and treatment technology (platform usage, data security)
- Biomedical signal processing (speech and hearing-related data analysis)
- Patient data privacy and ethics (HIPAA/PIPEDA compliance)
- Multilingual and cross-cultural communication (adapted to Canada's multicultural patient population).
- Human-machine collaborative treatment design (integrating AI tools into traditional therapies)

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- telepractice
- early intervention
- assistive technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical starting salary for an audiologist or speech therapist in Finland?**
A: According to the Labour Force Barometer, entry-level salaries in the public sector range from approximately €2,500 to €3,200 per month pre-tax, depending on the municipality and experience. Private practice may offer higher pay, especially after specialization.

**Q: Can I work as a speech therapist in Finland with a foreign degree?**
A: Yes, but your degree must be recognized by Valvira. You will need to demonstrate Finnish language proficiency (at least B1, often B2) and may need to complete supplementary studies or a competence test. The recognition process can take several months.

**Q: Are there opportunities for career advancement in this field in Finland?**
A: Yes, you can specialize in areas like pediatric speech therapy, neurological rehabilitation, or audiology implants. Experienced professionals can become head of a therapy unit, open a private practice, or move into research and teaching at universities.
