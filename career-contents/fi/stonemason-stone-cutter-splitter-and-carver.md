# Stonemason — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7113 (ISCO08)**

Stonemasons, stone cutters, splitters and carvers (ISCO-08 7113) shape and finish natural stone for construction, restoration, and decorative purposes. In Finland, this niche trade is essential for heritage building maintenance, cemetery headstone production, and high-end architectural cladding. The profession demands precision, physical stamina, and an eye for detail, with most work concentrated in southern Finland and regions with active building conservation.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 631. Employment outlook for stonemasons in Finland is stable but limited. Growth is driven by restoration of historic stone buildings (e.g., Helsinki Cathedral, Turku Castle) and increasing demand for natural stone in landscaping. However, automation and precast concrete alternatives reduce overall demand. New entrants are few due to the specialised skill set and aging workforce, creating niche opportunities for skilled craftspeople. Major employers include construction companies, monument workshops, and municipal maintenance units.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational qualification in stone working (e.g., Stonemason, 3 years) or on-the-job training. Experienced stonemasons can advance to foreman, estimator, or start their own business. Some specialise in restoration, earning premium rates for heritage projects. Continuing education in artisan carving or stone conservation (e.g., through the Finnish National Board of Antiquities) can lead to master craftsman status. The career is physically demanding, with many transitioning to supervisory or teaching roles after age 50.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for stone cutting and layout design
- Operating automatic stone cutting machines and CNC engraving machines
- Material quantity calculation and layout using BIM models
- Use robots for repetitive stone handling and laying

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using 3D scanning and digital twins for precise stone structure installation
- Use AR glasses to overlay design drawings on construction sites, reducing errors
- Using AI to optimize stone splicing plans, improving aesthetics and strength
- Conduct drone inspections of large stone structures and remotely monitor maintenance needs.

**Human moat:**

- Artistic judgment for complex stone carving and hand modeling
- Adaptive skills for integrated on-site stone and structure installation
- Empirical judgment of stone texture, color, and physical properties
- Ability to communicate customized design solutions with clients

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/BIM software operation (e.g., AutoCAD, Revit)
- CNC stone processing equipment programming and operation
- 3D scanning and digital modeling skills
- Engineering surveying and precision installation technology
- Drone operation and image analysis basics

**Key growth areas:**

- restoration
- cemetery maintenance
- urban landscaping
- natural stone

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a stonemason in Finland?**
A: Precise salary figures vary by experience, region, and employer. For official statistics, consult Statistics Finland's Structure of Earnings database for ISCO-08 7113. Typically, salaries follow collective agreements in the construction sector.

**Q: Can a non-EU stonemason get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer, you can apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The employer must prove that no suitable EU/EEA candidates are available (labour market test). The salary must meet the collective agreement minimum. You can apply at the Finnish embassy or online.

**Q: Is stonemasonry a declining trade in Finland?**
A: No, but it is a stable niche. While overall construction employs fewer stonemasons due to modern materials, specialised restoration and natural stone landscaping provide steady work. The aging workforce creates replacement demand.
