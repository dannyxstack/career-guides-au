# Plasterer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers in Finland apply plaster, stucco, gypsum boards, and other finishing materials to interior and exterior walls and ceilings. They work on new construction sites, renovation projects, and historic building restorations. The trade requires precision, physical stamina, and knowledge of materials and techniques. Plasterers often work alongside bricklayers, painters, and other construction professionals. The job can be physically demanding and involves working at heights, with dust and wet materials. Self-employment and subcontracting are common career paths for experienced plasterers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for plasterers typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational qualification, followed by on-the-job experience. Experienced plasterers can become foremen, site supervisors, or start their own company. Additional specialization in decorative plastering, fireproofing, or insulation coating can lead to higher pay. Some continue their education to become construction engineers or building inspectors. Union membership and moving to larger construction firms can offer better benefits and job security.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Energy-efficient plastering
- Decorative finishes
- Fireproofing

**Who is suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a plasterer in Finland?**
A: Entry-level plasterers earn around 2,400–2,800 €/month gross. Experienced professionals can earn 3,000–3,800 €/month. Self-employed plasterers may charge higher rates but have variable income. Overtime and special skills (e.g., decorative finishes) can increase pay.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to get a residence permit as a plasterer?**
A: Yes, for non-EU/EEA citizens, a confirmed job offer is required. The employer must prove that the position could not be filled locally. Plastering is on Finland's positive list, which streamlines the process. After 4 years of work, you may be eligible for a permanent residence permit.

**Q: Can I work as a plasterer in Finland without a formal vocational qualification?**
A: It is difficult without a recognized qualification, as most employers require at least a vocational degree or completed apprenticeship. However, with several years of experience and proof of skills, some employers may hire, but it's uncommon. The qualification is highly recommended.
