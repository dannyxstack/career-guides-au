# Blacksmith, Hammersmith and Forging Press Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7221 (ISCO08)**

Blacksmiths, hammersmiths and forging press workers shape metal using hammers, presses and other forging equipment. In Finland, they are employed primarily in the manufacturing sector, including machinery, shipbuilding, automotive components and custom metalwork. The occupation combines traditional craftsmanship with modern technology, such as CNC-controlled presses and automated forging lines. Demand is stable in niche areas like artistic blacksmithing, restoration of historical metalwork and specialized industrial components. Most workers are employed in small and medium-sized enterprises, and the work requires precision, physical stamina and attention to safety protocols.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational qualification or apprenticeship, leading to roles as journeyman or master blacksmith. Experienced workers can advance to supervisory positions, production managers or quality control specialists. Some blacksmiths specialize in artistic forging, restoration or tool-making. Further training in CNC programming or welding can open doors to higher-skilled roles. Self-employment in custom metalwork is also a viable path for those with entrepreneurial drive.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive forging (e.g., standard bolts, batch agricultural tools) is automatically completed by CNC forging machines.
- Welding path planning (simple right-angle welding) is performed automatically by the robot
- Heat treatment temperature and time control managed by AI closed-loop system.
- Metal surface polishing, burr removal, and other finishing steps are replaced by robotic arms
- Inventory management and order processing automated by ERP+AI predictive systems

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design of intricate wrought iron patterns (generative design creates structurally optimized Crystal Grain iron doors)
- AR glasses overlay welding guidance lines to improve precision in complex curved surface welding
- AI quality inspection system (visual inspection + acoustic analysis) for real-time monitoring of internal defects in forgings
- Digital twin simulation of forging stress distribution reduces trial-and-error costs
- Online customization platforms allow customers to preview ironwork effects via AI visualizers, shortening communication cycles

**Human moat:**

- The subtle feel of manual hammer forging and the artistic effect of hammer marks cannot be replicated by machines
- Judgment for on-site restoration of antique ironwork or special historical building components
- The craftsmanship of understanding artistic intent in custom projects and translating it into executable plans
- Intuition and experience in heat treatment of special metals (such as old iron with abnormal carbon content).
- Trust and creative integration in face-to-face communication with architects and designers

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/CAM (e.g. Fusion 360) and parametric design
- Master operation and debugging of AR-assisted welding/assembly equipment
- Learning metal 3D printing (powder bed fusion, directed energy deposition)
- Basic Python scripting for custom AI tools or data analysis
- Materials science and metallurgy (especially high-performance alloys, composite metals)
- Digital marketing and e-commerce operations (showcasing custom wrought iron works, securing global orders)

**Key growth areas:**

- Artistic Blacksmithing
- Custom Fabrication
- Restoration Work
- CNC Forging Automation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How is the salary determined?**
A: Salaries are based on collective agreements for the metal industry, with variations by experience, employer and specialization. Experienced workers in custom or industrial forging earn more, while entry-level positions start lower.

**Q: Can I get a residence permit as a blacksmith or forging press worker?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer and the employer can demonstrate that no suitable EU/EEA labour is available. You can apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) from your home country. Language skills in Finnish or Swedish are typically required for the work.

**Q: Is Finnish language required for education?**
A: Most vocational programs are taught in Finnish or Swedish. Basic Finnish language skills are usually required for admission, and fluency is necessary for the workplace and safety communication.
