# Child Care Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 5311 (ISCO08)**

Child care workers in Finland provide care and supervision for children in daycare centers, preschools, and other early childhood education settings. They support children's learning, play, and daily routines, often working under the guidance of early childhood education teachers. The occupation is regulated, requiring a vocational qualification in social and health care or a specific childcare diploma. Demand is steady due to high participation rates in early childhood education and a national shortage of qualified staff, especially in urban areas. Child care workers are primarily employed by municipalities and private daycare providers, with working conditions governed by collective agreements.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 44,429. The employment outlook for child care workers in Finland is positive, driven by a statutory right to daycare for all children under school age and increasing labor force participation by parents. The sector faces a chronic shortage of qualified personnel, particularly in the Helsinki region and growing cities. Retirements and turnover create ongoing vacancies. Government initiatives to improve staff-to-child ratios and raise qualification standards may further boost demand over the next decade. However, budget pressures on municipalities could slow hiring in some regions.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Finnish child care typically involves specialization (e.g., special needs care, family daycare) or advancement to lead child care worker roles. Some pursue further education to become early childhood education teachers (requires a bachelor's degree) or practical nurses with a child care focus. Additional training in bilingual care, multicultural education, or inclusion increases opportunities. With experience and additional credentials, child care workers can move into administrative or supervisory positions in daycare centers.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a child care worker in Finland?**
A: The gross monthly salary for a qualified child care worker in Finland typically ranges from €2,200 to €2,800, depending on experience, employer, and location. Municipal daycare centers follow collective agreements that set minimum salaries around €2,200 for beginners.

**Q: Can a non-EU child care worker get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Finnish employer and hold a recognized vocational qualification in early childhood education or social and health care. You must also meet the salary requirement (at least €1,334/month) and apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). Proof of Finnish language skills (B1) is typically required.

**Q: Do I need a Finnish qualification to work as a child care worker?**
A: Yes, a vocational qualification in social and health care (practical nurse) with a child care specialization or a specific vocational qualification in early childhood education is mandatory. Foreign qualifications must be recognized by the Finnish National Agency for Education (EDUFI).
