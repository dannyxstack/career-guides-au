# Biologists, Botanists, Zoologists and Related Professionals — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Biologists, botanists, zoologists and related professionals in Finland study living organisms and their environments, contributing to fields such as biodiversity conservation, biotechnology, and climate change research. They work in universities, research institutes, government agencies (e.g., Finnish Environment Institute SYKE), and private sector companies, particularly in the bioeconomy and pharmaceutical sectors. The profession demands strong analytical skills and often requires fieldwork and laboratory expertise. Finland's rich natural landscapes and strong investment in environmental monitoring create steady demand for these specialists, though competition for academic positions can be high.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,186. Employment outlook for biologists in Finland is positive, driven by growing environmental concerns, climate change mitigation, and the expansion of the bioeconomy. The Finnish government's focus on sustainable development and circular economy creates opportunities in environmental consultancy, wildlife management, and biotechnology. However, budget constraints in public research may slow academic hiring. Private sector roles in biotech and agritech are expected to grow. Overall, moderate growth is anticipated, with demand especially for experts in bioinformatics, environmental DNA (eDNA) analysis, and aquatic ecology.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for biologists in Finland typically begins with a Master's degree, followed by junior researcher or laboratory technician roles. Advancement to senior researcher, project manager, or lead scientist often requires a PhD. Mid-career options include moving into environmental policy, quality assurance, or R&D management. Many professionals also transition into consulting, teaching, or entrepreneurship in the bioeconomy. Continuous learning in IT, data analysis, and regulatory affairs is increasingly important. The field is competitive but rewarding for those with strong academic records and specialization.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)
- Manual sorting of ecological monitoring data (replaced by IoT sensors + AI).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Creativity in experiment design, hypothesis formulation and validation
- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)
- Responsibility attribution and professional certification under the regulatory framework.

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning applications
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., AlphaFold, BLAST)
- Data visualization and interactive reporting (e.g., R Shiny, Tableau)
- Automated laboratory equipment operation and process optimization
- Science communication and policy advocacy skills
- AI ethics and biosecurity regulations knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Biotechnology
- Environmental monitoring
- Climate change research
- Bioinformatics

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How does the salary of a biologist compare to other professions in Finland?**
A: Salaries for biologists are competitive but typically slightly below the average for all professionals. Entry-level positions start around €30,000 per year, while experienced researchers and lead scientists can earn up to €60,000. Private sector roles in biotechnology tend to offer higher pay than public research.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU biologists?**
A: Non-EU biologists can apply for a Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL) with a job offer, or an EU Blue Card if they meet the salary threshold. Both pathways require a valid work contract and relevant qualifications. The D-visa fast track is currently not applicable to this occupation.

**Q: Is a PhD necessary to work as a biologist in Finland?**
A: A Master's degree is the minimum requirement for most professional roles. A PhD is essential for academic research positions and can be advantageous for senior roles in private sector R&D.
