# Philosophers, Historians and Political Scientists — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2633 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers professionals researching and teaching philosophy, history, political science, and related fields. In Finland, most work in universities, research institutes, government agencies, and think tanks. The labour market is small and highly academic, with limited openings outside education and public administration. Political scientists find roles in policy analysis, while historians often work in cultural heritage. Competition for permanent positions is strong, and a doctoral degree is typically required for research or teaching posts.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,049. Employment outlook is stable but with few new openings. The Finnish government and university sectors have limited hiring budgets, leading to fixed-term contracts. Retirements may create some vacancies, but the number of PhD graduates exceeds job opportunities, especially in philosophy and history. Political scientists have slightly better prospects in public administration and EU institutions. Overall, the occupation is not a high-growth field, and securing a permanent position requires persistence and networking.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically moves from doctoral researcher or postdoc to university lecturer or senior researcher, and eventually to professor. In policy, the path goes from research assistant to policy advisor or senior expert. Many also work as independent researchers or consultants. Academic tenure is rare, so mobility between institutions is common. Mid-career professionals may transition to secondary education or public sector management, but advancement is slow and project-based.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic literature search and organization.
- Repeat delivery of standard course content
- Simple data analysis and statistical report generation
- Academic citation and format checking
- Data collection work of junior research assistants

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Deep pattern analysis of large-scale historical texts
- Real-time simulation and prediction of political science public opinion data
- Rapid comparative study of cross-language philosophical texts
- Development of personalized intelligent teaching aid tools
- Complex system modeling and visualization of policy impacts

**Human moat:**

- Propose original theoretical frameworks and conceptual innovations
- Ethical value judgments and moral debates
- Understanding and interpretation of cross-cultural historical contexts
- Design of Research Questions and Methodology
- Debate and peer review in academic communities

**Skills to build:**

- Python data analysis and NLP tools (e.g., Hugging Face)
- AI Ethics and Policy Evaluation Framework
- Digital humanities research methods
- Advanced quantitative methods (e.g., causal inference)
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication skills
- Academic entrepreneurship and project application skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Policy Analysis
- Public Administration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for this occupation in Finland?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries for researchers and early-career political scientists range from €35,000 to €55,000. University professors earn €60,000–€80,000. Salaries in government and think tanks are similar. Adjustments apply based on experience and sector.

**Q: Can I work as a philosopher or historian in Finland without a work visa?**
A: If you are an EU/EEA citizen, yes. For non-EU/EEA nationals, you need a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) or an EU Blue Card if the salary meets the threshold. The employer must sponsor the permit, and the role must be skilled.

**Q: How competitive is the job market for this field?**
A: Highly competitive. Finland produces many PhD graduates relative to academic openings. Fixed-term contracts are common. Networking and Finnish language skills are important for roles in government or local research institutes.
