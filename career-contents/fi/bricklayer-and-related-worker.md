# Bricklayers and Related Workers — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7112 (ISCO08)**

Bricklayers and related workers in Finland are skilled tradespeople who construct and repair brick, stone, and block structures, including walls, foundations, and facades. They work primarily in the construction industry, which has seen steady demand due to urban development and renovation projects. The occupation is essential for both new builds and maintenance of existing buildings, often collaborating with other construction professionals. Finland's cold climate requires specialized techniques in frost-resistant masonry, and sustainable construction practices are increasingly important. The field offers stable employment with opportunities for specialization in areas like stonework or restoration.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 544. The employment outlook for bricklayers in Finland is positive, driven by ongoing construction and renovation needs, especially in urban areas like Helsinki, Tampere, and Turku. The aging infrastructure and focus on energy-efficient building renovations support demand. However, economic cycles can affect the market, leading to periodic slowdowns. The shortage of skilled bricklayers, particularly those with experience in modern techniques, creates opportunities for newcomers with formal training. The industry is also adapting to green building practices, requiring upskilling.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for bricklayers in Finland typically starts with a vocational qualification in construction, followed on-the-job training. Many advance to become master bricklayers or site supervisors. Experienced workers may specialize in restoration, stone carving, or sustainable construction. Entrepreneurship is common, with some starting their own renovation businesses. Further education in construction management can lead to roles like project manager. Apprenticeships are a strong pathway, combining work and study.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable Construction
- Renovation
- Energy Efficiency
- Stone Work

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who enjoy hands-on, physical work and take pride in craftsmanship.
- People who can work outdoors in varying weather conditions.
- Those interested in sustainable building and energy-efficient construction.

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer desk jobs or indoor work with minimal physical activity.
- Those who are not comfortable working at heights or with heavy materials.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for bricklayers in Finland?**
A: Salaries are based on collective agreements in the construction sector. Entry-level bricklayers earn around €2,000-€2,500 per month, while experienced workers can earn €3,000-€3,500. Overtime and specialized skills (e.g., restoration) can increase pay.

**Q: Can a non-EU bricklayer get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, through a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). You need a job offer from a Finnish employer, and the salary must meet the minimum threshold (around €1,600-€2,000/month). The occupation is on the shortage list, which may expedite the process.
