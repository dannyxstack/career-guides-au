# Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and aquaculture labourers in Finland perform manual tasks in fish farming, fishing, and related aquatic operations, including feeding, harvesting, sorting, and cleaning. The sector is concentrated in coastal regions and archipelago, with growing emphasis on sustainable aquaculture. Employment is seasonal and often tied to small-scale enterprises, with limited entry barriers but reliance on physical stamina and practical skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; experienced labourers may advance to supervisory roles or become self-employed fishers. Formal qualification pathways exist through vocational qualifications in fish farming, but most workers learn on the job. The sector attracts individuals with a strong connection to coastal lifestyles, but long-term prospects are moderate due to industry consolidation and mechanization.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture
- Sustainable Fishing
- Automation
- Blue Economy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery and aquaculture labourer in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by employer and region, but the pre-tax annual salary typically ranges from €24,000 to €30,000 for full-time, year-round work. Seasonal workers may earn less. Collective agreements in the sector set minimum rates around €1,500/month.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work permit as a fishery labourer?**
A: Yes, it is possible via the Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL). The job must meet the salary threshold (€1,399/month in 2025) and the employer demonstrate that no suitable EU/EEA candidate is available. However, due to the low-skilled nature, applications may face higher scrutiny.

**Q: What are the long-term career prospects in this field?**
A: Progression is limited without further training. Some labourers become supervisors or start their own small fishing or fish farming businesses. Pursuing a vocational qualification can open paths to technician roles in aquaculture. Automation may reduce demand for manual labourers over time.
