# Motor Vehicle Mechanic and Repairer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7231 (ISCO08)**

Motor vehicle mechanics and repairers maintain, diagnose, and repair cars, vans, and light trucks in Finland. The profession is essential due to the country's large vehicle fleet and the increasing complexity of modern vehicles, including electric and hybrid models. Work is typically carried out in independent garages, dealership service centers, or fleet maintenance workshops. The demand for mechanics is steady, with ongoing need for both routine servicing and advanced technical repairs. Vocational training is the standard entry route, and continuous skill development is crucial to keep pace with technological advancements.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 24,073. The employment outlook for motor vehicle mechanics in Finland is stable but evolving. The rise of electric vehicles and advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) is shifting skill requirements, creating demand for mechanics trained in high-voltage systems and electronic diagnostics. Regional variations exist, with more opportunities in southern Finland and urban centers. Overall, retirement of the current workforce will sustain replacement demand. Competition may increase in popular areas, but specialization (e.g., EV or diagnostics) can enhance job prospects.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for motor vehicle mechanics in Finland typically starts with a vocational qualification followed by on-the-job experience. Experienced mechanics can advance to foreman, workshop manager, or open their own repair business. Specializing in electric vehicles, diagnostics, or specific brands often leads to higher pay and more complex work. Further education, such as a master mechanic certificate or technician-level qualifications, can open doors to supervisory or training roles. Continuous learning is essential to keep up with evolving automotive technology.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use diagnostic tools to read standard fault codes and provide common solutions
- Automatically generate standard maintenance work orders and parts lists from repair database.
- Automatically generate routine maintenance reminder reports from vehicle sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI diagnostic systems to quickly locate complex electrical or mechanical faults
- Overlay maintenance steps and component disassembly instructions via AR glasses
- Use predictive maintenance algorithms to detect potential vehicle issues early
- Using natural language processing tools to automatically record maintenance logs

**Human moat:**

- Creative troubleshooting and on-site debugging of non-standard faults
- Fine disassembly and calibration requiring tactile feedback
- Comprehensive vehicle condition assessment and repair plan decision-making
- Customer trust communication and personalized service.

**Skills to build:**

- Mastery of AI diagnostic tools (e.g., Dealer-Fix, TEXA IDC5)
- Learning hybrid/electric vehicle high-voltage system repair
- Familiar with in-vehicle network diagnostics and software update processes
- Practice using AR-assisted maintenance equipment
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn the basics of data analysis and predictive maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- Electric Vehicle Repair
- Diagnostics
- Hybrid Systems
- ADAS Calibration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for motor vehicle mechanics in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and employer. New graduates typically earn a lower starting wage, while experienced mechanics in specialized roles or large cities can expect higher pay. Median monthly earnings are in line with the average for skilled trades in Finland.

**Q: Can a non-EU motor vehicle mechanic get a residence permit to work in Finland?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals can apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) with a confirmed job offer. The permit is tied to the specific employer and occupation. The salary must meet the threshold set by the government (approximately €1,600–€2,000/month depending on the field). Experience and qualifications are assessed.

**Q: What are the opportunities for career advancement?**
A: Mechanics can progress to foreman, workshop manager, or become independent entrepreneurs. Specializing in electric vehicles, diagnostics, or certain brands can lead to higher pay. Advanced certifications and continuous training improve prospects.
