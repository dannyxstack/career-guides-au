# Armed Forces Occupations, Other Ranks — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 0310 (ISCO08)**

Other ranks in the Finnish Armed Forces include conscripts and professional soldiers serving in combat, support, and technical roles. Finland maintains a conscription system for men, with voluntary service for women. Professional other ranks (NCOs and specialists) form the backbone of the military. The occupation demands discipline, physical fitness, and adherence to military hierarchy. Service conditions are regulated by the Finnish Defence Forces, with training provided in Finnish or Swedish. Foreign nationals are rarely recruited, as citizenship is typically required for permanent roles.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 176. Employment outlook for military other ranks is stable but limited to Finnish citizens. The Defence Forces recruit professional soldiers periodically based on retirement and operational needs. Finland's NATO membership may slightly increase demand for specialised roles, but overall numbers remain regulated by budget and defence policy. Conscription ensures a continuous supply of trained reserves, reducing need for large professional intake. Growth is concentrated in cyber defence and international crisis management, though these are officer-heavy areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression from other ranks typically follows a structured path: from private (conscript or contract) to non-commissioned officer (NCO) through the NCO School, and optionally to officer via the Military Academy. Advancement depends on performance, leadership skills, and additional training. Specialisation opportunities exist in logistics, communications, engineering, and medical roles. Most professional soldiers serve fixed-term contracts, with possible renewal until age limits. Lateral entry from civilian to military is rare; most recruits start at basic training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Paper document filing and report generation
- Basic intelligence data filtering and tagging
- Manual video surveillance at fixed posts
- Manual recording of standard operating procedures

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drone reconnaissance and battlefield situational awareness
- AI-assisted threat identification and target locking
- Virtual reality simulation training and tactical deduction
- Intelligent scheduling and forecasting of logistics supply
- Real-time language translation and multi-source intelligence integration

**Human moat:**

- Close-quarters combat and physical control
- Real-world decision-making in complex battlefield environments
- Wartime leadership and morale maintenance
- Reassurance and communication with hostages/civilians

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much do armed forces other ranks earn in Finland?**
A: Salaries are determined by the Finnish Defence Forces pay scale based on rank, seniority, and duties. Professional soldiers start at a competitive monthly wage comparable to other public sector positions, with additional allowances for special skills, overseas missions, and shift work.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a soldier in the Finnish Defence Forces?**
A: Foreign nationals without Finnish citizenship are rarely recruited for other ranks. Exceptions may exist for NATO exchange officers, but those are typically officers. Conscripts must be Finnish citizens or permanent residents. A residence permit for employed persons is possible only if a rare job offer is secured, but it is not a common migration pathway.
