# Other Cleaning Workers — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other cleaning workers in Finland perform a variety of cleaning tasks in settings such as offices, hotels, hospitals, and industrial facilities. They may use specialized equipment and cleaning agents to maintain hygiene and cleanliness. This occupation is essential for public health and safety, with demand driven by the service sector and an aging population. Workers often find employment through staffing agencies or directly with cleaning companies. The job typically requires physical stamina and attention to detail.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for other cleaning workers often includes moving into supervisory roles, such as team leader or cleaning manager, or specializing in areas like hospital cleaning, industrial cleaning, or green cleaning. Additional training can lead to certification in occupational safety or hygiene. With experience, workers may start their own cleaning business. However, the career ladder is relatively flat without further education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to operate cleaning robots or smart cleaning equipment
- Basic computer operation and simple programming (e.g., setting cleaning routes)
- Understanding the Internet of Things (IoT) technology to monitor the status of cleaning equipment
- Knowledge of Disinfection and Chemical Safety in Learning Environments
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn data analysis fundamentals to optimize cleaning efficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- Green cleaning
- Industrial cleaning
- Healthcare cleaning
- Specialized cleaning

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaner in Finland?**
A: Salaries are based on collective agreements; for example, the minimum wage for a general cleaner under the cleaning services collective agreement is around €11–12 per hour, leading to an annual salary of approximately €20,000–€24,000 for full-time work.

**Q: Can a non-EU cleaner get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer with salary meeting the collective agreement minimum and the employer has sought local labor, you can apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL).

**Q: Is it possible to advance from cleaner to manager?**
A: Yes, with experience and possibly a vocational qualification, you can become a team leader or cleaning manager; some workers also start their own companies.
