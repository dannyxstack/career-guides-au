# Traditional Chief and Head of Village — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 1113 (ISCO08)**

Traditional chiefs and heads of villages in Finland are primarily found within the Sámi community, where they lead local governance and represent cultural heritage. This role is not a standard occupation in the Finnish labour market but exists in Sámi homeland areas (Sodankylä, Inari, Utsjoki). Responsibilities include mediating community decisions, preserving traditions, and liaising with municipal authorities. The position is honorific or elected, not salaried in the conventional sense, and falls under community leadership rather than employment statistics.

Younger Sámi members may step into leadership after years of community involvement and cultural education. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: Is this a paid position in Finland?**
A: Traditional chiefs and heads of villages in Finland are usually unpaid or receive honoraria from community funds. They are not employees in the formal labour market.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a traditional chief in Finland?**
A: It is extremely unlikely as the role requires deep cultural ties, Sámi language skills, and community acceptance. The role is not part of migration pathways.

**Q: What is the salary range for this occupation?**
A: There is no standard salary; compensation, if any, is minimal and community-determined. Official salary statistics do not include this role.
