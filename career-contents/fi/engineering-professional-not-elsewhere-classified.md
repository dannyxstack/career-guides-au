# Engineering Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2149 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers engineers specializing in fields not separately classified, such as environmental, energy, or biomedical engineering. In Finland, professionals work in consulting, industry, or research, focusing on sustainable solutions, energy efficiency, and digitalization. The role requires a strong technical foundation and often collaboration across disciplines. The Finnish labour market values these engineers for driving innovation in green technology and industrial automation.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from design or project engineering to senior or lead roles, then management or expert positions. Specialization in emerging fields like energy efficiency or automation accelerates advancement. Continuing education is common, often via employer-funded training or polytechnic programs. Networking via industry associations (e.g., TEK) aids mobility. Some engineers transition to entrepreneurship or consulting.

- Standard design parameter optimization
- Drafting technical documents
- Partial simulation runs and preliminary processing of results

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Quickly match customer needs with technical solutions
- Trend prediction and risk assessment based on big data

**Human moat:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an engineering professional in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by specialization and experience; entry-level positions start around €35,000–€42,000 annually, while senior engineers earn €50,000–€70,000. The median for all engineering professionals is approximately €48,000. Compensation is competitive, with benefits like occupational healthcare.

**Q: What is the fastest way to get a work visa as a non-EU engineer?**
A: The D-Visa fast track is ideal for specialists in shortage fields. It allows you to start working in Finland while the residence permit is processed. Alternatively, the Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL) takes 2–6 months. An EU Blue Card may also apply for highly skilled roles.

**Q: How are foreign engineering degrees recognized in Finland?**
A: The Finnish National Agency for Education (EDUFI) evaluates degrees for equivalence. Many engineering roles do not require formal recognition, but some regulated positions (e.g., in public infrastructure) may need approval from FISE or a similar body. Finnish language skills are often key to recognition.
