# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers perform physical tasks in mines, quarries, and gravel pits, including drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials. In Finland, this occupation is concentrated in regions like Lapland and Kainuu, where metal mines (e.g., copper, nickel, gold) and industrial mineral quarries operate. The work is seasonal in some quarries but year-round in larger mines. Employers value safety compliance and physical fitness. Automation is reducing demand for manual roles, but skilled labourers with machine operation certifications remain needed.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 4. Finland's mining sector faces a moderate labour shortage for experienced workers, but overall employment for labourers is expected to decline slightly due to automation and mechanization. However, new mine projects (e.g., lithium and battery minerals) may create temporary demand. The workforce is aging, and younger Finns are less attracted to remote, physically taxing jobs. Immigration from EU/EEA countries is common, but non-EU workers face permit restrictions unless employers demonstrate local unavailability.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from manual labourer to machine operator (e.g., excavator, haul truck), then to foreman or site supervisor. With experience, workers can move into blasting or drilling specialist roles, which require additional certifications. Some transition to maintenance or safety inspector roles. In Finland, vocational training (e.g., in mining technology) can accelerate advancement. Mobility between mines is high, but remote locations limit local opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- Mining
- Quarrying
- Mineral Extraction
- Circular Economy

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Physically fit individuals comfortable with heavy lifting, working outdoors in all weather, and remote locations
- People who prefer practical, hands-on work and are safety-conscious
- Those willing to work shifts (including nights) and tolerate noise, dust, and confined spaces

**Who is not suited**
- Individuals seeking desk jobs or high-tech careers with minimal physical labour
- Those who dislike cold climates, isolation, or repetitive manual tasks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pre-tax salary for a mining and quarrying labourer in Finland?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries for entry-level labourers range from approximately €30,000 to €40,000, depending on experience, shift allowances, and location. Experienced miners can earn up to €50,000.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for mining labourer jobs in Finland?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The employer must prove that no suitable EU/EEA worker is available. The job must meet wage and working condition standards. The residence permit is employer-specific, and changing jobs requires a new permit.
