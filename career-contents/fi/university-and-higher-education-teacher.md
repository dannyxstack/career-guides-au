# University And Higher Education Teacher — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2310 (ISCO08)**

University and higher education teachers in Finland deliver lectures, seminars, and tutorials at universities and universities of applied sciences. They conduct research, supervise students, and contribute to academic administration. Positions typically require a doctoral degree, especially for tenure-track roles. The labour market is competitive, with permanent positions often limited due to project-based funding. However, there is stable demand in fields like STEM, health sciences, and digitalization. Internationalization is strong, with many programs taught in English.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 18,543. Employment outlook for university teachers is stable overall but varies by discipline. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically follows a tenure-track path: assistant professor → associate professor → full professor. Alternatively, teaching-focused roles (university lecturer) can advance to senior lecturer. Emphasis on research output, grant acquisition, and international collaboration is crucial. Many start with fixed-term contracts before securing a permanent position. Pedagogical training (e.g., 60 ECTS) is often required. Mobility between Finnish universities and abroad is common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making
- Creative integration in overall curriculum design

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI education tools (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning).
- Data analysis and statistical modeling (Python/R)
- Prompt engineering and large model fine-tuning
- Blended instructional design (MOOC/flipped classroom)
- Academic writing and AI-assisted polishing
- Data Privacy and AI Ethics

**Key growth areas:**

- Digitalization
- Sustainability
- Health Sciences
- AI & Data Science

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for university teachers in Finland?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements (OVTES) and increase with seniority and performance. Entry-level lecturers earn a competitive middle-class income, while full professors earn significantly more. Exact figures depend on the university, field, and experience.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens become university teachers in Finland?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens can obtain a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) or an EU Blue Card if they have a job offer and meet salary and qualification requirements. Universities often sponsor work permits for qualified candidates.

**Q: Do I need a PhD to teach at a Finnish university?**
A: A PhD is typically required for tenure-track and permanent positions. Some lecturer roles may accept a master's degree with extensive experience, but a doctorate is strongly preferred for career advancement.
