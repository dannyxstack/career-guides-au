# Tailor — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

In Finland, tailors, dressmakers, furriers, and hatters are skilled craftspeople who create, alter, and repair garments and accessories. The profession is small but valued, particularly in custom tailoring, costume production for theatre and film, and the preservation of traditional clothing. Many professionals work as sole traders or in small ateliers, serving niche markets. The industry is supported by vocational education in clothing and textiles, and opportunities exist in both urban centers and rural areas with a focus on high-quality handmade goods.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational qualification in clothing and textiles, followed by apprenticeship or on-the-job training. Experienced tailors may advance to master tailor status (kisälli/mestari) or specialize in areas like haute couture, fur, or costume design. Many eventually start their own businesses, often combining retail with tailoring services. Further education in fashion design or pattern making can lead to roles in fashion houses, theatre companies, or museums. Entrepreneurship is common, offering control over creative direction and clientele.

- Automated sewing robot completes standard sewing tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design tools to quickly generate personalized patterns and styles

**Human moat:**

- Creative design and impromptu modification skills in handmade haute couture
- Fine handling and restoration of complex fabrics (e.g., leather, fur)
- Build trust with clients and understand personalized needs
- Artistry of traditional handicraft techniques (e.g., embroidery, beadwork)
- Deep understanding and ability to reconstruct clothing history and culture.

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tailor in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer, but the median monthly earnings for tailors in Finland are around €2,200–€2,800. Self-employed tailors may have variable income. For exact figures, refer to Statistics Finland or sector-specific collective agreements.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU tailors?**
A: Non-EU tailors can apply for a residence permit for employed persons (TTOL) with a valid job offer. Self-employed tailors may use the D-visa for entrepreneurs. EU/EEA citizens enjoy free movement and only need registration after 3 months.

**Q: How can I advance my career as a tailor in Finland?**
A: Further vocational training and master tailor certification can lead to higher positions. Specializing in areas like fur, bridal wear, or costume design offers niche opportunities. Many tailors eventually open their own atelier or combine tailoring with teaching.
