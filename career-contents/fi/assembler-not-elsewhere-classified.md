# Assemblers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 8219 (ISCO08)**

Assemblers not elsewhere classified (ISCO 8219) manually assemble products from components in Finnish manufacturing industries such as electronics, machinery, and metal products. They work on production lines, following detailed instructions and quality standards. The role requires precision, dexterity, and the ability to use hand and power tools. In Finland, these positions are common in export-oriented sectors and subcontracting chains.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for assemblers often starts with on-the-job training and leads to roles such as team leader, quality inspector, or production technician. With additional vocational qualifications (e.g., mechanical engineering), assemblers can advance to more skilled positions or supervisory roles. Some move into maintenance or automation support. The field offers stable employment but limited upward mobility without further education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive parts assembly (e.g., electronic component insertion, screw fastening)
- Vision-Based Quality Inspection (AI Vision System Replacement)
- Simple material handling and sorting (replaced by AMR)
- Record production data (replaced by automated MES system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for real-time assembly guidance, reducing training time
- Use collaborative robots for heavy or precision assembly, with worker supervision and fine-tuning
- Use AI predictive maintenance tools to monitor equipment and replace worn parts in advance
- Optimize processes based on digital twin simulation of assembly workflows

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard parts or unexpected anomalies (e.g., flexible adjustments when part tolerance is out of spec)
- Hand-finishing small-batch, multi-variety custom assemblies
- Make experience-based comprehensive judgments on assembly quality
- Cross-workstation collaboration and on-site problem-solving skills

**Skills to build:**

- Collaborative robot programming and debugging.
- Operation of digital twin and AR-assisted assembly systems
- Basic data analysis (reading from MES system to improve efficiency)
- Mechatronics maintenance skills
- Lean production and continuous improvement methods
- Cross-disciplinary communication (liaising with engineers, suppliers).

**Key growth areas:**

- Electronics assembly
- Precision assembly
- Green technology
- Medical devices

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What factors affect assembler salaries in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by industry, experience, and region. The metal and electronics sectors typically pay at the upper end. Collective agreements set minimum wages, and shift work or overtime can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as an assembler in Finland?**
A: Yes, through the Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The employer must prove that no suitable EU/EEA worker is available. The job must meet collective agreement terms and provide full-time work.

**Q: What are the typical career paths for assemblers in Finland?**
A: Starting as an assembler, you can advance to team leader, inspector, or technician with additional vocational training. Some progress into maintenance or automation roles.
