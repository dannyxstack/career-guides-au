# Health Professional (Other) — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

Health professionals not elsewhere classified (ISCO 2269) include diverse roles such as audiologists, dietitians, occupational therapists, speech therapists, and prosthetists. In Finland, these professionals work primarily in public healthcare (hospitals, health centers) and private clinics. The sector is regulated by Valvira, requiring specific education and licensing. Demand is growing due to an aging population and increased focus on preventive and rehabilitative care.

Finland's healthcare system faces a growing shortage of specialized health professionals, especially in aging-related fields like occupational and speech therapy. The government has increased training slots and recruitment from abroad. Digitalization and home care expansion create new opportunities. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression often begins with a Master's degree and clinical specialization. Many advance to senior clinical roles, management positions (e.g., head of department), or research. Continuing education is mandatory to maintain licensure. There is a path to university research and teaching. Mobility across EU/EEA is possible with recognition of qualifications, but Finnish language proficiency is critical for patient-facing roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate nutrition assessment reports and meal plans
- Preliminary analysis and classification of hearing test results
- Standard pronunciation practice and feedback in speech therapy
- Medical documentation organisation and medical summary generation
- Preliminary screening and sorting of some diagnostic information

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic tools improve accuracy of hearing and speech disorder identification
- Smart nutrition analysis platform optimizes personalized dietary plans
- Voice synthesis and recognition technology enhances language therapy interaction.
- AI-driven dynamic adjustment of rehabilitation plans
- Big data analytics supports epidemiology and efficacy studies

**Human moat:**

- Clinical judgment and comprehensive decision-making for complex cases
- Patient Emotional Support and Human Care
- Multidisciplinary team coordination and communication
- Personalized adjustment of treatment plans and ethical judgment
- Flexibility to handle unexpected situations

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted Diagnostic Tool Operation and Result Interpretation
- Health data analysis and statistics
- Digital health platforms and telemedicine applications
- Interdisciplinary Collaboration and Communication Skills
- Ethics and privacy protection knowledge
- Continuous learning and adapting to new technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Digital health
- Integrated care
- Rehabilitation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Finland admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Professionals seeking stable, regulated work in a high-trust healthcare system
- Individuals with strong Finnish language skills and flexibility to work in public sector
- Specialists in rehabilitation or therapy fields aiming for career progression through research

**Who is not suited**
- Those unwilling to undergo licensing process and language exams

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a health professional in this category in Finland?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries range from €35,000 (entry level) to €55,000 (experienced), with a median around €45,000. Public sector follows collective agreements; private sector may vary.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to apply for a work visa as a health professional?**
A: Yes, a valid job offer and a positive decision from Valvira (licensing) are required for a residence permit. You must also meet salary thresholds. For EU/EEA citizens, free movement applies.

**Q: How long does it take to get my foreign qualifications recognized in Finland?**
A: Recognition by Valvira typically takes 3-6 months, but can be longer if additional studies or language training are needed. Plan accordingly.
