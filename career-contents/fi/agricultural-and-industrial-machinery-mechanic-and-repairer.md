# Agricultural and Industrial Machinery Mechanics And Repairers — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7233 (ISCO08)**

Agricultural and industrial machinery mechanics and repairers maintain, diagnose, and repair machinery used in farming, forestry, and industrial production. In Finland, this occupation is vital for the agriculture and forestry sectors as well as for manufacturing and construction. The role requires strong technical skills and familiarity with both mechanical and electronic systems. Working conditions often involve outdoor and workshop environments, and the job is physically demanding but offers diverse tasks. With the increasing automation and digitalization of machinery, mechanics also need skills in diagnostics and computer-based systems.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 18,713. The demand for machinery mechanics in Finland is stable, driven by the need to maintain aging equipment and the shift towards more automated and environmentally friendly machinery. The green transition and renewable energy projects, such as wind and solar power installations, are creating new opportunities. However, the overall employment growth is moderate, and there is a shortage of skilled workers, especially in rural areas. Mechanization trends in agriculture and forestry continue, sustaining demand for qualified mechanics.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational qualification, followed by on-the-job experience. Mechanics can specialize in agricultural, forestry, or industrial machinery, or move into sales, service management, or entrepreneurship by starting their own repair workshop. Further vocational qualifications or specialization in areas like hydraulics, electronics, or welding can lead to higher wages and senior roles. Some mechanics transition to related fields such as heavy equipment operation or technical teaching.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Regular maintenance and simple troubleshooting (such as replacing filters, belts), automated system can monitor and alert
- Remote Monitoring and Predictive Maintenance, AI Analysis of Sensor Data for Automatic Alerts
- Spare parts inventory management and ordering, with system-optimized inventory
- Repair reports and document organization, AI-generated
- Basic parameter calibration, assisted by automation tools

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis: AI-assisted analysis of historical data and real-time parameters to provide troubleshooting suggestions
- Maintenance decision support: augmented reality (AR) displays repair steps and hidden parts
- Training and skill enhancement: AI mentors customize learning paths and simulate fault scenarios.
- Tool and equipment operation: AI optimizes and adjusts maintenance parameters, improving precision
- Customer communication: AI generates plain-language explanations and repair plans to build trust

**Human moat:**

- Creative repair of non-standard equipment, requiring hands-on modification and adaptability
- On-site safety judgment and emergency handling
- Client relationship maintenance and communication.
- First-time fault diagnosis and maintenance manual development for new machinery
- Teamwork and cross-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Machine Learning Basics (for diagnostic tools)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor technology
- Augmented reality repair tool usage
- Data analysis and interpretation skills
- Knowledge of electrical and electronic controls
- Traditional skills such as advanced welding and precision assembly

**Key growth areas:**

- green transition
- automation
- renewable energy
- digital diagnostics

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Hands-on individuals who enjoy mechanical problem-solving and working with tools.
- People who like varied tasks and are comfortable working outdoors or in workshops.
- Those interested in sustainable technologies and the green transition in agriculture and industry.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for this role in Finland?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by experience, sector, and region. They are generally in line with other technical trades in Finland. For current figures, consult Statistics Finland or industry salary surveys.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for this job?**
A: Yes, provided you have a job offer from a Finnish employer and meet the conditions for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The occupation is on the shortage list, which can facilitate the process. EU/EEA nationals do not need a visa.

**Q: Do I need a formal qualification to work as a machinery mechanic in Finland?**
A: While not legally mandatory, employers typically require a vocational qualification in machinery or a related field. International qualifications may be accepted if deemed equivalent. On-the-job training is possible but less common.
