# Domestic Cleaner and Helper — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9111 (ISCO08)**

Domestic cleaners and helpers perform cleaning and household tasks in private homes, such as sweeping, mopping, dusting, laundering, and basic organizing. In Finland, this occupation is characterized by part-time and informal employment, often filled by students, retirees, or immigrants on family ties. The sector is not highly regulated, and many positions are arranged through private agreements or small cleaning companies. Demand is steady due to aging population and dual-income households, but wages are low and benefits limited.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,096. Employment outlook is stable but with limited growth. The aging population increases demand for home cleaning services, especially in urban areas. However, automation (robot vacuums, smart home tech) may reduce demand for routine cleaning. Job turnover is high, and many positions are part-time. The sector is not expected to face labor shortages, as it attracts a flexible workforce. Immigration from EU/EEA countries provides a steady supply of workers, but non-EU workers face visa restrictions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most domestic cleaners and helpers remain in the same role. Some may advance to supervisory positions in cleaning companies, start their own small business, or specialize in eco-friendly or deep-cleaning services. Formal training is rarely required, but certification in cleaning techniques or languages can improve employability. The role is often a stepping stone for new immigrants or temporary workers, with few long-term career paths.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Schedule cleaning appointments, send reminders, and collect payments via chatbot
- Use computer vision to inspect cleaning quality and generate reports
- Automated inventory management: predict cleaning supply needs based on historical data and place orders
- Voice assistants control smart home devices (e.g., robot vacuum cleaners, automatic curtains)
- Standardized cleaning process documents generated and updated by AI.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI voice translation tool assists in communicating with foreign employers
- Smart devices (e.g., stain detection apps) to assist in choosing the best cleaning agent
- Data analysis tools optimize cleaning routes and time allocation
- Online platform automatically matches customers and cleaners, improving customer acquisition efficiency
- Augmented reality (AR) guidance for cleaning complex furniture.

**Human moat:**

- Fine manual cleaning (e.g., antiques, silk, and other special materials)
- Building long-term trust relationships and personalized services with employers
- Ability to handle unexpected situations (e.g., spills, damage) with flexibility
- manual labour and on-site presence (physical contact that cannot be replaced)
- Highly customized home organization and decluttering solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart cleaning equipment
- Basic English or a second foreign language (for smart assistants and client communication)
- Time Management and Digital Scheduling Tool Usage
- Knowledge of household chemical safety and intelligent proportioning
- Fundamentals of Customer Relationship Management (CRM) software
- Basic data analysis (to optimize service efficiency)

**Key growth areas:**

- Senior Care Cleaning
- Eco-Friendly Products
- Specialized Deep Cleaning
- Urban Services

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a domestic cleaner in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary, but are generally low. According to collective agreements, the minimum hourly wage for cleaning workers is around €10-12 per hour, depending on experience and employer. Full-time annual earnings are often under €25,000 before tax. Many workers receive additional benefits like meal vouchers.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a visa to work as a domestic cleaner in Finland?**
A: It is very difficult. The residence permit for employed persons requires a job offer with salary meeting a minimum threshold (€1,339/month in 2024) and that the employer cannot find a suitable worker in Finland or EU. For low-skilled cleaning, this condition is rarely met. Most non-EU cleaners come on family ties or study permits.

**Q: Do I need formal training to work as a domestic cleaner in Finland?**
A: No, formal training is not mandatory. Many workers learn on the job. However, a vocational qualification in cleaning (siivooja) can improve job opportunities and salary. Basic Finnish language skills are often required for communication with clients.
