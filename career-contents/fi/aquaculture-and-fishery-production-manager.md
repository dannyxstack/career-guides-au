# Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 1312 (ISCO08)**

Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers oversee the operations of fish farms, hatcheries, and fishing enterprises in Finland, focusing on sustainable production, resource management, and regulatory compliance. Finland's aquaculture sector is a growing part of the blue bioeconomy, with significant investments in land-based recirculating systems and offshore farming to meet demand for domestic protein. Managers coordinate breeding, feeding, harvesting, and water quality control while adhering to strict EU and national environmental standards. The role requires expertise in both biological sciences and business management, often involving collaboration with research institutions and export markets.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 15. Finland's aquaculture industry is expanding due to increased demand for sustainable seafood and government support for the blue bioeconomy. Employment outlook is positive, with growth driven by technological advancements and export opportunities. However, the sector faces challenges like climate change impacts and competition for coastal zones. Managers with skills in recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS), digital monitoring, and circular economy practices are in demand. The aging workforce in traditional fisheries also opens opportunities for new entrants. Overall, moderate growth is expected over the next decade, with some regional variation along the coast and in inland lake regions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from roles like fish farm technician or biologist, advancing to shift supervisor, then production manager. Larger companies may offer paths to regional or country-level management. Specialisation in areas like fish health, genetics, or processing can lead to senior technical roles. Continuing education in business administration or environmental management is beneficial. The field is relatively niche, so networking and practical experience are key. Some managers transition into consulting or entrepreneurship, especially in innovative farming systems. Academic roles in research and teaching are also possible for those with advanced degrees.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable aquaculture
- Recirculating systems
- Digital monitoring
- Export markets

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with strong leadership and business management skills who enjoy outdoor and marine environments.
- Individuals with a background in biology, environmental science, or aquaculture with hands-on technical aptitude.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and company size. Entry-level managers earn around €3,000-€3,500 per month, while experienced managers can earn €4,500-€6,000+ per month. Annual figures are typically €36,000-€72,000, with bonuses possible.

**Q: How difficult is it to get a work visa for this role as a non-EU/EEA citizen?**
A: It is manageable if you have a job offer with at least €1,399/month salary (TTOL permit). For the EU Blue Card, a higher salary (€4,081/month) is required. The process involves employer sponsorship and application to the Finnish Immigration Service. The occupation is on the shortage list, which may expedite processing.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities in this field?**
A: Managers can progress to regional director roles, start their own farming operations, or move into consulting, research, or policy development with the government or EU. Advanced degrees open doors to academic positions.
