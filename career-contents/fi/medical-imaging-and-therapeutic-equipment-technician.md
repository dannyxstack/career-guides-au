# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technicians, known as röntgenhoitajat in Finland, operate diagnostic imaging devices (X-ray, MRI, CT, ultrasound) and administer radiotherapy treatments. They work primarily in public hospitals and private clinics, with a strong role in patient care. The profession is regulated by Valvira, requiring a Bachelor's degree from a University of Applied Sciences (AMK) in Radiography and Radiotherapy. Finland's universal healthcare system ensures steady demand for imaging and therapy services, with emphasis on technological advancements and patient safety.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 3,080. Finland faces a growing demand for diagnostic imaging and radiotherapy services due to an aging population and increased cancer incidence. The number of radiography and radiotherapy professionals is expected to increase by about 1-2% annually, with many retirements among the current workforce creating job openings. Regional shortages exist, especially in remote areas. The government has prioritized healthcare workforce recruitment, including targeted migration routes for qualified professionals. Overall, employment prospects are positive with stable to growing demand over the next decade.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a radiographer or radiation therapist. With experience and further education (e.g., Master's degree in advanced clinical practice), technicians can move into specialized roles such as MRI or CT specialist, chief radiographer, or department manager. Opportunities also exist in equipment sales, training, or research, especially with the rise of AI-assisted diagnostics. Licensed professionals can also work as teachers in UAS programs. The field values continuous learning and adaptation to new technologies.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a medical imaging technician in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, location, and sector, but are competitive within the healthcare field. Entry-level positions typically offer lower pay, while experienced specialists and those in leadership roles earn higher salaries. Exact figures are available through union surveys and Statistics Finland.

**Q: What are the work permit requirements for non-EU medical imaging technicians?**
A: Non-EU professionals need a job offer from a Finnish employer. The main routes are the residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) or the EU Blue Card. The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled locally. The profession is on the shortage list, which can expedite processing. License from Valvira is also required.

**Q: Is Finnish language proficiency required to work as a radiographer?**
A: Yes, sufficient Finnish (or Swedish) language skills are mandatory for licensing, as patient communication and documentation are in the local language. Typically B1 level is required, and training is available through language courses.
