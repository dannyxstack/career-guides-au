# Physiotherapists — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Finland work in public healthcare (hospitals, health centers), private clinics, and increasingly in occupational health and elderly care. The profession is highly regulated with a protected title requiring a bachelor's degree from a university of applied sciences. Demand is strong due to an aging population and growing emphasis on preventive care and rehabilitation. The field is female-dominated but male participation is increasing. Finland has a well-developed healthcare system with high standards.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression often involves specialization in areas such as manual therapy, sports physiotherapy, pediatrics, or neurology. Experienced physiotherapists may advance to managerial roles, teaching at universities of applied sciences, or research positions. Continuing education and postgraduate degrees (e.g., master's in rehabilitation) can lead to higher salaries and more senior roles. Many professionals also pursue entrepreneurial opportunities by opening their own clinics.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient record organization and electronic health record entry
- Scoring and data analysis of standardized assessment scales.
- Generate routine reports on treatment progress
- Administrative tasks such as appointment scheduling and patient reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assists in analyzing patient movement data to optimize personalized treatment plans
- Utilize virtual reality technology for remote rehabilitation guidance and monitoring
- AI predicts patient recovery trajectories, adjusting intervention plans in advance
- Recommending optimal treatment exercise combinations based on big data
- Automatically generate patient education materials to improve compliance

**Human moat:**

- Manual therapy based on palpation and movement analysis
- Communication to build trust and emotional support with patients
- Clinical reasoning and real-time adjustments for complex cases
- Professional judgment in interdisciplinary team collaboration
- Flexibility in handling emergencies and patient individual differences

**Skills to build:**

- Master AI rehabilitation analysis software and remote monitoring platforms
- Study sports science and biomechanics data analysis
- Enhance the application of digital health tools.
- Enhanced Patient Communication and Motivation Skills
- Understand evidence-based practice and clinical decision support systems
- Continuously follow the latest research on rehabilitation AI

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Occupational health
- Rehabilitation
- Digital physiotherapy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapist in Finland?**
A: The median monthly salary for a physiotherapist in Finland is approximately 2,800–3,500 EUR gross, depending on experience and sector. Public sector salaries are often set by collective agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EU physiotherapist get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer and your qualifications are recognized by Valvira. You apply for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The process can take several months.

**Q: Is physiotherapy a shortage occupation in Finland?**
A: Yes, physiotherapists are on the national shortage list, especially in certain regions and for specialized fields like geriatric and neurological rehabilitation.
