# Other Language Teacher — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2353 (ISCO08)**

Other language teachers in Finland teach languages that are not Finnish, Swedish, or the most common foreign languages like English, including lesser-taught languages such as Russian, Spanish, French, German, or others. They work in various educational settings, from primary schools to adult education centers, universities, and private language schools. The demand for language teachers is driven by multiculturalism, internationalization, and the need for diverse language skills in business and public services.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 325. The employment outlook for other language teachers in Finland is stable, with moderate growth expected due to increasing immigration and the need for language skills in a globalized economy. However, the number of jobs is relatively small and concentrated in urban areas. Job opportunities arise from retirements and part-time positions. The shortage of certain language teachers, especially for less commonly taught languages like Russian or Spanish, persists, but competition can be strong for popular languages like French or German.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for other language teachers typically involves moving from classroom teaching to senior teacher roles, head of department, or specialization in curriculum development. Some transition to adult education, corporate training, or university teaching, which may require a higher degree. Advances in digital tools and online language learning platforms offer new opportunities for independent tutoring and business development. Permanent positions are common in public schools, while private language schools offer more flexibility but less job security.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic Grammar and Vocabulary Instruction
- Standardized exercise grading and feedback
- Language proficiency test score
- Simple conversation practice and correction

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Personalized learning path design and recommendation (AI analyzes student weaknesses)
- Immersive virtual reality language environment creation (AI-generated situational dialogues)
- Real-time voice correction and pronunciation analysis
- Cultural background knowledge integration and dynamic courseware generation

**Human moat:**

- Emotional support and motivation for learning
- Cross-cultural communication and contextual interpretation
- Facilitating complex high-level discussions
- Creative writing and literary appreciation guidance.
- Emergency classroom interaction management

**Skills to build:**

- Use of AI teaching tools (e.g., ChatGPT, speech recognition platforms)
- Data Analysis and Learning Outcome Evaluation
- Course design and blended teaching ability
- Knowledge of industry-specific language applications (e.g., business, law)
- Multilingual and Cross-Cultural Communication Skills
- Digital content creation (video, interactive courseware)

**Key growth areas:**

- Online language teaching
- Corporate language training
- Integration courses for immigrants
- Lesser-taught languages

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an other language teacher in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by employer and education level. In public schools, the monthly salary for a qualified teacher is roughly 2,500-3,500 EUR, corresponding to an annual salary of 30,000-42,000 EUR. Private language schools may offer lower pay.

**Q: Can I teach a foreign language in Finland without speaking Finnish?**
A: Yes, for teaching your native language (e.g., English, Spanish) in private language schools or as a foreign language teacher in secondary schools, you may not need Finnish. However, for integration courses or public school positions, Finnish or Swedish proficiency is mandatory.
