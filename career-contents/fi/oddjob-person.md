# Odd-job Person — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons perform a variety of simple, manual tasks for short-term or one-off assignments, such as cleaning, carrying, moving goods, assisting in events, or light maintenance. In Finland, this occupation is common in temp agencies, property maintenance, and event services. Work is often part-time or seasonal, with varying hours. The job does not require formal qualifications, but basic Finnish language skills and reliability improve employability.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 264. The outlook for odd-job persons in Finland is stable but limited. Demand exists in temporary staffing for events, construction support, and cleaning, but automation and digital platforms are reducing needs for general manual labor. The COVID-19 pandemic temporarily boosted demand for disinfecting and light logistics. Overall, job openings are moderate and primarily filled by students, short-term workers, or those with flexible availability.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is minimal; odd-job persons may move into more specialized roles like cleaner, maintenance assistant, or warehouse worker with on-the-job experience. Some transition to supervisor roles in temp agencies. Long-term career development usually requires additional training, such as a vocational qualification in cleaning services or logistics. The role is often a stepping stone to more stable employment.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- temporary staffing
- event services
- light maintenance
- property management

Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**

- Students or part-time workers balancing other activities.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Finland?**
A: Hourly wages typically range from €10 to €14 per hour, depending on the task and employer. Monthly earnings for full-time work (if available) are around €1,800–€2,400 before tax, but many positions are part-time or irregular.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a residence permit for odd-job work?**
A: It is very difficult. The occupation is not considered skilled, and the salary often falls below the minimum required for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The employer must prove a labor shortage, which is rare for these roles. EU/EEA citizens have the easiest access.

**Q: What are the main employers for odd-job persons in Finland?**
A: Temporary employment agencies (e.g., StaffPoint, Barona), property management companies, event organizers, and small maintenance firms are the primary employers. Many jobs are found through online platforms or word-of-mouth.
