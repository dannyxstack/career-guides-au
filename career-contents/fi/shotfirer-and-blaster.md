# Shotfirer and Blaster — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters are specialized tradespeople who handle explosives in Finland's mining, quarrying, and construction sectors, including large infrastructure and demolition projects. The role demands rigorous safety adherence and certification from Tukes (Safety and Chemicals Agency). With Finland's active mining industry (copper, nickel, gold) and ongoing infrastructure development, demand is steady, especially in remote northern regions. The occupation requires a vocational qualification and a blasting license, with advancement possible to supervisor or mine management roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a blasting assistant, advancing to licensed blaster after apprenticeships and certification. With experience, one can specialize in underwater, seismic, or controlled demolition blasting. Further steps include blasting supervisor, safety inspector, or mine manager. Continuing education in explosives technology and safety management enhances prospects. The path is structured but offers clear milestones and increasing responsibility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Mining
- Infrastructure projects
- Demolition
- Quarrying

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shotfirer in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, location, and sector, but are generally competitive due to demand and risk. Entry-level blasters earn around €30,000–€35,000 annually; experienced blasters can earn €45,000–€55,000 or more, with overtime and remote area allowances.

**Q: Can a non-EU shotfirer get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, through the residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). You need a job offer from a Finnish employer who can demonstrate that no suitable local labour is available. The permit requires meeting a minimum salary threshold and holding the necessary Finnish certifications (e.g., blasting license).

**Q: What education is required to become a shotfirer in Finland?**
A: A vocational qualification in mining or rock drilling (3 years) or an apprenticeship (2–4 years) is typical. Additionally, a blasting license from Tukes is mandatory. Both pathways include on-the-job training and safety courses.
