# Valuer and Loss Assessor — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 3315 (ISCO08)**

Valuers and loss assessors (arvioijat) in Finland provide expert valuations for real estate, personal property, and insurance claims, serving banks, insurance companies, appraisal firms, and government agencies. They determine market values, replacement costs, and damage estimates for transactions, taxation, and claims settlement. The role demands strong analytical skills, knowledge of property markets, and valuation methods. In Finland, the profession supports the real estate, insurance, and finance sectors, with digitalization influencing routine valuations while complex assignments require human judgment.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior assistant, advancing to senior valuer, team leader, or manager. Specialisation in commercial, industrial, or agricultural property or loss adjusting enhances prospects. Certification from the Finnish Association of Real Estate Valuers (SKV) is highly valued and often required for senior roles. Further training in sustainable building valuation, digital tools, or international standards opens additional pathways. Experienced professionals may also become consultants or start their own firms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Preliminary data collation and analysis for automated property valuation reports
- Standard loss amount calculation based on historical data
- Online image assessment for minor vehicle damage
- Automatic Classification and Entry of Insurance Claim Documents
- Input parameters for conventional real estate valuation models

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to identify anomalies and patterns in loss images.
- Leverage big data analytics to predict market value trends
- AI-assisted rapid generation of complex assessment report drafts
- Using natural language processing to automatically handle customer inquiries
- Improve valuation accuracy through machine learning models

**Human moat:**

- On-site inspection and physical access capability
- Independent judgment for complex or non-standard damage
- Legal and ethical compliance responsibilities
- Client trust building and communication negotiation
- Creativity in handling special cases

**Skills to build:**

- Machine learning fundamentals and model interpretation
- Geographic information systems and drone operation.
- Data visualization and analysis tools (e.g., Python, R)
- AI-assisted assessment software operation
- Enhance communication and client management skills
- Interdisciplinary knowledge (e.g., law, finance)

**Key growth areas:**

- real estate

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for valuers in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and location. Entry-level positions offer around €30,000–€40,000 annually, while experienced valuers earn €50,000–€70,000. Insurance loss assessors may have similar ranges. Certification and specialisation can boost earnings.

**Q: How can a non-EU valuer obtain a work visa for Finland?**
A: The most common route is the Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL), requiring a job offer with salary above €1,339/month. Alternatively, the EU Blue Card requires €3,499/month and a higher education degree. EU/EEA citizens need no permit.

**Q: Do I need a specific licence to work as a valuer in Finland?**
A: No mandatory licence, but professional certification (e.g., Certified Real Estate Valuer from SKV) is often required by employers or for certain assignments. Loss assessors may need insurance-specific training.
