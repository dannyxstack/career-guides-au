# Mixed Crop And Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

Mixed crop and livestock farm labourers perform a variety of manual tasks on farms that combine crop cultivation and animal husbandry. In Finland, this role is common on small to medium-sized family farms, especially in rural areas of Ostrobothnia, South-West Finland, and Eastern Finland. Duties include planting and harvesting crops, feeding and caring for livestock, maintaining farm equipment, and general farm upkeep. The work is seasonal, with peak demand during planting and harvest times. Many labourers are employed on a temporary or part-time basis, often through agricultural staffing agencies. The occupation requires physical stamina and willingness to work outdoors in variable weather conditions.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 11. Employment prospects for mixed crop and livestock farm labourers in Finland reflect broader trends in agriculture: consolidation of farms into larger units and increasing use of automation. The number of traditional labour-intensive farms is declining, but there remains steady demand for manual labour during peak seasons, especially in organic farming and niche production. The Finnish agricultural sector faces an ageing workforce, creating some replacement demand. However, overall job openings are limited and competition is moderate. The occupation is sensitive to EU agricultural policy and global market prices for dairy, meat, and grain. Seasonal labour shortages are occasionally reported, but permanent positions are scarce.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for farm labourers is limited without additional training. Many remain in casual or seasonal roles, but some advance to become farm managers, herd managers, or pursue specialised roles in organic farming, animal husbandry, or agritourism. In Finland, vocational qualifications in agriculture (e.g., maatalousalan perustutkinto) can enable transition to more skilled positions. Entrepreneurship is a possible path for those who acquire land or lease a farm. However, due to the declining number of farms, many labourers eventually leave the sector for other manual trades or retrain for emerging green jobs in environmental land management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using automated tractors for plowing, sowing, and harvesting
- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots
- Use drones to inspect farms and produce yield maps
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans based on historical data and weather forecasts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining smart agricultural equipment
- Using data analysis to improve crop yields and herd health
- Develop comprehensive agricultural strategies to balance ecology and economy
- Collaborate with AI for precision agriculture management
- Train new employees in using digital tools

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of sudden mechanical faults
- Unstructured work in complex terrain or extreme weather conditions.
- Animal emergency medical care and behavioural intervention
- Compliance with organic certification and animal welfare standards.

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural drone operation and data analysis certification
- Use of precision agriculture software (e.g., FarmOS, Climate FieldView)
- Basic electrical and mechanical maintenance skills
- Digital literacy: Excel/data recording apps
- Basics of crop and livestock health management
- Training for safe operation of AGV/robot equipment

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Precision agriculture
- Seasonal labour
- Niche livestock

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mixed crop and livestock farm labourer in Finland?**
A: Salaries are modest, typically ranging from €1,600 to €2,000 per month for full-time work, with seasonal variations. Overtime and piece-rate work (e.g., berry picking) can increase earnings. The average annual gross salary is about €22,000–24,000. Note that many positions are part-time or seasonal, so annual earnings may be lower.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens get a work visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Non-EU workers can apply for a Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL) or a seasonal work permit if they have a job offer. The pay must meet the minimum threshold (€1,240/month in 2025). However, the occupation is not on the shortage list, so the permit is granted for the specific job and employer. Seasonal permits are limited to 6 months per year and do not lead to permanent residence.
