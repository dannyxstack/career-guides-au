# Welder and Flame Cutter — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7212 (ISCO08)**

Welders and flame cutters are essential in Finland's metal industry, shipbuilding, construction, and manufacturing sectors. They join or cut metal parts using various techniques, including MIG, TIG, and arc welding, as well as flame cutting. The profession requires precision, technical skill, and adherence to safety standards. With an aging workforce and sustained industrial activity, welders remain in steady demand across Finland, particularly in regions with strong maritime and engineering clusters.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 7,212. Finland's welding workforce faces a shortage due to retirements and a limited influx of new trainees. Demand is projected to remain stable, driven by investments in infrastructure, renewable energy (e.g., offshore wind), and shipbuilding. Automation may shift some tasks, but skilled manual welders are still needed for complex and custom work. The medium-term outlook is positive for qualified professionals who stay updated with certifications.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with vocational training and entry-level positions. Experienced welders can advance to specialist roles, such as welding inspector or supervisor, or move into entrepreneurship by starting their own workshop. Continuous learning in new techniques (e.g., robotic welding) and obtaining international certifications (e.g., ISO 3834) can enhance opportunities. Some welders transition to related fields like metal fabrication or engineering.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch standardised metal parts automatic welding (e.g., car body welding, pipe joints)
- Robot programming and path planning based on CAD models replace manual teaching
- Online weld quality inspection (visual inspection + AI defect detection)
- Automatic adjustment of welding parameters and welding process monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted welding parameter optimization (real-time recommendation of current/voltage based on material thickness and ambient temperature)
- Augmented reality (AR) welding guidance system improves novice training efficiency
- Drones/crawler robots equipped with welding modules for high-altitude or hazardous welding
- Digital twin welding simulation reduces material waste from test pieces
- AI-driven multi-robot collaborative welding group control

**Human moat:**

- On-site manual welding of non-standard workpieces and confined spaces (e.g., on ships, inside pipes)
- Welding of pressure vessels and load-bearing structures with high stress and fatigue life requirements
- Welding process development for dissimilar metals and special alloys (titanium, aluminum-magnesium alloys)
- Welding quality responsibility determination and repair decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Programming and debugging of welding robots (e.g., FANUC, KUKA, ABB)
- Welding process simulation software (e.g., Simufact Welding, SYSWELD)
- Automated welding quality control and testing methods
- Digital manufacturing basics (CAD/CAM, BOM management)
- Advanced certifications such as International Welding Engineer (IWE)
- Operation of AR/VR welding simulation training systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Shipbuilding
- Steel structures
- Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a welder in Finland?**
A: Salaries for welders range from €30,000 to €45,000 per year, depending on experience, certifications, and sector. Entry-level positions may start around €28,000, while experienced specialists can earn up to €50,000.

**Q: How can a non-EU welder obtain a work visa for Finland?**
A: The main route is the Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL). You need a job offer from a Finnish employer, and the salary must meet the minimum threshold (approx. €1,600/month in 2025). Processing takes 2–4 months. EU Blue Card is possible only for very high salaries.

**Q: Do I need a Finnish welding certification to work in Finland?**
A: Yes, you typically need a recognized welding certificate (e.g., ISO 9606) to prove competence. Foreign certifications may be accepted if they meet EN standards, but it's advisable to get certified in Finland or through an accredited body.
