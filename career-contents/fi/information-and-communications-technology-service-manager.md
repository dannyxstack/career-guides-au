# Information and Communications Technology Services Manager — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 1330 (ISCO08)**

Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Services Managers plan, direct, and coordinate the provision of ICT services within an organization or as external providers. In Finland, this occupation is pivotal in driving digital transformation across industries, from tech startups to large corporations and the public sector. The role involves overseeing IT service delivery, infrastructure, cybersecurity, and project management, often requiring a blend of technical expertise and leadership skills. Finland's strong ICT sector, including hubs like Helsinki and Tampere, offers robust demand for these professionals. Typical responsibilities include budget management, vendor negotiations, and ensuring compliance with data protection regulations like GDPR.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 4,012. Finland's employment outlook for ICT Services Managers remains positive due to the country's heavy investment in digitalization, particularly in industries such as telecommunications, software development, and public sector e-services. The aging population and shortage of skilled ICT professionals create sustained demand for managers who can optimize resources and drive innovation. The rise of remote work and cybersecurity threats further fuels need for robust ICT management. The Bank of Finland and Technology Industries of Finland project stable growth, with many organizations seeking to modernize legacy systems and adopt cloud-native solutions. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for ICT Services Managers in Finland typically starts from senior technical roles (e.g., senior developer, IT architect) or business analysis. Advancement can lead to executive positions such as Chief Information Officer (CIO) or Chief Technology Officer (CTO). Many professionals pursue cross-sector moves between private and public sectors, or into consultancy. Finnish employers value continuous learning, with opportunities to specialize in emerging fields like cloud services, cybersecurity, or AI management. Leadership experience in international environments is particularly beneficial. The role offers clear pathways to higher management levels, especially with demonstrated success in digital transformation projects.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical visa process for an ICT Services Manager moving to Finland from outside the EU?**
A: A non-EU ICT manager typically applies for a Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL) or an EU Blue Card. The process involves securing a job offer, then submitting the application online with supporting docs (passport, employment contract, degree, etc.). Processing takes 1–3 months. The EU Blue Card has higher salary and experience requirements but offers additional benefits. If the employer is pre-approved, a D-visa may allow faster entry.

**Q: What salary range can an ICT Services Manager expect in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and location. Entry-level managers may earn around €50,000–€65,000, while senior managers can earn €80,000–€120,000 or more, especially in Helsinki. The top 10% may exceed €150,000. Large tech companies and consulting firms often pay above average. The threshold for the EU Blue Card (€3,500/month) is typically exceeded for this role.

**Q: Is Finnish language required for ICT Services Manager positions in Finland?**
A: Not always, but highly beneficial. In many international tech companies and startups, English is the working language. However, for roles in the public sector or smaller Finnish firms, Finnish is often required. Learning Finnish improves career prospects and integration.
