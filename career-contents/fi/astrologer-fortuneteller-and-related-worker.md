# Astrologer, Fortune-Teller and Related Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 5161 (ISCO08)**

Astrologers, fortune-tellers and related workers in Finland provide guidance and insights based on esoteric practices such as astrology, tarot reading, palmistry, and other divination methods. The profession is small and operates primarily as self-employment or within wellness and spiritual centres. There is no official regulation, and practitioners typically rely on reputation and word-of-mouth. The field is not considered a mainstream career path, with demand linked to interest in alternative spirituality and personal development. Most practitioners have other income sources, and the occupation is not listed on shortage lists. The labour market for such roles is niche and stable at a very low level.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression in Finland for astrologers and fortune-tellers is informal. There is no structured career path; advancement comes from building a client base, reputation, and possibly expanding into related fields like life coaching or event hosting. Some practitioners diversify by teaching courses or writing books. Income is variable and often combined with other work. Professional development is through personal study and networking. Formal recognition is not required, but membership in esoteric associations may enhance credibility. The field suits those with entrepreneurial spirit and strong interpersonal skills.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for an astrologer or fortune-teller in Finland?**
A: Income varies widely. Many practitioners earn less than €15,000 per year if part-time; full-time established professionals may earn €20,000-€30,000. Most rely on multiple income streams. There is no official salary data.

**Q: Is it possible to get a Finnish work visa as an astrologer or fortune-teller?**
A: It is challenging. The occupation does not meet typical skilled migration criteria. While a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) is theoretically possible with a job offer from a Finnish employer, such offers are extremely rare. Self-employment requires a viable business plan and sufficient income, which is hard to demonstrate in this field.

**Q: Do I need any official registration or license to practice in Finland?**
A: No specific professional registration is required. However, if you offer services as a business, you must register with the Trade Register and Tax Administration, and obtain a business identity code (Y-tunnus). Liability insurance is recommended.
