# Tree and Shrub Crop Grower — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 6112 (ISCO08)**

In Finland, tree and shrub crop growers—including berry, fruit, and Christmas tree farmers—operate primarily on small-to-medium-sized farms in southern and central regions. The sector faces an aging workforce and relies on seasonal labour. Organic production and farm-to-table trends are creating new opportunities, while climate change introduces both challenges (pests, weather) and potential for new crops.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 13. Employment in tree and shrub crop growing is stable but declining in traditional products like apples; berries (especially organic) and Christmas trees show growth. The need for modernisation and digitalisation (precision agriculture) may create demand for tech-savvy growers. Seasonal labour shortages persist, and the sector is not on the official shortage list for permanent residence.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or seasonal picker. With vocational training, one can become a farm manager or start their own farm. Business skills, organic certification, and direct marketing (e.g., farm shops, U-pick) are common paths. Some growers diversify into agritourism or forestry.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated fruit picking using AI vision and robotic arms
- Automated irrigation and fertilization scheduling based on sensor data
- Automatically monitor crop health and pests using drones and satellite imagery
- Automated pruning and shaping operations (e.g., grapevines)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze soil and meteorological data to optimize planting plans and variety selection
- Precisely identify early diseases through computer vision and recommend localized pesticide application.
- Uses predictive models to optimize harvest timing and market sales strategies
- Use digital twin technology to simulate the impact of different management strategies on production yields

**Human moat:**

- Ability to flexibly adjust operations in complex terrain or extreme weather
- Holistic management intuition and experience for multi-year tree growth cycles
- On-site coordination and negotiation with seasonal workers, cooperatives, and buyers.
- Emergency decision-making for handling unexpected events (e.g., frost, pest outbreaks)

**Skills to build:**

- Precision agriculture technologies (e.g., variable rate irrigation, drone operation and image analysis)
- Agricultural data analysis and basic machine learning applications
- Smart sensor network maintenance and troubleshooting
- Sustainable agriculture practices (carbon accounting, soil health management)
- Digital tool integration (farm management software, ERP systems)
- Knowledge of crop physiology and new breeding techniques

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Berry growing
- Forestry integration
- Precision agriculture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for tree and shrub crop growers in Finland?**
A: Salaries typically range from €24,000 to €30,000 pre-tax per year, depending on experience, farm size, and seasonal bonuses. Farm owners' income varies widely based on crop yields and market prices.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a residence permit for this occupation?**
A: Yes, through the Residence Permit for an Employed Person (TTOL) if you have a valid job offer meeting minimum salary requirements (usually €1,399/month). Seasonal work up to 9 months is also possible via a D-visa, but it does not lead to permanent residence.

**Q: What training is available for this career?**
A: Vocational qualifications in agriculture (2-3 years) are offered at many vocational schools (ammattiopisto). Practical experience on farms is highly valued. University of Applied Sciences degrees provide deeper business and agronomy knowledge.
