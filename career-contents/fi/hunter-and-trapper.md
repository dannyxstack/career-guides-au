# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

Hunters and trappers in Finland pursue wild game and fur-bearing animals for meat, fur, sport, or population control, often working in remote rural areas or alongside forestry and conservation efforts. The profession is deeply rooted in Finnish tradition, but commercial hunting is limited; most practitioners are self-employed or seasonal workers. Licensing and regulations are strict, with mandatory training and exams.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hunting License (Metsästyskortti) | Finnish Wildlife Agency (Suomen riistakeskus) | Required | Required for all hunters; obtained after passing a written exam and a shooting test. |
| Firearm License (Aseiden hallussapitolupa) | Local police department | Required | Required to possess and use hunting firearms; involves background check. |
| Game Meat Handling Certificate (Riistan käsittelykurssi) | Approved training providers | Optional | Mandatory for selling game meat commercially; covers hygiene and processing. |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most hunters and trappers operate independently. Advancement often involves specializing in a type of game (e.g., large carnivores, waterfowl) or expanding into related fields like wildlife photography, guiding hunting tourism, or conservation work. Formal education is not required for entry, but certifications in game meat handling or wildlife management can enhance opportunities. The profession typically remains a lifelong pursuit rather than a career ladder.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Wildlife population monitoring and counting (via AI image recognition and drone automation)
- Capture data recording and report generation (AI automatic sorting and visualization).
- Predicting animal activity patterns (machine learning models replacing empirical judgment)
- License application and compliance document processing (automated document system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze satellite imagery and infrared camera data to optimize monitoring efficiency
- Improves success rate of precise capture and release through real-time tracking with drones and AI.
- Develop science-based wildlife management strategies using data analysis tools
- Using VR to train on trap setting and safe operation in complex environments

**Human moat:**

- Physical and manual skills for setting and maintaining field traps
- Quick decision-making for unexpected situations (e.g., animal injuries, bad weather)
- Interpersonal communication and negotiation with local communities and landowners.
- Responsibility for ecological ethics judgement and compliance operations

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Ecological modeling and population statistics
- Basic data analysis using Python or R
- Wildlife conservation regulations and ethics
- Project management and basic business skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Game management
- Ecotourism
- Conservation hunting
- Wildlife control

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Outdoor enthusiasts comfortable with solitary work in remote areas
- Individuals with strong knowledge of wildlife and ecological balance
- Self-disciplined people able to handle irregular hours and physical demands

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer urban settings or predictable routines
- Those uncomfortable with handling firearms or field dressing animals

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a hunter or trapper in Finland?**
A: Salaries are highly variable as most are self-employed. Commercial hunters may earn from €20,000 to €40,000 annually, but many rely on supplementary income.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national work as a hunter in Finland?**
A: Generally difficult, as the occupation is not in shortage and requires local licensing. A job offer from a Finnish employer and a residence permit (TTOL) may be possible for specialized roles like game management.
