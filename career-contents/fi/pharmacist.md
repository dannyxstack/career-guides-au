# Pharmacist — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2262 (ISCO08)**

Pharmacists in Finland are regulated healthcare professionals who ensure safe and effective use of medications. They work in community pharmacies, hospitals, and the pharmaceutical industry. The profession is split into two levels: farmaseutti (bachelor's) and provvisori (master's). Finland's aging population drives steady demand, but strict licensing and limited pharmacy ownership positions keep competition moderate, with shortages in rural areas.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 1,602. Employment is expected to remain stable with slight growth due to an aging population and advances in pharmacotherapy. Rural areas face chronic shortages, while urban centers are competitive. Digitalization and expanded clinical roles may create new opportunities. The number of pharmacy licenses is capped, limiting overall growth but ensuring job security.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farmaseutti, then further study to become a provvisori, which opens roles in hospital pharmacy, industry, or pharmacy ownership (apteekkari). Specializations include clinical pharmacy, pharmacovigilance, and regulatory affairs. Continuing education is mandatory to maintain licensure.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine prescription review and drug interaction checks
- Standardized drug dispensing and dose calculation
- Inventory management and automatic replenishment
- Drug information retrieval and simple medication consultation
- Automatic bill reconciliation with insurance systems

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to predict individual drug responses and adverse reaction risks
- Personalized medication regimen optimization based on clinical data
- Smart triage and remote patient medication follow-up
- Improved Vaccine Management and Promotion Efficiency
- Pharmacoeconomic analysis and real-world evidence generation

**Human moat:**

- Medication management and clinical decision-making for complex cases.
- Face-to-face patient consultation and medication adherence intervention
- Coordination and communication within multidisciplinary healthcare teams
- Ethical decision-making and pharmaceutical legal compliance judgment
- Patient education on emerging therapies (e.g., gene therapy)

**Skills to build:**

- AI-driven clinical decision support system operation
- Pharmacogenomics and precision medication analysis
- Telemedicine and digital health tool application
- Statistical data analysis and real-world research skills
- Medical ethics and privacy protection compliance knowledge
- Vaccine management and public health skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Clinical pharmacy
- Pharmaceutical industry
- E-health and digitalization
- Aging population

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a pharmacist in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and region. Community pharmacists typically earn competitive wages, while hospital and industry roles offer higher pay. The profession is generally well-compensated within the healthcare field.

**Q: Can a non-EU pharmacist work in Finland?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer, a license from Valvira, and proof of proficiency in Finnish or Swedish. The most common visa is the residence permit for an employed person (TTOL).

**Q: What is the career progression for a pharmacist?**
A: After earning a master's and license, pharmacists can specialize in clinical pharmacy, industry, or become pharmacy owners. Management roles in hospital pharmacy or regulatory agencies are also common.
