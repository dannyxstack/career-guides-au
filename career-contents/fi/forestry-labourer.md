# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers in Finland perform manual tasks in silviculture, timber harvesting, and forest maintenance, including planting, thinning, pruning, and felling. The work is physically demanding and seasonally dependent, often in remote areas. Finland's vast forest resources (over 70% land cover) sustain a significant forestry sector, with labour demand fluctuating based on market cycles and mechanization trends. Employment is concentrated in rural regions of eastern and northern Finland, and tasks are increasingly supported by mechanized harvesting, though manual work remains for steep terrains and young stands. The occupation is typical of lower-skilled entry-level positions in the forestry value chain, with limited career advancement without additional training.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 314. The outlook for forestry labourers in Finland is stable but declining slowly due to mechanization and automation in harvesting and silviculture. Employment opportunities persist for seasonal and contract work, especially in pre-commercial thinning and planting. The green transition and bioeconomy may sustain demand for manual work in carbon sink and biodiversity projects. However, long-term trends favor machine operators and forestry technicians. Labour shortages are not acute, but remote regions face recruitment difficulties. The occupation is not listed on shortage lists for residence permits.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited without further education. Forestry labourers may advance to machine operators (e.g., harvester or forwarder operator) after vocational training, or to forestry technician roles via a vocational qualification (two years). Some transition to supervisory positions (forestry foreman) or into related environmental fields. Apprenticeship programs exist but are not common. The path to permanent employment often requires multiple seasonal contracts.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- Bioeconomy
- Renewable energy
- Carbon sink projects
- Biodiversity restoration

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Physically robust individuals who enjoy outdoor work in all weather conditions
- People comfortable working in remote, forested areas with minimal supervision
- Seasonal workers seeking short-term contracts (e.g., students)

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry labourer in Finland?**
A: Salaries are generally based on collective agreements (forestry sector). The median monthly pay is around €2,200–€2,500, equating to an annual gross of €26,400–€30,000. Overtime and piecework can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a residence permit as a forestry labourer?**
A: Yes, through the TTOL permit if you have a job offer meeting the income threshold (€1,399/month in 2024). However, it is rarely granted because employers usually prefer local or EU workers. The occupation is not on the shortage list, and applications face high scrutiny.

**Q: What is the career progression for a forestry labourer?**
A: With experience and vocational training, you can become a machine operator (harvester/forwarder), forestry technician, or foreman. Further education (e.g., tradenomi in forestry) opens management roles.
