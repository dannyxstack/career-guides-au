# Metal Moulder and Coremaker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7211 (ISCO08)**

Metal moulders and coremakers are skilled workers in Finland's foundry industry, responsible for creating molds and cores used in metal casting. They work in manufacturing facilities producing components for sectors like automotive, machinery, and energy. The occupation requires precision, physical stamina, and knowledge of metallurgy and casting techniques. Finland's engineering sector has a long history, but the foundry industry faces restructuring due to automation and global competition, leading to stable but slowly declining employment opportunities.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 872. Employment for metal moulders and coremakers in Finland is expected to remain stable with a slight decline, as automation and digital simulation replace some manual tasks. However, demand for skilled workers in specialized casting (e.g., lightweight alloys, complex geometries) and maintenance of automated equipment may sustain openings. The industry is concentrated in southern and western Finland, with opportunities in small to medium-sized foundries. Overall, the occupation is not expected to see strong growth, but replacement needs due to retirements will create moderate job opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training in metalworking, followed by on-the-job experience to become a skilled moulder. With experience, one can advance to supervisor, foundry technician, or quality control specialist. Further training in automation, 3D printing, or simulation can lead to roles in process development or management. Some may specialize in art casting or high-precision aerospace components. The path is hands-on and practical, with opportunities for lifelong learning through vocational adult education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate 3D printing paths for sand moulds/cores using CAD models
- AI vision system detects casting defects in real-time and automatically sorts them
- Robots automatically complete sand mold assembly and pouring operations
- AI optimizes pouring parameters and adjusts cooling system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI simulation software assists in predicting casting shrinkage and porosity locations, guiding process improvements.
- AR headset displays sand core assembly sequence and position calibration
- AI-driven quality analysis system generates defect root cause reports
- The digital twin system monitors the entire casting process in real time and warns of anomalies

**Human moat:**

- Process innovation capability for handling non-standard, small-batch castings
- Skills in manual repair and fine-tuning of complex sand moulds/cores
- On-site decision-making ability for multi-variety quick mold changes.
- Team coordination and safety risk management responsibilities

**Skills to build:**

- Process parameter adjustment for 3D printed sand molds/cores
- Casting simulation software (Magma, ProCAST) operation and result interpretation
- Basic maintenance of automated production lines (robots, PLC)
- Metallurgy and Defect Analysis (metallography, X-ray)
- Data-driven process optimization (statistical process control)
- Basic operations and maintenance of digital twin systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation in foundries
- 3D printing of molds
- Lightweight metal casting
- Digital twin simulation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for metal moulders in Finland?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by experience and employer. Entry-level positions may start lower, while experienced moulders earn higher wages, typically with shift or hazardous work supplements.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a residence permit to work as a metal moulder in Finland?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Finnish employer that meets the minimum income threshold (around €1,300 per month) and the employer can show that the position could not be filled locally (labour market testing). The permit is employer-specific.

**Q: Do I need a formal qualification to work as a metal moulder in Finland?**
A: While not mandatory, a vocational qualification in metalworking or foundry technology is highly valued. Many employers require at least an occupational safety card, and formal training improves job prospects and career advancement.
