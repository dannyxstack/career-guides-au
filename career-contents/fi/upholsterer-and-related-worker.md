# Upholsterers and Related Workers — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 7534 (ISCO08)**

In Finland, upholsterers and related workers specialize in reupholstering furniture, manufacturing new upholstered items, and repairing or restoring furniture, often working in workshops, furniture stores, or for specialized companies. The occupation requires manual dexterity, attention to detail, and knowledge of fabrics and materials. It is part of the manufacturing sector and offers opportunities for self-employment.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 762. The employment outlook for upholsterers in Finland is stable with modest demand, primarily due to replacement needs as older workers retire. Growth is limited by competition from low-cost imported furniture and automation in large-scale manufacturing. However, demand for high-quality custom upholstery and restoration services persists, especially in affluent areas like Helsinki. Overall, the occupation is not expected to grow significantly but will remain viable for skilled workers.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving from apprentice to experienced upholsterer, then to supervisor or owner of a small workshop. Some may specialize in antique restoration, marine upholstery, or automotive interiors. Further training in business management can lead to entrepreneurial opportunities. The path often includes vocational education and on-the-job learning.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD software for standardized layout and cutting
- Sewing machine operation for simple straight stitching
- Mechanical cutting and forming of foam and filling materials
- Automated recording of inventory management and material ordering
- Repetitive filling and fastening tasks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates and modifies soft package patterns
- AR/VR tools visualize final results, reducing rework
- Machine vision detects fabric defects, improving quality
- Digital cutting beds improve material utilization rates
- Smart supply chain systems optimize material procurement

**Human moat:**

- Customized complex patterns and hand-sewing techniques
- The ability to communicate with clients and understand personalized needs
- Experience handling special fabrics (e.g., antique textiles, leather)
- Flexibility for on-site installation, adjustment, and repair
- Comprehensive assessment of furniture structure and comfort

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/3D modeling software (e.g., Rhino, SketchUp)
- Proficient in operating digital cutting and sewing equipment
- Familiar with AR/VR presentation tools
- Develop project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and eco-design trends
- Learn basic programming to customize automated workflows

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom upholstery
- Antique restoration
- Marine upholstery
- Bio-based materials

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an upholsterer in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary depending on experience and employer, but typically align with median earnings for manufacturing trades in Finland. Entry-level positions may start around €25,000 annually, while experienced upholsterers can earn up to €40,000.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU upholsterers?**
A: Non-EU citizens can apply for a Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) if they have a job offer from a Finnish employer. The permit requires that the job meets the minimum salary threshold. The D-visa fast track is generally not available for this occupation unless the employer is a registered fast-track company.

**Q: Is upholstery considered a shortage occupation in Finland?**
A: Upholsterer is not currently on the national shortage list. However, there may be regional demand, especially for custom and restoration work. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.
