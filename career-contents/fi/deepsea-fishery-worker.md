# Deep-Sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 6223 (ISCO08)**

Deep-sea fishery workers in Finland operate fishing vessels in the Baltic Sea and the Atlantic, targeting species such as herring, sprat, and cod. The occupation involves long shifts at sea, handling nets, processing catch, and maintaining vessels. It is a small, traditional sector facing sustainability challenges and quotas. Most workers are Finnish or EU/EEA nationals, with limited entry for third-country nationals due to low salaries and seasonal demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited in deep-sea fishing. Workers typically start as deckhands and may advance to boatswain or skipper with experience and licenses. Some move into related sectors like aquaculture or seafood processing. The job is physically demanding and seasonal, often combined with other work. Formal qualifications (e.g., vocational degree in fisheries) improve prospects but are not mandatory.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing vessel navigation and route planning (AIS + autopilot)
- Fish catch classification and initial weighing (computer vision)
- Fish school detection and net deployment position optimization (AI sonar analysis)
- Predictive maintenance for fishing vessel equipment (IoT + machine learning)
- Catch tracking and compliance report generation (automated data entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fish school prediction (combining meteorological and oceanographic data)
- Real-time deck safety monitoring (AI alert wearable)
- Automation of catch processing (robot-assisted)
- Resource management and sustainable fishing strategies (AI models)
- Market sales data analysis (providing optimal auction timing)

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making in complex maritime environments (storms, currents)
- Manual net repair and deck emergency handling
- Judging fish behavior based on experience (limited AI data)
- Communication and coordination with fleet and shore teams
- Flexible operations to adapt to seasonal and regulatory changes

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Computer Vision Applications in Fisheries
- Data analysis and prediction tools (Python, R)
- Drone operation and maintenance
- Marine sensor and IoT device management
- Sustainable fisheries regulations and certifications
- Deck automation system operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fishing
- Aquaculture
- Seafood processing
- Marine conservation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deep-sea fishery worker in Finland?**
A: Salaries are modest, generally below the national average, reflecting seasonal work. Monthly earnings range from €1,800 to €2,500 for deckhands, with skippers earning more. Income can vary based on catch and company.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals get a work visa for deep-sea fishing in Finland?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The job must meet the minimum salary threshold (€1,399/month in 2024) and pass labor market testing. Most employers prefer EU/EEA workers. A residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) is possible but uncommon.
