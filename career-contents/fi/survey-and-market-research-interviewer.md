# Survey and Market Research Interviewer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 4227 (ISCO08)**

Survey and market research interviewers collect data through interviews, questionnaires, or online panels for market research, opinion polls, and social studies. In Finland, they are employed by research institutes, marketing agencies, and public sector bodies. The role often requires fluency in Finnish or Swedish, strong communication skills, and ability to follow structured protocols. Many positions are part-time or project-based, with flexible hours. Digitalization has increased remote and online interviewing, but phone and face-to-face methods remain common, especially for specialized studies.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,822. Employment outlook for survey interviewers in Finland is stable but competitive. Demand is driven by ongoing market research, customer satisfaction surveys, and government statistics. Automation and online self-administered surveys may reduce need for interviewers, but high-quality data still requires human interaction for complex topics. Growth areas include digital health surveys, customer experience measurement, and multicultural research. Job opportunities are concentrated in the Helsinki region and larger cities. Short-term contracts are typical, making career progression dependent on building expertise in specific research methods or moving into project management.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically starts from interviewer roles and can advance to senior interviewer, team leader, or research assistant. With experience and additional training, some move into survey design, data analysis, or market research management. In Finland, relevant education in social sciences, business, or statistics enhances prospects. Language skills (Finnish, Swedish, English) are critical. The field offers opportunities for those interested in consumer behavior or social trends, but advancement may require further studies or transitioning to other research roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised questionnaire surveys conducted by phone or online
- Data entry and basic cleaning
- Initial screening and recruitment of respondents
- Execution of interviews with preset scripts
- Summary statistics of simple opinion polls

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Designing more precise questionnaires and sampling plans
- Use AI analysis tools to mine deep insights from data
- Handling complex, semi-structured interviews and sensitive topics.
- Sentiment analysis using natural language processing techniques
- Provide training data and quality verification for AI systems

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and rapport
- Understand and manage complex, emotional respondent reactions
- Cross-cultural, multilingual communication skills
- Strategic thinking for project design and result interpretation.
- Data quality ethical judgment and compliance awareness

**Skills to build:**

- Data Analysis and Visualization (Python/R, Tableau)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT, SurveyMonkey AI)
- Questionnaire design and experimental methods
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
- Basic programming and automation scripts
- Domain knowledge (e.g., healthcare, finance)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital surveys
- Customer experience
- Healthcare research
- Multilingual panels

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a survey interviewer in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer, but hourly wages are common, ranging from €10 to €15 per hour. Full-time annual equivalents are roughly €20,000-€30,000. Exact figures depend on collective agreements and project duration.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work permit as a survey interviewer?**
A: Yes, if the job meets the income threshold for a residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). However, positions are often part-time or short-term, which may complicate permit eligibility. Specialists and EU Blue Card routes do not apply as the skill level is not considered high.

**Q: Do I need a degree to work as a survey interviewer in Finland?**
A: No, a degree is not mandatory. Many employers provide on-the-job training. However, fluency in Finnish or Swedish is often required. A vocational qualification in business or social sciences can be an advantage.
