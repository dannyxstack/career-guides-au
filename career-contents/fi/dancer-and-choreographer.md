# Dancer and Choreographer — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

Dancers and choreographers in Finland work in theatres, dance companies, festivals, and TV productions. The field is small but culturally significant, with employment often project-based or freelance. Most professionals are based in the Helsinki metropolitan area, though regional theatres and events offer some opportunities. The occupation requires strong physical fitness, creativity, and often formal training from a vocational school or university of applied sciences. Competition is high, and many dancers supplement income through teaching.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 367. The employment outlook for dancers and choreographers in Finland is stable but limited. Public funding for the arts supports a core of professional companies, but positions are scarce and turnover low. The rise of digital media and event productions creates some new opportunities, but the market remains highly competitive. Freelance and part-time work is common. There is no specific shortage, and most positions are filled by locals or EU nationals.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from dancer to choreographer or artistic director, or into teaching and administrative roles in dance institutions. Many dancers diversify into related fields like dance pedagogy or movement therapy. Networking and reputation are crucial. Professional development often involves continuous training and international exposure. Union membership (Suomen Tanssitaiteilijain Liitto) is common but not mandatory.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic dance teaching: movement breakdown and correction
- Group choreography (automatically generated based on music)
- Replacing real human extras in virtual scenes
- Automatic editing and publishing of dance videos
- Automatic dance notation generation and translation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dance creation AI assistant (generating movement sequences, exploring styles)
- VR/AR rehearsal and stage effect simulation
- Data analysis optimizes performance choreography (audience preferences, spatial layout)
- AI-driven clothing/lighting design integration
- Personalized Online Interactive Course Tutoring

**Human moat:**

- Live improvisation and emotional expression
- Unique artistic style and innovation
- Preservation of cultural rituals and traditional dances
- Real-time interaction and empathy with audiences
- Complex narrative and cross-media fusion creation

**Skills to build:**

- AI choreography tools (e.g., ChoreoAI, Choreography)
- Motion Capture and Virtual Production Technology
- Basic programming (Python, Web development)
- Digital Content Operations and Social Media Marketing
- Cross-media creation (VR/AR, interactive installations)
- Data analysis and visualization

**Key growth areas:**

- Contemporary dance
- Festival production
- Digital performance
- Dance therapy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What salary can a dancer expect in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary widely; freelance dancers may earn €20,000–35,000/year, while company dancers might earn €30,000–45,000/year. Choreographers often negotiate per project.

**Q: Can a non-EU dancer get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, through the Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL). The employer must demonstrate that the position cannot be filled locally. Self-employment is possible but requires a different permit.
