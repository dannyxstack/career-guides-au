# Gardener — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 6113 (ISCO08)**

Gardeners in Finland maintain green spaces including private gardens, public parks, nurseries, and greenhouses. The role involves planting, pruning, fertilizing, pest control, and landscape upkeep. Demand is driven by urban green infrastructure projects and a growing emphasis on sustainability. Seasonal variations influence workload, with peak employment in spring and summer. The profession offers opportunities in both public and private sectors, with increasing focus on organic and ecological practices.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 6,400. Employment for gardeners in Finland is expected to remain stable with moderate growth. Urban greening initiatives and the expansion of green roofs and vertical gardens create new opportunities. However, automation and budget constraints in municipalities may limit growth. Climate change may extend growing seasons but also introduce new pests and diseases. The sector faces challenges in attracting young workers, which may open doors for trained migrants, especially those with specialized skills in organic horticulture or landscape management.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for gardeners often starts from vocational training or apprenticeships. With experience, one can advance to supervisory roles such as head gardener or landscape manager. Further education in horticulture, landscape design, or environmental management enables specialization in areas like arboriculture, greenhouse production, or ecological restoration. Many gardeners become self-employed or start their own landscaping businesses. Certification in plant protection or occupational safety enhances employability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic lawnmowers for grass cutting.
- Use robots for precision planting and fertilization
- Automatically detect pests and diseases using AI image recognition
- Scheduling and maintenance of automated irrigation systems
- Use drones for large-area spraying operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Analyze soil data using AI tools to optimize fertilization plans
- Use plant identification apps to quickly diagnose problems and provide care suggestions
- Simulate landscape effects through digital twin technology to assist design
- Use smart devices to monitor environmental conditions and predict plant growth needs.
- Client communication and proposal demonstration using VR/AR

**Human moat:**

- Creative design and aesthetic judgment for complex landscapes
- Empirical diagnosis of atypical plant problems
- Communicate with clients to understand needs and provide personalized services
- Ability to respond to emergencies such as sudden weather and diseases.
- Fine pruning and styling handcraft skills.

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart gardening equipment (e.g., automatic pruning robots)
- Data analysis and soil/plant monitoring software usage
- Drone operation and aerial image analysis
- Landscape design software (e.g., AutoCAD, SketchUp)
- Customer relationship management and communication skills
- Basic programming and automation scripting (such as Python)

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban gardening
- Green infrastructure
- Sustainable landscaping
- Organic horticulture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a gardener in Finland?**
A: The median monthly salary for gardeners is around EUR 2,200–2,600 pre-tax, depending on experience and sector. Public sector jobs may offer slightly lower pay but better benefits.

**Q: Can a non-EU gardener get a work permit for Finland?**
A: Yes, through the Residence permit for an employed person (TTOL) if you have a job offer that meets the minimum salary and full-time requirements. However, gardening is not on the shortage list, so employers must demonstrate that no suitable EU/EEA candidate is available.

**Q: What training is needed to become a gardener in Finland?**
A: Vocational education in horticulture (2–3 years) is the most common path. Many employers also value practical experience and certificates such as the occupational safety card. Higher education degrees open advanced roles.
