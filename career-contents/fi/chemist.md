# Chemist — career analysis · Finland

**Occupation code: 2113 (ISCO08)**

Chemists in Finland work in research and development, quality control, and production across industries such as pharmaceuticals, chemicals, biotechnology, and environmental services. They conduct experiments, analyze substances, and develop new products or processes, often collaborating with engineers and other scientists. The profession requires a strong foundation in chemistry and analytical skills, with roles available in private companies, universities, and government research institutes.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 1,719. Finland's chemistry sector is stable with moderate growth, driven by the green transition, bioeconomy, and pharmaceutical R&D. Chemists are in demand particularly in the Helsinki region, Turku, and Oulu, where major employers like Orion, Neste, and Kemira are based. While overall employment is balanced, specialists in organic chemistry, analytics, and process chemistry are sought after. The aging workforce in some segments may create replacement demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a laboratory analyst or research assistant, advancing to senior chemist, project manager, or team leader. A Master's degree is the minimum for most roles, and a PhD opens doors to senior research positions in industry or academia. Specialization in areas like medicinal chemistry, polymer chemistry, or environmental analysis can lead to expert roles. Continuing education and certifications in Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) or quality management are valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine sample preparation and automated instrument operation
- Automatic identification and report generation for standard analytical data (e.g., HPLC, GC spectra)
- Repetitive inspection and pass/fail determination in quality control
- Manual retrieval and extraction of literature data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted experimental design: predicts reaction conditions and product properties via machine learning
- Abnormal pattern recognition: AI analyses complex data to identify potential faults or new discoveries
- Multi-source data integration: AI integrates literature, lab records, and supply chain information to support decision-making
- Virtual experiment simulation: AI digital twins reduce physical trial and error

**Human moat:**

- Design of creative experimental schemes and hypothesis generation
- Interdisciplinary problem-solving (e.g., collaborating with engineers, biologists)
- Regulatory compliance judgment and ethical responsibility
- Qualitative analysis of highly non-standard samples or new substances

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and data analysis.
- Machine learning basics and cheminformatics tools
- AI-assisted laboratory automation system management
- Interdisciplinary communication and project management
- Advanced statistics and Design of Experiments (DoE)

**Key growth areas:**

- Pharmaceuticals
- Green Chemistry
- Biotechnology
- Printed Electronics

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a chemist in Finland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector; entry-level chemists earn around €35,000-€40,000 per year, while experienced professionals can earn up to €55,000 or more in senior roles.

**Q: Can a non-EU chemist get a work visa for Finland?**
A: Yes, chemists are on Finland's shortage list, making them eligible for a residence permit for employed persons (TTOL) or an EU Blue Card if they meet salary and qualification criteria.

**Q: What is the career outlook for chemists in Finland?**
A: The outlook is positive, especially in pharmaceuticals, green chemistry, and environmental sectors. Specialization in emerging fields can enhance job prospects.
