# Labourers in rubber and plastic products manufacturing — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 95104 (NOC)**

Engages in material handling, machine feeding, product packaging, and cleaning in rubber and plastic product manufacturing; no professional qualifications needed, but must adapt to repetitive labor and factory environment.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Typically start as entry-level laborers, then advance to machine operator or production line leader with experience; some companies offer in-house training, long-term can shift to quality control or maintenance.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated material handling systems replace manual raw material transport
- Automatic feeding and unloading devices replace manual feeding and unloading
- Automated palletizers and packaging lines replace product packaging tasks
- Visual Inspection Systems Replacing Manual Quality Checks
- IoT and sensors replace manual equipment monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-driven predictive maintenance systems help workers identify equipment faults
- Digital twin technology used to train workers on new equipment
- Intelligent production scheduling system optimises production processes and improves worker efficiency
- Augmented reality (AR) headsets assist complex assembly or repairs.
- Robotic co-working systems and human-robot collaboration to share heavy tasks

**Human moat:**

- Factory safety supervision and emergency response require human judgment
- Non-standard product adjustment and rapid mold change require experience
- Creative repair and fault diagnosis for equipment breakdowns
- Interpersonal skills for team coordination and training new employees
- Sensory recognition of material anomalies (e.g., changes in viscosity)

**Skills to build:**

- Industrial robot operation and programming
- PLC basics and automation system monitoring
- Predictive maintenance and data analysis
- Knowledge of plastic/rubber material processes
- Basic English reading (operating interfaces and manuals)
- Safety and lean production certification

**Key growth areas:**

- Manufacturing
- Manual Labor
- Repetitive Tasks
- Industrial Growth

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the approximate hourly wage for this occupation?**
A: Depending on province and experience, junior hourly rate approx. CAD 15-18, senior up to CAD 20-25, including overtime.

**Q: Is it easy to immigrate to Canada with this occupation?**
A: Very difficult; usually not eligible for Express Entry, only via LMIA work visa or a few low-skilled PNP streams, with limited quotas.

**Q: Are there promotion opportunities?**
A: Can advance to machine operator, production line team leader or quality controller, but requires additional training and experience.
