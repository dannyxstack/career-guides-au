# Agricultural service contractors, farm supervisors and specialized livestock workers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 80020 (NOC)**

In Canada, agricultural technicians/agrologists are typically classified as agricultural service contractors or farm supervisors, responsible for soil management, crop production, and farm machinery operation. This occupation can lead to immigration through provincial nomination or Express Entry, especially suitable for those with farm work experience.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Agricultural technicians can advance to farm supervisor, agricultural consultant, or farm manager. With experience, they can start their own business as agricultural service contractors or move into management roles at agritech companies.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated collection of soil moisture and nutrient data with report generation
- Optimizing fertilization/irrigation plans based on historical yield and weather data.
- Use drones to patrol fields and automatically identify pest and disease areas
- Automated agricultural machinery scheduling and path planning

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted precision fertilization decisions to increase crop yield
- Uses computer vision to monitor livestock health in real time and warn of disease.
- Smart sensor network optimizes irrigation and energy consumption
- Machine learning predicts market demand to guide production planning

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency response and complex fault diagnosis
- Unstructured communication and coordination with farm workers and contractors
- Ethical judgment of animal welfare and herd health management
- Comprehensive production decisions across seasons and sites

**Skills to build:**

- Precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- UAV operation and remote sensing data analysis
- Python/R basics and agricultural data analysis
- IoT device configuration and maintenance
- Project management and team management skills
- Biosecurity protocols and animal health management

**Key growth areas:**

- PNP Agri-Food Immigration Pilot
- Precision Agriculture
- Sustainable Farming
- Farm Labour Shortage

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) | Most provinces have agriculture immigration streams, such as Ontario Employer Sponsorship, BC Regional Pilot, Saskatchewan Farm Owner/Worker programs. |
| Subclass EE (Express Entry) | Meet Federal Skilled Worker or Canadian Experience Class requirements, need work experience and English CLB 7; agri-tech may get provincial nomination bonus points |
| Subclass AFIP (Agri-Food Immigration Pilot) | For agri-food industry workers, including mushrooms and livestock, work 12 months to apply for permanent residency |

**PR friendliness: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual salary level for agricultural technicians in Canada?**
A: Junior about CAD 30,000-40,000, intermediate CAD 40,000-55,000, senior CAD 55,000-70,000, farm supervisor can reach over CAD 60,000.

**Q: How can agricultural technicians immigrate to Canada?**
A: Common pathways: through PNP agricultural streams (e.g., BC, Saskatchewan), Agri-Food Immigration Pilot (AFIP), or Express Entry (requires 1 year of Canadian work experience).

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become an agricultural technician in Canada?**
A: Typically requires a 2-3 year diploma from a community college or a university bachelor's degree in a related field such as agricultural technology, crop science, etc.
