# Industrial instrument technicians and mechanics — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 94213 (NOC)**

Canadian NOC 94213 corresponds to Plastic and Rubber Production Machine Operators, typically manufacturing workers requiring a high school diploma. This occupation is not in the high-skilled immigration category; pathways mainly through Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) or employer sponsorship, which are challenging.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Entry-level operators can advance to senior operators or team leaders, with opportunities to transfer to quality control or maintenance roles. On-the-job training or short courses are needed for skill enhancement. Long-term development relies on industry experience and technological updates.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Visual inspection of product appearance defects (replaced by AI visual inspection systems)
- Simple and repetitive loading/unloading operations (replaced by robotic automation).
- Standard setting and monitoring of production parameters (AI-optimized and adaptive control systems take over)
- Product counting and packaging (replaced by automated packaging lines)
- Basic quality data recording (replaced by IoT auto-collection and AI analysis)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI predictive maintenance systems to detect equipment faults early, reducing downtime
- Adjust temperature, pressure, and other parameters through AI process optimization recommendations to improve yield
- Using augmented reality (AR) to assist in repairing complex equipment, improving efficiency
- Using digital twins to simulate production processes, optimizing mold change and commissioning
- Optimize production plans through AI scheduling systems to improve overall efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Ability to diagnose and manually fix sudden equipment failures.
- Experience in precision mold adjustment and process fine-tuning
- Customised operation skills for non-standard products
- Coordination and communication with upstream and downstream processes and on-site problem solving
- Safety awareness and emergency response capabilities

**Skills to build:**

- PLC programming and industrial robot operation and maintenance
- Use of predictive maintenance software (e.g., IBM Maximo)
- Production process data analysis and AI tool application (e.g., basic Python)
- 3D printing and rapid prototyping technologies (used for mold repair and modification)
- English communication and basic technical document reading ability
- Basics of automation system integration (sensors, actuators)

**Key growth areas:**

- Manufacturing
- Provincial Nominee
- Employer-Specific
- Non-STEM

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a plastic and rubber production machine operator in Canada?**
A: Entry-level: approximately CAD 32,000–42,000; mid-level: CAD 42,000–52,000; senior: CAD 52,000–65,000. Hourly wage: approximately CAD 16–33.

**Q: Can I immigrate to Canada as a plastic and rubber production machine operator through Express Entry?**
A: This occupation falls under NOC TEER 4 and typically does not meet Express Entry requirements. A more viable pathway is through Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) or employer sponsorship (LMIA) for immigration.

**Q: Is Canadian certification required to practice this occupation?**
A: Provincial certification is usually not required, but some provinces may require a safety training certificate. Most employers provide on-the-job training.
