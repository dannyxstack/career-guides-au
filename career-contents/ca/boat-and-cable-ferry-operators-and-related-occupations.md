# Boat and cable ferry operators and related occupations — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 75210 (NOC)**

Operate and drive small ferries, cable ferries, and similar vessels to safely transport passengers and vehicles, performing simple mechanical maintenance and deck work.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Vessel Operator Proficiency (SVOP) | Transport Canada | Required | Essential for operating small commercial ferries, requires passing a written exam and practical test |
| Radio Operator Certificate (ROC-M) | Transport Canada | Required | Used for ship communication |
| First aid and CPR certificate | Canadian Red Cross or equivalent organization | Optional | Some employers require |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Typically start as deckhand or ordinary seaman, gain experience to become captain or senior operator, some shift to maritime management or port operations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Autonomous driving system replaces conventional route navigation and berthing operations
- Remote monitoring and sensors replace manual lookout and environmental monitoring
- Automated ticketing and passenger counting systems replace manual ticketing and counting
- AI scheduling system optimizes flight plans, reducing manual planning
- Automated fault diagnosis tools replace simple mechanical inspections

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making systems enhance emergency response and complex situation handling
- Real-time data analysis enhances fuel efficiency optimization and emission control
- Remote operation centers support multi-vessel monitoring, improving operational efficiency
- Automated recording system simplifies compliance reports and log management
- AR/VR training systems enhance crew skill training and safety drills

**Human moat:**

- On-the-spot judgment and manual operation skills in emergencies
- Adaptive decision-making in adverse weather and unconventional scenarios
- Communication with passengers and emergency evacuation management
- On-site diagnosis and repair of complex mechanical faults
- Responsibility to comply with maritime regulations and undergo regulatory inspections

**Skills to build:**

- Remote monitoring and automation system operation and troubleshooting
- Data analysis tools (e.g., Python, Tableau) for efficiency optimization
- Marine Electrical and Electronic Systems Maintenance Skills
- Emergency management and crisis communication training
- Basic concepts of artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Maritime cybersecurity basics

**Key growth areas:**

- Stable demand
- Regional employment
- Seasonal fluctuations
- Limited automation risk

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for this occupation in Canada?**
A: Entry-level annual salary approx. CAD 32,000-42,000; mid-level CAD 42,000-55,000; captain level up to CAD 55,000-70,000. Salary is significantly affected by seasonality and region.

**Q: Is this occupation easy to immigrate to Canada?**
A: This occupation falls under NOC TEER 3 and may qualify for immigration through provincial nomination, especially for applicants with employer sponsorship. The Federal Express Entry typically requires a higher skill level.

**Q: What certificates are needed for this job?**
A: Must hold a Small Vessel Operator Certificate and a Restricted Operator Certificate with Aeronautical and Maritime qualification issued by Transport Canada; some employers also require a first aid certificate.
