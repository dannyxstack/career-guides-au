# Fish and seafood plant workers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 94142 (NOC)**

Engages in cleaning, cutting, packaging, freezing and other processing work for fish and seafood, typically in factories or on fishing vessel processing workshops; a junior position in the food processing industry.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level positions with limited promotion paths, can advance to processing line supervisor or quality inspector. Some workers move into aquaculture or fisheries management through experience. Relies on experience or internal training for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cutting lines replace manual work such as de-heading, gutting, and slicing.
- Vision systems automatically sort fish by size, species, and freshness
- Packaging line automation completes weighing, sealing, and labeling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI vision-assisted manual inspection of fish quality defects
- Data analysis optimizes production scheduling and raw material utilization
- Automated monitoring system warns of equipment failures, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Handle abnormal situations (e.g., machine jams, abnormal fish)
- Fine manual operations (e.g., special cutting, deboning).
- Health and safety regulatory responsibility
- Team collaboration and on-site communication

**Skills to build:**

- Basic operation and maintenance of PLC and automation equipment
- Food processing quality control and HACCP certification
- Data analysis basics (Excel/BI tools)
- Industrial robot collaboration skills
- Cross-departmental communication and team management.
- Knowledge of food safety regulations and compliance

**Key growth areas:**

- seasonal work
- food processing
- LMIA dependent
- manual labour

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment) | Employers need to prove they cannot find local workers and can apply for LMIA to support work visas. Applicable to temporary foreign workers. |
| Subclass PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) | Maritime provinces (e.g., NSNP, NLPNP) may have provincial nomination programs for food processing workers, but usually require employer sponsorship. |
| Subclass TFWP (Temporary Foreign Worker Program) | The most common pathway: obtaining a closed work permit through LMIA, engaging in seasonal processing work. |

**PR friendliness: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals willing to engage in physical labor and repetitive work

**Who is not suited**
- People seeking fast immigration or high-paying careers.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the approximate annual salary of a fish processing worker?**
A: Entry-level positions annual salary about C$28,000-35,000, hourly wage around minimum wage level ($15-17). Experienced supervisors can reach C$42,000-50,000.

**Q: Do fish processing workers have skilled migration pathways?**
A: Very limited. Usually only temporary work via LMIA work permits; a few provinces (e.g., Newfoundland) may have PNP options for long-term employees, but the threshold is high.
