# Labourers in textile processing and cutting — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 95105 (NOC)**

Operate textile processing and cutting machines, handle fibers, yarns and fabrics, or cut fabrics by hand. Work in textile mills, garment factories or carpet factories, performing auxiliary tasks such as feeding materials, handling and cleaning.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Usually start as general laborers and can advance to machine operators or production line leaders. Need to gain experience and learn specific machine operations; some may move into quality inspection or maintenance. Industry mobility is high, but large-scale promotion opportunities are limited.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI vision-guided automatic cutting machine replaces manual cutting
- Automated handling robots replace feeding and handling tasks
- Smart textile machines replace the preparation and winding of fibres and yarns.
- AI quality inspection system replaces manual inspection of fabric defects
- Cleaning robots replace workshop cleaning tasks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI predictive maintenance systems to reduce machine breakdowns and downtime
- Optimize production line efficiency with AI scheduling software
- AI-assisted design tools improve fabric utilization in cutting plans
- Digital twin simulation for training new employees on operating complex equipment
- AI-driven inventory management system reduces raw material waste

**Human moat:**

- Flexible cutting techniques for special materials (e.g., stretch fabrics, leather)
- Ability to quickly diagnose and manually repair machine faults
- Non-standard design and adjustments for customized client requirements
- Experience in multi-process coordination and on-site management
- Safety procedure execution and emergency response

**Skills to build:**

- Operating intelligent equipment such as CNC cutting machines and automatic fabric spreaders
- Basic programming and CAD/CAM software usage (e.g., AutoCAD, Lectra)
- Data Analysis and Report Creation (Excel, Power BI)
- Lean production and Six Sigma management methods
- Robot collaboration and Industry 4.0 system interface operation
- Basics of Mechanical Maintenance and Electrical Fault Diagnosis

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation risk
- Slow growth
- Textile manufacturing
- Manual cutting

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the approximate hourly wage for a textile labourer in Canada?**
A: Typically CAD 14-20 per hour, depending on province and experience. Overtime is paid at 1.5 times the base rate.

**Q: Can this occupation immigrate to Canada through a fast-track program?**
A: Very difficult, because NOC 95105 is TEER 5 (low-skilled), which does not meet federal Express Entry requirements. Only possible through LMIA work permits or a few provincial nomination pathways.
