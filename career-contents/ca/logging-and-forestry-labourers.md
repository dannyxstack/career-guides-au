# Logging and forestry labourers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 85120 (NOC)**

Performing physical labor in logging, afforestation and forest maintenance, including land clearing, tree planting, operating chainsaws and other equipment, providing basic support for timber processing and forestry production.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Entry-level laborers can advance to skilled loggers or equipment operators; with experience, may become foremen or forest resource management assistants; some transition into forestry technicians or sustainability-related roles through training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using chainsaws for simple, repetitive logging operations
- Manual measurement and marking of trees to be felled
- Manual recording of logging quantity and location data
- Repetitive woodland clearing and shrub trimming
- Navigating using paper maps or simple GPS

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drones for forest surveying and health monitoring
- AI-assisted decision system optimises logging routes and timing
- Real-time safety and fatigue monitoring via wearable devices
- Automated machinery (e.g., harvesters) increases single-person timber processing volume
- Using satellite imagery and AI to predict forest pests and diseases

**Human moat:**

- Mobility adaptability in complex terrain and adverse weather
- Sensory judgment of abnormal equipment noises and tree diseases
- On-site decision-making in emergencies (e.g., mechanical failure, fire).
- Unstructured communication and collaboration with team members
- Experiential insights into wildlife behavior and soil conditions

**Skills to build:**

- Operate and maintain automated forestry machinery (e.g., unmanned harvesters)
- Conduct forest surveys using drones and remote sensing technology
- GIS (Geographic Information System) and data collection analysis
- Basic programming and AI tools (e.g., use of predictive models)
- Safety monitoring and intelligent wearable device maintenance
- Sustainable forestry management and ecological assessment

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal workforce
- Mechanization impact
- Rural employment
- Sustainable forestry

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for logging laborers?**
A: Canadian logging labor wages are low, with entry-level annual salaries around CAD 30,000-40,000, but due to seasonal work, actual income may be lower. Mid-level experienced workers can earn CAD 40,000-55,000.

**Q: As a logging labourer, is it easy to immigrate to Canada?**
A: Not easy. This occupation is classified as low-skilled, usually requires an LMIA work visa, and cannot directly immigrate through Express Entry. A few provinces' PNP may include this occupation, but competition is fierce.

**Q: What career development opportunities are there for timber fallers?**
A: Can advance to skilled loggers, equipment operators (e.g., skidders), foremen, or become forestry technicians, forest conservation officers through training.
