# Concrete, clay and stone forming operators — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 94103 (NOC)**

Operate machinery and manual tools to shape concrete, clay, and stone materials into bricks, pipes, tiles, etc. Work mainly in factories and construction sites.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Start as a junior operator, can advance to senior operator, team leader, or production supervisor. Some workers move into more specialized roles such as mold making or quality inspection through apprenticeships or training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Concrete strength data recording and report generation
- Curing time calculation based on weather and material parameters
- Pouring progress tracking and automatic scheduling
- Material quantity estimation and replenishment reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI vision inspection systems to monitor concrete surface flatness and cracks in real time
- Optimise pouring sequence and formwork design through BIM models and AI
- Using drones and AI for large-area ground leveling measurement
- Using AI to predict concrete strength during curing and optimize formwork removal time
- Automatically adjusts water-to-cement ratio via smart devices to improve quality consistency

**Human moat:**

- Hand troweling and burnishing techniques for complex surfaces
- On-site assessment of concrete workability and timely adjustment
- Ability to work flexibly in different weather and site conditions
- Real-time communication with construction team to resolve unexpected issues

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of smart equipment like laser screeds and automatic trowels
- BIM model reading and on-site coordinate positioning
- Concrete strength prediction AI tool usage
- Drone inspection and data analysis fundamentals
- Knowledge of green concrete additives and new materials.
- Basic English communication (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- Infrastructure Spending
- Housing Demand
- Automation
- Green Building

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with good physical fitness and ability to adapt to repetitive work
- Someone who values safety regulations and is willing to work in a factory environment
- Those looking to quickly enter the construction industry without pursuing higher education

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike physical labor and noisy environments
- People seeking high salary and fast career advancement
