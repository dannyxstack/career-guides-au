# Agricultural Service Contractors, Farm Supervisors and Specialized Livestock Workers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 84120 (NOC)**

In Canada, beef cattle farm workers correspond to NOC 84120, responsible for cattle feeding, grazing, and health maintenance. This occupation is skilled trade and can lead to immigration through PNP or AFIP, but usually requires employer sponsorship.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Junior workers can advance to farm supervisor or ranch manager, or move into meat processing or agricultural equipment operation. With experience, they can apply for permanent residency and run a small farm.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones or fixed cameras to automatically monitor livestock health and activity, replacing manual patrols
- Automated feeding systems precisely dispense feed based on data, replacing manual feeding
- Automated environmental control (ventilation, heating) adjusts based on sensors, replacing manual operation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes historical market data and weather forecasts to assist in determining optimal slaughter time and price negotiation.
- Wearable devices + AI predict diseases, enabling early isolation of sick animals and reducing veterinary costs
- Digital twin simulation of farm management plans to optimize grazing layout and resource allocation

**Human moat:**

- Emergency veterinary judgment and treatment (e.g., dystocia, disease outbreaks)
- Complex interpersonal communication and team management (managing worker morale, coordinating tasks)
- Regulatory compliance responsibilities (final interpretation and enforcement of food safety and animal welfare regulations)
- Intuitive experience with specific livestock (e.g., identifying subtle signs of illness)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis and spreadsheet skills (e.g., Excel, farm management software)
- Operating and maintaining IoT devices (sensors, automatic feeding systems)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Basic veterinary knowledge (vaccine management, common diseases)
- Project management and cost accounting skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Agri-Food Immigration Pilot
- Provincial Nominee
- Seasonal Work
- Regional Labour Shortage

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for a beef cattle farm worker in Canada?**
A: Entry-level workers earn approximately CAD 28,000-35,000 annually; experienced workers up to CAD 45,000; supervisors up to CAD 55,000. Wages are usually hourly, around CAD 15-25 per hour.

**Q: Can I immigrate to Canada as a beef cattle farm worker?**
A: Yes, but mainly through the Agri-Food Immigration Pilot (AFIP) or Provincial Nominee Program (PNP). Requires employer sponsorship and meeting language and education requirements; typically need to work in Canada for 12 months before applying for permanent residency.

**Q: Is this career competitive?**
A: Entry-level positions are competitive due to low barriers and many seasonal workers. Experienced workers face less competition; farms in remote areas are easier to get jobs.
