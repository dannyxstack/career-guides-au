# Labourers in fish and seafood processing — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 95107 (NOC)**

Perform physical tasks such as cleaning, cutting, packaging, and freezing in fish and seafood processing plants; no high education required but must adapt to cold, humid environments.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

This role is entry-level physical labor with limited advancement paths; can transition to quality inspector, machine operator, or supervisor, but usually requires additional training or experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cutting machines replace manual fish cleaning, scaling, and gutting
- AI vision sorting system replaces manual grading and quality inspection
- Automated packaging line replaces manual weighing, sealing, and labeling.
- Smart freezer storage management system replaces manual handling and inventory recording

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted quality monitoring enhances defect detection accuracy
- Intelligent scheduling system optimizes production line processes to reduce waste
- Digital twin simulation trains new workers on operations to improve efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Flexible judgment for handling irregular or damaged products
- Handle emergency response to sudden mechanical failures or order changes
- Physical endurance and collaboration in low-temperature, humid environments
- Non-standardized sensory evaluation of freshness, odor, etc.

**Skills to build:**

- Basic mechanical maintenance and troubleshooting.
- Basic operation of AI visual quality inspection systems
- Data entry and simple production report analysis
- Food safety and hygiene standards (HACCP)
- Collaborative robot (cobot) operation and safety protocols

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal
- Rural Employment
- Export Industry
- Low Barrier to Entry

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for this position?**
A: Entry-level workers earn about CAD 28,000-35,000, mid-to-senior levels up to CAD 40,000-48,000, but heavily influenced by seasonality and region.

**Q: Is it easy to immigrate to Canada as a fish processing labourer?**
A: Difficult. This occupation is low-skilled (TEER 5), does not meet federal skilled migration requirements, and only a few provincial PNP programs have low-skilled categories requiring LMIA employer sponsorship.
