# Trappers and hunters — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 85104 (NOC)**

Set traps or use firearms to capture animals for fur, meat, or other by-products, primarily working in remote or wilderness areas under strict regulations.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,200. Demand for Canadian hunters and trappers is limited, influenced by wildlife populations, fur prices, and recreational hunting activities. Due to the small occupation size and remote locations, job opportunities are few with moderate competition.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Usually start as an apprentice or assistant, then gain field skills and experience to work independently. Career progression is limited; may shift to wildlife management, guiding, or conservation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Wildlife population data analysis and report generation
- Market trend forecasts for fur and meat prices
- Hunting license online application and compliance record automation.
- Automated sorting and mapping of GPS tracking data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drone-assisted reconnaissance of animal tracks and territory
- AI image recognition for rapid identification of fur type and quality
- Smart trap sensors monitor in real time and trigger remote alerts
- Machine learning optimises hunting routes and seasonal strategies
- AI-driven safety warning systems (weather, terrain risks)

**Human moat:**

- Fine manual skills for setting and removing traps in wild complex environments
- In-depth local knowledge of animal behavior and ecosystems
- Ethical judgment and real-time decision-making for legal, humane hunting
- Hands-on experience handling fresh meat and hides

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- Advanced route planning with GIS and GPS
- Basic data analysis and reporting software
- Use of AI image recognition tools
- Humanitarian Trapping and Animal Welfare Certification

**Key growth areas:**

- Specialized
- Rural
- Seasonal
- Traditional

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Love for the outdoors and wilderness survival
- Experience in hunting or trapping
- Able to accept seasonal work and remote environments

**Who is not suited**
- Dislike of animal slaughter or bad weather.
- Seeking stable income and urban lifestyle

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Is the income of a trap hunter stable?**
A: Unstable. Income heavily depends on fur market prices, catch volume, and weather, often requiring other jobs.

**Q: Can foreigners become hunters in Canada through skilled migration?**
A: Very difficult. This occupation is usually not on the Express Entry or PNP lists, and requires provincial licensing and local experience; it is recommended to obtain an LMIA work permit first.
