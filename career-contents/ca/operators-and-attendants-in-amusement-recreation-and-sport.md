# Operators and attendants in amusement, recreation and sport — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 65211 (NOC)**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level positions can advance to team leader or operations manager, with some moving into event planning or facility maintenance.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated ticketing and fare collection systems replace manual work
- Intelligent scheduling system replaces manual shift planning
- AI customer service chatbots answer common questions online
- Self-service lockers and equipment rental reduce manual operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI optimizes queue management, improving on-site scheduling efficiency.
- Smart surveillance system for security patrol and early warning
- AI analyzes tourist behavior data to assist event planning and pricing
- VR/AR devices enhance interactive experiences, requiring human guidance and maintenance

**Human moat:**

- Emergency response to sudden equipment failure or tourist injury
- Personalised services for tourists with special needs (e.g., disabilities, children)
- Interpersonal communication for maintaining order and emotional support
- Operations requiring physical flexibility (e.g., climbing gym safety officer, water facility rescue)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic AI tool usage (e.g., self-service equipment maintenance, data entry)
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Basic first aid and safety knowledge
- Multilingual communication skills (for tourist attractions)
- Basic equipment mechanical maintenance skills
- Digital marketing and social media operations (for small venues)

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal employment
- Tourism recovery
- Customer service
- Event management
