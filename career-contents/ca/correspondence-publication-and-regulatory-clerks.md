# Correspondence, publication and regulatory clerks — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 14301 (NOC)**

Correspondence, publishing, and regulatory affairs clerks handle correspondence, document publication, and regulatory compliance for corporate or government entities, including editing, proofreading, filing, and distributing official documents.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Junior clerks can advance to senior clerk or supervisory roles through experience, and some may transition to specialist regulatory affairs or publishing coordination positions. Obtaining relevant diplomas or certificates helps progress to management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Letter template writing and auto-reply
- Document formatting standardisation and basic proofreading
- routine information retrieval from regulatory databases
- Automated scheduling of archiving and distribution processes
- Generate standard compliance report drafts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-powered smart summarization and impact analysis of regulatory changes
- Using NLP tools to quickly review contract clause compliance
- Automated multilingual letter translation and localization adaptation
- AI-driven document quality check and consistency verification
- Smart Workflow Orchestration and Task Priority Prompts

**Human moat:**

- Interpret ambiguous regulatory clauses and provide professional opinions
- Handle highly sensitive correspondence involving commercial secrets
- Tone control and strategy adjustment in crisis communication
- Cross-departmental coordination and document coordination for complex projects
- Ensure legal compliance and brand consistency of final publications

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming (Python) and automation scripting.
- Regulatory document management systems (e.g., DocuSign, SharePoint)
- Prompt engineering and result review for AI text generation tools (e.g., ChatGPT)
- Data visualization tools (Tableau) for compliance data reporting
- Ability to update knowledge of IP and regulatory laws
- Project management and cross-department communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Administrative Support
- Document Management
- Regulatory Compliance
- Publishing Operations

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for a communication and regulation clerk?**
A: Entry-level salary about $32,000-$40,000 CAD, mid-level $40,000-$50,000, senior $50,000-$62,000. Salary varies by region, experience, and employer size.

**Q: Is this occupation suitable for immigrating to Canada?**
A: Not very suitable. This is a low-skilled administrative role, generally not meeting Express Entry or PNP skilled migration requirements; only in a few cases can a work visa be obtained through LMIA employer sponsorship.
