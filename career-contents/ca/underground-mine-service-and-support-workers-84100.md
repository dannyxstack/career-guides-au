# Underground mine service and support workers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 84100 (NOC)**

Perform support tasks such as support, ventilation, and track laying in underground mines to ensure safe and efficient mining operations.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Junior workers can advance to mine technician or team leader through experience and certification; some may move into mine safety supervision or management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automate generation of routine safety inspection and compliance reports
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters like gas and ventilation via sensors with automatic alarms
- Automated dispatching of mining equipment (e.g., autonomous trucks, drills) to reduce manual intervention
- Use AI to analyze historical data to predict equipment failures and schedule preventive maintenance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI integrates multi-source data to provide real-time decision support (e.g., optimal mining paths, risk heat maps)
- Enhance on-site inspection efficiency by overlaying underground maps and hazard tips via AR headsets
- AI-driven training simulator helps assistant supervisors practice emergency handling.
- Automating administrative tasks frees up time to focus on team management and safety culture

**Human moat:**

- On-site Judgment and Emergency Command in Critical Situations (e.g., gas explosion, collapse)
- Building Trust with Miners, Motivating Teams, and Managing Interpersonal Conflicts
- Intuition and experience with abnormal environmental cues (e.g., sensing pressure changes)
- Take legal responsibility and make ethical decisions that cannot be programmed
- Integrated decision-making in complex underground environments with multiple variables and uncertainties

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and visualization (e.g., Python, Power BI)
- Automated monitoring system operation and troubleshooting
- AI/Machine Learning fundamentals and model application understanding
- Digital twin and simulation software usage
- Change Management and Digital Leadership
- Cybersecurity fundamentals (protecting OT systems)

**Key growth areas:**

- Mining
- Resource Extraction
- Skilled Trades
- Labour Shortage

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment) | Employers must prove inability to hire local workers; work visa can be applied after obtaining LMIA |
| Subclass PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) | Provincial nominee programs (e.g., Ontario, BC) target mining workers |
| Subclass Express Entry (Express Entry (FSW/CEC)) | Must meet NOC TEER 2 requirements, but typically requires LMIA or provincial nomination points |

**PR friendliness: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Good physical fitness, able to adapt to underground work environment
- Willing to work in remote areas.
- Focus on safety, with a team-oriented spirit

**Who is not suited**
- Unsuitable for confined spaces or dark environments
- Sensitive to dust and noise

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for underground mine service and support workers?**
A: Entry-level worker annual salary approx. CAD 55k-70k, experienced up to CAD 90k-110k, usually includes allowances and overtime.
