# Fishing vessel deckhands — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 84121 (NOC)**

Perform fishing operations, gear maintenance, deck cleaning, and catch handling on fishing vessels; a key part of Canada's fishing workforce.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Generally start as an ordinary deckhand, gain experience and can be promoted to fishing gear supervisor or ship's officer. Some may move into aquaculture, fisheries management, or related maritime roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual handling of fishing gear deployment and retrieval (replaced by automated winches and robotic arms)
- daily deck cleaning and maintenance (taken over by robotic cleaning systems)
- Catch sorting and initial processing (computer vision and robotic arm automated sorting)
- Navigational lookout and collision avoidance (covered by autonomous driving and sensor systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Catch monitoring and fishing decision-making (AI analysis of fish sonar and satellite data to precisely recommend work locations)
- Equipment failure prediction (sensor and machine learning to alert maintenance timing)
- Safety monitoring (AI video analysis to detect deck hazards)
- Catch quality grading (computer vision-assisted rapid grading)

**Human moat:**

- On-the-spot judgment and emergency response in urgent situations (e.g., manual operation in severe weather)
- Fine repair of fishing gear and creative problem-solving on-site
- Deck team coordination and communication (unstructured task coordination)
- Responsibility for regulatory compliance and environmentally responsible fishing

**Skills to build:**

- Basic operation of ship automation control systems
- Digital tools (tablets/handheld terminals) and simple data analysis
- Fishery sensor and IoT equipment maintenance
- Understanding and oversight of AI-assisted decision-making systems
- Safety regulations and emergency response (enhanced)
- Basic mechanical repair and electronics knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal
- Coastal
- Resource-dependent
- Stable

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Enjoys outdoor physical work and does not mind working at sea
- Ability to adapt to seasonal unemployment and unstable income from fluctuating catches
- Willing to live in a remote coastal area

**Who is not suited**
- Those prone to seasickness or unable to withstand prolonged sea conditions
- Those seeking stable salary and fixed working hours

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a deckhand on a fishing vessel?**
A: Entry-level deckhand annual income approximately CAD 28,000-40,000, mid-level approximately CAD 35,000-55,000, senior or with mate certification can reach CAD 45,000-70,000. Income is significantly affected by season and catch volume.

**Q: What immigration pathways can foreign nationals use to become a deckhand on Canadian fishing vessels?**
A: Mainly through LMIA employer-sponsored work visa. A few coastal provinces may have fisheries-related PNP, but typically require local employer support. This occupation is not on the Express Entry or in-demand list.
