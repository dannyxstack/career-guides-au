# Aquaculture and marine harvest labourers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 85102 (NOC)**

Engaging in the cultivation, harvesting, and initial processing of aquatic plants and animals such as fish and shellfish, including feeding, net maintenance, harvesting, and cleaning.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Usually start as a general laborer, then gain experience to advance to farm supervisor or technician; a few may move into aquaculture management or quality control roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding system replaces manual scattering
- Drone/underwater robot inspection of cages replaces manual patrols
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor, replacing manual sampling
- Automated sorting and counting systems replace manual sorting
- Robotic arms replacing manual net pulling in fishing operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Data analysis tools assist in optimizing breeding density and feeding strategies
- Remote sensing and GIS technology enhance sea area site selection and disaster warning
- Machine vision aids early disease identification, improving diagnostic accuracy
- Automated record-keeping systems improve production ledger management efficiency
- Predictive models assist in weather and market supply-demand forecasting.

**Human moat:**

- Equipment maintenance and troubleshooting in complex environments
- Empirical diagnosis of sudden illnesses or abnormal behaviors
- Government/community communication and compliance management
- On-site decision-making for biosafety and emergency response
- Fine manual operations such as parent selection and surgical tagging

**Skills to build:**

- Automated farming system operation and maintenance
- Basic troubleshooting of sensors and IoT devices
- Basic data analysis and visualization
- Drone/underwater robot operation
- Aquatic disease diagnosis and biosecurity knowledge
- Sustainable aquaculture regulations and certification standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture expansion
- Sustainable seafood
- Coastal communities
- Seasonal labor

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment) | Employers must prove they cannot find local workers; after obtaining an LMIA, they can apply for a work visa. |
| Subclass Atlantic Immigration (Atlantic Immigration Program) | Atlantic provinces employer sponsorship, may be applicable for aquaculture positions |
| Subclass PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) | Some provinces (e.g., BC, Newfoundland) may recruit aquaculture workers through provincial nominations, but usually require employer sponsorship. |

**PR friendliness: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Enjoys outdoor and physical work, adaptable to marine and cold environments
- Willing to start from the bottom, interested in the aquaculture industry.
- Patient with repetitive work, safety-conscious

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking high income or rapid promotion
- Unable to adapt to seasonal unemployment, prefers indoor work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for aquaculture workers?**
A: Junior hourly rate about 15-20 CAD, annual income 30,000-40,000; experienced workers can reach 50,000-60,000 CAD.

**Q: Can aquaculture workers immigrate to Canada through skilled migration?**
A: This occupation is generally not in the high-skilled Express Entry category, relying mainly on LMIA work permits or employer-sponsored programs like the Atlantic Immigration Program.
