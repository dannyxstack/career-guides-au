# Bricklayers — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 72320 (NOC)**

Bricklayer lays bricks, concrete blocks, stone, etc., for structures like walls and chimneys. Demand is stable in Canadian residential and commercial construction, and bricklayers can immigrate through EE or PNP skilled immigration with high feasibility.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Bricklayers can advance to site foremen, project managers, or self-employed contractors. With experience, they can obtain Red Seal certification to enhance qualifications, or specialize in high-value areas like historical building restoration and high-end stone tiling.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Residential Construction
- Infrastructure Projects
- Skilled Trades Shortage
- Red Seal Endorsement

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass EE (Express Entry (FSW/CEC)) | Through Federal Skilled Worker or Canadian Experience Class immigration, meeting language, education, and work experience requirements; bricklayers typically fall under trade category (NOC 72320) and may need provincial nomination points. |
| Subclass PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) | Provincial skilled worker nomination programs, such as Ontario Skilled Trades, BC Skilled Immigration, etc., high demand for bricklayers, with EE gaining 600 points after nomination. |

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Physically fit, strong hands-on skills, adaptable to outdoors and high-intensity work
- Willing to learn skills through apprenticeships and accept lower initial pay
- Have construction experience and want to immigrate to Canada for blue-collar work

**Who is not suited**
- Those not suited for physical labor or sensitive to dust/weather
- Those seeking quick high salaries and unwilling to start from entry-level.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a bricklayer in Canada?**
A: Entry-level about CAD 35,000-45,000, mid-level 50,000-70,000, senior or self-employed up to 90,000+.

**Q: Is it difficult for bricklayers to immigrate to Canada?**
A: Relatively easy, as it is a skilled trade in demand; can be invited through Provincial Nominee Program or EE, but requires employer sponsorship or relevant work experience.

**Q: How do foreign bricklayers get Canadian certification for their experience?**
A: Requires ECA credential assessment and passing the provincial trade exam; some experience can be counted as apprenticeship credits, shortening training period.
