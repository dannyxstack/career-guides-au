# Dental technologists and technicians — career analysis · Canada

**Occupation code: 32112 (NOC)**

Dental technicians and prosthetists make and repair dental prostheses such as dentures, crowns, and bridges. They work manually or with CAD/CAM technology using various materials and equipment based on dentists' prescriptions and impressions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior technicians can start as lab assistants and become certified dental technicians after gaining experience. Experienced individuals can advance to lab supervisor, manager, or open their own dental lab. Continuous learning in digital dental technology (e.g., CAD/CAM) supports career development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Traditional plaster model making (replaced by intraoral scanning)
- Waxing and investment casting (replaced by direct CAD/CAM milling or 3D printing)
- Morphological design of standard crowns and bridges (basic form auto-generated by AI).
- Repetitive denture setup work (digital denture setup software accelerates the process)
- Some aspects of manual sanding and polishing (with automated polishing equipment becoming widespread)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use CAD software for personalized restoration design, overlaying aesthetic parameters
- Use AI scanning data to automatically generate occlusion and contact points, improving efficiency
- Using 3D printing to quickly produce trial pieces, shortening clinical feedback cycles
- Optimize color matching using digital colorimeters and AI recommendations
- Use data-driven predictive tools to optimize prosthesis lifespan design

**Human moat:**

- Clinical judgment and adjustment for complex occlusal relationships and gnathological issues
- Communicating aesthetic requirements with dentists (e.g., personalized contours, staining features)
- Creative solutions for handling rare or unusual cases
- Refined and customized artisan techniques (e.g. ceramic layering and stacking effects)
- Experiential knowledge of materials science and biocompatibility

**Skills to build:**

- Proficient in at least one CAD design software (e.g., exocad, 3Shape Dental System)
- Learn 3D printing slicer software and post-processing techniques
- Familiar with operation of intraoral scanners and digital impression processing
- Basic programming or scripting ability (for customized parameter design).
- Knowledge of digital colorimeters and color management
- Remote collaboration and digital workflow management

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital Dentistry
- CAD/CAM
- Geriatric Care
- Cosmetic Dentistry

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level of dental technicians?**
A: Dental technician salaries in Canada vary by experience and region. Entry-level approx. C$35,000-C$45,000, experienced mid-level C$45,000-C$60,000, senior or lab supervisor up to C$60,000-C$80,000.

**Q: What are the pathways for dental technicians to immigrate to Canada?**
A: Main pathways include Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) and Provincial Nominee Program (PNP). NOC 32112 is TEER 2, meeting FSW requirements. Some provinces like Ontario and British Columbia have specific PNP streams for healthcare occupations. Employer-sponsored LMIA can also support work permits and subsequent immigration.

**Q: Do dental technicians need a provincial license?**
A: Most provinces require dental technicians to register or obtain a license to practice. For example, Ontario requires registration with the College of Dental Technologists of Ontario. Other provinces like British Columbia require certification. Specific requirements may include completing an accredited program, passing exams, and continuing education.
