# Ships' Deck Crew Member — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ships' deck crew members perform manual tasks on vessels such as ferries, cargo ships, and offshore support vessels in Denmark. They handle mooring, cargo securing, maintenance, and lookout duties. The occupation is part of Denmark's substantial maritime sector, which includes shipping, fishing, and offshore energy. Most positions are covered by collective agreements. While automation is reshaping some tasks, demand remains steady for routine deck work on domestic and international routes. The role requires physical fitness, sea-readiness, and completion of basic safety training (STCW). It suits those who enjoy working outdoors in a team-oriented, safety-critical environment.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 249. The employment outlook for ships' deck crew in Denmark is stable but with limited growth. The industry faces automation of some tasks (e.g., mooring systems, autonomous navigation), which may reduce demand for unskilled crew. However, the Danish shipping fleet is modern and requires crew for ferries, offshore supply, and international trade. Brexit and geopolitical shifts have slightly increased demand for EU crew. Overall, openings will primarily come from retirements, with competition moderate. Further training (e.g., for deck officer roles) improves prospects.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman (AB) with experience and additional STCW certificates. Ambitious crew can pursue deck officer training at maritime academies (e.g., SIMAC in Svendborg) to become ship navigators or captains. Onshore opportunities exist in port operations, maritime logistics, or offshore wind maintenance. The path requires dedication to ongoing certification and willingness to spend extended periods at sea.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and AI tool usage (e.g., route optimization software)
- Data analysis and dashboard interpretation skills
- Skills in remotely operating drones or robots.
- Advanced maritime regulations and certification updates
- Soft skills: cross-cultural communication and crisis management
- Digital forensics and cybersecurity fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

- offshore wind
- ferry operations
- arctic shipping
- automation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a ships' deck crew member in Denmark?**
A: Annual pre-tax salary ranges from DKK 350,000 to DKK 400,000, depending on experience, vessel type, and collective agreements. Overtime and sea allowances may increase total compensation.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as deck crew in Denmark?**
A: It is difficult. Non-EU citizens generally need a work permit under the Pay Limit Scheme (requires salary above ~DKK 465,000, unlikely) or be part of an approved company's Fast-track scheme. Most deck crew positions are filled by EU/EEA nationals.

**Q: Is there a shortage of deck crew in Denmark?**
A: No, the occupation is not on the official shortage list (Positive List). Supply is generally sufficient.
