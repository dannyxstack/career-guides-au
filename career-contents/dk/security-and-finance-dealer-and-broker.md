# Securities and Finance Dealer and Broker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3311 (ISCO08)**

Securities and finance dealers and brokers in Denmark execute trades, advise clients, and manage portfolios within investment banks, asset management firms, and trading floors, predominantly in Copenhagen. They are regulated by the Danish Financial Supervisory Authority and must hold a mandatory securities dealer license. The role demands strong analytical skills and knowledge of financial markets. While traditional trading roles face automation pressure, opportunities are expanding in sustainable finance and fintech. The profession is not on the official shortage list but remains a skilled occupation eligible for work permits under the Pay Limit Scheme for high-income earners.

The employment outlook for securities dealers and brokers in Denmark is stable with moderate growth, driven by increased financial activity and the rise of green finance and digital assets. However, automation and algorithmic trading may reduce demand for traditional roles. Copenhagen remains the primary hub, with niche opportunities in compliance and risk management. The aging workforce may create replacement demand. Overall, the profession faces modest expansion but requires continuous upskilling to remain competitive.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★½).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior dealer or broker, advancing to senior trader, portfolio manager, or head of trading. Some transition to advisory roles or specialize in derivatives, equities, or sustainable investing. Professional certifications such as the Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) and the Danish Securities Dealer License are key to advancement. Networking within Copenhagen's financial district is crucial. The path requires resilience and adaptability to market cycles and regulatory changes.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically execute stock, bond, and commodity orders through algorithms, no manual order placement
- AI automatically generates market analysis reports and investment briefs
- AI-driven chatbots handle common customer inquiries (e.g., account inquiries, transaction confirmations)
- Rule-based system automatically identifies potential suspicious transactions and triggers alerts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes customer transaction history and risk preferences to assist in generating personalized investment portfolio recommendations
- Real-time market sentiment monitoring and event impact simulation to improve decision quality for advisors
- Automatically generate compliance checklists and regulatory reports to reduce paperwork time
- Predict upselling and cross-selling opportunities through customer behavior models

**Human moat:**

- Build deep trust relationships, understand clients' non-financial needs (e.g., family wealth inheritance, emotional needs)
- Provide qualitative judgment and real-time adjustments to investment strategies in complex market environments
- Take on personalized responsibility for one-on-one consulting and crisis communication with high-net-worth clients
- Ethical judgment and interpretation concerning regulatory grey areas and new financial products

**Skills to build:**

- Financial data analysis tools (e.g., Python, R, SQL).
- Understanding of AI/machine learning applications in portfolio optimization
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and AI-enhanced customer interaction platforms
- Regulatory technology (RegTech) and compliance automation tools
- Understanding of complex financial derivative pricing models
- Soft skills: negotiation, empathy, crisis communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable finance
- Fintech
- Algorithmic trading
- Compliance and risk

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical starting salary for a securities dealer in Denmark?**
A: Starting salaries range from 400,000 to 600,000 DKK annually, with potential for bonuses.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a securities dealer in Denmark?**
A: Yes, via the Pay Limit Scheme if the salary exceeds the threshold, or through the Fast-track Scheme if the employer is certified.

**Q: What education is required to become a securities dealer in Denmark?**
A: A bachelor’s degree in finance or economics is typical, followed by the mandatory Securities Dealer License from the Danish FSA.
