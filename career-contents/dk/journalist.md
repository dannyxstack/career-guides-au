# Journalist — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

Journalists in Denmark work across print, broadcast, and digital media, with a strong presence of public service and private media houses. The profession is regulated by the Danish Union of Journalists. Competition exists, but opportunities arise in digital and multimedia content. Many journalists are employed by large media conglomerates or work as freelancers, contributing to a dynamic and evolving industry.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an internship or trainee position, moving to reporter or sub-editor roles. Experienced journalists can become editors, columnists, or specialize in areas such as business or culture. Many also transition to communication roles in private companies or public institutions, leveraging their writing and analytical skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standard news briefs (e.g., financial reports, sports scores)
- Speech-to-text and automatic subtitle generation
- Fact-checking and data reconciliation (e.g., verifying statistical data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted data analysis and visualization (quickly identify trends)
- AI-recommended topics and audience preference prediction
- AI compiles multilingual information summaries

**Human moat:**

- Exclusive interviews and in-depth investigations (interpersonal insight and trust)
- Ethical judgment and sensitive issue gatekeeping
- Creative storytelling (features, commentary)

**Skills to build:**

- Data journalism (Python/R/data visualization)
- Multimedia production (video/podcast editing)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT-assisted writing)
- Fact-checking and information verification
- Audience Analysis and Digital Marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital journalism
- Data journalism
- Multimedia production
- Podcasting

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong language skills (Danish and English) and excellent writing abilities.
- Passionate about storytelling, current events, and digital media.
- Adaptable to changing media landscape and comfortable with deadlines.

**Who is not suited**
- Those preferring routine, predictable work without tight deadlines.
- Individuals uncomfortable with public scrutiny or working under pressure.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a journalist in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer, but are generally competitive and based on collective agreements. Entry-level journalists typically earn around 30,000 DKK per month, while experienced journalists can earn 45,000+ DKK. For precise data, refer to Statistics Denmark (LONS20).

**Q: Can a non-EU journalist work in Denmark?**
A: Yes, non-EU journalists can work via the Pay Limit Scheme if the salary meets the threshold (465,000 DKK in 2023) or the Fast-track Scheme if hired by a certified company. A Danish employer and relevant qualifications are required.

**Q: Is Danish language proficiency mandatory for journalists?**
A: Yes, strong Danish language skills are essential for most journalistic roles in Denmark, as the primary audience is Danish. English-language media exist but are limited.
