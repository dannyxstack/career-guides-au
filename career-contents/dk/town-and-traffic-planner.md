# Town and Traffic Planner — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2164 (ISCO08)**

Town and traffic planners in Denmark design sustainable urban spaces and transport systems, balancing aesthetics, functionality, and environmental goals. They work with municipalities, consulting firms, and government agencies to shape cities, neighbourhoods, and infrastructure. Denmark's strong emphasis on green mobility, compact cities, and climate adaptation drives demand for professionals who can integrate cycling, public transport, and pedestrian-friendly layouts. Planners often use GIS, BIM, and participatory methods. The role requires a master's degree in urban planning or a related field, analytical skills, and the ability to navigate complex regulations.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 449. Employment outlook for town and traffic planners in Denmark is stable to growing, fueled by urbanisation, climate adaptation initiatives, and major infrastructure investments (e.g., Metro expansion, new train lines). The Danish government's goal to reduce car traffic and promote cycling and public transport creates steady job opportunities. Digitalization (GIS, BIM, smart city tools) also boosts demand. Competition for positions exists, but qualified candidates with specialisations in sustainable mobility or climate resilience are sought after. Public sector roles in municipalities are common, while private consultancies offer dynamic project-based work.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression often starts as a planning assistant or consultant, moving to senior planner, project manager, or department head. Many begin in municipal planning departments or engineering consultancies. Specialisation in areas like sustainable mobility, climate adaptation, or urban regeneration accelerates advancement. Experienced planners may become chief planners or directors. Networking through the Danish Association of Town Planners (Byplanforeningen) and continuous learning (e.g., GIS certifications) enhance prospects. Some transition to academia or policy roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generates preliminary land use analysis report
- Performing standardized traffic flow simulations.
- Batch process geospatial data and create basic maps
- Compile routine regulatory compliance checklists
- Automatically generate meeting minutes and preliminary public opinion summaries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to simulate multiple planning scenarios and assess environmental impacts.
- Analyzing public opinion and extracting key demands using natural language processing
- Predict urban growth trends and infrastructure needs based on machine learning
- Automatically identify land use changes in satellite imagery
- Optimize transport network design to reduce commute time and carbon emissions

**Human moat:**

- Political judgment and negotiation skills to balance multiple stakeholders' interests
- Handling conflicting public opinions and reaching consensus
- Deep understanding and intuition of local socioeconomic context
- planning decisions bearing legal and ethical responsibilities
- Creative ability to design unique community spaces

**Skills to build:**

- Master advanced GIS analysis and automation (ArcPy, QGIS)
- Learn machine learning fundamentals and urban analysis applications (Scikit-learn, TensorFlow)
- Improve data visualization and communication skills (Tableau, D3.js)
- Keep up with updates to urban planning related laws and regulations
- Develops public participation and conflict mediation skills
- Learn sustainable infrastructure design principles

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable mobility
- Climate adaptation
- Urban planning
- GIS

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a town planner in Denmark?**
A: Entry-level salary ranges from 380,000 to 450,000 DKK per year; experienced planners earn 500,000-650,000 DKK; senior/lead planners can exceed 700,000 DKK. Public sector salaries may be slightly lower than private consulting.

**Q: Can non-EU town planners get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer and meet the Positive List criteria (relevant master's degree and salary threshold) or the Pay Limit Scheme. The Positive List includes 'Planlægger (by- og trafik.)'. The Fast-track scheme for certified companies is also an option.

**Q: How can I advance my career as a town planner in Denmark?**
A: Specialise in sustainable mobility, climate adaptation, or digital planning. Gain experience in municipal planning or large infrastructure projects. Membership in the Danish Association of Town Planners and continuing education can open senior roles.
