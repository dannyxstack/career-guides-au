# Data Entry Clerk — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 4132 (ISCO08)**

Data Entry Clerks in Denmark perform routine data input, verification, and record-keeping tasks across public and private sectors. The role is widespread in administrative departments, financial services, and logistics, often as an entry-level office position. While many positions require only basic computer literacy and Danish language skills, some employers prefer candidates with vocational training in office administration. Automation and digitalization are reducing demand for pure data entry, but tasks are evolving to include database management and quality control.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,171. Employment prospects for Data Entry Clerks in Denmark are stable but declining gradually due to automation and offshoring of repetitive tasks. Opportunities remain in smaller firms, healthcare administration, and local government where human oversight is valued. Upskilling in areas like data analysis or customer service can improve job security. The occupation is not on the official shortage list, and competition is moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression often involves moving into more complex administrative roles, such as office coordinator, accounts assistant, or database supervisor. Some clerks transition into IT support after acquiring additional certifications. Employers increasingly seek candidates who can handle digital records and use software like ERP systems. Continuous learning is key to advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually enter data from paper or electronic documents into databases or spreadsheets
- Check format and basic accuracy of input data
- Repetitive batch data entry, such as invoices and forms
- Basic data cleaning and deduplication

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses AI tools to automatically extract document data, with humans handling exceptions.
- Use automated scripts to verify data consistency and improve efficiency
- Analyze data trends and generate reports through AI-assisted analysis

**Human moat:**

- Complex data judgment and anomaly handling capability
- Client communication and business understanding (e.g., explaining data deviations)
- Cross-system data integration and process optimization
- Maintain data privacy and security compliance

**Skills to build:**

- Learn Python or VBA for automated data processing
- Proficiency in advanced Excel functions (e.g., Power Query, macros)
- Familiar with OCR and RPA tools (e.g., UiPath)
- Data quality management and database basics (SQL)
- Basic data analysis and visualization (e.g., Tableau)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digitalization
- Automation
- Data Management
- Remote Work

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Data Entry Clerk in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by industry and experience but are generally in the range of DKK 30,000–35,000 per month before tax for full-time work. Entry-level positions may start lower, while those with specialized skills can earn more. Check the latest collective agreements or Statistics Denmark for precise figures.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for a Data Entry Clerk job in Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult. The Pay Limit Scheme requires a salary above about DKK 465,000 annually, which is above typical data entry pay. The Positive List does not include this occupation. EU/EEA citizens can work freely; for others, alternative routes like family reunification or study may apply.
