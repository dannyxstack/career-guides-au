# Crop Farm Labourer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9211 (ISCO08)**

Crop farm labourers perform manual tasks on farms, such as planting, weeding, harvesting, and packing crops. In Denmark, this role is predominantly seasonal and filled by EU/EEA workers under free movement rules. The occupation requires physical stamina and often involves irregular hours and outdoor work in varying weather conditions. It is not classified as a skilled occupation for immigration purposes, and salaries typically fall below the threshold for work permit schemes aimed at third-country nationals.

Danish agriculture faces a labour shortage, especially during peak seasons, leading to reliance on migrant workers from other EU countries. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited for crop farm labourers. With experience, a labourer may become a supervisor or team leader, or specialise in tasks like tractor driving or pesticide application. Some transition to year-round roles in livestock or greenhouse operations. Further education in agriculture (e.g., a vocational degree) can lead to positions such as farm manager or agricultural technician. However, most workers remain in seasonal labour roles due to the low barriers to entry.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual seeding and transplanting: replaced by automated seeders and transplanting robots
- Weed control and pest monitoring: AI visual recognition combined with precision spraying robots.
- Routine irrigation operations: sensors and smart irrigation systems automatically regulate
- Crop maturity detection and harvesting: computer vision and robotic arms automatically assess and pick
- Field data recording: automatic collection and upload by IoT sensors

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining complex agricultural machinery: AI-assisted diagnostics and remote guidance improve efficiency
- Precision agriculture data analysis: using AI to optimize planting density and fertilization timing
- Crop health monitoring: AI-assisted early disease detection and intervention suggestions
- Quality grading and packaging: AI vision systems assist manual sorting with high speed and precision

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making ability for sudden anomalies (e.g., extreme weather, equipment failure)
- Flexible operation in complex terrain and multi-crop mixed planting environments
- Interpersonal collaboration and communication with farmers and technology suppliers
- Quality assessment based on local experience (e.g., optimal harvest window)

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural machinery operation and basic maintenance.
- Use of precision agriculture tools (e.g., GPS navigation, drones)
- Data analysis and agricultural software application
- Basic programming and automation system configuration
- Read technical documentation in English or Italian
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Greenhouse production
- Mechanised harvesting
- Precision agriculture

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Physically resilient individuals who enjoy outdoor work and are comfortable with repetitive tasks.
- People seeking seasonal or short-term employment in a rural setting.
- Workers open to travelling and living temporarily in Denmark (often from other EU countries).

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking high-paying or permanent, year-round employment.
- Individuals preferring indoor, climate-controlled, or office-based work.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a crop farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, but a common range is DKK 130-150 per hour (pre-tax). Annualised for full-time seasonal work, this would be around DKK 250,000-270,000 per year. Overtime and bonuses may apply during peak harvest periods.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a crop farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: It is difficult. The occupation is not on the Positive List for skilled work, and salaries usually do not meet the Pay Limit Scheme threshold. The Seasonal Work Permit exists for up to 6 months but is rarely used for this role; EU/EEA workers fill most positions.

**Q: Is previous farming experience required?**
A: No, most employers provide on-the-job training. However, a driver's licence and physical fitness are often necessary.
