# Teaching Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2359 (ISCO08)**

Teaching professionals not elsewhere classified cover a diverse range of educators in Denmark, including instructors in adult education, vocational training, special education, language schools, and private tutoring. They work outside the mainstream public school system but play a vital role in upskilling the workforce and integrating immigrants. Employment is often project-based or part-time, though permanent positions exist at adult education centers and vocational colleges. The field requires strong pedagogical skills and, generally, fluency in Danish, except in international or language-specific settings. Denmark's emphasis on lifelong learning and reskilling supports stable demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically moves from teaching to senior instructor, coordinator of educational programs, or management of training departments. Experienced professionals may specialise in curriculum development or educational consultancy. Some transition into public school teaching if they acquire the formal teacher degree. Further education (e.g., a master's in adult education) can lead to roles in educational leadership or policy. Continuous professional development is encouraged and often employer-sponsored.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a teaching professional not elsewhere classified in Denmark?**
A: The average annual gross salary ranges from 400,000 to 550,000 DKK, depending on sector (public vs. private), experience, and qualifications. Part-time or hourly positions are common and may pay around 250-350 DKK per hour for freelance trainers.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as teaching professionals in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. They must have a valid work permit, e.g., under the Pay Limit Scheme if the salary exceeds 487,000 DKK/year, or as a researcher if applicable. Danish language at C1 level is usually mandatory, and foreign qualifications must be recognised by the Danish Agency for Higher Education. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.
