# Information and Communications Technology User Support Technician — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3512 (ISCO08)**

Information and Communications Technology User Support Technicians in Denmark, commonly known as IT-supportere, provide frontline technical assistance to end-users, troubleshoot hardware and software issues, and manage helpdesk operations. They are employed across public and private sectors, including large corporations, IT service companies, and government institutions. With Denmark's high digitalization rate, these technicians are vital for maintaining IT infrastructure and ensuring smooth operations. The role often involves on-site and remote support, requiring strong communication and problem-solving skills. The position is typically entry-level in IT, with many candidates holding a vocational education or an Academy Profession degree in IT support.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 10,150. The employment outlook for IT support technicians in Denmark is positive. As businesses continue to digitize and adopt cloud-based services, the need for on-the-ground IT support is expected to remain stable. However, some level of automation in helpdesk tasks (e.g., chatbots) may reduce demand for junior roles, but overall employment is projected to grow moderately due to increasing cybersecurity needs and the expansion of IT systems. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for IT support technicians in Denmark typically follows a path from first-line support to second-line or specialist roles, such as network administrator or systems administrator. Many advance by obtaining vendor certifications (e.g., Microsoft, Cisco) and gaining experience with specific technologies. Further development can lead to IT project management, IT security, or infrastructure architecture. The Danish labor market values hands-on experience, and many professionals pursue further education such as a Professional Bachelor's degree in IT to expand their opportunities. The role is often a stepping stone to more specialized IT positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automate common IT issues (e.g., password resets, network connection resets) via AI chatbots
- AI auto-diagnosing hardware faults and recommending replacement parts
- Automated script writing and execution (e.g., system log analysis, patch installation)
- Knowledge base retrieval and answer delivery (AI replaces manual document searching)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted root cause analysis for complex problems (providing fault trees, log correlation suggestions)
- AI-driven monitoring and early warning system to proactively detect potential system failures
- Automatically generate customer reports and technical documents to improve communication efficiency
- AI tools accelerate new employee training (simulating fault scenarios)
- Real-time multilingual translation support to expand service scope

**Human moat:**

- Diagnosis and debugging of cross-system integration issues (involving legacy systems, cloud services, etc.).
- Empathetic communication and conflict resolution with clients under high pressure
- Creative problem-solving for non-standard, undocumented faults
- Decision-making in safety-sensitive scenarios (e.g., disconnecting network vs. restoring data)
- Deep adaptation and optimization of software and hardware for specific business scenarios

**Skills to build:**

- Cloud infrastructure (AWS/Azure/GCP) management and troubleshooting
- Automation operation tools (Ansible, Terraform, PowerShell)
- AI/ML fundamentals (able to understand model outputs, fine-tune prompts)
- Cybersecurity basics (e.g., vulnerability patching, auditing)
- Data analysis and visualization (SQL, Python, Power BI)
- Advanced client management and project management certifications (ITIL, PMP)

**Key growth areas:**

- cloud services
- cybersecurity
- remote support
- digital transformation

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an IT support technician in Denmark?**
A: Entry-level salaries for IT support technicians start around 330,000 DKK per year. With experience and certifications, salaries can range from 400,000 to 450,000 DKK annually. Senior roles or specialists may earn higher.

**Q: Can I get a work visa for Denmark as an IT support technician?**
A: Yes, if you are a non-EU/EEA citizen, you can apply for a work permit under the Positive List scheme if you have a job offer and the occupation is on the list (it is). Alternatively, the Pay Limit Scheme is available if the salary exceeds the threshold. EU/EEA citizens can work freely without a permit.

**Q: What type of education is most common for IT support technicians in Denmark?**
A: The most common education is a 2-year vocational education (EUD) in IT support (IT-supporter), often combined with an apprenticeship. Alternatively, a 2-year Academy Profession degree in IT support is also popular. Many technicians also hold industry certifications like CompTIA A+.
