# Health Care Assistant — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 5321 (ISCO08)**

Health care assistants in Denmark, known as social- og sundhedsassistenter, provide practical nursing and personal care in hospitals, nursing homes, home care, and psychiatric facilities. They are integral to the Danish welfare system, working under registered nurses or other health professionals. The role combines elements of nursing assistance and social care, requiring both clinical skills and empathy. With an aging population, demand is consistently high across all regions, especially in elderly care and community services.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 25,666. Employment prospects for health care assistants in Denmark are very strong due to demographic shifts and government priorities in elder care. The sector faces a chronic shortage, with thousands of positions unfilled each year. Municipalities and regions are actively recruiting, offering stable public-sector employment. The need is expected to grow further as the population ages, making this a secure career choice with good job security.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for health care assistants includes specialization in areas like dementia care, rehabilitation, or psychiatry. With additional training, they can become registered nurses or pursue management roles in care settings. Many also take on supervisory duties. The profession offers clear pathways through the Danish vocational education system, with opportunities for lifelong learning and skill upgrading.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic monitoring and recording of patient vital signs.
- Basic health data entry and electronic medical record updates
- Use AI tools for medication reminders and simple checks
- Partial patient communication and information inquiry (e.g., AI chatbot)
- Scheduling and materials management (optimized by AI systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic tools help identify early symptoms
- Smart wearable devices remotely monitor patients, allowing assistants to focus on emergencies
- AI translation tools improve communication with multilingual patients
- Automated document generation reduces paperwork time, increasing direct care
- Virtual reality simulation training enhances nursing skills.

**Human moat:**

- Providing emotional support and human care
- Performing complex personal care tasks such as bathing and feeding
- Quick decision-making and flexible response in emergency situations
- Builds trust relationships and team collaboration
- Handling unstructured patient needs and discomfort

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in AI-powered nursing record and monitoring systems
- Learn basic data analysis and health information technology
- Enhancing Communication and Cross-Cultural Nursing Skills
- Participating in virtual reality simulation training
- Obtain certification in aged care or rehabilitation nursing
- Learn psychological support and crisis intervention skills

**Key growth areas:**

- elderly care
- home care
- psychiatric care
- rehabilitation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a health care assistant in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by region and experience. Generally, they range on public sector pay scales, with starting levels around mid-200s to low-300s thousand DKK per year.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as health care assistants in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the Positive List. A job offer and authorization from the Patient Safety Authority are required. Danish language proficiency is mandatory.

**Q: Do I need to speak Danish to work as a health care assistant?**
A: Yes, Danish language skills are essential for patient communication and obtaining professional authorization. Minimum B1-B2 level is typically required.
