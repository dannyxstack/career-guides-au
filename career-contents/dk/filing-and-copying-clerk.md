# Filing and Copying Clerk — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 4415 (ISCO08)**

Filing and copying clerks in Denmark perform routine administrative tasks such as organizing documents, operating photocopiers and scanners, and maintaining physical or digital filing systems. These roles are typically found in government offices, private companies, and service organizations. The position requires basic computer literacy and attention to detail. Due to automation and digitalization, demand for traditional filing clerks is declining, though opportunities remain in public institutions and specialized archives.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 308. The employment outlook for filing and copying clerks in Denmark is stable to declining. Automation and digital document management reduce the need for manual filing, but niche roles in archives, libraries, and some government departments persist. Most new openings come from staff turnover rather than growth. The occupation is not on any shortage list, and wages are modest. Employment is concentrated in the public sector and larger private firms.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression is limited. With experience, clerks may move into senior administrative roles or specialize in records management. Further training in digital archiving, data management, or IT systems can lead to better-paid positions. Many use this role as an entry-level job before transitioning to other administrative or clerical work. The path requires additional qualifications for higher responsibility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual document sorting and filing, replaced by digital document management systems
- Copying and organizing paper documents, replaced by digital scanning and cloud storage
- Document retrieval and borrowing registration, automatically completed by AI-driven search tools
- Document version control and updates, automated process management system takeover
- Daily office supply management, automated replenishment via smart procurement system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Improve document entry efficiency using OCR and intelligent classification tools
- Use document management platforms (e.g., SharePoint) for team collaboration and permission control
- Use data analysis tools to generate document usage reports and optimize the filing system
- Apply RPA to automate repetitive tasks, freeing up time to handle exceptions or complex documents.
- Conduct document content review and compliance checks with AI assistance.

**Human moat:**

- Professional judgment for handling unstructured or ambiguous documents
- Cross-departmental communication and coordination to resolve document disputes
- Understanding tacit knowledge and unwritten rules within an organization
- Monitor automated systems operations and correct errors
- Maintain security and privacy of sensitive or classified documents

**Skills to build:**

- Document management system (e.g., Alfresco, DocuWare) operation
- Basic programming or RPA tool (e.g., UiPath) application
- Data analysis and reporting (Excel advanced features, Power BI)
- Digital literacy and information management
- English (handling multilingual documents)
- Communication and problem-solving skills.

**Key growth areas:**

- Digitalization
- Records management
- Document scanning
- Archiving systems

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a filing and copying clerk in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are modest, typically between 280,000 and 320,000 DKK annually before tax, depending on experience and sector. The occupation is not among high-paying roles.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa as a filing clerk?**
A: It is difficult because the salary rarely meets the Pay Limit Scheme threshold (487,000 DKK in 2025) and the job is not on the Positive List. Most come via family reunification or EU free movement.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: Limited without further education. Some become senior administrative assistants or records managers. Pursuing a diploma in information management can open doors in larger organizations.
