# Subsistence Fisher, Hunter, Trapper or Gatherer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6340 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers individuals who harvest wild resources for personal consumption, including fishing in inland and coastal waters, hunting game, trapping fur-bearing animals, and collecting wild plants, berries, mushrooms, or shellfish. In Denmark, these activities are typically regulated through licensing (jagt- og fisketegn) and are largely recreational rather than occupational. A very small number of people derive primary income from subsistence harvesting, often supplemented by part-time work or social benefits. The sector is declining due to urbanization, strict environmental regulations, and the availability of commercial alternatives.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is virtually non-existent as an occupational category. Those who do engage may obtain additional licenses for specific game species or fishing methods. Some transition to commercial fishing (ISCO 622) or hunting guides (ISCO 5152), but this requires significant capital and regulatory compliance. The path typically involves starting as a hobbyist, then possibly selling small surpluses under special permits. Formal education is unnecessary beyond mandatory licenses. Few see this as a long-term career due to low and unstable income.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using smartphones, GPS, simple apps)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Basic data analysis (capture records, weather patterns)
- Knowledge of sustainable resource management

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a subsistence fisher in Denmark?**
A: Subsistence fishers typically have no or very low cash income from fishing. If any surplus is sold, earnings are minimal. Most rely on other jobs or benefits. Official salary statistics do not cover this occupation separately.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national move to Denmark to work as a hunter or gatherer?**
A: Generally, no. There is no specific immigration route for subsistence occupations. Non-EU/EEA nationals would need a work permit under a skilled scheme, which this occupation does not qualify for. Only EU/EEA free movement applies, but is limited to self-sufficiency activities.

**Q: Is subsistence fishing considered a skilled occupation for migration in Denmark?**
A: No. It is not on any positive list or shortage list. The occupation requires no formal qualifications and does not meet the criteria for skilled migration. It is not a viable route for obtaining a residence or work permit in Denmark.
