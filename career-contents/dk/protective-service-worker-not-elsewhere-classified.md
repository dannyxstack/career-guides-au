# Protective Services Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective services workers in Denmark include security guards, private investigators, and event safety personnel. The sector is regulated by the Danish Police, with mandatory training for security roles. Demand is steady due to security needs in retail, events, and critical infrastructure.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression from security guard to supervisor, operations manager, or specialized roles like dog handler or technical surveillance. Additional certifications in management or cybersecurity can lead to higher positions. Some transition to police or military careers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI video surveillance automatically detects abnormal events and alerts
- Automated visitor registration and identity verification systems replace manual registration
- AI-driven patrol robots replacing fixed-route patrols

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI video analysis assisting real-time threat assessment and decision-making
- AI report generation tools quickly summarize event logs
- AI voice assistant supports emergency response communication and coordination
- AI training simulators improve exercise effectiveness

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency judgment and crisis intervention ability
- Interpersonal communication and persuasion with clients, visitors, and law enforcement
- Legal compliance and licensing liability (e.g., use-of-force boundaries)
- Risk assessment intuition in complex physical environments

**Skills to build:**

- AI monitoring system operation and data analysis
- Basic cybersecurity knowledge (integration of physical security and IT)
- Emergency communication and coordination software (e.g., Duress system).
- Advanced training in conflict resolution and counter-terrorism awareness.
- Data privacy regulations and compliance (Australian Privacy Act)
- Digital reporting and visualization tools (e.g., Tableau)

**Key growth areas:**

- Event security
- Surveillance technology
- Corporate security
- Private investigation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for protective services workers in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by role, location, and experience. Entry-level positions are typically covered by collective agreements in the security industry.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as protective services workers in Denmark?**
A: It is difficult as the occupation is not on the Positive List. The Pay Limit Scheme may be an option if the salary is high enough, but typical salaries are below the threshold. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: What training is required?**
A: Mandatory security guard course approved by the Danish Police, which includes legal and practical training. Private investigators require a separate license.
