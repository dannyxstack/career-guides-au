# Stationary Plant and Machine Operator Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 8189 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers operators of stationary industrial machinery and plant not elsewhere classified, such as in manufacturing, energy production, water treatment, and recycling. In Denmark, these roles are concentrated in the industrial and energy sectors, often in automated or semi-automated environments. Workers monitor and control equipment, ensure production quality, and perform basic maintenance. The job requires technical understanding, attention to safety, and ability to work shifts.

Employment for stationary plant operators is expected to remain steady, with moderate demand in energy (especially wind and biogas), waste management, and food processing. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a machine operator, advancing to senior operator, team leader, or shift supervisor. Further training can lead to specialization (e.g., process technician, automation technician) or move into maintenance or quality control. Some operators transfer to technical schools as instructors. The field offers stable employment with opportunities for upskilling through vocational adult education (AMU courses).

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated production quality inspection using sensors and AI vision
- Predictive maintenance scheduling for equipment based on historical data
- Standardized material handling and packaging processes.
- Adaptive adjustment and optimization of production parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted predictive maintenance improves equipment reliability
- Real-time production data dashboards assist operational decisions
- Smart scheduling system optimizes multi-machine coordination efficiency
- Remote expert collaboration system enables rapid fault diagnosis

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of non-standard mechanical faults.
- Ability to flexibly switch between multiple varieties and small batch production
- Perception of subtle anomalies like unusual equipment noises and vibrations.
- Safety management and emergency shutdown procedures

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and PLC debugging.
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) data analysis
- Collaborative robot (KUKA/Universal Robots) operation
- Lean production and Six Sigma methods
- Understanding of AI tools in industrial applications

**Key growth areas:**

- Green energy plants
- Process automation
- Waste-to-energy
- Digital monitoring

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a stationary plant operator in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Entry-level operators earn around DKK 300,000-350,000 annually, while experienced operators can reach DKK 400,000-480,000. Shift allowances and overtime add to base pay.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work visa as a stationary plant operator?**
A: This occupation is not on the Positive List for skilled shortage, so the main route is the Pay Limit Scheme. However, operator salaries often fall below the threshold (DKK 31,000/month). An employer-specific fast-track scheme may apply for large companies, but individual cases are rare.
