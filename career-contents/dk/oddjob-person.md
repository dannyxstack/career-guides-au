# Odd-Job Person — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons in Denmark perform a variety of simple, non-routine tasks such as cleaning, gardening, moving, and small repairs for households and small businesses. This occupation is largely informal or part-time, often filled by local or EU/EEA workers. Despite low formal education requirements, it offers flexible employment but limited job security and benefits.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is minimal; odd-job persons often remain in entry-level positions unless they gain skills in a specific trade (e.g., cleaning, gardening) or start their own business. Some may transition to cleaner, handyman, or care assistant roles. Long-term advancement requires upskilling through vocational education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- domestic cleaning
- property maintenance
- gardening
- handyman services

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Denmark?**
A: Odd-job persons are often paid hourly wages, typically below the minimum for skilled work. Common rates range from 110 to 150 DKK per hour for cleaning or gardening tasks, but earnings vary widely.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work permit as an odd-job person in Denmark?**
A: No. Denmark's work permit schemes (e.g., Pay Limit Scheme, Positive List) require higher education or high salary. Odd-job work does not meet these criteria, so non-EU nationals cannot obtain a residence and work permit for this occupation.

**Q: Can an odd-job person progress to a trade like electrician?**
A: Yes, but it requires enrolling in a vocational education program, which typically takes 3-4 years. Odd-job experience may provide relevant practical skills, but formal apprenticeship is needed.
