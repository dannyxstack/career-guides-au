# Agricultural and Forestry Production Manager — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 1311 (ISCO08)**

Agricultural and Forestry Production Managers in Denmark oversee farming, forestry, and related operations, ensuring efficient production of crops, livestock, or timber. They manage staff, budgets, and compliance with environmental and animal welfare regulations. The role is vital in Denmark's highly mechanized and export-oriented agricultural sector, which focuses on dairy, pork, cereals, and sustainable forestry. With Denmark's green transition, managers increasingly integrate digital tools, precision farming, and renewable energy on farms. The occupation suits individuals with practical experience and leadership skills, often combined with formal education in agriculture or business. Workplaces range from family-owned farms to large commercial estates and forestry enterprises.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 120. Employment for Agricultural and Forestry Production Managers in Denmark is stable but declining slightly due to farm consolidation and automation. The number of farms is decreasing while average farm size increases, creating demand for skilled managers with business acumen. Organic farming and bioenergy production are growth niches, requiring expertise in new technologies. The workforce is aging, with many farm owners retiring, opening opportunities for younger managers. Overall, job openings will arise from replacements rather than expansion, with moderate competition for top-tier positions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Denmark typically starts with practical farming experience, a vocational agricultural degree (e.g., agricultural technician), and then an advanced management programme. Many managers eventually become farm owners or co-owners, often through family succession or partnerships. Specialization in organic farming, forestry, or agro-tourism can differentiate candidates. Continuing education in finance, sustainability, and digital farm management is increasingly valued. With experience, managers can move into advisory roles, agricultural consultancy, or leadership in larger cooperatives.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically collect and organize crop growth data, weather reports, etc., reducing manual data entry.
- Using AI systems to automatically generate production reports and compliance documents
- Automatically detect crop diseases, pests, and growth anomalies using sensors and drone imagery
- Automated irrigation and fertilization scheduling based on AI prediction models
- Automated inventory tracking and supply chain scheduling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze soil, climate, and market data to optimize planting plans and crop rotation
- Early detection of pests, diseases, and nutrient deficiencies using machine vision and predictive models, improving decision accuracy
- Combining historical yield and price data, AI-assisted development of sales strategies and pricing
- Using digital twins and simulation technology to optimize farm resource allocation and operational processes
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection enhance early warning capabilities for forest fires and illegal logging

**Human moat:**

- Cross-departmental communication and team leadership, especially interaction with manual workers
- Tacit knowledge of local ecosystems and agricultural practices (e.g., soil feel, phenological observations)
- Rapid judgment and unstructured decision-making in response to sudden catastrophic events (e.g., extreme weather, disease outbreaks).
- Maintaining long-term trust relationships with suppliers, distributors, and regulatory bodies
- Develop ethical sustainability goals and implement social responsibility plans

**Skills to build:**

- Master agricultural IoT and sensor integration technology
- Learning to use drones and satellite data for precision agriculture management
- Familiarity with AI-assisted decision-making platforms (e.g., crop models, price prediction software)
- Improve data analysis skills to interpret AI reports and drive improvements
- Learn basic Python programming and automation scripts to customize data pipelines
- Understanding of agricultural robot operation and maintenance basics

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable farming
- Precision agriculture
- Organic production
- Biomass and renewable energy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an Agricultural Production Manager in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by farm size and region, but a typical monthly pre-tax salary ranges from DKK 30,000 to 40,000 (approx. DKK 360,000-480,000/year). Managers with a degree or large operations may earn up to DKK 45,000/month. The Pay Limit Scheme threshold (DKK 448,000/year) is at the higher end, so many managers may just exceed it.

**Q: Can a non-EU Agricultural Production Manager get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, if the salary meets the Pay Limit Scheme (≥ DKK 448,000/year) or if the job is on the Positive List. Agricultural managers are not on the Positive List, but the Pay Limit is the main route. Alternatively, if you are a researcher or have a fast-track certified employer, other options exist. However, most agricultural manager jobs pay below the threshold, making it challenging for non-EU applicants.

**Q: What education is required to become a farm manager in Denmark?**
A: Typically, a combination of practical experience and formal agricultural education is needed. Most managers hold an Agricultural Technician degree (3.5-4 years) or a bachelor’s degree in agricultural science or economics. The Green Certificate (Grønt Bevis) is mandatory for farm ownership/management, and ongoing training is common.
