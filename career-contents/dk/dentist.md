# Dentists — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2261 (ISCO08)**

Dentists in Denmark are highly trained healthcare professionals responsible for diagnosing, preventing, and treating oral diseases and conditions. They work primarily in private practices or public dental clinics under the Danish public healthcare system. The profession is regulated by the Danish Patient Safety Authority, requiring authorization to practice. Denmark has a strong tradition of oral health, with a high demand for dental services driven by an aging population and increased focus on preventive care. Dentists often collaborate with dental hygienists and assistants.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,246. Employment outlook for dentists in Denmark remains positive due to a sustained shortage, particularly in rural and peripheral regions. The aging population increases the need for restorative and prosthetic treatments. Digitalization and new technologies are creating opportunities for specialized services. However, competition in urban areas may be higher. Overall, the profession offers stable employment prospects with a growing emphasis on preventive and geriatric dentistry.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for dentists in Denmark typically includes specialization in fields like orthodontics, oral surgery, or pediatric dentistry after additional training. Many dentists eventually become practice owners or partners. Opportunities in research and academia are available, often requiring a PhD. The Danish Dental Association supports professional development. Some dentists also work in public health administration or as consultants for insurance companies.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening and anomaly marking of X-rays and CT scans (AI reading)
- Administrative paperwork such as patient appointment, billing, and medical record organization
- Standardized preventive procedures like teeth cleaning and fluoride application (automated dental chair)
- Basic intraoral scanning and digital model generation
- Automated review and filling of insurance claim documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems provide treatment suggestions, improving diagnosis accuracy
- Simulate surgical paths via digital twin to optimize implant/orthodontic planning
- Real-time voice recording and medical record generation, freeing doctor's attention
- Personalized prevention plan recommendations based on patient historical data
- Virtual reality training systems enhance complex surgical skills

**Human moat:**

- Complex Surgical Decisions and Manual Operations (e.g., Root Canals, Tooth Extractions)
- Patient communication, empathy, and behaviour management (especially for children/anxious patients)
- Interdisciplinary Comprehensive Diagnosis (e.g., Oral-Systemic Disease Links)
- Emergency management and clinical judgement for sudden conditions
- Professional licensing and legal liability

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted Diagnostic Tool Operation and Result Interpretation
- Digital oral scanning and 3D printing design
- Patient interaction via telehealth platforms
- Basic data analysis and clinical research
- Patient communication and digital health education
- Continuous learning of automated workflow integration

**Key growth areas:**

- Geriatric dentistry
- Digital dentistry
- Preventive care
- Tele-dentistry

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Are dentist salaries regulated in Denmark?**
A: Dentist salaries are typically determined by collective agreements between the Danish Dental Association and employers, varying by region and experience. Private practice earnings can differ significantly based on patient volume.

**Q: Can non-EU dentists work in Denmark?**
A: Yes, non-EU dentists can apply for a work visa under the Positive List for Skilled Work (Higher Education) or the Pay Limit Scheme, provided they have a job offer and meet language and authorization requirements.

**Q: Do I need to speak Danish to work as a dentist?**
A: Yes, Danish language proficiency at B2 level is mandatory for authorization to ensure effective patient communication and record-keeping.
