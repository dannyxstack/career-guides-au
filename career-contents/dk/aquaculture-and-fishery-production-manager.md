# Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 1312 (ISCO08)**

Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers oversee the daily operations of fish farms, hatcheries, and fishing enterprises in Denmark. They manage breeding, feeding, health monitoring, and harvesting to ensure sustainable and profitable production. Denmark's aquaculture sector is expanding, driven by demand for sustainable seafood and technological innovation. These managers coordinate staff, budgets, and compliance with environmental and food safety regulations. The role requires a blend of biological knowledge, business acumen, and leadership skills. Denmark's strong focus on clean water and efficient recirculating systems offers a dynamic environment for professionals in this field.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 51. Employment outlook for Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers in Denmark is positive. The sector is growing due to increased investment in land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) and organic fish farming. The Danish government supports the blue economy and sustainable food production, creating new opportunities. However, the industry faces challenges such as regulatory complexity and access to skilled labor. Overall, demand for experienced managers is expected to rise, particularly in salmon and trout farming. Competition may be moderate; candidates with technical expertise and management experience will have an advantage.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Denmark typically starts with a bachelor's degree in aquaculture, fisheries science, or related fields. Entry-level roles may include production assistant or farm technician. With experience, one can advance to production manager, regional manager, or technical director. Further specialization in RAS, fish health, or sustainability can lead to higher positions. Many managers also pursue continuous education in business management or environmental regulation. Denmark offers strong support for lifelong learning, and certifications in HACCP or ISO standards enhance mobility within the industry.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable aquaculture
- recirculating systems
- salmon farming
- blue economy

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with a background in biology, marine science, or agricultural management who enjoy hands-on leadership.
- Professionals interested in sustainable food production and innovation in aquaculture technology.
- People willing to work in rural coastal areas or on island communities where many farms are located.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer desk-based office jobs with minimal outdoor or hands-on work.
- Individuals averse to regulatory compliance and detailed record-keeping required for environmental permits.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for an Aquaculture Production Manager in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and farm size. Entry-level managers earn around 420,000 DKK, while experienced managers can earn 550,000-700,000 DKK per year. Bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EU aquaculture manager get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the Positive List or you meet the Pay Limit Scheme salary threshold (approx. 465,000 DKK/year). Alternatively, the Fast-track Scheme applies if you have a job offer from a certified company.

**Q: What education is needed to become an aquaculture manager in Denmark?**
A: A bachelor's degree in aquaculture, fisheries science, or related field is typical. Vocational training combined with experience is also possible. Some employers require a master's degree for senior roles.
