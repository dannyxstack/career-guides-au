# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers are essential workers in Denmark's large agricultural sector, primarily in pig and dairy farming. They perform manual tasks such as feeding, milking, cleaning stables, and basic animal care. Many positions are year-round or seasonal, often filled by EU/EEA workers due to free movement.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 126. Denmark's agricultural sector is undergoing structural changes with larger farms and increased automation, but demand for manual labour remains steady, especially in pig production. Labour shortages are common, leading to reliance on EU workers. Brexit may reduce available labour from some non-EU countries, but EU free movement continues to provide a steady supply.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited without further training. Experienced labourers can become team leaders or supervisors, or pursue vocational training to become agricultural technicians or farm managers. Many workers remain in the same role long-term, especially in family-run farms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic health data entry and case organization
- Translation and localisation of standard health education materials
- Appointment management and follow-up reminder calls
- Basic chronic disease knowledge Q&A (e.g., diabetes diet)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze community health data to identify high-risk groups and trends
- Use generative AI to create multilingual, culturally adapted educational videos and diagrams
- Preliminary health screening and triage via AI voice assistants
- Use AI-enabled remote monitoring tools to track chronic patient conditions
- AI-assisted report writing and policy proposal generation

**Human moat:**

- Cultural safety practices: deep understanding of Indigenous customs, history, and trust building
- Community Relationship Maintenance: Face-to-Face Emotional Support and Crisis Intervention
- Cross-cultural medical translation and advocacy: accurately convey medical information while preserving patient dignity
- Complex Ethical Decisions: Balancing Traditional and Modern Medicine
- On-site visits and home care: hands-on work that cannot be replaced by a screen

**Skills to build:**

- Master community health data analysis tools (e.g., Power BI, Tableau)
- Learn to create AI-driven health education content (e.g., Canva, Synthesia)
- Familiarity with telehealth platforms and electronic health record systems
- Basic natural language processing knowledge: for evaluating AI translation quality
- Trauma-informed care and mental health first aid certification
- Project management skills: coordinating multiple partners

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- pig production
- dairy automation
- animal welfare

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by region and farm type but are generally below the national average. Entry-level positions often start near minimum wage levels, with experienced workers earning slightly higher. Overtime and accommodation may be provided.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a livestock farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult for non-EU citizens to obtain a work permit for this role, as the salary rarely meets the Pay Limit Scheme threshold (approx. 465,000 DKK/year) and it is not on the Positive List. EU/EEA citizens can work freely without a permit.
