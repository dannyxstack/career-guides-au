# Kitchen Helper — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9412 (ISCO08)**

Kitchen helpers in Denmark assist in food preparation, cleaning, and basic kitchen tasks in restaurants, canteens, and institutional kitchens. The role is typically entry-level, requiring no formal qualifications, with on-the-job training common. The Danish hospitality sector has steady demand, particularly in tourist-heavy areas and large institutions, with many positions filled by EU/EEA migrants.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 11,116. Employment of kitchen helpers is projected to remain stable due to high turnover and constant need in the service sector. Automation in large-scale kitchens may reduce some low-skilled tasks. Many positions are part-time, often held by students, immigrants, and labour market entrants. The occupation is not on shortage lists and offers limited wage growth.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving to commis chef or cook roles after completing a vocational education (e.g., Gastronomy Assistant programme). Some advance to kitchen managers in smaller establishments. Others leave the sector for better-paying jobs. Formal training can significantly improve prospects, but many stay in the role long-term.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Weigh ingredients according to recipe
- Manual dishwashing
- Scrub kitchen surfaces
- Cutting standard vegetables
- Transporting ingredient inventory

**Where AI augments the role:**

- use automatic vegetable cutters to improve efficiency
- Uses intelligent inventory system to manage ingredients
- Learn new dishes through digital recipes.
- Operating automated dishwashing line
- Using cleaning robots to maintain floor hygiene

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability in emergency situations
- Sensory assessment of ingredient quality
- Collaboration with chefs and waitstaff.
- Personal responsibility for hygiene checks
- Flexibility for non-standard tasks

**Skills to build:**

- Basic automated equipment operation and maintenance
- Digital inventory management
- Food safety and hygiene monitoring system
- Basic cooking skills
- Multitasking coordination ability
- English communication (tourism and hospitality)

**Key growth areas:**

- Food service
- Institutional catering
- Tourism
- Part-time employment

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a kitchen helper in Denmark?**
A: Hourly wages range from 110 to 130 DKK before tax, varying by experience and location. Annual income for full-time work is approximately 220,000–260,000 DKK.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a kitchen helper in Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult as the job does not meet the skill or salary requirements for a work permit. Only EU/EEA citizens can freely work in this role.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: With training, one can become a cook or kitchen manager. Many complete a vocational education in gastronomy to progress.
