# Community Health Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3253 (ISCO08)**

Community health workers in Denmark, known as sundhedsplejersker, are healthcare professionals primarily employed by municipalities to provide preventive care, health promotion, and home-based support. They work with children, elderly, and vulnerable populations, focusing on reducing hospital admissions and improving community well-being. The role is increasingly vital due to Denmark's aging population and shift toward decentralized care. Workers typically hold a nursing degree with specialized training in public health or community nursing, and must be authorized by the Danish Patient Safety Authority.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a registered nurse, followed by a postgraduate specialization in community health. Experienced workers can advance to coordinator or managerial roles within municipal health departments. Some move into policy, education, or research. Continuing education is encouraged, with opportunities to specialize in areas such as child health, home care, or public health management. The field values practical experience and adaptability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate health promotion materials and FAQ content
- using chatbots for initial health screening and appointment reminders
- Automatically record and organize customer follow-up records and basic data entry
- Generate personalized health action plan drafts based on standard protocols
- Automatically identify community health trends and generate reports via algorithms

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalized health advice: generate customized intervention plans based on resident health data
- Smart customer relationship management system: automatically flag high-risk families and remind follow-up
- Multilingual real-time translation: facilitating seamless communication with residents from different language backgrounds
- VR/AR simulation training: for health literacy education and behavior change skill practice
- Predictive analytics tools: identify potential health crises in communities and plan resources in advance.

**Human moat:**

- Build deep trust and empathetic communication
- Creative problem-solving in complex social situations.
- Cultural sensitivity assessment and localized intervention design
- Multi-agency coordination and advocacy for community resource integration
- Intuitive judgment and ethical decision-making in unstructured information

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis and visualization (e.g., Excel, Tableau)
- Proficient in operating CRM and health information exchange platforms
- Learn to use AI health tools (e.g., ChatGPT, AI assessment assistants)
- Enhance cross-cultural communication and community fundraising skills
- Participate in data privacy and ethics training
- Develop skills in project management and community program evaluation

**Key growth areas:**

- Preventive home care
- Digital health monitoring
- Elderly care
- Community outreach

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a community health worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries follow municipal collective agreements. Entry-level workers earn around 350,000-400,000 DKK annually, with increases based on experience and specialization.

**Q: Can a non-EU community health worker get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, via the Positive List or Pay Limit Scheme, but you must first obtain authorization from the Danish Patient Safety Authority, which requires recognized qualifications and Danish language skills.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist?**
A: You can specialize in areas like geriatrics or public health, or move into coordination, management, or teaching roles.
