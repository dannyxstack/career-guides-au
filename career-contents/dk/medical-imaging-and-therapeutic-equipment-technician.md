# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Medical imaging and therapeutic equipment technicians, commonly known as radiographers in Denmark, operate X-ray, MRI, CT, and radiotherapy machines for diagnosis and treatment. They work in hospitals and private clinics, adhering to strict radiation safety protocols. With an aging population and advanced imaging technologies, demand is high. The role requires a professional bachelor's degree and authorization from the Danish Patient Safety Authority.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,729. Employment outlook is positive due to demographic shifts and increasing reliance on medical imaging for early diagnosis. Denmark faces a shortage of radiographers, especially in rural areas. Technological advancements like AI-assisted imaging create new roles but also require continuous upskilling. The government prioritizes healthcare staffing, supporting recruitment through fast-track schemes.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for radiographers in Denmark?**
A: The annual pre-tax salary for radiographers ranges from 400,000 to 500,000 DKK, depending on experience and sector. Public hospitals follow collective agreements; private clinics may offer higher pay.

**Q: Can non-EU radiographers obtain a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, radiography is on the Positive List of shortage professions, so non-EU applicants can apply for a work permit under this scheme. A job offer from a Danish employer is required, meeting salary thresholds (currently 465,000 DKK/year).

**Q: Do I need to speak Danish to work as a radiographer in Denmark?**
A: Yes, Danish language proficiency is mandatory for patient communication and to obtain professional authorization. Most employers require at least B2 level.
