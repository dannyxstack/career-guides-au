# Health Professional Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

This ISCO group covers specialized health professionals such as audiologists, dietitians, occupational therapists, and speech therapists. In Denmark, these roles are essential to the healthcare system, particularly in hospitals, municipalities, and private clinics, focusing on rehabilitation, prevention, and patient education.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a bachelor's degree, followed by authorization and specialization through master's degrees or further training. Experienced professionals can advance to managerial roles, clinical specialist positions, or research. Private practice ownership is an option for some specialities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate nutrition assessment reports and meal plans
- Preliminary analysis and classification of hearing test results
- Standard pronunciation practice and feedback in speech therapy
- Medical documentation organisation and medical summary generation
- Preliminary screening and sorting of some diagnostic information

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic tools improve accuracy of hearing and speech disorder identification
- Smart nutrition analysis platform optimizes personalized dietary plans
- Voice synthesis and recognition technology enhances language therapy interaction.
- AI-driven dynamic adjustment of rehabilitation plans
- Big data analytics supports epidemiology and efficacy studies

**Human moat:**

- Clinical judgment and comprehensive decision-making for complex cases
- Patient Emotional Support and Human Care
- Multidisciplinary team coordination and communication
- Personalized adjustment of treatment plans and ethical judgment
- Flexibility to handle unexpected situations

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted Diagnostic Tool Operation and Result Interpretation
- Health data analysis and statistics
- Digital health platforms and telemedicine applications
- Interdisciplinary Collaboration and Communication Skills
- Ethics and privacy protection knowledge
- Continuous learning and adapting to new technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Rehabilitation services
- Mental health care
- Digital health tools

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with a strong desire to help others and improve quality of life
- Those who enjoy specialized, patient-focused healthcare roles in multidisciplinary teams
- Professionals interested in preventive care, rehabilitation, and health education

**Who is not suited**
- People uncomfortable with direct and often emotional patient interaction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for this profession in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by speciality and sector. Public sector salaries are determined by collective agreements, starting around 38,000-42,000 DKK per month for entry-level, rising with experience and seniority. Private practice earnings can vary.

**Q: Do I need a job offer before applying for a work visa as a health professional?**
A: Yes, for both Positive List and Pay Limit schemes, you must have a confirmed job offer from a Danish employer. EU/EEA citizens do not require a visa but must register within the first three months.

**Q: Can I bring my family under a work permit in Denmark?**
A: Yes, accompanying family members can apply for residence permits. They may have the right to work and study, depending on the permit type.
