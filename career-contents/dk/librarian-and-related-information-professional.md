# Librarians and Related Information Professionals — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2622 (ISCO08)**

In Denmark, librarians and related information professionals manage and organize collections of books, digital resources, and data across public libraries, academic institutions, research organizations, and private companies. They assist users in finding relevant information, curate digital archives, and support knowledge management. The role has evolved significantly with digitalization, requiring skills in data curation, IT systems, and user education. Denmark has a strong public library tradition, and demand exists in both urban and rural areas. While public libraries remain a core employer, opportunities are growing in corporate information services, digital humanities, and open data initiatives.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,036. Employment outlook for librarians in Denmark is stable but undergoing transformation. Digitalization reduces demand for traditional cataloguing but increases need for information specialists who can manage digital repositories, support research data management, and teach digital literacy. Public sector budgets are tight, but retirements create openings. Job growth is expected in specialized roles such as data librarians, knowledge managers, and IT-related positions within libraries. Rural areas may face recruitment challenges. Overall, the occupation is not a high-growth field, but skilled professionals with IT and data competencies have favorable prospects.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts after completing a professional bachelor's degree in library and information science. New graduates often begin as library assistants or junior librarians in public or academic libraries. With experience and additional training, they can advance to senior librarian, subject specialist, or head of department. Some move into knowledge management or information architecture in the private sector. Further education, such as a master's degree in information science or a PhD, opens opportunities in research libraries, digital humanities, and higher education teaching. Leadership roles require managerial skills and often a postgraduate diploma.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cataloging and metadata generation (AI can automatically extract keywords and classification numbers from full text)
- Basic reference inquiries (chatbots answer common questions like opening hours, borrowing rules)
- document delivery and reservation processing (AI scheduling system automatically allocates resources)
- Collection inventory and shelf checking (RFID + AI vision automatic identification of misplaced books)
- Overdue reminder notices and fine calculations (automated email/SMS system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- In-depth targeted search (AI analyzes user needs, selects high-relevance literature from multiple databases).
- Digital Literacy Education (AI-powered interactive learning scenarios teaching database search strategies)
- Digitization of special collections and knowledge graph construction (AI-assisted recognition of manuscript text and linking historical events)
- Reader behavior analysis (AI mining borrowing data to optimize collection purchasing and space layout)
- Multilingual resource recommendations (AI translation matching non-English users' needs)

**Human moat:**

- Critically evaluating information quality (judging authority and bias of AI-provided results)
- Complex research consultation (interdisciplinary issues requiring understanding of user true intent and context)
- Community service and reading promotion (organizing activities, building emotional connections with readers)
- Digital rights and privacy management (deciding which data can be public and how to use it compliantly)
- Cross-institutional collaboration and resource sharing (negotiating license agreements, promoting open access)

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool application (e.g., querying databases with natural language, training small classification models)
- Data ethics and privacy protection (understanding algorithmic bias, personal information protection regulations)
- User experience design (designing AI-assisted search interfaces and self-service processes)
- Digital curation (managing digital collection lifecycle, including metadata standards)
- Project Management and Change Management (coordinating teams and processes when introducing AI systems)
- Basic programming (Python/API calls, automating repetitive tasks)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital Curation
- Data Management
- Information Architecture
- Digital Humanities

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy organizing and curating information, helping others find resources, and working with both digital and physical materials.
- Individuals with strong analytical, research, and IT skills who want to work in a collaborative environment.
- Those interested in lifelong learning, cultural heritage, and serving the community.

**Who is not suited**
- Individuals seeking high financial rewards or rapid career advancement, as librarian salaries are moderate and promotions take time.
- People who dislike routine tasks, such as cataloguing or administrative work, or who prefer minimal interaction with the public.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a librarian in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Public librarians typically earn between 300,000 to 400,000 DKK annually, while academic and corporate librarians may earn 400,000 to 550,000 DKK. Senior positions and specialized roles can exceed 600,000 DKK. The Pay Limit Scheme for migration requires a minimum annual salary of around 465,000 DKK.

**Q: Can a non-EU librarian get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, through the Pay Limit Scheme if the offered salary meets the threshold (465,000 DKK in 2023). Alternatively, if the librarian is a researcher (e.g., at a university), the Fast-Track Scheme may apply. Librarians are not currently on the Positive List, so the Pay Limit or Researcher schemes are the main routes.

**Q: What education is required to work as a librarian in Denmark?**
A: A professional bachelor's degree (3.5 years) or a master's degree (2 years) in Library and Information Science from a recognized institution. The title 'bibliotekar' is protected. Foreign qualifications must be assessed by the Danish Agency for Higher Education and Science. Proficiency in Danish is typically required.
