# Optometrists and Ophthalmic Opticians — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2267 (ISCO08)**

Optometrists and ophthalmic opticians in Denmark are regulated healthcare professionals who examine eyes, prescribe corrective lenses, and diagnose visual disorders. They typically hold a professional bachelor's degree in optometry from a university college and are authorized by the Danish Patient Safety Authority. The profession is in high demand due to an aging population and increased digital eye strain, leading to a recognized shortage on the Positive List, making it attractive for skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for optometrists includes specialization in areas like pediatric optometry, contact lenses, or low vision. With experience, one can become a clinic manager, open a private practice, or move into research and teaching at university colleges. Some pursue further education to become ophthalmologists. Continuing education is encouraged through courses and certifications, offering opportunities for professional growth.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement
- AI analyzes corneal topography data to generate preliminary lens fitting plans.
- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Learning to operate digital optometric equipment and AI-assisted diagnostic systems
- Master the use of remote eye testing platforms
- Improve communication and customer management skills
- Learn basic data analysis to interpret AI reports
- Track new eye health technologies (e.g., OCT interpretation).
- Strengthen personalized eyewear fitting and problem-solving skills.

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- digital eye strain
- preventive care
- tele-optometry

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an optometrist in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by experience and sector. Starting salaries are above the national average, and experienced optometrists earn significantly more. The Pay Limit Scheme threshold serves as a reference point for high earners.

**Q: Can I work in Denmark as an optometrist under the Positive List?**
A: Yes, optometrist is on the Positive List for higher education shortage, making it eligible for a work permit under this scheme. You need a job offer in the field and a relevant degree.

**Q: What language skills are required?**
A: Danish language proficiency is typically required for patient communication, usually at least B2 level. Some employers may accept other Scandinavian languages.
