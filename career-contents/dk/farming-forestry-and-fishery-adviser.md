# Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2132 (ISCO08)**

Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers provide expert guidance to primary producers on crop and livestock management, sustainable practices, and compliance with EU and Danish agricultural policies. In Denmark, these advisers play a crucial role in the green transition, helping farmers adopt climate-friendly methods and precision farming technologies. They work for agricultural advisory services, government agencies, or as independent consultants.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from agricultural technician or agronomist roles, moving to specialist adviser in areas like crop production, livestock, or forestry. Experienced advisers may become team leaders, policy advisors, or start their own consultancy. Continuous education in precision agriculture and climate adaptation is essential.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate standardized soil test reports and crop growth cycle analyses
- Automated fertiliser recommendation based on historical data and satellite imagery
- Draft basic sustainable forest management plans
- Automatically aggregate fishing catch data and generate compliance reports
- Answer common pest and disease control questions (knowledge base chatbot)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize precision agriculture decision-making recommendations using AI remote sensing analysis and big data
- Simulate long-term environmental impacts of different planting/breeding strategies using AI models
- Real-time monitoring of weather and market data, dynamically adjusting advisor recommendations
- Automates data collection and initial analysis, allowing consultants to focus on farmer communication and customized solutions
- AI-assisted generation of visual reports to improve communication efficiency with farmers

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and long-term relationships with farmers
- On-site problem diagnosis based on local experience and intuition
- Handling complex, unique sustainability conflicts involving multiple stakeholders
- Adaptability to unstructured, low-information-density environments (e.g., remote farms)
- Cross-disciplinary integration (agriculture, ecology, economics) providing customized solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI tools (e.g., remote sensing analysis platforms, agricultural decision support systems)
- Data science fundamentals (Python/R, statistical modeling, data visualization).
- Precision agriculture technology (drones, sensors, IoT data analysis)
- Environmental and social impact assessment methodology
- Communication and change management skills (to promote adoption of new technologies among farmers)
- Continuous learning and industry knowledge updates (policy, market, biotechnology).

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainability
- digital agriculture
- organic farming
- carbon farming

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals passionate about sustainable agriculture and environmental stewardship.
- Advisers enjoy problem-solving and direct interaction with farmers and foresters.
- Those who thrive in both office-based analysis and outdoor fieldwork.

**Who is not suited**
- Those uncomfortable with variable weather and rural, often isolated work environments.
- People who prefer strict 9-to-5 office routine without travel.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Farming Adviser in Denmark?**
A: Salaries for experienced advisers range from 450,000 to 550,000 DKK annually, with higher pay for specialists in high-demand fields like organic farming or carbon accounting.

**Q: Is a work visa required for agricultural advisers from outside the EU?**
A: Yes, non-EU/EEA nationals need a work permit, typically via the Pay Limit Scheme if earning above the threshold, or through the fast-track scheme if employed by an approved company.

**Q: Can I start my own consultancy as an agricultural adviser?**
A: Yes, many experienced advisers become independent consultants. You'll need to build a client base and often acquire certification from SEGES to enhance credibility.
