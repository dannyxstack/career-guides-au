# Shoemakers And Related Workers — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7536 (ISCO08)**

Shoemakers and related workers in Denmark design, create, repair, and modify footwear, including boots, orthopedic shoes, and custom-made items. The occupation combines traditional handcraft with modern techniques and materials. Most shoemakers work in small workshops, repair shops, or specialty stores, with a minority employed in larger manufacturing plants. The profession has a long history in Denmark, but numbers have declined due to competition from imported mass-produced footwear. However, there is a niche demand for high-quality, custom footwear and orthopedic repairs, particularly in larger cities like Copenhagen, Aarhus, and Odense. The work requires precision, creativity, and knowledge of materials such as leather, rubber, and synthetics.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for shoemakers in Denmark typically starts with a vocational training program (EUD) lasting 3–4 years, combining school-based learning and apprenticeship in a workshop. After obtaining the 'Skomageruddannelsen' (shoemaker certificate), workers can become skilled employees or advance to master level ('Mesterskomager'), which requires additional exams and experience. Further specialization in areas like orthopedic footwear, fashion, or industrial production is common. Some shoemakers open their own workshops or become self-employed. The profession offers limited hierarchical advancement but allows for expertise development and entrepreneurship.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- repetitive manual operations such as shoe upper sewing and sole bonding
- Routine machine operations such as leather cutting and component sorting
- Visual inspection in quality checks (replaced by computer vision).
- Inventory Management and Order Processing (ERP System Automation)
- Last replication and scaling (3D scanning and digitization alternatives).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design software to generate creative shoe sketches and color schemes
- Using 3D modeling and virtual try-on to reduce physical sample shoe production
- Optimizing leather cutting layouts through data analysis to reduce waste
- Customized customer communication and foot measurement via digital platforms.
- using augmented reality (AR) to assist complex repairs and renovations

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment of leather texture and defects in hand-finishing and restoration
- Deep communication and aesthetic co-creation with clients in high-end custom products
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional shoemaking techniques (e.g., Goodyear welt structure)
- Comprehensive craftsmanship flexibly addressing unconventional issues in small workshops

**Skills to build:**

- Proficient in CAD/CAM footwear design software (e.g., Shoemaster)
- Learn 3D modeling and rapid prototyping (RP technology)
- Familiar with automated cutting and sewing robot operation
- Knowledge of leather material science and sustainable processes
- Develop skills in client needs analysis and customised design
- Use e-commerce and social media for brand marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom footwear
- Orthopedic shoemaking
- Repair services
- Sustainable materials

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to become a shoemaker in Denmark?**
A: The vocational training program (EUD) for shoemaking typically lasts 3.5 years, combining school-based learning and apprenticeship. After obtaining the certificate, one can pursue a master program lasting 1-2 additional years.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as a shoemaker in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The occupation is not on the Positive List for skilled work. Non-EU migrants can apply under the Pay Limit Scheme if the salary exceeds DKK 465,000 annually, which is rare for shoemakers. Alternatively, the Fast-track Scheme may be an option for specialized roles in certified companies.

**Q: What is the job market like for shoemakers in Denmark?**
A: The job market is small and specialized. Most opportunities are in repair and custom shoemaking. Competition is low, but positions are limited. Self-employment and niche services are common paths.
