# Plasterer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

A plasterer in Denmark applies plaster, stucco, and other materials to interior and exterior walls, ceilings, and facades, often working on new construction, renovation, and restoration projects. The occupation requires a blend of manual dexterity, physical stamina, and knowledge of materials and techniques. With a strong construction sector and focus on sustainable building, plasterers are in steady demand across both residential and commercial projects. EU/EEA citizens enjoy free movement, while non-EU workers may qualify through shortage lists.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Plasterers typically start as apprentices in a 3.5-year vocational training program, earning a journeyman's certificate (svendebrev). Many join unions and negotiate wages via collective agreements. Experienced plasterers can become master craftsmen (mestersvend), start their own business, or move into site supervision. Specialization in decorative plaster, facade restoration, or sustainable materials offers niche career growth.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Sustainable buildings
- Eco-friendly materials
- New construction

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a plasterer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, location, and collective agreements. According to Statistics Denmark, the average monthly salary for plasterers is competitive with other skilled trades, but exact figures depend on overtime and sector. Consult the latest wage statistics from the Danish Construction Association.

**Q: Can non-EU plasterers get a work permit in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the Positive List for skilled work (vocational), plasterers can apply for a work permit with a job offer. EU/EEA citizens have free movement. The Pay Limit Scheme is another option but requires a high salary.

**Q: What training is required to become a plasterer in Denmark?**
A: Typically, a 3.5-year vocational education and training (VET) programme combining school and apprenticeship, leading to a Svendebrev (journeyman's certificate). Equivalent foreign qualifications may be recognized. Adult training courses are available for upskilling.
