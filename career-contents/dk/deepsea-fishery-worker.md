# Deep-sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6223 (ISCO08)**

Deep-sea fishery workers operate commercial fishing vessels in Denmark's oceans, primarily in the North Sea and Baltic Sea. This occupation involves catching fish and shellfish using various methods such as trawling, longlining, and netting. The work is physically demanding and requires endurance, as trips can last several days. The Danish fishing industry is a traditional sector, but it faces challenges from quotas, environmental regulations, and competition. Employment opportunities are limited and often based on experience and networking.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 31. The outlook for deep-sea fishery workers in Denmark is challenging due to declining fish stocks, stricter quotas, and an aging workforce. Automation and consolidation of fishing vessels may reduce demand for labour. However, sustainable fishing practices and niche markets for high-quality seafood offer some opportunities. EU fishing policies heavily influence the industry, and young entrants are needed but face barriers such as high start-up costs for licenses and vessels.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts as a deckhand and can lead to positions such as boatswain, first mate, or captain. Many fishermen remain in the same role throughout their career due to the flat hierarchy on smaller vessels. Larger commercial operations may offer advancement through experience and additional certifications. Some diversify into aquaculture, fish processing, or maritime safety roles. There is limited formal career structure; success largely depends on practical skills and reliability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing vessel navigation and route planning (AIS + autopilot)
- Fish catch classification and initial weighing (computer vision)
- Fish school detection and net deployment position optimization (AI sonar analysis)
- Predictive maintenance for fishing vessel equipment (IoT + machine learning)
- Catch tracking and compliance report generation (automated data entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fish school prediction (combining meteorological and oceanographic data)
- Real-time deck safety monitoring (AI alert wearable)
- Automation of catch processing (robot-assisted)
- Resource management and sustainable fishing strategies (AI models)
- Market sales data analysis (providing optimal auction timing)

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making in complex maritime environments (storms, currents)
- Manual net repair and deck emergency handling
- Judging fish behavior based on experience (limited AI data)
- Communication and coordination with fleet and shore teams
- Flexible operations to adapt to seasonal and regulatory changes

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Computer Vision Applications in Fisheries
- Data analysis and prediction tools (Python, R)
- Drone operation and maintenance
- Marine sensor and IoT device management
- Sustainable fisheries regulations and certifications
- Deck automation system operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fishing
- Aquaculture
- Marine conservation
- Seafood processing

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deep-sea fishery worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary greatly depending on the type of fishing, vessel size, and catch. On average, a deckhand can earn between 25,000 and 40,000 DKK per month before tax, with share of catch bonuses potentially increasing earnings.

**Q: Can non-EU/EEA citizens work as deep-sea fishery workers in Denmark?**
A: Non-EU/EEA citizens generally need a work permit. The Pay Limit Scheme requires a high salary threshold (DKK 487,000 annually), which is uncommon in fishing. Alternatively, if the occupation is on the Positive List (currently not), or via the Fast-track scheme for certified companies, but these are rare for this role. Practical training or family reunification may be other paths.

**Q: What education is needed to become a deep-sea fishery worker?**
A: While formal education is not mandatory, a vocational training as a fisherman (3-4 years) is recommended. It includes practical experience and mandatory safety certificates. Many fishermen learn on the job and obtain necessary licences through short courses.
