# Translator And Interpreter — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2643 (ISCO08)**

Translators, interpreters, and other linguists in Denmark work across a range of settings including government agencies, private companies, conference centers, and hospitals. The profession is relatively small but essential for the country’s international ties, with particular demand for Danish-English and less common language pairs. Digitalization and machine translation have impacted some routine translation tasks, but specialized and sensitive interpreting remains in demand. Most professionals are self-employed or work through agencies, and Danish language proficiency is a prerequisite for most roles.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 583. Employment for linguists in Denmark is expected to remain stable, with a slight increase in demand for interpreters in public services due to immigration and multilingual integration. The rise of remote interpreting and localization of digital content creates new opportunities. However, competition is strong, and jobs requiring high security clearance (e.g., defence) are limited. Growth areas include medical and legal interpreting, as well as game and software localization.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a degree in translation or interpreting, followed by several years of freelance or agency work. Many linguists specialize in a field such as law, medicine, or technology to increase their rates. Experienced professionals may move into project management, quality assurance, or teaching. Authorization as a state-authorized translator (statsautoriseret translator) can boost credibility and open doors to government contracts. Some interpreters pursue certification from the Court Administration.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard conference interpreting (e.g., business negotiations)
- Simple telephone interpreting or community interpreting
- Basic subtitling/transcription proofreading
- Standardized oral exam scoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time captioning assisting interpretation (AI providing background information)
- Term base management (AI automatically extracts terms)
- AI speech-to-text note-taking assistance during simultaneous interpretation
- Remote interpreting platforms (AI optimizes audio/video).
- Quality self-check (AI playback comparison against standard translation)

**Human moat:**

- Cultural context understanding and emotional conveyance
- Rapid decision-making under high pressure
- Confidentiality and ethical judgment
- Interpretation of Non-Verbal Signals (Tone, Body Language)
- Transcreation and rhetorical refinement

**Skills to build:**

- Specialized domain knowledge (legal, medical, financial)
- AI-assisted translation tools (e.g., Otter.ai, SDL Trados)
- Remote interpreting platform operations
- Terminology management and data annotation
- On-site equipment commissioning and troubleshooting
- Advanced cross-cultural communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- localization
- conference interpreting
- legal translation
- medical interpreting

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a translator or interpreter in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by language pair, specialization, and experience. Freelancers set their own rates; employed interpreters and translators earn around 350,000–550,000 DKK annually.

**Q: Can I get a work visa as a translator or interpreter in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer and meet the requirements for the Positive List (e.g., a relevant degree) or the Pay Limit Scheme. EU/EEA citizens can work freely.

**Q: Is it necessary to be authorized or certified to work as a translator in Denmark?**
A: Not for most jobs, but state authorization (statsautoriseret translator) is highly valued. Court interpreting requires separate authorization from the Court Administration.
