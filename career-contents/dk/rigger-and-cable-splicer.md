# Rigger and Cable Splicer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7215 (ISCO08)**

Riggers and cable splicers in Denmark assemble, install, and maintain rigging equipment for construction, telecom, and energy projects, including offshore wind farms. They work with steel cables, pulleys, winches, and splicing tools, ensuring safety and precision. The role is critical in infrastructure development, often requiring certification for working at height and handling heavy loads.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Denmark typically starts with a vocational education (e.g., as a construction technician or electrician) followed by on-the-job training. Experienced workers can move into supervisory roles (e.g., site manager) or specialise in areas like offshore rigging or high-voltage cable splicing. Continuing education in safety and new technologies enhances advancement prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Image recognition-based automatic detection of cable wear
- Use drones for overhead cable inspection
- Automatically generate rigging load calculations and configuration plans
- Installation path planning using digital twin simulation
- Automated report generation and compliance document filling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AR headset displays complex rigging connection steps and safety annotations
- AI real-time analysis of tension data and early warning of potential breakage risks
- Smart wearable devices monitor worker posture and fatigue to prevent occupational injuries
- machine learning-based defect identification and repair guidance for cable joints
- Digital twin platforms enable remote expert collaboration for complex lifting operations

**Human moat:**

- Improvise and erect scaffolding in adverse weather or confined spaces
- Manual slinging and adjustment of non-standard components

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a rigger and cable splicer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and collective agreements. Entry-level positions start around 300,000 DKK annually, while experienced workers can earn up to 450,000 DKK or more, especially in offshore wind. Wages must meet the applicable collective bargaining agreement or the Pay Limit Scheme if a work permit is required.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a rigger in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the Positive List for skilled work or if the employer is a certified company under the Fast-track scheme. Alternatively, the Pay Limit Scheme may apply if the salary exceeds the annual threshold (currently 465,000 DKK). EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: Do I need Danish language skills to work as a rigger?**
A: While some employers may require basic Danish for safety communication, many international projects (e.g., offshore wind) use English as the working language. However, learning Danish can improve job prospects and integration.
