# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

Hunters and trappers in Denmark manage wildlife populations through regulated hunting and trapping, contributing to biodiversity, game management, and rural livelihoods. The occupation is deeply rooted in Danish tradition but subject to strict licensing and conservation laws.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from obtaining a hunting license to assisting experienced hunters or gamekeepers. With experience, one can become a wildlife manager, hunting guide, or self-employed contractor. Advanced training in ecology or nature management opens roles in government agencies or conservation organizations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Wildlife population monitoring and counting (via AI image recognition and drone automation)
- Capture data recording and report generation (AI automatic sorting and visualization).
- Predicting animal activity patterns (machine learning models replacing empirical judgment)
- License application and compliance document processing (automated document system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze satellite imagery and infrared camera data to optimize monitoring efficiency
- Improves success rate of precise capture and release through real-time tracking with drones and AI.
- Develop science-based wildlife management strategies using data analysis tools
- Using VR to train on trap setting and safe operation in complex environments

**Human moat:**

- Physical and manual skills for setting and maintaining field traps
- Quick decision-making for unexpected situations (e.g., animal injuries, bad weather)
- Interpersonal communication and negotiation with local communities and landowners.
- Responsibility for ecological ethics judgement and compliance operations

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Ecological modeling and population statistics
- Basic data analysis using Python or R
- Wildlife conservation regulations and ethics
- Project management and basic business skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Wildlife management
- Sustainable hunting
- Eco-tourism
- Biodiversity conservation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a hunter in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary widely; most hunters work part-time or as a hobby. Full-time employment in game management can earn around 300,000–400,000 DKK annually, but many derive income from related activities like guiding or land management.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens become hunters in Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult. Work permits for hunting alone are rarely granted. Non-EU citizens would typically need a high-skill nature management job, which may qualify under the Pay Limit or Positive List schemes, but hunting itself is not a listed skilled occupation.

**Q: Do I need formal education to become a hunter?**
A: No formal degree is required, but you must pass the Danish hunting test and obtain a license. Many aspiring hunters take a course offered by the Danish Hunters' Association. Additional education in nature management can enhance career prospects.
