# Heavy Truck And Lorry Drivers — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 8332 (ISCO08)**

Heavy truck and lorry drivers in Denmark operate large vehicles (over 3.5 tonnes) to transport goods locally, nationally, and internationally. They form a critical backbone of the Danish logistics sector, serving industries like retail, manufacturing, and construction. Drivers are employed by transport companies, wholesalers, and industrial firms. The role requires a valid driving license category C+E (CE), professional competence certificate (CPC), and medical fitness. With the growth of e-commerce and just-in-time delivery, demand remains stable. The work often involves long hours, overnight stays, and physical loading/unloading. Danish is generally required for communication with dispatchers and customers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 16,519. The outlook for heavy truck drivers in Denmark is positive due to persistent shortages. An aging workforce and low youth interest create recruitment challenges. The shift toward greener transport, including electric trucks, will require new skills. E-commerce growth fuels demand for regional and last-mile distribution. Cross-border transport remains strong, especially with Germany and Sweden. Automation (platooning, autonomous trucks) is emerging but not expected to reduce jobs significantly in the short term. The Danish government and industry are promoting vocational training to fill gaps.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for Danish truck drivers typically starts with local or regional haulage. With experience and additional certifications (e.g., ADR for dangerous goods, tanker, refrigerated), drivers can qualify for specialized or international routes. Some move into transport planning, fleet management, or become owner-operators. Further education (e.g., transport logistics diploma) opens supervisory roles. There are also opportunities in training or safety inspection. The occupation offers stable employment but limited vertical mobility without additional training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

- Autonomous driving system operation and monitoring
- Use of logistics scheduling software and multimodal transport platforms
- Basic Maintenance of New Energy Trucks (Electric/Hydrogen)
- Safety compliance and digital document management
- Customer communication and on-site problem solving
- Basic data analysis (fuel efficiency, route optimization)

**Key growth areas:**

- E-commerce
- Green transition
- Cross-border logistics
- Specialized transport

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a heavy truck driver in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are regulated by collective agreements and vary by experience, region, and type of transport. Entry-level drivers earn around the minimum for the industry, while experienced drivers on international routes can earn significantly more. Benefits often include paid overtime, travel allowances, and pension contributions.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes, if the occupation is on the Danish Positive List for Skilled Work (which includes heavy truck drivers) and you have a relevant vocational education. Alternatively, the Fast-track Scheme for certified companies or the Pay Limit Scheme (high salary threshold) may apply. EU/EEA nationals have free movement.

**Q: Do I need formal education to become a truck driver in Denmark?**
A: A valid CE license and CPC are mandatory. Formal vocational education (EUD) is common but not strictly required if you obtain the license and CPC through adult training (AMU) or private courses. However, many employers prefer EUD graduates.
