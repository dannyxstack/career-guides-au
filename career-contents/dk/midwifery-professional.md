# Midwifery Professional — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2222 (ISCO08)**

Midwifery professionals in Denmark provide comprehensive care to women during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period, as well as newborn care. They work in hospitals, birth clinics, and increasingly in home-birth settings. The profession is highly regulated, requiring authorization from the Danish Patient Safety Authority. Midwives are in high demand due to a growing focus on maternal health and natural birth options. The role is autonomous and involves clinical decision-making, emergency management, and holistic support for families.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,992. Denmark's ageing midwifery workforce and rising birth rates in certain regions create a steady demand for new midwives. The government has prioritized maternal health, leading to expansion of birth services. Employment prospects are strong, particularly in larger hospitals and in community-based midwifery. However, competition for positions in major cities like Copenhagen can be moderate. Overall, the outlook is positive with growth expected to continue.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression often includes specialization in areas such as antenatal care, neonatal intensive care, or breastfeeding counselling. Experienced midwives can advance to leadership roles like head midwife or clinical coordinator. Some pursue research or teaching at university colleges. The profession offers good work-life balance, with options for part-time and flexible schedules. Further education in public health or management opens additional opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic entry and organization of routine prenatal electronic records
- Guideline-based routine pregnancy risk assessment report generation
- Automatic Analysis of Birth Monitoring Data and Anomaly Alerts
- AI voice automated outbound calls and basic screening for postpartum follow-up
- Automatic Filling and Coding of Standardized Descriptions in Medical Records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assisted fetal monitoring interpretation to improve anomaly recognition accuracy
- AI-assisted measurement and preliminary diagnosis of prenatal ultrasound images
- AI recommendation for personalized birth plans based on historical data
- AI-driven continuous obstetric education simulation training and skill assessment
- Real-time AI translation and information integration in telemedicine consultations

**Human moat:**

- Manual assistance maneuvers and emergency judgment in complex deliveries
- Emotional communication, psychological comfort, and education with mothers and families
- Clinical Leadership and Decision-Making in Cross-Disciplinary Team Collaboration
- Rapid clinical reasoning in unpredictable obstetric emergencies
- Sensitive understanding of maternal cultural background and personal preferences

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI-assisted diagnostic tools in obstetrics (e.g., smart monitors)
- Clinical data analysis and information system management
- Telehealth consultation and digital health education skills
- Interventional prenatal examination techniques (e.g., ultrasound-guided)
- Cross-cultural communication and psychological support skills
- Simulation training for maternal and infant emergencies and team collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- maternal health
- home births
- hospital births
- women's health

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a midwife in Denmark?**
A: The pre-tax annual salary for a midwife in Denmark typically ranges from 450,000 to 550,000 DKK, depending on experience, location, and employer.

**Q: Can non-EU midwives obtain a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, non-EU midwives can apply under the Positive List for Skilled Work (Higher Education) or the Fast-track scheme if they have a job offer from an approved company.

**Q: Do I need Danish authorization to practice as a midwife?**
A: Yes, all midwives must be authorized by the Danish Patient Safety Authority. EU/EEA qualifications are recognized; non-EU qualifications require an assessment.
