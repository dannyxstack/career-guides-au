# Welder and Flame Cutter — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7212 (ISCO08)**

Welders and flame cutters in Denmark join and cut metal parts using various welding techniques, including gas, arc, and laser. They work in shipbuilding, construction, offshore energy, and manufacturing. The profession is in high demand due to a national shortage of skilled tradespeople, especially in the renewable energy and infrastructure sectors. Most welders are trained through vocational education and apprenticeships, often combining classroom learning with on-the-job training. The Danish labour market values certifications in specific welding methods, such as TIG or MIG, and adherence to strict safety standards. Foreign welders may find opportunities through the Positive List for skilled work, as the occupation qualifies for streamlined immigration procedures.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 4,980. Denmark faces a persistent shortage of qualified welders, driven by an ageing workforce and increasing demand from offshore wind, bridge construction, and ship repair. The government has included welders on the Positive List for skilled work, indicating strong labour market needs. Employment is expected to grow moderately over the next decade, with particular opportunities in green energy projects and infrastructure upgrades. Automation may reduce demand for basic welding tasks, but skilled welders capable of complex, certified work will remain essential. The industry also offers possibilities for self-employment or subcontracting.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts with a vocational education (e.g., EUD as a welder) followed by specialised certifications in advanced techniques like orbital welding or underwater welding. Experienced welders can advance to supervisor, inspector, or instructor roles, or move into quality control and non-destructive testing. Some pursue further training to become welding engineers or project managers. The ability to work in multiple sectors (e.g., offshore, construction, industrial) enhances mobility. Continuous learning and certifications from the Danish Safety Technology Authority (Sikkerhedsstyrelsen) or international bodies (e.g., AWS, IIW) are key to higher earnings and job security.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch standardised metal parts automatic welding (e.g., car body welding, pipe joints)
- Robot programming and path planning based on CAD models replace manual teaching
- Online weld quality inspection (visual inspection + AI defect detection)
- Automatic adjustment of welding parameters and welding process monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted welding parameter optimization (real-time recommendation of current/voltage based on material thickness and ambient temperature)
- Augmented reality (AR) welding guidance system improves novice training efficiency
- Drones/crawler robots equipped with welding modules for high-altitude or hazardous welding
- Digital twin welding simulation reduces material waste from test pieces
- AI-driven multi-robot collaborative welding group control

**Human moat:**

- On-site manual welding of non-standard workpieces and confined spaces (e.g., on ships, inside pipes)
- Welding of pressure vessels and load-bearing structures with high stress and fatigue life requirements
- Welding process development for dissimilar metals and special alloys (titanium, aluminum-magnesium alloys)
- Welding quality responsibility determination and repair decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Programming and debugging of welding robots (e.g., FANUC, KUKA, ABB)
- Welding process simulation software (e.g., Simufact Welding, SYSWELD)
- Automated welding quality control and testing methods
- Digital manufacturing basics (CAD/CAM, BOM management)
- Advanced certifications such as International Welding Engineer (IWE)
- Operation of AR/VR welding simulation training systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Pipeline construction
- Structural steel
- Shipbuilding

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a welder in Denmark?**
A: The average pre-tax annual salary for a certified welder is around 450,000 DKK, varying by experience, sector, and location. Offshore and speciality welders may earn significantly more.

**Q: Can a non-EU welder get a work permit for Denmark?**
A: Yes, welders are on the Positive List for skilled work, allowing streamlined applications. Alternatively, the Pay Limit Scheme applies if the job offers a salary above the threshold (currently 465,000 DKK/year).

**Q: Is vocational training in Denmark free for international students?**
A: EU/EEA citizens can access free vocational education. Non-EU students may pay tuition (approx. 60,000 DKK/year), but many employers sponsor training through apprenticeship schemes.
