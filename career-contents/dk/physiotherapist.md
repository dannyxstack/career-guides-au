# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Denmark work in both the public and private healthcare sectors, primarily in hospitals, municipal health centres, and private clinics. They help patients recover from injury, manage chronic conditions, and improve mobility and quality of life. The profession is regulated by the Danish Patient Safety Authority, and authorization is required to practice. Danish is the primary working language, though some private clinics may serve English-speaking clients. The occupation benefits from strong demand due to an aging population and increased focus on rehabilitation and preventive care.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 8,080. Employment prospects for physiotherapists in Denmark remain very positive, driven by demographic trends and healthcare policy emphasis on early intervention and outpatient care. The number of elderly citizens is rising, increasing the need for musculoskeletal and geriatric rehabilitation. Additionally, sports and workplace injury prevention programmes are expanding. Public sector positions are stable, and private practice opportunities are growing, especially in urban areas. However, competition for some specialised roles may increase as the number of graduates rises.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins in a public hospital or municipal health centre, often with a focus on general rehabilitation. After a few years, physiotherapists may specialise in areas such as sports physiotherapy, manual therapy, paediatrics, or neurology, often through postgraduate courses or a master's degree. Experienced physiotherapists can move into management roles, become clinical supervisors, or open their own private practice. Continuing education is highly valued, and many pursue certifications in evidence-based treatment methods.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient record organization and electronic health record entry
- Scoring and data analysis of standardized assessment scales.
- Generate routine reports on treatment progress
- Administrative tasks such as appointment scheduling and patient reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assists in analyzing patient movement data to optimize personalized treatment plans
- Utilize virtual reality technology for remote rehabilitation guidance and monitoring
- AI predicts patient recovery trajectories, adjusting intervention plans in advance
- Recommending optimal treatment exercise combinations based on big data
- Automatically generate patient education materials to improve compliance

**Human moat:**

- Manual therapy based on palpation and movement analysis
- Communication to build trust and emotional support with patients
- Clinical reasoning and real-time adjustments for complex cases
- Professional judgment in interdisciplinary team collaboration
- Flexibility in handling emergencies and patient individual differences

**Skills to build:**

- Master AI rehabilitation analysis software and remote monitoring platforms
- Study sports science and biomechanics data analysis
- Enhance the application of digital health tools.
- Enhanced Patient Communication and Motivation Skills
- Understand evidence-based practice and clinical decision support systems
- Continuously follow the latest research on rehabilitation AI

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- sports rehabilitation
- preventive care
- digital health

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong manual dexterity, empathy, and interpersonal skills.
- Those interested in rehabilitation, preventive healthcare, and patient education.
- People who enjoy working directly with patients over extended periods.

**Who is not suited**
- Individuals who dislike repetitive physical tasks or working in close physical proximity with others.
- Those who prefer not to work in a healthcare setting or with sick/injured individuals.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a physiotherapist in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements in the public sector and market rates in private practice. Physiotherapists generally earn a stable income with opportunities for supplements from specialisation and overtime.

**Q: Can non-EU physiotherapists work in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer and meet requirements for the Positive List for skilled work or the Pay Limit Scheme. The occupation is on the shortage list, which eases the process. Danish language proficiency is typically required for authorization.

**Q: Is it necessary to have a master's degree to progress in physiotherapy in Denmark?**
A: Not mandatory, but a master's degree or specialisation courses can open doors to higher-level clinical roles, management, or private practice. Many physiotherapists pursue postgraduate education to stay competitive.
