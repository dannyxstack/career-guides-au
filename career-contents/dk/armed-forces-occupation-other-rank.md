# Armed Forces Other Ranks — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 0310 (ISCO08)**

Armed Forces Other Ranks in Denmark encompass enlisted personnel and non-commissioned officers who form the backbone of the Danish Defence. They serve in the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Home Guard, performing a wide range of operational, technical, and support roles. Employment is through the state, with a strong emphasis on national security, international peacekeeping, and domestic crisis response. The labour market for this occupation is distinct from civilian sectors, being wholly within the public sector and subject to military regulations. Recruitment is primarily through conscription (for men) and voluntary enlistment, with later career options for those who sign on as career soldiers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for other ranks in Denmark follows a structured path from private (menig) to non-commissioned officer (konstabel and sergent). Advancement is based on time in service, performance evaluations, and completion of specialised courses. Opportunities exist to transition to officer ranks through the sergeant programme or by attending the Royal Danish Defence College. Many choose to serve for 4–12 years before moving to civilian careers, where military skills are valued in security, logistics, and management. The Danish Defence also offers educational support and pension benefits, making long-term service attractive.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for an other rank in the Danish military?**
A: Salaries are regulated by collective agreements and depend on rank and years of service. Entry-level privates earn a base salary supplemented by allowances, with annual increments. No exact figures are provided here, but compensation is competitive with public sector standards.

**Q: Can a foreign national join the Danish armed forces as an other rank?**
A: Generally no; Danish citizenship is a legal requirement for military service. Exceptions are extremely rare and may apply to certain specialist roles, but these are not considered other ranks. EU/EEA free movement does not cover military employment.

**Q: What are the typical career progression steps for an other rank in Denmark?**
A: After basic training, soldiers may advance to constable, then sergeant, with opportunities for further specialization in fields like intelligence, engineering, or cyber defence. Promotions are based on performance and additional training.
