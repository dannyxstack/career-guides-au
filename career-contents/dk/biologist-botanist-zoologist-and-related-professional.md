# Biologist — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Biologists in Denmark work in diverse fields including environmental consultancy, pharmaceutical R&D, agri-tech, and university research. The country's strong life science sector, combined with its focus on sustainability and green transition, creates steady demand for professionals with expertise in molecular biology, ecology, and bioinformatics. Most positions require a master's degree (cand.scient.), and Danish proficiency is often expected for roles involving public-sector advisory or environmental monitoring. The labour market values interdisciplinary skills, especially at the intersection of biology and data science.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,523. Employment outlook for biologists is moderate, with growth driven by environmental regulation, climate adaptation, and the expansion of the biotech and pharmaceutical industries. However, competition for academic research positions remains high, while private-sector roles in environmental consulting and agricultural technology are more accessible. The Danish government's green transition plans and increased funding for life sciences support long-term demand, but entry-level jobs can be scarce outside of Copenhagen and Aarhus.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression often begins as a research assistant or environmental consultant, advancing to project manager, senior scientist, or head of department. Many biologists transition into regulatory affairs, quality assurance, or science communication. A PhD is typically required for senior research roles, but not for most industry positions. Networking and specialisation (e.g., in environmental impact assessment or bioinformatics) can accelerate advancement. Some professionals also move into policy roles in government agencies such as the Danish Environmental Protection Agency.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)
- Manual sorting of ecological monitoring data (replaced by IoT sensors + AI).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Creativity in experiment design, hypothesis formulation and validation
- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)
- Responsibility attribution and professional certification under the regulatory framework.

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning applications
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., AlphaFold, BLAST)
- Data visualization and interactive reporting (e.g., R Shiny, Tableau)
- Automated laboratory equipment operation and process optimization
- Science communication and policy advocacy skills
- AI ethics and biosecurity regulations knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Environmental biotechnology
- Bioinformatics
- Climate adaptation
- Pharmaceutical R&D

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for biologists in Denmark?**
A: Entry-level biologists earn around 350,000-400,000 DKK/year. With 5+ years of experience, salaries range from 450,000-550,000 DKK/year. Senior roles and PhD-level positions can exceed 600,000 DKK/year.

**Q: Can non-EU biologists get a work visa in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer meeting the Pay Limit Scheme threshold (448,000 DKK/year in 2025) or qualify as a researcher under the Fast-track Scheme. The Positive List for skilled shortage does not currently include biologists, but individual assessment may apply.

**Q: Is a PhD necessary to work as a biologist in Denmark?**
A: Not for most industry roles in environmental consulting or biotech, where a master's degree is sufficient. However, for academic research or senior R&D positions in pharma, a PhD is typically required.
