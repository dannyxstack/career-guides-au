# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Workers in Denmark engage in capturing fish and other aquatic organisms in lakes, rivers, and coastal areas. The profession is part of a traditional industry facing modernization and sustainability challenges. Denmark's fisheries sector is highly regulated with quotas and environmental considerations. Workers typically operate small boats using nets, traps, or lines, and may also maintain gear and process catches. The occupation is concentrated in coastal communities and inland lake regions, with a declining workforce as consolidation reduces the number of small-scale fishers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a crew member on a fishing vessel, advancing to deckhand, then to skipper of a small boat. With additional certifications, one can operate larger coastal vessels or move into aquaculture. Self-employment as an independent fisher is common but requires capital for boat and gear. Many fishers diversify into fish processing, seafood sales, or marine tourism. Danish vocational training (EUX) and safety courses are essential for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fisheries
- Local seafood
- Aquaculture integration
- Recreational fishing

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who enjoy outdoor work and being on the water.
- Those with a strong work ethic and willingness to work irregular hours in varying weather conditions.
- Self-starters interested in small-scale entrepreneurship and local food systems.

**Who is not suited**
- People seeking a predictable 9-to-5 routine with stable year-round employment.
- Those uncomfortable with physical labor, isolation at sea, or the risk and uncertainty of fishing.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for an inland and coastal waters fishery worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary greatly depending on vessel size, catch volume, and experience. Generally, crew members earn between DKK 300,000 and 600,000 per year before tax, with skippers earning more. Income can fluctuate with seasonal catches and market prices.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as a fishery worker in Denmark?**
A: Non-EU citizens face significant barriers. The occupation is not on the Positive List for skilled migrants, and the Pay Limit Scheme requires a high salary rarely met in this field. EU/EEA citizens can work freely. Third-country nationals may explore the Fast-track Scheme if employed by a certified company, but opportunities are limited.
