# Field Crop and Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Denmark manage the cultivation of arable crops, vegetables, and fruits on farms. They plan planting, irrigation, fertilization, and pest control, and operate machinery. The sector is characterized by large-scale, highly mechanized farms and a growing emphasis on organic and sustainable production. Danish agriculture is export-oriented, with strong cooperatives and strict environmental regulations. Many growers are self-employed or work on family farms, while others are employed on larger estates. The occupation requires practical skills, knowledge of agronomy, and increasingly digital literacy for precision farming.

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 75. Employment in field crop and vegetable growing is stable but facing structural changes. The number of farms is declining due to consolidation, but productivity is rising. Organic farming is expanding, driven by consumer demand and EU subsidies. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or apprentice, advancing to farm manager or independent grower. Many growers attend agricultural colleges for vocational training (e.g., agricultural technician or production manager). Opportunities exist in advisory roles, agri-business, or research. Specialization in organic farming, greenhouse production, or precision agriculture can enhance career prospects. However, the path often requires capital to purchase or lease land, which can be a barrier. Self-employment is common, with succession from family farms being a traditional route.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Precision agriculture
- Greenhouse technology
- Sustainable practices

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a field crop and vegetable grower in Denmark?**
A: Earnings vary widely depending on farm size, ownership, and crop type. Salaried employees often earn between 300,000 and 400,000 DKK annually, while self-employed growers' income depends on market prices and subsidies. The Pay Limit Scheme threshold is typically not met.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national work as a field crop grower in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. EU/EEA free movement covers EU citizens. Non-EU nationals need a work permit, typically via the Pay Limit Scheme (high salary) or the Positive List for skilled shortage. Field crop growers are not on the Positive List, so the Pay Limit Scheme is the only option if the salary exceeds ~465,000 DKK, which is uncommon.

**Q: What are the main career advancement opportunities?**
A: Progression includes becoming a farm manager, specializing in organic or greenhouse production, or starting your own farm. Further education in agricultural business or technology can lead to advisory roles or agri-entrepreneurship.
