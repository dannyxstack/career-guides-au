# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners in Denmark are primarily employed by specialized cleaning companies or work as self-employed contractors, servicing commercial buildings, residential complexes, and public institutions. Demand is steady, driven by maintenance needs and new construction. The job involves working at heights using ladders, scaffolding, or hydraulic platforms, and requires physical fitness and attention to safety. Danish language skills are often necessary for client communication.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much can a window cleaner earn in Denmark?**
A: Earnings vary based on experience, location, and employment type (employee vs. self-employed). Wages are generally above the minimum hourly rate for cleaning work, with experienced cleaners earning more, especially in commercial high-rise jobs.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens become window cleaners in Denmark?**
A: Non-EU citizens need a work permit. Window cleaning is not on the Positive List, so the main route is the Pay Limit Scheme, which requires a salary above approximately 465,000 DKK per year—a high threshold for this occupation. The scheme is rarely used for window cleaners.

**Q: What is the typical career path for a window cleaner in Denmark?**
A: Most start as apprentices or trainees, then become skilled window cleaners. Opportunities include specializing in high-rise or industrial cleaning, supervising teams, or starting your own business. Continuing education in safety and new technologies is beneficial.
