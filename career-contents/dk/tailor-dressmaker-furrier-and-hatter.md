# Tailor, Dressmaker, Furrier or Hatter — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors, dressmakers, furriers and hatters make, alter and repair custom garments and headwear. In Denmark, this trade is part of a niche but resilient craft sector, with employment concentrated in small ateliers, bespoke shops, theatre costumes, and film production. The profession requires precision, creativity and knowledge of textiles. While industrial garment manufacturing has largely moved abroad, demand remains for high-quality tailoring, bridal wear, and restoration of vintage clothing. Danish apprenticeships combine vocational school with on-the-job training, and mastery can lead to self-employment or specialization in areas like leatherwork or period costumes.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 132. Employment in tailoring and related crafts is expected to remain stable but limited in scale. Automation and cheap imports continue to reduce mass production jobs, but bespoke services and repair work are growing slowly due to sustainability trends and demand for unique garments. The number of apprenticeships is small, and competition for positions with established masters is tight. Self-employment is common, and those with strong online presence or niche specializations (e.g., bridal, historical costumes) fare best. The overall outlook is flat, with no significant growth forecast.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a 3.5-year vocational education and training (EUD) programme, followed by employment as a skilled tailor or dressmaker. After some years of experience, one can become a master craftsman (svendebrev) and open a workshop, or specialize in areas like furrier work, hat making, or costume design. Some move into teaching at vocational schools or into quality control for textiles. The path is linear and skill-intensive, with strong opportunities for self-employment but limited upward mobility without business acumen.

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

- Build trust with clients and understand personalized needs
- Artistry of traditional handicraft techniques (e.g., embroidery, beadwork)
- Deep understanding and ability to reconstruct clothing history and culture.

**Skills to build:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tailor or dressmaker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and employment type. For a skilled tailor employed by a workshop, monthly earnings typically range from DKK 25,000 to 32,000 before tax. Self-employed tailors may earn less initially but can achieve higher incomes with a strong client base.

**Q: Can a non-EU tailor get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult. The occupation is not on the Positive List for skilled work, and typical salaries are below the Pay Limit Scheme threshold of DKK 465,000 per year. The most feasible route is through the Fast-track Scheme if employed by a certified company, but such opportunities are rare for this craft.
