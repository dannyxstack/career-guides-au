# Garment Patternmaker and Cutter — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7532 (ISCO08)**

Garment patternmakers and cutters create patterns and cut fabric for clothing and textile products in Denmark's fashion and apparel industry. They work in small tailoring shops, larger clothing manufacturers, or as self-employed artisans. The occupation requires precision, creativity, and knowledge of materials. In Denmark, the sector emphasizes sustainable production and technical textiles, with a shift towards digital pattern-making and automated cutting technologies. Employment is concentrated in Copenhagen and a few other regions, with opportunities in custom tailoring, workwear, and high-end fashion.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a cutter or sample maker, advancing to patternmaker or production manager. Experienced professionals may specialize in corsetry, leather, or sportswear, or become self-employed. Further education in textile design or fashion technology can lead to roles in product development or quality control. In Denmark, trade unions offer continued training, and networking within the industry is key for higher-end positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fashion
- Technical textiles
- Automated cutting
- Made-to-measure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garment cutter in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, but are generally in line with other skilled trades. Starting salaries are lower, while experienced patternmakers earn more. Pay is often determined by collective agreements and can include overtime.

**Q: Can a non-EU patternmaker get a work permit for Denmark?**
A: It is challenging as the occupation is not on the Positive List. However, if you secure a job offer with a salary above the Pay Limit Scheme threshold (approx. 487,000 DKK annually), you may qualify. Alternatively, you could come as a student and later apply.

**Q: What career advancement options exist in Denmark?**
A: With experience, you can move into patternmaking, production management, or quality control. Further education in textile technology or design can lead to roles in product development. Self-employment is also common among experienced professionals.
