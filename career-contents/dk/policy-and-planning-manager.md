# Policy and Planning Manager — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 1213 (ISCO08)**

Policy and planning managers in Denmark oversee the development, implementation, and evaluation of policies and strategic plans within government ministries, municipalities, and large public sector organizations. They lead teams of analysts and advisors to formulate policies in areas such as welfare, taxation, and infrastructure, ensuring alignment with national priorities and legal frameworks. The role requires deep knowledge of Danish public administration, excellent analytical skills, and the ability to navigate complex stakeholder landscapes.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a policy analyst or advisor, often with a master's degree in political science or public administration. Advancement to manager level requires several years of relevant experience and proven management skills. Many managers hold additional qualifications in public management. The top career path leads to director roles in ministries or agencies. Networking within the Danish public sector is essential.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically collect and organise policy-related data (e.g., economic indicators, regulatory changes)
- Generate initial policy impact analysis report and summary
- Quantified results of simulated policy options (e.g., budget impact model)
- Monitoring news and social media trends and generating public opinion briefs
- Format and submit compliance documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI tools to quickly compare policy frameworks across countries and identify best practices
- Analyze public feedback using natural language processing tools to optimize policy communication strategies
- Use predictive models to assess long-term socio-economic benefits of policies
- Leverage collaborative AI platforms to efficiently manage cross-departmental policy-making processes
- Use AI to generate multiple versions of policy drafts for leadership review

**Human moat:**

- Making balanced and value-based judgments amidst conflicting interests of multiple parties
- Build trust and conduct political negotiations with senior officials, the public, and others.
- Deep understanding of local culture, history, and power structures
- Assume ultimate responsibility in situations of uncertainty and ethical grey areas
- Proactive policy framework for innovative design rather than optimizing existing solutions

**Skills to build:**

- AI data analysis tools (e.g. Python/R, SQL, and automated reporting platforms)
- Policy modeling and simulation software (e.g., System Dynamics)
- Stakeholder management and conflict mediation skills
- AI ethics and bias recognition capabilities
- Digital governance and e-government platform operation
- Agile Project Management and Cross-Departmental Collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- green transition policy
- digitalization of public sector
- healthcare policy planning
- regional development

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Analytical thinkers with a strong interest in public policy and governance.
- Strategic planners who enjoy shaping long-term national and local strategies.
- Excellent communicators fluent in Danish with a collaborative leadership style.

**Who is not suited**
- Individuals who avoid bureaucratic processes and formal hierarchies.
- Non-Danish speakers not willing to achieve fluency in Danish.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Policy and Planning Manager in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements, experience, and the employing organization. For accurate data, refer to Statistics Denmark's LONS20 earnings statistics. As a general indication, monthly salaries for senior managers in the public sector are competitive but vary widely.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens become Policy and Planning Managers in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Non-EU citizens require a job offer and a work permit, typically through the Pay Limit Scheme or Fast-track Scheme. Danish language proficiency is almost always a requirement, as the role involves policy analysis and stakeholder communication in Danish.

**Q: What is the typical career progression for a Policy and Planning Manager?**
A: Most start as policy analysts or advisors after a master's degree. After 5-10 years of experience, they may become a head of unit or policy manager. Advancement to director level requires strong results and a network within the public sector.
