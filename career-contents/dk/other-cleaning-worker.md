# Other Cleaning Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other Cleaning Workers in Denmark perform a variety of cleaning tasks in settings such as offices, hotels, hospitals, and private homes. This occupation does not require formal education and is often entry-level, with many workers employed on part-time or temporary contracts. The role is fundamental to maintaining hygiene and cleanliness standards across sectors, but it is generally low-paid and has limited career progression. Due to high demand for cleaning services, there is a constant need for workers, but the occupation is not considered skilled and thus not eligible for most work-based immigration schemes for non-EU/EEA nationals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cleaning workers in Denmark is limited. Advancement typically involves moving into supervisory roles, such as team leader or cleaning manager, often requiring additional training or experience. Some workers transition to specialized cleaning (e.g., industrial or hospital cleaning) or start their own cleaning businesses. Formal education is not required, but courses in cleaning technology or management can aid advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to operate cleaning robots or smart cleaning equipment
- Basic computer operation and simple programming (e.g., setting cleaning routes)
- Understanding the Internet of Things (IoT) technology to monitor the status of cleaning equipment
- Knowledge of Disinfection and Chemical Safety in Learning Environments
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn data analysis fundamentals to optimize cleaning efficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- Hygiene standards
- Green cleaning
- Elderly care facilities
- Commercial cleaning

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaning worker in Denmark?**
A: The median annual salary for cleaning workers is approximately 280,000 DKK before tax, with variations based on sector, experience, and location. Hourly wages often start around 120 DKK for entry-level positions.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national get a work visa for cleaning jobs in Denmark?**
A: It is very difficult. The Pay Limit Scheme requires an annual salary of at least 465,000 DKK (2023), which is far above cleaning wages. The Positive List does not include cleaning workers. Only EU/EEA nationals have free access.

**Q: What is the career progression for a cleaning worker in Denmark?**
A: Progression is limited but possible. Experienced workers can become team leaders or supervisors. Some pursue certifications in industrial cleaning or start their own cleaning business. Formal education in service management can lead to management roles.
