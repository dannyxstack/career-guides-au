# Elementary Worker Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9629 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers a wide range of unskilled or semi-skilled tasks in Denmark, including cleaning, simple manual labour, kitchen help, and warehouse assistance. Workers are often employed across sectors such as hospitality, retail, cleaning services, and logistics. The role requires physical stamina and flexibility, with minimal formal education requirements. Typical employers include private cleaning companies, hotels, restaurants, and retail chains. Many workers are part-time or temporary, and the job serves as an entry point into the Danish labour market for those with limited qualifications or language skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most workers remain in similar roles throughout their careers. Advancement to supervisory positions is possible with experience and language skills, but formal training is rarely required. Some may specialise in areas like industrial cleaning or kitchen management. However, without further education, upward mobility is constrained. The occupation typically serves as a stepping stone for newly arrived immigrants or those re-entering the workforce.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple goods handling and stacking: replaced by autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)
- Basic cleaning (e.g., sweeping, mopping): replaced by automatic floor scrubbers or robotic vacuums
- Item classification and sorting: replaced by computer vision systems
- Mail or document distribution: replaced by digital notification systems
- Inventory counting assistance: replaced by RFID and drones

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Task scheduling and priority arrangement: AI optimizes daily work lists
- Equipment failure warning: AI analyzes sensor data to remind of maintenance
- Customer communication records: AI automatic transcription and classification of feedback
- Safety compliance checks: AI-assisted identification of hazardous areas
- Basic communication in multilingual environments: assisted by real-time translation tools

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability to unstructured environments (e.g., ad-hoc assembly tasks)
- Complex emotional communication and customer service (e.g., appeasing dissatisfied customers)
- Cross-task flexible decision-making (when AI recommendations are unclear)
- Fine manual operations (e.g., art packaging)
- Teamwork and on-site coordination skills

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (managing tasks using tablets/mobile phones)
- Operate semi-automated equipment (e.g., cleaning robots)
- Basic Data Analysis (Understanding Task Efficiency Reports)
- Interpersonal communication and customer service skills
- Basic English or a second language (to adapt to multicultural work environments)
- Safety and compliance basic training

**Key growth areas:**

- Cleaning
- Warehouse
- Kitchen

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an elementary worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and region but are generally at the lower end of the Danish labour market. Typical hourly wages range from 120 to 150 DKK for unskilled work. Annual salaries are often below the Pay Limit Scheme threshold, making non-EU migration difficult.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit for an elementary worker job?**
A: It is very difficult because the salary is usually below the Pay Limit Scheme minimum (about 465,000 DKK/year), and the occupation is not on the Positive List. Non-EU citizens would need to qualify under the Fast-track Scheme if the employer is certified, but the job must be skilled. Thus, EU/EEA free movement is the main route for this occupation.

**Q: Is there room for career progression in elementary jobs?**
A: Progression is limited without further education. Some workers advance to team leader or supervisor roles after several years of experience and by taking short courses. However, most remain in similar positions. To significantly improve prospects, workers often pursue vocational training in areas like cleaning technology or kitchen management.
