# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

Mixed crop and livestock farm labourers in Denmark perform manual tasks on farms that combine crop cultivation and animal husbandry. This role is often seasonal, with peaks during planting and harvest, and is common in the country's agricultural sector. Labourers may assist with feeding animals, cleaning stables, weeding, harvesting, and basic machinery operation. The position requires physical stamina and a willingness to work outdoors in varying weather conditions. Many workers are employed on a temporary basis, and the job is typically accessible without formal qualifications, making it a common entry point into agriculture. EU/EEA free movement allows easy access for European workers, while non-EU recruitment is rare due to low salary levels.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for farm labourers is limited but possible. With experience, workers can become skilled farm operators or supervisors. Many move into more specialised roles such as livestock technician or crop manager after completing vocational training. Taking courses in tractor driving, pesticide handling, or organic farming can improve prospects. Some workers transition to permanent positions on larger farms or into related fields like agricultural machinery sales or inspection. The job is often a stepping stone for those seeking long-term careers in Denmark's agricultural sector.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using automated tractors for plowing, sowing, and harvesting
- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots
- Use drones to inspect farms and produce yield maps
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans based on historical data and weather forecasts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining smart agricultural equipment
- Using data analysis to improve crop yields and herd health
- Develop comprehensive agricultural strategies to balance ecology and economy
- Collaborate with AI for precision agriculture management
- Train new employees in using digital tools

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of sudden mechanical faults
- Unstructured work in complex terrain or extreme weather conditions.
- Animal emergency medical care and behavioural intervention
- Compliance with organic certification and animal welfare standards.

**Skills to build:**

- Basic electrical and mechanical maintenance skills
- Digital literacy: Excel/data recording apps
- Basics of crop and livestock health management

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are generally based on collective agreements for agriculture. They vary by region, experience, and farm type, but are typically below the national average. Exact figures are available through Statistics Denmark or trade unions.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a farm labourer in Denmark?**
A: It is very challenging. Non-EU nationals generally require a work permit under schemes like the Pay Limit Scheme, which requires a high salary. Farm labourer salaries rarely meet this threshold, so working in this occupation is largely limited to EU/EEA nationals.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist?**
A: With experience, farm labourers may progress to skilled positions such as farm manager or specialist livestock worker, often after completing additional vocational training.
