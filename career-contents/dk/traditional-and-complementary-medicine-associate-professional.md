# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Associate Professional — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and complementary medicine associate professionals in Denmark provide non-conventional health treatments such as acupuncture, herbal medicine, and naturopathy. While not part of the mainstream healthcare system, these practitioners are popular among Danes seeking holistic and preventive care. The field is largely unregulated, meaning practitioners often operate as self-employed or in private clinics. Demand is driven by an aging population, increased interest in wellness, and a preference for natural therapies. However, professional recognition varies, and there is no official authorization body, which affects credibility and insurance reimbursement.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression in Denmark for alternative therapists typically involves building a private practice or joining established wellness clinics. Advancement comes from specialization (e.g., sports acupuncture or fertility support), obtaining additional certifications, and gaining a loyal client base. Some practitioners expand into teaching or product sales. There is no formal career ladder; success depends on marketing and reputation. Many work part-time alongside other health-related jobs. Collaboration with conventional doctors is rare but increasing in integrative medicine settings.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a traditional and complementary medicine associate professional in Denmark?**
A: Earnings vary widely due to self-employment. A full-time practitioner may earn between DKK 250,000 and 400,000 annually before tax, but many work part-time or have lower incomes. Established practitioners with loyal clients can earn more, but entry-level incomes are often below DKK 200,000.

**Q: Can I work as an alternative therapist in Denmark with a non-EU passport?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult unless you qualify under the Pay Limit Scheme (requires a job offer with salary > DKK 465,000) or the Positive List (not currently on the list). Most non-EU therapists come through family reunification or as students. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: Is alternative medicine regulated in Denmark?**
A: No, it is largely unregulated. There is no official authorization or mandatory certification. Practitioners can operate freely, but they cannot claim to treat specific diseases or use titles like 'doctor'. Professional associations offer voluntary certification for credibility.
