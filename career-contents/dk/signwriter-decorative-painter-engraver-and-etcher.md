# Signwriters, Decorative Painters, Engravers and Etchers — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7316 (ISCO08)**

In Denmark, signwriters, decorative painters, engravers, and etchers are skilled artisans who create and apply lettering, decorative painting, and engraved designs on various surfaces. They work in small workshops, restoration firms, or as self-employed craftspeople. The occupation requires a blend of artistic talent and precision, often serving niche markets such as heritage buildings, custom signage, and fine art reproduction. While not a major occupation by volume, it holds cultural significance in preserving traditional techniques.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |

| Master's Certificate | Danish Business Authority | Optional | Voluntary advanced qualification. Allows independent business operation and training of apprentices. Requires several years of experience and further exams. |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 167. Employment outlook for this occupation in Denmark is stable but limited. Demand is driven by restoration projects, custom signage for businesses, and the luxury goods sector. Automation and digital printing have reduced the need for traditional handcraft in some areas, but high-end, bespoke work remains resilient. Opportunities are concentrated in larger cities and tourist areas. The workforce is aging, so openings may arise from retirements, but overall growth is flat.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves completing a vocational apprenticeship (EUD) and obtaining a Journeyman's Certificate. Experienced artisans may pursue a Master's Certificate, allowing them to take on apprentices and run their own business. Many choose self-employment, offering specialized services. There is limited room for hierarchical advancement; instead, progression comes from reputation, specialization (e.g., restoration or gilding), and taking on larger, higher-profile projects. Continuous learning of new techniques and materials is essential.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a signwriter in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary with experience, region, and specialisation. Entry-level workers earn an hourly wage around the skilled trades minimum, while experienced craftspeople with high demand skills can earn significantly more, especially in restoration or custom work.

**Q: Can a non-EU signwriter get a work visa for Denmark?**
A: Yes, primarily through the Pay Limit Scheme if the salary meets the threshold. The Fast-track Scheme may also apply if the employer is certified. However, the occupation is not on the Positive List, so the job must be genuinely skilled and the salary competitive.

**Q: What education is required to become a signwriter in Denmark?**
A: The standard path is a 3.5- to 4-year vocational education (EUD) combining school and apprenticeship. After passing the final exam, you receive a Journeyman's Certificate. Some learn through shorter courses or on-the-job training, but the formal qualification is strongly recommended for employment.
