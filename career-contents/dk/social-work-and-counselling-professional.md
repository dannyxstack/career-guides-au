# Social Work and Counselling Professionals — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2635 (ISCO08)**

Social work and counselling professionals in Denmark work primarily in municipal social services, hospitals, and private practices. They address issues such as child welfare, mental health, addiction, and integration. The profession is regulated with a protected title for social workers ('socialrådgiver'), requiring a professional bachelor's degree. Demand is driven by an aging population, increased focus on mental health, and integration of refugees and immigrants. The work often involves interdisciplinary collaboration and is heavily influenced by Danish welfare policies and administrative procedures.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 16,381. The employment outlook for social work and counselling professionals in Denmark is positive, with steady demand in public sector roles. Shortages exist in child welfare, psychiatric care, and elderly services, especially in rural areas. Government initiatives aim to recruit more professionals, but language barriers and bureaucratic complexity can limit supply. The occupation is on the Positive List for skilled work, indicating labour market needs. However, public sector budget constraints may slow hiring growth in some municipalities.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with casework or frontline counselling, then advances to specialist roles (e.g., family therapist, addiction counsellor) or management (team leader, department head). Many pursue a master's degree (e.g., in social work or psychology) for clinical or supervisory positions. Continuing education is encouraged, and professionals can move into policy, research, or private practice. The field offers stability but limited private-sector earnings growth compared to other professions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate case reports and progress records
- Initial psychological assessment based on standardized questionnaires
- Crisis hotline initial screening and information triage
- Scheduling appointments and case management system operation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze social media data for early warning of community mental health risks
- Using virtual reality therapy to assist in PTSD treatment
- AI-supported multilingual automatic transcription and sentiment analysis improve consultation efficiency.
- Data-driven case management optimizes resource allocation.

**Human moat:**

- Establishes deep trust relationships and empathetic interaction
- Handling Ethical Dilemmas and Complex Legal Cases
- Flexible communication strategies in multicultural contexts

**Skills to build:**

- Digital literacy and use of AI tools (e.g., data analysis, VR therapy)
- Trauma-Informed Care and Crisis Intervention in Practice
- Cross-disciplinary collaboration skills (with healthcare and education sectors)
- Research evaluation and evidence-based practice methods
- Self-care and burnout management
- Multicultural competence and interpretation of Italian local policies

**Key growth areas:**

- mental health
- child welfare
- elderly care
- integration

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Can non-EU social workers get a work permit in Denmark?**
A: Yes, if they have a job offer from a Danish employer and meet the Positive List requirements (a relevant bachelor's degree and sufficient Danish language skills, usually B2). The permit is tied to the employer and occupation.

**Q: What education is needed to become a social worker in Denmark?**
A: A professional bachelor's degree in social work (socialrådgiveruddannelsen) from a university college is required. The degree is 3.5 years and includes practical placements. EU citizens can study for free; non-EU pay tuition.

**Q: What are the career prospects for social workers in Denmark?**
A: The field offers stable employment in municipalities and regions. Experienced workers can specialize in areas like family therapy or addiction counselling, or move into management. A master's degree opens up clinical roles and higher positions.
