# Child Care Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 5311 (ISCO08)**

Child care workers in Denmark assist pedagogues in the daily care and development of children in daycare centres, kindergartens, and after-school programmes. They supervise play, prepare meals, and support children's social and cognitive growth. The role is regulated under the social pedagogy framework, with a strong emphasis on play-based learning and inclusion. Workers typically hold a vocational qualification or a short pedagogical course, though many gain experience on the job. Demand is stable due to high birth rates and political focus on early childhood education.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 43,289. Employment outlook for child care workers in Denmark is stable. The government is expanding childcare access, creating steady demand. However, competition for permanent positions exists, especially in cities. Demographic trends suggest continued need, but austerity measures may limit growth. The occupation is not on the Positive List, so non-EU migration is restricted. EU/EEA workers have free movement, but salaries for child care workers are below the Pay Limit Scheme threshold, making work permits for third-country nationals challenging.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves transitioning to a pedagogue role by completing a 3.5-year bachelor's degree in early childhood education (pædagog). Many start as child care workers to gain experience before pursuing higher education. Specialisations in areas like special needs, language stimulation, or outdoor pedagogy are growing. Opportunities for leadership positions (e.g., pedagogical leader) require the bachelor's degree. Continuous professional development is encouraged through municipalities.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a child care worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by municipality and experience, but as of 2023, the monthly gross salary for a full-time child care worker is typically between DKK 24,000 and 30,000 (DKK 288,000–360,000 annually). Union agreements and collective bargaining influence exact figures.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens get a work visa as a child care worker in Denmark?**
A: It is difficult. The occupation is not on the Positive List, and salaries often fall below the Pay Limit Scheme threshold (DKK 465,000 annually). Most child care workers from outside the EU/EEA come through family reunification or as refugees with work permission. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.
