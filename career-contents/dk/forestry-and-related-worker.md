# Forestry Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry and related workers in Denmark perform practical tasks in forest management, including planting, thinning, harvesting, and maintaining forest areas. They work for public and private forest owners, often using machinery and hand tools. The occupation is integral to Denmark's commitment to sustainable forestry, carbon sequestration, and biodiversity. Workers may also engage in nature conservation, recreational trail maintenance, and wildfire prevention. The role requires physical stamina and a strong environmental awareness.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 230. Employment in forestry is expected to remain stable with moderate demand due to increased focus on climate adaptation, bioenergy, and sustainable forest management. Denmark's goal of expanding forest cover and enhancing natural habitats supports steady job opportunities. However, automation and mechanisation may reduce the need for manual labour. The workforce is aging, creating openings for younger entrants. The sector is also influenced by EU agricultural and environmental policies.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a forestry worker, advancing to team leader or specialised technician (skov- og naturtekniker) after vocational training. Further education can lead to forest manager roles or self-employment. Workers may specialise in areas such as arboriculture, nature restoration, or game management. The field offers a clear career ladder within public agencies (e.g., Naturstyrelsen) or private forestry companies.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable forestry
- Climate adaptation
- Bioenergy
- Forest conservation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a forestry worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are not published here, but pay follows collective agreements in the agricultural and forestry sector. Entry-level workers can expect around 280,000-320,000 DKK annually, with experienced workers earning more.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a forestry worker in Denmark?**
A: It is challenging. The occupation is not on the Positive List, and the pay limit scheme requires a high salary. EU/EEA free movement is the primary route.
