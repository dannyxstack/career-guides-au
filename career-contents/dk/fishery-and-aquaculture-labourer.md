# Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and aquaculture labourers perform manual tasks on fishing vessels, in fish farms, and at landing sites, including net handling, fish sorting, feeding, and equipment maintenance. In Denmark, this occupation is concentrated in coastal regions and on islands, often seasonal or part-time. The industry faces challenges from quota restrictions and labour shortages, but EU/EEA workers freely fill many positions. Automation and sustainable practices are slowly reshaping traditional roles.

However, growth in organic and land-based aquaculture may create new jobs. An aging workforce and difficulty attracting younger workers could lead to recruitment challenges, especially for skilled positions. EU/EEA free movement helps cover seasonal demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited without additional training. Labourers can advance to become vessel skippers, farm managers, or quality controllers after gaining experience and relevant certifications. Some transition into processing or sales. The industry offers on-the-job learning, but formal vocational education (e.g., fisheries technician) improves prospects. Entrepreneurship in small-scale aquaculture is an emerging path.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Land-based aquaculture
- Sustainable fishing practices
- Organic seafood
- Processing automation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Danish Immigration Service (nyidanmark.dk)](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy physical outdoor work and being on or near the water
- Individuals comfortable with shift work, early mornings, and variable weather
- Those willing to train for safety and basic vessel/husbandry skills

**Who is not suited**
- People seeking a desk job or regular 9-to-5 hours
- Individuals who are sensitive to seasickness or cold conditions

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery labourer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience, but entry-level labourers often earn around 280,000–350,000 DKK annually before tax. Overtime and bonuses may apply. Note that positions are often seasonal.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA citizen get a work visa as a fishery labourer?**
A: It is difficult because the job typically falls below the Pay Limit Scheme threshold and is not on the Positive List. EU/EEA free movement covers most labour needs, so non-EU workers rarely get permits for this occupation unless they have specialized skills.

**Q: What are the main employers in Denmark for this occupation?**
A: Small-scale fishing vessels, fish farms (especially in Jutland and on islands like Bornholm), and aquaculture companies producing rainbow trout, mussels, or salmon. Municipalities and port authorities also hire labourers for maintenance.
