# Jewellery And Precious Metal Worker — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 7313 (ISCO08)**

Jewellery and precious metal workers in Denmark design, create, repair, and restore jewellery, watches, and other fine metal items. They work with gold, silver, platinum, and gemstones, using techniques like casting, soldering, stone setting, and engraving. Most are employed in small artisan workshops, jewellery retail stores, or are self-employed. The trade requires a strong sense of aesthetics, manual dexterity, and patience. The Danish goldsmith tradition is historically significant, and today the sector is known for high-quality craft and design, with a focus on custom orders and repairs.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 96. The employment outlook for jewellery and precious metal workers in Denmark is stable but limited. The sector is small, with few new job openings each year, mainly due to retirements. Demand is driven by repairs and custom-made jewellery, but competition for positions is moderate. Economic fluctuations affect luxury spending. There is a niche demand for skilled workers in high-end design and restoration. Overall, the occupation offers steady but not rapid growth, with opportunities mainly in Copenhagen and larger cities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a 4-year vocational apprenticeship (guldsmedeuddannelse), progressing from novice to skilled journeyman. After several years, one may become a master goldsmith (guldmed mester) by passing a master's examination. Masters can open their own workshops or teach. Some specialize in stone setting, engraving, or watch repair. Others move into jewellery design or retail management. Continuing education in new techniques like laser welding or 3D printing for jewellery is increasingly valuable.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated polishing and grinding: AI-controlled robotic arms can efficiently perform basic grinding and polishing, replacing repetitive manual labor.
- CAD-assisted design modeling: AI generates multiple design variants, reducing the need for manual drafting and wax pattern carving.
- Quality inspection: computer vision systems automatically detect gem flaws and metal defects, replacing visual inspection.
- Inventory and order management: AI automatically optimises material procurement and order scheduling, reducing administrative positions.
- Basic repairs: standardized ring resizing, chain repair, etc., can be done by automated equipment.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex custom design: AI generates design sketches, which craftsmen refine, boosting creative efficiency.
- Customer preference analysis: AI analyzes sales data to assist designers in creating styles that better align with target markets.
- Material simulation: AI simulates the appearance of different metal and gemstone combinations, reducing the cost of producing physical samples.
- Education training: AR/VR combined with AI teaching to accelerate beginners' learning of carving and inlay techniques.
- Repair solution recommendations: AI scans damaged jewelry and suggests optimal repair paths to assist craftsmen in decision-making.

**Human moat:**

- Handcrafted carving and artistic creation: unique aesthetic sense and manual dexterity that AI cannot replicate.
- Customer trust and personalized service: Jewelry purchases involve high emotional value, making human communication irreplaceable.
- Complex setting and restoration: high technical difficulty gem setting and antique restoration rely on artisan experience.
- Security and compliance responsibility: authenticity and origin of precious metals and gems (e.g., conflict-free diamonds) require human endorsement.

**Skills to build:**

- AI design tools: Master AI plugins for CAD software such as Rhino 3D and MatrixGold.
- 3D printing and modeling: learn digital manufacturing processes, such as 3D printing wax patterns and lost-wax casting.
- Gemology certification: Obtain professional qualifications such as GIA to enhance appraisal authority.
- Customer experience design: learn personalized marketing and AR virtual try-on technology.
- Sustainable supply chain knowledge: understanding blockchain traceability and fair trade certification.
- Data analysis: using AI tools to analyze market trends and guide design strategies.

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom design
- Repair services
- Artisan jewellery
- Luxury goods

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a jewellery and precious metal worker in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. A newly qualified journeyman can expect around 300,000-350,000 DKK annually. Experienced workers or masters may earn 400,000-500,000 DKK. Self-employed income depends on clientele.

**Q: Can a non-EU jewellery worker get a work permit in Denmark?**
A: Yes, primarily through the Pay Limit Scheme if the annual salary meets the threshold (487,000 DKK in 2024). Alternatively, if the job is on the Positive List for the skilled shortage (currently not the case for this occupation), a permit may be easier. EU/EEA citizens can work freely.
