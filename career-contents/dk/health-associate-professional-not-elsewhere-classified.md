# Optician — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 3259 (ISCO08)**

Opticians in Denmark are regulated health professionals who fit, dispense, and manage eyewear and contact lenses. They work in optical retail stores, clinics, and hospitals, playing a key role in vision care. The profession requires a 3-year professional bachelor's degree and mandatory authorization from the Danish Patient Safety Authority. With an aging population and increased screen usage, demand for opticians remains steady. Digitalization and teleoptometry are emerging trends, while competition from online retailers challenges traditional brick-and-mortar models. Opticians often collaborate with ophthalmologists and optometrists.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 94. Employment outlook for opticians in Denmark is positive, driven by an aging population and rising awareness of eye health. The shift toward digital services, such as online frame selection and teleoptometry, creates new opportunities but also demands digital literacy. The shortage of opticians is expected to persist, particularly in rural areas, making job prospects favorable. However, automation of basic lens grinding may reduce demand for some routine tasks.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for opticians typically starts with a bachelor's degree, then specialization in contact lenses, low vision, or practice management. Many opticians become store managers or pursue further education to become optometrists. Others move into sales roles with optical equipment manufacturers. The profession offers good opportunities for those willing to relocate or take on supervisory roles in larger chains.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an optician in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Newly qualified opticians earn around DKK 300,000-350,000 per year, while experienced opticians can earn up to DKK 450,000.

**Q: Do I need Danish language skills to get a visa as an optician?**
A: Yes, for authorization and the Positive List, you need Danish proficiency at least B2 level. For the Pay Limit Scheme, language is not required but strongly recommended for job success.

**Q: Can I work as an optician in Denmark with a foreign degree?**
A: Yes, but you need to have your qualifications assessed by the Danish Patient Safety Authority and may need to complete supplementary training (typically 1-2 years) to meet Danish standards.
