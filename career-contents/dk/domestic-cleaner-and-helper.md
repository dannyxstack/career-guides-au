# Domestic Cleaner and Helper — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9111 (ISCO08)**

Domestic cleaners and helpers perform cleaning tasks in private households, including dusting, mopping, vacuuming, and laundry. In Denmark, this occupation is characterised by high demand, particularly in urban areas with dual-income families and an ageing population. Many workers are employed part-time or through cleaning agencies, and a significant proportion are immigrants from EU/EEA countries. The job requires no formal education, but on-the-job training is common. Wages are relatively low, often below the threshold for skilled work permits, making it accessible mainly to EU/EEA nationals or those with family ties.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for domestic cleaners in Denmark is limited but possible. Experienced workers can advance to supervisory roles in cleaning companies or become self-employed, offering specialised services like deep cleaning or eco-friendly cleaning. Some may transition into commercial cleaning or facilities management. Further training through AMU courses can lead to certifications as a 'professional cleaner' or 'cleaning technician,' which can open doors to higher-paying positions in offices, hospitals, or industrial settings. However, without additional education, upward mobility is slow.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Schedule cleaning appointments, send reminders, and collect payments via chatbot
- Use computer vision to inspect cleaning quality and generate reports
- Automated inventory management: predict cleaning supply needs based on historical data and place orders
- Voice assistants control smart home devices (e.g., robot vacuum cleaners, automatic curtains)
- Standardized cleaning process documents generated and updated by AI.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI voice translation tool assists in communicating with foreign employers
- Smart devices (e.g., stain detection apps) to assist in choosing the best cleaning agent
- Data analysis tools optimize cleaning routes and time allocation
- Online platform automatically matches customers and cleaners, improving customer acquisition efficiency
- Augmented reality (AR) guidance for cleaning complex furniture.

**Human moat:**

- Fine manual cleaning (e.g., antiques, silk, and other special materials)
- Building long-term trust relationships and personalized services with employers
- Ability to handle unexpected situations (e.g., spills, damage) with flexibility
- manual labour and on-site presence (physical contact that cannot be replaced)
- Highly customized home organization and decluttering solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart cleaning equipment
- Basic English or a second foreign language (for smart assistants and client communication)
- Time Management and Digital Scheduling Tool Usage
- Knowledge of household chemical safety and intelligent proportioning
- Fundamentals of Customer Relationship Management (CRM) software
- Basic data analysis (to optimize service efficiency)

**Key growth areas:**

- Urbanization
- Aging population
- Green cleaning
- Service economy

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a domestic cleaner in Denmark?**
A: The salary for domestic cleaners in Denmark is relatively low, typically below the threshold for the Pay Limit Scheme. Many are paid hourly, and rates vary by region and employer. Specific figures are not provided here, but the occupation does not meet the salary requirements for skilled work permits.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals get a work permit as a domestic cleaner in Denmark?**
A: Generally, no. Domestic cleaning is not considered a skilled occupation, and the salary is below the Pay Limit threshold. Non-EU nationals would need to qualify through other schemes, such as the Positive List or Fast-track, which this occupation does not meet. Family reunification or asylum may be possible, but not standard work permits.
