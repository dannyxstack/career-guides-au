# Legal Professional (Other) — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2619 (ISCO08)**

Legal professionals not elsewhere classified encompass a diverse range of legal roles in Denmark, including corporate legal advisors, compliance officers, and contract managers. They provide legal guidance to organizations, ensure regulatory compliance, and manage legal risk. The Danish legal market is robust, with strong demand in sectors like finance, IT, and pharmaceuticals. Most positions require a master's degree in law (cand.jur.) and fluency in Danish. While the profession is stable, competition is high for entry-level roles, and specialization is key to advancement.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for legal professionals typically begins as a junior legal advisor or assistant, advancing to senior advisor, head of legal, or partner in a law firm. Further specialization in areas like M&A or intellectual property can lead to higher positions. Many professionals also move in-house to corporate legal departments. Continuous education is valued, with possibilities to obtain a PhD or specialized certifications. Networking and publication are important for reputation building.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic contract review and clause comparison
- Template-based drafting of legal documents
- Legal regulation retrieval and case summary generation.
- Information extraction and classification in due diligence.
- Automated generation of standard compliance reports.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Compliance Risk Assessment and Early Warning
- Intellectual property layout analysis
- Customer legal risk education and training

**Human moat:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as legal professionals in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Most non-EU legal professionals need a work permit through the Pay Limit Scheme or a researcher permit. The Positive List for higher education does not currently include legal professionals due to sufficient domestic supply. Additionally, fluency in Danish and knowledge of Danish law are typically required.

**Q: What education is needed to become a legal professional in Denmark?**
A: The standard education is a master's degree in law (cand.jur.) from a Danish university, which takes five years (bachelor + master). It is offered in Danish at universities like Copenhagen, Aarhus, and Southern Denmark. International students can also pursue this degree, but must be proficient in Danish. Additional certification may be needed for specific roles.
