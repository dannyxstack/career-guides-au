# Philosopher, Historian or Political Scientist — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 2633 (ISCO08)**

Philosophers, historians and political scientists in Denmark are typically employed in universities, research institutions, public administration, think tanks, and media. They analyse societal structures, historical developments, and philosophical questions, contributing to policy advice, education, and public debate. The Danish labour market for these professionals is highly competitive and niche, with most positions requiring advanced degrees (master's or PhD). While demand is stable in academia and the public sector, private sector opportunities are limited. Foreign candidates may find opportunities through research fellowships or EU free movement, but the market is small and Danish language skills are often required.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,580. Employment outlook for philosophers, historians and political scientists in Denmark is stable but slow-growing. University positions are limited and highly competitive, with a trend towards short-term contracts and project-based funding. Public sector roles in ministries, municipalities, and NGOs offer moderate opportunities, but budget constraints can limit hiring. There is a small but steady demand for political analysts, especially in international affairs and EU policy, given Denmark's active role in the EU. However, overall growth is below average for other academic professions, and many graduates pursue alternative careers in teaching, journalism, or business.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression in Denmark for these fields typically follows an academic path: from research assistant to assistant professor, then associate professor and full professor. Alternatively, experienced professionals move into senior policy advisor roles in government or international organizations. Mid-career professionals may also transition into management in the cultural sector, museums, or archives. Danish language proficiency is a significant advantage for progression, especially in public sector roles. Networking and publications are crucial for academic advancement, while public sector careers often require demonstrated policy analysis skills.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a philosopher, historian or political scientist in Denmark?**
A: Salaries vary by sector: researchers at universities start around 400,000-450,000 DKK/year, while senior advisors in government can earn 500,000-600,000 DKK/year. PhD stipends are approximately 330,000 DKK/year.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as a philosopher/historian/political scientist in Denmark?**
A: Yes, but it is highly competitive. Non-EU citizens need a job offer from a Danish employer. The Fast-track scheme for researchers is a common route if the employer is approved. Alternatively, the Pay Limit Scheme (salary > 465,000 DKK/year) or the Positive List (currently not covering this occupation specifically) may apply. Danish language skills are often required.

**Q: Is Danish language necessary for these roles?**
A: In academia, English is widely used, especially at the PhD and postdoc level. However, for public sector and teaching roles, Danish proficiency is essential. Many research positions require Danish only for teaching or public outreach.
