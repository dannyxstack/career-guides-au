# Manufacturing Labourer Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Denmark

**Occupation code: 9329 (ISCO08)**

Manufacturing labourers not elsewhere classified (ISCO-08 9329) perform simple manual tasks in factories and production facilities across Denmark. They assist machine operators, pack products, clean workspaces, and handle materials. This occupation is common in food processing, assembly plants, and packaging industries. It typically requires no formal education, but on-the-job training is provided. The role is often a stepping stone into the Danish labour market for EU/EEA nationals, but it offers limited career advancement and is vulnerable to automation.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for manufacturing labourers in Denmark is limited to roles such as team leader or machine operator after gaining experience and possibly completing vocational training. Some may transition into related fields like warehouse logistics or quality control. The occupation is often entry-level and attracts workers with low formal qualifications. To advance, workers typically need to pursue a relevant vocational education (e.g., industrial operator) which can lead to higher-skilled positions with better pay and stability.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive material handling and palletizing
- Basic cleaning and tidying work
- Simple packaging and box sealing operation
- Manual quality visual inspection and counting
- fixed route material delivery

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use handheld terminals or AR glasses to receive digital instructions, improving accuracy
- Operate semi-automated equipment, such as collaborative robot-assisted assembly
- Monitor production line anomalies through data dashboards and report promptly
- Use AI-driven predictive maintenance tools to provide equipment status feedback
- Learn basic programming, adjust automation workstation parameters

**Human moat:**

- Flexible physical ability for handling unstructured scenarios (e.g., irregular workpiece handling)
- Ability to quickly adapt to task switching in high-mix, low-volume production
- Communication and adaptability to collaborate with colleagues on complex assembly
- Rapid diagnosis and manual intervention for on-site anomalies (e.g., equipment jams, quality deviations)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy: scanning, touchscreen operation, digital records
- Basic operation and safety protocols for collaborative robots.
- Simple sensor identification and alarm processing
- Lean production and 5S management
- Automated logistics system (AGV/conveyor) anomaly handling
- Basic English or technical document reading

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation adaptation
- Lean production
- Quality control
- Sustainability

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Denmark does not take part in the EU Blue Card but runs its own skilled-migration schemes, such as the Pay Limit Scheme and the Positive List for occupations facing shortages. Eligibility depends on your job offer and salary, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit for a manufacturing labourer job in Denmark?**
A: Generally no. This occupation does not meet the skill or salary thresholds for schemes like the Pay Limit Scheme or Positive List. Only EU/EEA citizens can freely take up such positions.

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a manufacturing labourer in Denmark?**
A: Salaries are modest and typically follow collective agreement rates for unskilled work. Most earn around the minimum wage level, often between 250,000 and 300,000 DKK annually before tax, depending on industry and shift allowances.

**Q: How can I progress from a manufacturing labourer to a better position?**
A: Progression usually requires completing a vocational training programme, such as an industrial operator (1-2 years), which can lead to roles like machine operator, team leader, or quality controller. Some employers offer internal training schemes.
