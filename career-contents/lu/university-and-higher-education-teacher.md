**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a university teacher in Luxembourg?**
A: Salaries for university teachers in Luxembourg are based on civil service scales for public institutions (e.g., University of Luxembourg). A starting assistant professor earns around €70,000–€90,000 annually, including benefits. Full professors can earn €100,000–€150,000 or more. Salaries are competitive and reflect the high cost of living.

**Q: What work visa options exist for non-EU university teachers in Luxembourg?**
A: Non-EU university teachers can obtain an EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, requiring a job offer with a salary above the threshold (€58,968 in 2025). Alternatively, a standard work permit (authorisation de séjour) is possible. Both require employer sponsorship and approval from the Ministry of Labour. EU/EEA nationals have free movement.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a university teacher in Luxembourg?**
A: Typically, a PhD is required for assistant professor or higher positions. Teaching and research experience, publications, and fluency in English are essential. French or German is often preferred due to Luxembourg’s multilingual environment. Appointments are made by universities, with public positions following civil service procedures.
