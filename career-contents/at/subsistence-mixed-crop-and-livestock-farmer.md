## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Austria?**
A: Subsistence farmers in Austria primarily produce for personal consumption, so income varies. Many rely on off-farm employment and EU subsidies. Official statistics from Statistik Austria indicate that small farms reported an average net income of around €15,000 per year, but this is not a fixed salary and depends on individual circumstances.

**Q: What work visa or EU Blue Card options exist for subsistence farmers from non-EU countries to work in Austria?**
A: Subsistence farming is not typically eligible for the EU Blue Card, which requires a tertiary degree and a minimum salary threshold. Non-EU nationals may instead apply for a Red-White-Red Card for key workers in agriculture if they have a qualifying job offer. Seasonal work permits are also available for temporary stays, but long-term settlement for subsistence-only farming is challenging.

**Q: What are the main tasks and challenges for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Austria?**
A: Daily tasks include planting, weeding, feeding livestock, and maintaining pastures. Challenges include low profitability, reliance on subsidies, and compliance with EU regulations. Many farmers work off-farm to supplement income. Climate change and land fragmentation are growing concerns. Despite challenges, subsistence farming supports self-sufficiency and preserves traditional agricultural practices.
