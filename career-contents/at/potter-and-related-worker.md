**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use a potter's wheel or molds for batch forming of standardized vessels
- Mechanical reproduction of basic carving and patterns
- Automated monitoring and adjustment of kiln temperature and time
- Automatic calculation and mixing of glaze formulas

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Quickly design complex ceramic shapes using 3D modeling software

- Use AR technology to show customers virtual finished ceramic products
- Analyze market data to optimize product design and pricing

**Human moat:**

- Irreproducible personal style and artistic expression in handmade pottery
- Ability for manual adjustments to handle customized, non-standard requirements.
- Deep perception and control over clay material properties
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional craft culture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for potters in Austria?**
A: In Austria, potters typically earn a gross monthly salary between €1,800 and €2,500, depending on experience, employer, and region. Apprentices earn less, while master craftspeople or those in high-end ceramics may earn more. Salaries are often governed by collective bargaining agreements in the craft sector.

**Q: What work visa routes exist for non-EU potters to work in Austria?**
A: Non-EU potters can work in Austria via the Red-White-Red Card for skilled workers or the EU Blue Card. The Red-White-Red Card requires a qualified profession, a job offer, and meeting criteria like qualifications and language skills. The EU Blue Card needs a university degree or equivalent and a minimum salary threshold (€3,666.67/month in 2025; lower for shortage occupations). Potters may not be on the shortage list.

**Q: What training is required to become a potter in Austria?**
A: In Austria, the typical path is a 3-year apprenticeship (Lehre) in ceramics or pottery, combining on-the-job training with vocational school. Apprentices must pass a final exam. Alternatively, a master craftsman qualification (Meisterprüfung) allows self-employment. Some attend specialized art schools or university programs in applied arts.
