**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repeated on-site demonstrations of product standard functions
- Using pre-recorded videos or AI-generated content to replace on-site explanations
- Frequently asked customer questions; replaced by chatbots
- Product parameter query and comparison, replaced by self-service terminals or AR applications

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes customer data to assist in customizing personalized presentation scripts
- Real-time speech transcription and translation, supporting multilingual audiences
- AR/VR technology enhances the immersion and interactivity of product displays
- Sales data dashboard helps presenters adjust strategies in real time

**Human moat:**

- Soft skills for building emotional trust and client relationships
- Creativity and narrative skills in complex product experiences
- Flexible adaptation for cross-cultural and non-standard scenarios

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Sales Demonstrator in Austria?**
A: Salaries for Sales Demonstrators in Austria typically range from €1,800 to €2,500 gross per month for full-time work, depending on experience, industry, and region. Collective bargaining agreements may set minimums; for example, in retail, the minimum is around €1,700. Part-time roles are common, with pro-rated pay.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a Sales Demonstrator in Austria via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is generally not suitable for Sales Demonstrators because it requires a university degree and a minimum salary of €60,000+ annually (2024). Non-EU nationals typically apply for a Red-White-Red Card or a General Employment Permit, which require a job offer and a labor market test. Check with the Austrian Public Employment Service (AMS) for details.

**Q: What are the main duties of a Sales Demonstrator in Austria?**
A: Sales Demonstrators promote and demonstrate products at trade shows, retail stores, or events. They engage customers, explain features, answer questions, and aim to boost sales. They may set up displays, distribute samples, and report feedback. Fluent German is often required, and employers may seek experience in specific sectors like electronics or cosmetics.
