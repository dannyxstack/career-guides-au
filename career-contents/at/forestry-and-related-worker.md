**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry worker in Austria?**
A: The salary for forestry workers in Austria varies by region and experience. According to collective agreements, the minimum monthly gross wage ranges from €2,000 to €2,400. With several years of experience or additional qualifications, earnings can exceed €3,000. Actual pay depends on the employer and specific duties.

**Q: How can a non-EU national work as a forestry worker in Austria?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work in Austria via the Red-White-Red Card for skilled workers if forestry is listed as a shortage occupation. The EU Blue Card requires a higher education degree and high salary, making it less common for forestry workers. Alternatively, a standard work permit may be possible through a specific employer sponsorship and labor market test.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for forestry workers in Austria?**
A: Forestry workers can advance to positions like forester, forest manager, or specialist roles in forest conservation and planning. Further training at schools such as the Forestry Training Centre in Ossiach or acquiring a master craftsman certificate opens doors. Many work for the Austrian Federal Forests (ÖBf) or private enterprises, with options for self-employment.
