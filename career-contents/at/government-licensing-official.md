- Automatically review standard application forms and identify missing or non-compliant items

- Automatically generate standard licensing or denial letters based on rules

- AI-assisted analysis of applicant background and risk, providing decision recommendations

- Handle common inquiries via chatbots, freeing up staff for complex cases

**Human moat:**

- Handle complex cases involving values, public interest, and social impact
- Explain and defend audit decisions, respond to appeals and public challenges
- Maintain consistency and fairness in policy interpretation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Government Licensing Official in Austria?**
A: Government Licensing Officials in Austria are public-sector employees. According to the collective agreement, starting salaries depend on pay grade (e.g., v1/v2) and range from approximately €2,600 to €3,200 gross per month. With experience and higher grades, salaries can reach €4,000+ gross monthly. Additional allowances may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a Government Licensing Official in Austria via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified non-EU workers, but public-sector positions often require Austrian or EU citizenship. Alternatively, the Red-White-Red Card for key workers may be possible if the employer proves no suitable EU candidate is available. A valid job offer and recognition of qualifications are mandatory.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a Government Licensing Official in Austria?**
A: Typically, a completed secondary education (Matura) and specialized training at a public administration academy (Verwaltungsakademie) are required. Alternatively, a university degree in public administration, law, or a related field is advantageous. Proficiency in German (at least B2) is essential. Career progression includes higher pay grades and supervisory roles.
