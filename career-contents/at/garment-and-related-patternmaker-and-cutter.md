**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for garment patternmakers and cutters in Austria?**
A: In Austria, salaries for garment patternmakers and cutters vary by experience and region. Entry-level positions typically earn around €1,800–€2,300 gross per month, while experienced professionals can earn €2,500–€3,500. Collective bargaining agreements in the textile industry may set minimum rates.

**Q: Can garment patternmakers and cutters obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Austria?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals may qualify for the Red-White-Red Card for skilled workers in shortage occupations, which includes garment trades like patternmaking and cutting. The EU Blue Card is also possible if the job requires a university degree or equivalent experience and meets salary thresholds. Both require a job offer and employer sponsorship.

**Q: What training is required to become a garment patternmaker or cutter in Austria?**
A: In Austria, garment patternmakers and cutters typically complete a 3-year apprenticeship in textile or clothing manufacturing, combining on-the-job training with vocational school. Alternatively, a specialized secondary school (HTL) focusing on clothing technology offers a diploma. Continuing education is available for advanced patternmaking and CAD skills.
