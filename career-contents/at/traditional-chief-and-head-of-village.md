## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for a traditional chief or head of village in Austria?**
A: In Austria, the role of a traditional chief or head of village is not a formal paid position. The closest equivalent is an elected mayor (Bürgermeister) in small municipalities. Salaries vary widely; for villages under 500 inhabitants, part-time mayors typically earn between €20,000 and €40,000 gross annually. Larger towns may offer higher compensation.

**Q: Can a foreign national work as a traditional chief or head of village in Austria under the EU Blue Card scheme?**
A: The EU Blue Card targets highly qualified non-EU workers with a university degree and a job offer meeting salary thresholds. The occupation of traditional chief is not recognized as a skilled position in Austria. Such roles are usually filled by local residents and often require Austrian citizenship. Therefore, the EU Blue Card is not a viable route for this occupation.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a head of village in Austria?**
A: In Austria, village heads are not official positions. Local governance is led by elected mayors who need strong community ties and leadership skills. No formal educational requirements exist, though many mayors have backgrounds in administration, business, or politics. Elections occur every 5–6 years. Ceremonial traditional roles have no specific qualifications and are not recognized in the formal labor market.
