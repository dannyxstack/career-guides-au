**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated telephone or online booking system for handling customer appointments

- Automated billing and payment processing based on customer historical data
- Smart mirrors or apps guide customers to self-preview hairstyles

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI hairstyle design tool recommends haircuts based on face shape and hair texture

- AI analyzes customer preferences and fashion trends to provide personalized styling advice.

**Human moat:**

- Fine tactile and manual judgment in haircutting, perming, and dyeing
- Communicate directly with clients to understand their personalized needs and aesthetic preferences
- Creative styling ability, designing novel hairstyles suitable for specific clients
- Live body language interaction to create a trusting and comfortable service atmosphere

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a hairdresser in Austria?**
A: Entry-level hairdressers earn around €1,500–€2,000 gross monthly, while experienced professionals can reach €2,500–€3,500. Many work on commission or are self-employed. Collective bargaining agreements set minimum rates, varying by region and salon type. Self-employed hairdressers have variable income based on clientele and location.

**Q: What are the work visa options for hairdressers in Austria?**
A: Non-EU hairdressers can apply for a Red-White-Red Card if they have a valid job offer and meet qualification criteria (e.g., completed apprenticeship). The EU Blue Card requires a recognized university degree, which is less common for hairdressers. A job seeker visa allows 6 months to find employment. The employer must prove labor market testing to the AMS.

**Q: What training is required to become a hairdresser in Austria?**
A: The standard path is a 3-year apprenticeship (Lehre) combining on-the-job training at a salon with classes at a vocational school. This concludes with the final apprenticeship exam (Lehrabschlussprüfung). Alternatively, students can attend a specialized vocational secondary school focusing on hairdressing. After training, registration with the Austrian Economic Chambers (WKO) is required for professional practice.
