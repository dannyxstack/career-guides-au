## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for psychologists in Austria?**
A: Entry-level psychologists earn around €2,500–€3,500 gross per month, while experienced professionals in clinical or organizational roles can earn €4,000–€5,500. Private practice incomes vary widely. Salaries are higher in Vienna and lower in rural areas. Collective bargaining agreements (Kollektivvertrag) set minimums in sectors like healthcare and social services.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for psychologists moving to Austria?**
A: Non-EU psychologists can apply for the Red-White-Red Card for highly qualified workers or skilled workers in shortage occupations. Psychology is listed as a shortage occupation in some regions. Alternatively, the EU Blue Card requires a university degree and a job offer with a salary above €3,672 gross monthly (2024). Both routes require recognition of your degree by the Austrian equivalence authority (ENIC-NARIC).

**Q: What are the main steps to becoming a licensed psychologist in Austria?**
A: You need a Master’s degree in psychology from a recognized university, followed by a supervised clinical internship (5–6 years) and a board exam for clinical practice. Alternatively, you can work in organizational or research settings without clinical licensure. Registration with the Austrian Psychological Chamber (Berufsverband Österreichischer Psycholog/inn/en) is mandatory for independent practice.
