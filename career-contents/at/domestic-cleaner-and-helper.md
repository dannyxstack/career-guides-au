**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Schedule cleaning appointments, send reminders, and collect payments via chatbot
- Use computer vision to inspect cleaning quality and generate reports
- Automated inventory management: predict cleaning supply needs based on historical data and place orders
- Voice assistants control smart home devices (e.g., robot vacuum cleaners, automatic curtains)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI voice translation tool assists in communicating with foreign employers
- Smart devices (e.g., stain detection apps) to assist in choosing the best cleaning agent
- Data analysis tools optimize cleaning routes and time allocation
- Online platform automatically matches customers and cleaners, improving customer acquisition efficiency
- Augmented reality (AR) guidance for cleaning complex furniture.

**Human moat:**

- Building long-term trust relationships and personalized services with employers
- Ability to handle unexpected situations (e.g., spills, damage) with flexibility

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for domestic cleaners and helpers in Austria?**
A: Domestic cleaners and helpers in Austria typically earn between €1,500 and €2,000 gross per month for full-time work, depending on experience and region. The collective agreement (Kollektivvertrag) for cleaning staff sets a minimum wage of roughly €1,650 gross monthly. Part-time or self-employed roles may vary.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as domestic cleaners through the EU Blue Card or other visa routes in Austria?**
A: The EU Blue Card requires a university degree and a gross annual salary of at least €45,275 (2024), which domestic cleaning jobs do not meet. Other work permits like the Red-White-Red Card are for skilled shortages, and cleaning is not listed. Work visas for domestic helpers are only possible in exceptional cases, such as key workers or family reunification.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for domestic cleaners and helpers in Austria?**
A: Career advancement is limited but possible. Experienced cleaners can become team leaders, supervisors, or start their own cleaning business. Austria offers voluntary vocational training in cleaning technology, which can lead to higher roles in facility management. However, formal qualifications are not required for entry-level positions.
