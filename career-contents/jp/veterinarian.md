# Veterinarian — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 123 (JSCO)**

Professional engaged in diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of animal diseases. Handles a wide range from small animals to livestock, and involved in public health and food safety.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Veterinary School (6-year program) | 6 years | $2,500,000~$5,000,000 (Large difference between national and private universities. Private can exceed 5 million yen annually.) |
| Passing the National Veterinary Examination | 0 years | $0~$100,000 (Exam fee approx. 10,000 yen.) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

| Veterinary Pathology Specialist | Japanese Society of Veterinary Pathology | Optional | Optional qualification to enhance expertise. |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 37,000. Demand for veterinarians is steady due to increased pet demand and food safety awareness. However, there is a supply-demand gap between urban and rural areas.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a veterinarian?**
A: New graduates earn about 3.5–4 million yen; with 10 years of experience, 7–12 million yen. Clinic owners may earn more but face business risks.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a veterinarian in Japan?**
A: A Japanese veterinarian license is required, and Japanese language ability is also important. The Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is common, but you must meet the educational requirements.

**Q: What kind of education is needed to become a veterinarian?**
A: After graduating from a 6-year veterinary program, you must pass the national exam to obtain a license. Tuition varies greatly by university.
