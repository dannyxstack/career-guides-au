# Travel and Tourism Guide — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 421 (JSCO)**

A profession that provides tourism information to domestic and international travelers, plans and arranges itineraries, and offers on-site guidance.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domestic Travel Service Manager | Japan Association of Travel Agents | Required | A mandatory national qualification in the travel industry, needed for planning and selling travel products. |
| General Travel Service Manager | Japan Association of Travel Agents | Optional | A higher qualification for handling all travel products including overseas travel. |
| Completed Itinerary Management Training | Japan Association of Travel Agents | Optional | A qualification required for tour escort duties. |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

With the spread of AI and online booking systems, information provision tasks are easily automated, but visa/data/phone and face-to-face services remain human roles.

With experience and language skills, advancement to supervisor, management, or specialized tour planner is possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard tourist information provision
- Automatic itinerary arrangement
- FAQ response chatbots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use of multilingual translation tools
- VR tourism preview creation
- Demand forecasting through data analysis

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal communication skills
- Flexible problem-solving
- Deep local knowledge and networks

**Skills to build:**

- Multilingual skills
- Digital marketing
- Crisis management skills
- Cross-cultural understanding

**Key growth areas:**

- Inbound tourism
- Sustainable tourism
- Multilingual support
- Digital transformation (DX)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a travel/tour guide?**
A: Typically JPY 2.5-3.5 million in the first year. With experience and management roles, can exceed JPY 5 million.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a travel/tour guide in Japan?**
A: The 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa is typical. Specified Skilled Worker (Accommodation) is also possible, but job duties are limited.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: Obtaining a Travel Service Manager license and strengthening language skills are effective. Being able to plan specialized tours (e.g., ecotours) is highly valued.
