# Seaweed and Shellfish Collector — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 483 (JSCO)**

A fishery worker who collects seaweed and shellfish in coastal areas for food and processing. Involves traditional manual labor and plays a role in preserving local resources.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school diploma | 3 years | $0~$300,000 (Public high school tuition, etc.) |
| Fisheries school or Fisheries high school | 3 years | $500,000~$1,500,000 (Including practical training and textbook fees) |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,500. The aging of fishery workers and lack of successors are severe, with labor shortages expected to continue. Utilizing foreign workers is being considered to sustain coastal fisheries.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Obtaining independent fishing rights requires years of experience. There is a path to join a fishing cooperative and advance to boat owner or co-owner.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing ground exploration (drones, satellite data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Prediction of optimal fishing ground timing/location (AI)
- Automated water quality monitoring

**Human moat:**

- Delicacy of manual work
- Local knowledge and experience
- Weather judgment

**Skills to build:**

- aquaculture technology
- Data analysis (fishery management)
- Marketing (sixth industrialization)

**Key growth areas:**

- aquaculture technology
- sixth industrialization
- algal biomass
- measures against seaweed bed decline

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People confident in physical strength
- Those who love the sea and nature
- People who can handle irregular work hours

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike physical labor
- Those seeking stable income

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Is income from seaweed/shellfish harvesting stable?**
A: Unstable due to catch volume. Annual income around 2.5-6.5 million yen, varies by region and experience.

**Q: Can foreigners do this job?**
A: Possible with Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Fisheries) status. Requires Japanese proficiency (approx. N4) and passing skills test.

**Q: How to advance your career?**
A: Join a fishery cooperative, gain experience, then obtain fishing rights independently or move into aquaculture, processing, etc. (6th industrialization).
