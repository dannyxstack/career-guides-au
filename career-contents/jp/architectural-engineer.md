# Architectural Engineer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 091 (JSCO)**

Professional handling building design, construction management, and regulatory compliance. The architect license is central, supporting safety and quality in the construction industry.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| University (Department of Architecture) | 4 years | $2,500,000~$5,000,000 (Approx. 540,000 JPY/year at national universities, 1.5 million JPY at private. Total for 4 years.) |
| Vocational School (Department of Architecture) | 2 years | $1,200,000~$2,500,000 (Total including entrance fee.) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-Class Architect | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism | Optional | Can design and supervise construction of high-rise buildings and large-scale structures. Requires 4+ years of practical experience. |
| Second-Class Architect | Prefectural Governor | Optional | Can design and supervise construction of small to medium-sized buildings. Requires 2+ years of practical experience. |
| Construction Management Engineer | Construction Industry Promotion Fund | Optional | National qualification for construction management. Has Grade 1 and Grade 2. |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 340,000. Demand declined slightly after the Tokyo Olympics, but stable long-term demand continues due to aging infrastructure, disaster prevention renovations, and urban redevelopment. Chronic labor shortage.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

After gaining practical experience, obtaining a First-Class Architect license allows promotion to design office manager or general contractor construction manager. Women's participation is increasing.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Partial automation of structural calculations
- Simple code compliance checks
- Standard drawing creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- 3D design and simulation with BIM
- AI-based energy performance prediction
- Construction management linked with construction robots

**Human moat:**

- On-site flexible judgment
- Client negotiation and consensus building
- Design creativity and aesthetics
- Code interpretation and permitting procedures

**Skills to build:**

- BIM/CIM operation skills
- Programming (Python, Dynamo, etc.)
- Knowledge of energy-efficient building design
- Project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- BIM/CIM
- Energy-efficient buildings
- Wooden architecture
- Renovation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of an architectural engineer?**
A: Entry-level: approx. 3.2–4 million JPY, mid-level: 4.5–6 million JPY, senior (first-class architect): 7–10+ million JPY. These figures include overtime pay.

**Q: Is it easy for foreigners to obtain a work visa as an architectural engineer?**
A: Possible with a 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa, but requires a university degree or 10+ years of experience. The Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Construction) visa is also an option, but requires passing a skills test. The path to permanent residency is somewhat long.

**Q: How many years does it take to obtain a first-class architect license?**
A: After graduating from a university architecture department, at least 4 years of practical experience required. Eligible to take the exam at the earliest 4 years after graduation. Difficulty is high, pass rate about 15%.
