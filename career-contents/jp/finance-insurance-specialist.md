# Finance & Insurance Specialist — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 184 (JSCO)**

A specialized profession in financial institutions such as banks, securities firms, and insurance companies, handling asset management, risk management, and financial advice.

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales Representative Qualification | Japan Securities Dealers Association | Required | Mandatory for working at securities firms |
| AFP (Certified by Japan Association for Financial Planners) | Japan Association for Financial Planners | Optional | Equivalent to 2nd grade FP Technician |
| CFP (International Certification) | Japan Association for Financial Planners | Optional | After AFP, practical experience and exam |
| Securities Analyst | Japan Securities Analysts Association | Optional | CMA certification |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Certifications (AFP, CFP, Securities Analyst) are key to career advancement. After mid-career, paths open to management or specialist roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple data entry and document creation
- Routine risk assessment report creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Building advanced risk analysis models
- Asset management simulations for individual clients

**Human moat:**

- Building interpersonal trust
- Complex financial advice and ethical judgment

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and AI literacy
- Advanced financial planning knowledge
- Customer communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Fintech
- Asset management advice
- Risk management
- Sustainable finance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Numerically strong and analytical
- Customer-oriented with strong communication skills
- People who are active in acquiring qualifications

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer simple tasks
- People who dislike risk and cannot adapt to change

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for finance/insurance professionals?**
A: For large companies, the starting salary for university graduates is about ¥220,000–250,000 per month, which translates to an annual income of approximately ¥3.5–4.5 million (including bonuses).

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work as finance/insurance professionals in Japan?**
A: The Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is common. Advanced specialized knowledge or a degree is required.

**Q: What qualifications are useful for career advancement?**
A: AFP, CFP, and Certified Securities Analyst (CMA) are valued. CFP is particularly internationally recognized.
