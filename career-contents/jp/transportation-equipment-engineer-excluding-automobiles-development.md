# Transportation Equipment Engineer (excluding automobiles) (Development) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 075 (JSCO)**

Engineers engaged in the design and development of transportation equipment such as ships, aircraft, and railway vehicles. They leverage the latest technology to improve safety and performance.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

After gaining research and development experience at major manufacturers or graduate schools, they advance to project leader or management roles in research departments. Independent work leveraging expertise is also possible.

- Performance simulation

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Average annual income of transport equipment engineers?**
A: Around 10 years experience, approx. 8 million yen. Major aircraft manufacturers may exceed 10 million.

**Q: How can foreigners work in Japan in this occupation?**
A: Generally, a 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa is required. Conditions include university graduation or over 10 years of practical experience.

**Q: What are the ways to advance your career?**
A: Obtaining a Professional Engineer license or a master's/doctoral degree from graduate school can position you at the core of R&D.
