# Deposit/Counter Clerk — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 262 (JSCO)**

Responsible for counter operations at banks and credit unions, including accepting deposits, processing deposits/withdrawals, transfers, and passbook entries.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 180,000. Demand for counter clerks is declining long-term due to fewer branches and the spread of ATMs/internet banking, though face-to-face services for elderly customers sustain some demand.

**Competition: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Obtaining qualifications and career advancement can lead to roles in exchange/loan counters or sales positions. Within banks, switching from general to comprehensive positions is possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Deposit/withdrawal processing
- Transfer/remittance processing
- Passbook entry
- Balance inquiry response
- Simple address change procedures

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Customer needs analysis (AI-driven proposals)
- Identity verification and screening for high-value transactions
- Support for complex inheritance procedures
- In-person consultation (asset management, housing loans)

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal Skills (Trust Building)
- Complex Financial Knowledge (Taxation, Inheritance)
- Human Touch in Elderly Care
- Regulatory Compliance & Judgment

**Skills to build:**

- Knowledge of Financial Products (Investment Trusts, Insurance)
- Financial Planning
- Digital Tool Proficiency
- Foreign Languages (for International Visitors)

**Key growth areas:**

- Decreasing trend
- Labor saving
- Elderly customer service
- Retail banking

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for a deposit/savings teller?**
A: About 2.8–3.5 million yen for entry-level, 3.5–4.5 million for mid-level, and 4–5.5 million for veterans. The average salary for bank employees varies by institution but generally ranges from 4–6 million yen.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a teller?**
A: An Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is required. Japanese N2 or above, financial knowledge or work experience are needed. Also, banks hiring foreigners is limited, mostly after studying abroad or for permanent residents.

**Q: Are there career advancement opportunities?**
A: By obtaining banking business certifications or FP qualifications, one can advance to loan counters or external sales. Some banks offer conversion exams for general positions.
