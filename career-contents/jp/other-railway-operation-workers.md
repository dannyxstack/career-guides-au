# Other Railway Operation Workers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 609 (JSCO)**

Drivers of streetcars, freight railways, monorails, etc. Primarily transport passengers and cargo safely on non-JR lines and local routes.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school diploma | 3 years | $0~$3,000,000 (Public high school tuition is free) |
| In-house training by railway companies | 1–2 years | $0~$500,000 (Training provided at company expense after hiring) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Locomotive Engineer's License (Class A) | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism | Required | Required for railway operation |
| Class 2 Driver's License (Large) | Public Safety Commission | Optional | May be required for driving large vehicles |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 8,000. Employment is expected to decline gradually due to the abolition of local lines and the introduction of automated driving technology. However, manual operation is still needed on routes where automation is difficult.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

---

## 4. Salary range

| Experience | Annual salary (JPY) | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| Entry level (0–3 years) | $3,500,000~$4,500,000 | Base salary + various allowances (night shift, holidays, etc.) |
| Mid-level (4–10 years) | $4,500,000~$6,000,000 | Salary increase based on driving experience |
| Veteran (10+ years) | $6,000,000~$8,000,000 | Includes instructor or managerial positions |

---

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Internal promotion from conductor or station staff to driver is common. With experience, advancement to instructor driver or management is possible. Large companies offer generous qualification allowances.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Constant Speed Driving
- Simple Acceleration/Deceleration Operations
- Schedule-compliant Running

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Coordination with Obstacle Detection Systems
- Operation Management Data Analysis
- Anomaly Judgment

**Human moat:**

- Ability to Handle Unexpected Events
- Passenger Safety Assurance and Evacuation Guidance
- Route-specific Driving Techniques

**Skills to build:**

- AI Monitoring System Operation
- Operation Data Analysis
- Emergency Response Training

**Key growth areas:**

- Local railways
- Freight transport
- Safety Technology
- Automated Driving Compatibility

---

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who can strictly follow rules
- People with sustained concentration
- People who can handle irregular work hours

**Who is not suited**
- People who struggle with irregular schedules
- People who find night/early morning shifts difficult

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for this occupation?**
A: For beginners: 3.5-4.5 million yen, intermediate: 4.5-6 million yen, veteran: 6-8 million yen.

**Q: Can foreigners work in this occupation?**
A: Currently, train driving is not covered by Specified Skilled Worker status, and work visa requirements are strict, making it difficult as an immigration route.

**Q: Is a career change from this occupation possible?**
A: Utilizing driving skills and safety knowledge, transitioning to railway maintenance/management or operations manager roles is possible.
