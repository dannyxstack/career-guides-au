# Shipboard and Shore Stevedore — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 702 (JSCO)**

Workers who load/unload cargo onto ships and handle coastal stevedoring at ports. They operate cranes and forklifts and perform manual cargo handling.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High School Graduate | 3 Years | $0~$0 (Standard path after compulsory education) |
| Vocational School (Port Logistics) | 2 Years | $1,000,000~$2,000,000 (In case of private vocational schools) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forklift Operation Skills Training | Prefectural Labor Bureau | Optional | Not mandatory but preferred |
| Mobile Crane Operator | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Optional | Required for large crane operation |
| Slinging Skills Training | Prefectural Labor Bureau | Optional | Required for hoisting heavy loads |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 42,000. Due to increased port cargo volume and labor shortages, stable demand is expected in the medium term. Automation impact is limited, but compliance with stricter safety regulations is necessary.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income for stevedoring?**
A: Starting at ¥3-4 million annually. Over ¥5 million possible with experience and qualifications. Varies by port.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work?**
A: The main path is Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Port Transport). Requires skills test (port cargo handling) and Japanese N4 level.

**Q: Can I start with no experience?**
A: Yes. Many workplaces offer on-the-job training. Support for acquiring forklift licenses etc. is also available.
