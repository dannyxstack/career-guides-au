# Transport Machinery Inspector (excluding automobiles) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 584 (JSCO)**

A specialist who inspects the quality and safety of transport machinery such as railway vehicles, ships, and aircraft. Inspects dimensions, functions, and appearance of finished products and parts to ensure compliance with standards.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

At major manufacturers and railway companies, advancement is based on skill certification and in-house qualifications. Career paths include lead inspector or quality control manager, requiring extensive experience and credentials. At SMEs, the focus is on specialist roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Visual appearance inspection
- Simple dimensional measurement
- Comparison and judgment against standard values

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Image analysis for non-destructive testing (ultrasonic, X-ray)
- Statistical analysis of inspection data
- Optimization of inspection processes

**Human moat:**

- Final product overall judgment
- Root cause investigation and judgment in case of abnormalities
- Coordination with customer requirements

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced non-destructive testing certifications
- Quality control methods (e.g., QC 7 tools)
- Statistical analysis and data science

**Key growth areas:**

- Railway vehicle inspection
- Aircraft maintenance inspection
- Non-destructive testing
- Quality control DX

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for transport machinery inspection workers?**
A: The average annual income in Japan's manufacturing industry is about 4.3 million yen, but for transport machinery inspection workers, it ranges from 3 to 7 million yen depending on experience and qualifications. At major railway and airline companies, it can exceed 5 million yen.

**Q: Are there visas for foreigners to work in this occupation?**
A: The 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa is available, but requires a university degree or at least 10 years of relevant work experience. It is not covered by the Specified Skilled Worker system, and the Technical Skills visa requires advanced proficiency, making the immigration route restrictive.

**Q: Can I enter this field without experience?**
A: Possible even with just a high school diploma, but vocational school training or practical experience in mechanical fields is advantageous. At large companies, having relevant certifications at the time of hiring may be required.
