# Telecommunications equipment installer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 674 (JSCO)**

A construction engineer who lays and connects communication cables and optical fibers, and installs and maintains communication equipment such as base stations and exchanges.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school diploma | 3 years | $0~$0 (Public high school tuition is mostly free) |
| Vocational school (Telecommunications) | 2 years | $1,500,000~$2,500,000 (Approximate annual tuition including materials) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrician (Class I & II) | Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry | Required | Class II for low-voltage, Class I for high-voltage work. Essentially mandatory. |
| Telecommunications Engineer | Japan Data Communications Association | Optional | Required for transmission line work. Comprehensive communication is standard. |
| Chief Telecommunications Engineer | Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications | Optional | Advantageous for large-scale equipment design and management. For career advancement. |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 265,000. Demand is stable due to 5G expansion and optical fiber growth, but high turnover among young workers leads to chronic labor shortages.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After joining, start with on-site work, gain experience to become team leader or site supervisor. Possible to advance to management or start your own business.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automation of simple piercing tasks and repetitive wiring
- Manpower reduction through AI-optimized route searching

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-based automatic routing design
- Work instruction display via AR glasses
- Drone-assisted inspection support

**Human moat:**

- Flexible cable handling in high places or confined spaces
- On-site situation assessment and adjustments
- Fault diagnosis of electrical/communication circuits

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI construction management tools
- Improvement of optical fiber fusion splicing technology
- Basic knowledge of electrical circuits
- Leadership and safety management

**Key growth areas:**

- 5G/optical lines
- Base station expansion
- FTTH
- Existing facility upgrades

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy physical work
- People with dexterity and no fear of heights
- People interested in electricity/telecommunications

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer desk work only
- People uncomfortable with danger/heights

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for telecommunications equipment construction workers?**
A: Around 4-5 million JPY with about 5 years of experience. Possible 6+ million with qualifications or management roles.

**Q: Can foreigners obtain a Japanese visa for this occupation?**
A: It is expected to become a target field for Specified Skilled Worker (i) from 2025. A transition route from the Technical Intern Training Program is also being considered.

**Q: How can I advance my career in this occupation?**
A: It is advisable to obtain qualifications such as electrician or telecommunications engineer and aim for roles like site leader or chief engineer.
