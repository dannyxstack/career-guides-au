# Other Fishery Workers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 489 (JSCO)**

Engaged in fishing but performing tasks not classified under specific subcategories such as fishing, aquaculture, or net making.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 12,000. The number of fishery workers is declining, but there is steady demand due to aging. Shortage of workers in coastal fisheries is a challenge.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

The path to becoming a skilled fisherman is long, taking over 10 years to independence. Experience at fisheries cooperatives or companies, plus boat licenses and fishing rights, are needed.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fish finding
- Voyage planning optimization

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Catch data analysis
- Determining net placement locations

**Human moat:**

- Manual adjustment and repair of fishing gear
- Experiential judgment of weather and sea conditions
- Fish species identification and quality assessment

**Skills to build:**

- Marine data analysis
- Fisheries ICT equipment operation
- Aquaculture technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Fisheries resource management
- Aquaculture technology
- Fisheries successors
- Smart fisheries

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those confident in physical strength
- Those who love nature and are patient
- Those who don't mind early morning/night shifts

**Who is not suited**
- Those who desire a regular lifestyle
- Those who prefer land-based work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of fishery workers?**
A: 2.5-3.5M JPY in first year, over 5M possible with experience. Varies greatly with catch.

**Q: How can foreigners work in fishery?**
A: The main routes are Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Fisheries) or Technical Intern Training. JLPT N4 level required.

**Q: How to become independent in fisheries?**
A: A boat license, fishing rights acquisition, and fishing boat purchase funds are needed. Joining a fisheries cooperative is often required.
