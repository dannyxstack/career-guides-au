# Other Teachers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 199 (JSCO)**

Professions that provide instruction in diverse educational settings such as various schools, cram schools, and corporate education, excluding teachers defined under the School Education Law.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career paths are diverse; with experience, one can advance to positions such as school director, curriculum developer, or independent business owner.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardized teaching material creation
- Automated scoring and test creation
- Basic repetitive exercise instruction

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Assisting in individual learning plan development
- Teaching improvement through learning data analysis
- Online class operation support

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal communication
- Empathy
- Student motivation management
- Flexible instruction based on situation

**Skills to build:**

- Educational technology application
- Data literacy
- Coaching skills
- Cross-cultural Understanding

**Key growth areas:**

- Online education
- Adult education
- Career support
- Language education

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Passionate about teaching children or adults
- Strong communication skills and ability to explain clearly
- Flexible and able to adapt to diverse learners

**Who is not suited**
- Prefers routine
- Dislikes public speaking

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of other teachers?**
A: Salary varies greatly by experience and employment type, but for regular employees it is typically around 3.5 to 5 million yen annually; for part-time, around 2.5 to 3.5 million yen.

**Q: Can foreigners work as other teachers in Japan?**
A: Yes, but a residence status such as 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' or 'Professor' is required. It is relatively easier for language teachers, but expertise and Japanese proficiency are needed.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: It is effective to obtain certifications to enhance expertise (e.g., career consultant) or develop management skills to aim for positions like school director or curriculum developer.
