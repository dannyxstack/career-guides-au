# Captain, Navigator, Chief Engineer, Engineer (Fishing Vessel) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 482 (JSCO)**

Marine engineer responsible for commanding fishing operations, navigation planning, and engine management as captain, navigator, chief engineer, or engineer on a fishing vessel.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 12,000. The fishery industry faces a serious labor shortage, especially with the ongoing decline in young fishers. While automation and labor-saving technologies are advancing, demand remains strong for experienced captains and engineers.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a fishing vessel captain?**
A: Depending on experience and catch, a mid-size fishing vessel captain earns about 7-12 million yen annually. Years with good catches yield even more.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to become a captain of a Japanese fishing vessel?**
A: Currently, captains and chief engineers are not eligible for technical intern training or Specified Skilled Worker visas. They also have low applicability for highly skilled professional or Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visas, making it extremely difficult for foreigners to work in these roles. Training as fishery crew or Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Fisheries) is possible, but they cannot hold senior positions.

**Q: Please tell me the path to career advancement.**
A: Navigator (3rd Class → 2nd Class → 1st Class) → Captain, or Engineer (3rd Class → 2nd Class → 1st Class) → Chief Engineer. There is also a path to Fishing Master, Fleet Commander, or management positions in fishing companies on land.
