# Other Motor Vehicle Drivers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 619 (JSCO)**

Occupations driving vehicles other than passenger cars, such as taxis, hired cars, dump trucks, and trailers. Workers supporting logistics and passenger transport on the front lines.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large Vehicle License | Public Safety Commission (Driver's License Examination Center) | Required | Required for large truck operation |
| Trailer License | Public Safety Commission (Driver's License Examination Center) | Required | Required for trailer operation |
| Class 2 License | Public Safety Commission (Driver's License Examination Center) | Optional | Required for taxi/hired car operation |
| Dangerous Goods Handler Certificate | Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry | Optional | Valid for transporting dangerous goods |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 350,000. The logistics industry faces a serious labor shortage, especially for truck drivers, where demand is high. Many retire due to aging, and new young entrants are needed, but improving working conditions remains a challenge.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Most gain experience on the job after hiring, acquire large or trailer licenses, and may advance to management or self-employment. Female drivers are increasingly active in recent years.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Constant-speed driving on highways
- Monotonous long-distance delivery
- Automated parts of parking and stopping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-based optimal route suggestions
- Utilization of safety driving support systems
- Integration of operation management and AI

**Human moat:**

- Risk prediction and situational judgment
- Emergency response
- Customer service and communication (taxis, etc.)

**Skills to build:**

- Understanding and monitoring AI/autonomous driving systems
- Operation management skills
- Health management and safe driving techniques

**Key growth areas:**

- Logistics 2024 Problem
- Last-mile delivery
- Autonomous driving technology
- Driver shortage

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Someone who doesn't mind long-distance driving
- Someone confident in focus and stamina
- Someone flexible with routes and schedules

**Who is not suited**
- Someone who struggles with long hours of sitting
- Someone weak to sudden schedule changes

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a driver?**
A: In the first year, it is around 3-4 million yen, and with experience, it can reach 5-7 million yen or more. However, it heavily depends on overtime pay, so working hours tend to be long.

**Q: Can foreign nationals work as drivers?**
A: Currently, the target of Specified Skilled Worker (i) is scheduled to be expanded, and it is expected to become possible in the future. However, obtaining a Japanese driver's license and Japanese language proficiency are mandatory conditions.

**Q: Are there paths to independence or career advancement?**
A: There are methods such as contracting with a transportation company as an individual business owner, or aiming for a managerial position as a transport manager. Also, by obtaining a large vehicle license or trailer license, you can take on higher-paying jobs.
