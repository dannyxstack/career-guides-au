# Temporary Item Custodian — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 422 (JSCO)**

Workers who temporarily store and manage customer luggage at coin lockers or baggage storage in stations and commercial facilities.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 48,000. Demand is stable due to recovery in tourism, but employment is expected to remain flat or slightly decline due to trends toward labor saving and automation.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

With the introduction of coin lockers and automatic check-in machines, human tasks are declining. Many tasks are being replaced by AI and automation.

In large facilities, mainly part-time or temporary workers. Accumulating experience can lead to managerial or leadership roles, but career scope is narrow.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Baggage storage and handover
- Simple inquiries
- Fee calculation and payment processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Multilingual customer service
- Customer complaint handling
- Security management

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal service
- Flexible response
- Complaint handling

**Skills to build:**

- Foreign languages
- IT literacy
- Problem-solving skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Tourism
- Cashless
- Multilingual support
- Automation

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who like interacting with others
- People who are meticulous and good at organizing
- People who are confident in their physical strength

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike repetitive tasks
- People who are not good at standing work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a temporary storage worker?**
A: Hourly wage conversion yields annual income around 2.5 to 3.5 million JPY. Many are part-time, few are full-time.

**Q: How can foreigners do this job?**
A: Currently not covered by Specified Skills. May be possible as a student part-time or under other statuses.

**Q: How to advance your career?**
A: You can also polish your customer service skills and transition to the travel or hospitality industry.
