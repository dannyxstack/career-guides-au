# Welfare Facility Guidance Specialist — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 162 (JSCO)**

A professional in social welfare facilities who develops care plans for users, instructs staff, and manages operations. Responsible for improving service quality and human resource development.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 85,000. Demand for welfare facilities continues to rise due to aging population. However, from 2025 onward, the shortage of care workers will intensify, making the specialist role even more critical. Working conditions are also improving.

**Competition: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

AI and RPA support data analysis and record-keeping for care plan creation, but human interaction and team guidance remain central. AI improves efficiency, making the specialist's role even more important.

Career starts as a care worker or life counselor. After gaining practical experience, advance to chief or manager, then to welfare facility guidance specialist. Further goals include facility director or community-based integrated care leader.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Creating record documents
- Scheduling adjustments
- Drafting simple care plans

**Where AI augments the role:**

- User assessment
- Team management
- Individual care plan adjustments

**Human moat:**

- Empathy
- Interpersonal negotiation skills
- Ethical judgment

**Skills to build:**

- Data Analysis
- ICT Utilization
- Management Skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Shortage of care workers
- Community-based integrated care
- Quality improvement of services

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who find fulfillment in working with the elderly or disabled
- People with leadership skills to manage a team
- People with a strong interest in improving welfare services

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer routine work
- People with low stress tolerance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a welfare facility guidance specialist?**
A: The average annual income is about 4.2 million yen, but ranges from 2.8 million to 6.5 million yen depending on experience and position.

**Q: What visa is needed for foreigners to work as welfare facility guidance specialists in Japan?**
A: Generally, an 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa is required. However, a national social worker certification and practical experience are needed, making the bar high.

**Q: Can I become one without experience?**
A: A social worker certification and practical experience are required. It's common to first gain experience as a care worker or life counselor.
