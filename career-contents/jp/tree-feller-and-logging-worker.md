# Tree Feller and Logging Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 472 (JSCO)**

Fell standing trees using chainsaws and heavy machinery in forests, cut them to appropriate lengths, and collect/transport them. Performs on-site logging operations.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| No special educational background required (on-the-job training) | 1 year | $0~$100,000 (Some national and prefectural training programs are free) |
| Forestry Engineer Training Course | 6 months | $200,000~$500,000 (Private training also available) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chainsaw Special Education | Prefectural Labor Bureau | Required | Mandatory for chainsaw work |
| Special Education for Felling Operations | Prefectural Labor Bureau | Required | Mandatory for felling work |
| Forestry Cableway Work Chief | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Optional | Required when using cableways for logging |
| Vehicle-Type Construction Machinery Operation Skills Training | Prefectural Labor Bureau | Optional | For forestry machinery operators |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 45,000. Aging workforce and labor shortages in forestry are severe, with young entrants expected. Mechanization is advancing, but fieldwork remains labor-intensive.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression from worker to team leader and site supervisor. Can aim for skills as high-performance forestry machinery operators or managerial roles in forest cooperatives.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple felling work
- Part of mechanical log skidding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drone measurement of terrain and trees
- AI work plan optimization
- Sensor utilization for safety management

**Human moat:**

- Judgment in dangerous sites
- Machine operation in complex terrain
- Teamwork and safety culture

**Skills to build:**

- High-performance forestry machinery operation
- GIS and drone utilization
- Safety management
- Basics of forest ecology

**Key growth areas:**

- Forestry
- Chainsaw
- High-performance forestry machinery
- Forest maintenance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income for logging, timber processing, and skidding workers?**
A: Entry-level: 3–4 million JPY, intermediate: 4–5 million JPY, advanced: 5–6.5 million JPY. Varies by region and forest cooperative.

**Q: Can foreigners work in this occupation?**
A: It is eligible under Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Forestry) or engineering/skilled visa. Requires Japanese language and skill tests, but possible.

**Q: What is the future outlook for this job?**
A: Forestry workers are decreasing, but demand remains stable due to labor shortages. Mechanization is advancing, requiring adaptation as machine operators.
