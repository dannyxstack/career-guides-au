# Physician — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 121 (JSCO)**

Medical professional who examines, treats, and performs surgery on patients, contributing to health recovery and maintenance. Requires advanced knowledge and skills.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Medical school (6-year program) | 6 years | $3,500,000~$6,000,000 (National university annual tuition approx. 530,000 yen; private averages over 3 million yen.) |
| Initial clinical training | 2 years | $0~$0 (Trained as a resident while receiving a salary.) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical license | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Required | Obtained upon passing the National Medical Licensing Examination; required for all physicians. |
| Primary board certification | Each board (e.g., Japanese Society of Internal Medicine) | Optional | Certifies specialty based on board system; de facto mandatory. |
| Subspecialty board certification | Each related society | Optional | Recommended for physicians seeking advanced expertise. |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 330,000. With the aging population and declining birthrate, medical demand is increasing. Particularly in rural areas and specific fields like emergency, obstetrics, and pediatrics, severe staff shortages are expected to continue.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After obtaining a medical license, complete initial clinical training (2 years) followed by specialized training (3–5 years). After obtaining board certification, common career paths include hospital employment, private practice, or a university hospital position.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple image diagnosis (e.g., detecting abnormalities in chest X-rays)
- Primary screening of pathology slides
- Automation of routine medical interviews

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Improved accuracy in image diagnosis
- Navigation support during surgery
- Extraction and summarization of information from electronic medical records

**Human moat:**

- Building trust with patients
- Integrated judgment of complex pathologies
- Ethical judgment and empathy
- Proficiency in procedures

**Skills to build:**

- Data literacy
- Ability to utilize AI tools
- Interpersonal communication
- Leadership in team-based medical care

**Key growth areas:**

- Home medical care
- Emergency medicine
- Rural medicine
- Palliative care

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a doctor?**
A: The average annual income of employed doctors is about 12-15 million yen, but varies greatly by specialty, region, and age. Private practitioners tend to have higher incomes.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a doctor in Japan?**
A: You must pass the Japanese national medical exam and obtain a medical license. Then acquire a residence status such as 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' and work at a medical institution. Japanese language ability (N1 level) is virtually mandatory.

**Q: What is the shortest route to becoming a doctor?**
A: After graduating from high school, entered a six-year medical school and passed the national medical exam. After two years of initial clinical training, we can practice medicine independently as a doctor.
