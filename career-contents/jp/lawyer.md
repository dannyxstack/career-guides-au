# Lawyer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 173 (JSCO)**

Japanese lawyers are highly specialized professionals handling a wide range of legal work including court representation, legal consultation, and contract drafting. As the core of the legal profession, they support the judicial system.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After passing the bar exam, one completes judicial training (1 year) and registers as a lawyer. Subsequently, they gain experience at law firms or corporate legal departments, advancing to partner or independent practice. Establishing a specialized field is key to career growth.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a lawyer?**
A: First year: 5-8 million yen; mid-career: 10-15 million yen; partner or independent: over 20 million yen. Varies by firm size and specialty.

**Q: How can a foreigner become a lawyer in Japan?**
A: Must pass the Japanese bar exam and receive approval from the Ministry of Justice. However, as a foreign law attorney, there is also a visa (Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services) limited to handling home country law.

**Q: What is the career path for a lawyer?**
A: Various environments including corporate legal departments, law firms, and government offices. Independent practice is also common, and specializing broadens career options.
