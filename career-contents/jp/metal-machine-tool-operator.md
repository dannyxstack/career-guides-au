# Metal Machine Tool Operator — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 523 (JSCO)**

A specialist who operates machine tools such as lathes, milling machines, and grinders to cut and process metal parts. Skilled craftsmanship supporting the manufacturing industry base.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career advancement possible through skills certification and multi-skilling. Path from NC/MC operator to programming and production management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple manual lathe work
- Repetitive mass production processes
- Automation-compatible tool change processes

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Creating machining programs via CAM
- On-machine measurement data analysis
- AI-based tool wear prediction

**Human moat:**

- Processing complex one-off shapes
- Changeover decision-making
- Troubleshooting skills

**Skills to build:**

- NC Programming
- CAD/CAM Operations
- Robot Teaching
- Multi-process Mastery

**Key growth areas:**

- Automotive parts processing
- Aircraft parts processing
- Semiconductor manufacturing equipment parts
- Die and mold manufacturing

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of metal machine tool operators?**
A: For beginners, annual income is about 3-4 million yen, for intermediate 4-5.5 million yen, and for skilled workers 5-7 million yen. It can increase further with skill certification and multi-skilling.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work in this occupation?**
A: Specified Skilled Worker (i)/(ii) (molding/industrial machinery, etc.) or Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (Engineering) applies. Japanese language ability and skill tests are required.

**Q: What are the career advancement paths?**
A: After obtaining grade 1 or 2 of the skill test, there are paths to become an NC programmer or production manager. Acquiring multi-skills like welding and assembly is advantageous.
