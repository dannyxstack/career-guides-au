# Transportation machinery (excluding automobiles) maintenance and repair workers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 554 (JSCO)**

A profession involving inspection, maintenance, and repair of transportation machinery such as aircraft, railway vehicles, and ships. Requires advanced specialized knowledge and skills.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Vocational school (Maintenance department) | 2 years | $2,000,000~$3,000,000 (Aviation maintenance departments, etc., can be relatively expensive.) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aircraft mechanic (First/Second Class) | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism | Required | Required for aircraft maintenance. First class allows final confirmation after maintenance. |
| Gas welding skill training completion | Prefectural Labour Bureau | Required | Required for welding work. |
| Railway vehicle maintenance technician | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Optional | National qualification for railway maintenance. Has Grade 1 and 2. |
| Ship mechanic (Grade 1 and 2) | Japan Ship Maintenance Association | Optional | Qualification for ship repair and maintenance. Voluntary but recommended. |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 180,000. Demand for railways and aviation is steady, and labor shortages persist due to the aging of skilled workers. Maintenance and inspection demand in particular is stable, so employment prospects are good.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Starting as an apprentice, one can advance to intermediate level by passing the skill test. Further promotion to senior skilled worker or managerial positions is possible. Aircraft mechanics, etc., require qualification acquisition.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Partial automation of periodic inspections
- AI-based initial fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-powered anomaly detection systems
- Drone-based aircraft inspection
- AR-guided maintenance procedures
- Big data-driven failure prediction

**Human moat:**

- Identifying causes of complex malfunctions
- Working in confined spaces
- Judgment based on intuition and experience

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool operation
- Data analysis
- Electrical/electronic engineering knowledge
- English (aircraft maintenance manuals)

**Key growth areas:**

- Aircraft maintenance
- Railway vehicle maintenance
- Ship repair
- Shinkansen maintenance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a mechanic?**
A: Approximately ¥200,000-250,000/month for vocational school graduates, with annual income around ¥3.5 million. Major airlines tend to offer higher starting salaries.

**Q: Can foreigners become aircraft mechanics in Japan?**
A: Yes, under Specified Skilled Worker (i). Requires JLPT N4 and passing a skills test. Aircraft mechanic certification requires national exams, so Japanese proficiency is key.

**Q: What qualifications should I get for career advancement?**
A: Aircraft Maintenance Engineer (Class 1) or Railway Vehicle Maintenance Technician (Grade 1) are advantageous. For management, gain experience and advanced certifications.
