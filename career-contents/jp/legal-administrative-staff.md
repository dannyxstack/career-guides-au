# Legal Administrative Staff — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 179 (JSCO)**

Assist lawyers and judicial scriveners with document preparation and legal research; work in law firms or corporate legal departments.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

| Judicial Scrivener (Shihoshoshi) | Ministry of Justice | Optional | Enables advanced legal work as a legal professional |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

AI and automation are advancing in document creation and contract review, but tasks requiring human expertise such as legal judgment and client interaction will remain.

Career advancement possible through experience and obtaining qualifications like Administrative or Judicial Scrivener. Transition to in-house legal departments is common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Document template creation
- Primary screening of patent searches
- Checking contracts for typos and errors

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drafting legal documents
- Case law search and analysis
- Compliance risk assessment

**Human moat:**

- Applied legal interpretation
- Understanding client needs
- Ethical judgment
- Negotiation and coordination skills

**Skills to build:**

- AI and legal tech operation
- English contract skills
- Project management
- Risk awareness

**Key growth areas:**

- Corporate Legal Affairs
- Compliance
- Intellectual Property
- M&A Support

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People skilled in detailed paperwork
- People who like researching laws and regulations
- People who don't mind painstaking investigations

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer creative work
- People not good at direct negotiation with others

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for this occupation?**
A: About ¥4.5 million with 5 years of experience, and over ¥6 million after obtaining qualifications.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work in Japan?**
A: The 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa is common. Requires graduation from a Japanese university or a combination of work experience and academic background.

**Q: What qualifications should be obtained for career advancement?**
A: Administrative scriveners and judicial scriveners are typical. In corporate legal affairs, compliance certifications are also useful.
