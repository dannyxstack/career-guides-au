# Telecommunications Equipment Operator — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 246 (JSCO)**

Engineer who operates and monitors telephone exchanges and wireless communication equipment to ensure stable operation of communication networks.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who can focus on machine operation and monitoring
- Meticulous people who can faithfully follow procedures
- People available for night shifts and rotating shifts

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike monotonous routine work
- People who prefer jobs centered on interpersonal communication

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much is the annual income for telecommunications equipment operators?**
A: For entry-level, around ¥3.2-4 million; mid-level, ¥4.5-6 million; advanced, ¥6.5-8 million. Larger telecom companies tend to offer higher salaries.

**Q: What visa is needed for foreigners to work as telecommunications equipment operators in Japan?**
A: The Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services or Specified Skilled Worker (i/ii) visas are common. Requirements include academic background, work experience, and Japanese language ability.

**Q: What is the career path?**
A: From field operations, you can advance to network engineer or monitoring center manager. Obtaining certifications (e.g., Chief Telecommunications Engineer) helps with promotion.
