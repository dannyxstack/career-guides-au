# Care Worker (Medical/Welfare Facilities) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 361 (JSCO)**

Professionals who support daily living of the elderly and disabled in medical and long-term care facilities, including assistance with meals, bathing, and excretion.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High School (Caregiving Course) | 3 years | $0~$500,000 (Tuition is effectively free at public high schools. Separate costs for materials, etc.) |
| Certified Care Worker Training School (Vocational School/Junior College) | 2 years | $2,000,000~$4,000,000 (Approximate tuition fees. Scholarship programs available.) |
| Practical Training for Unskilled Workers | 6 months | $100,000~$300,000 (Correspondence courses available. Equivalent to Initial Training for Care Workers upon completion.) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practical Training Completion | Designated by Prefectural Governor | Required | Required for home-visit care and certain caregiving tasks. |
| Certified Care Worker | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Optional | National qualification. Most common certification in caregiving. |
| Initial Training for Care Workers Completion | Designated by Prefectural Governor | Optional | Entry-level qualification easily obtained by beginners. |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After obtaining Certified Care Worker qualification, career advancement to care manager or facility director is possible. Skill improvement via practical training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Record-keeping (automatic input of care records)
- Monitoring (anomaly detection via sensors/cameras)
- Partial bathing assistance (automatic bathing device)
- Partial feeding assistance (robot arm)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Excretion care (timing prediction via sensors)
- Rehabilitation (walking support with robot suits)
- Communication (translation/voice assistance)
- Shift scheduling (AI optimization)

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal communication (empathy, active listening)
- Flexible decision-making (emergency response)
- Physical assistance (transfer, bathing, etc.)
- Family coordination and psychological support

**Skills to build:**

- Fundamentals of Caregiving Skills
- AI/IoT Literacy
- Communication Skills
- Team Management

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging society
- Care robots
- Foreign care workers
- Home care

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income for care work?**
A: Entry-level: about 2.9-3.6 million yen; experienced: 4.5-6 million yen. Lower than other industries, but various allowances and qualification bonuses are available.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need for care work?**
A: Mainly 'Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Care)' or 'EPA'. The former requires JLPT N4 equivalent and a skills test. The latter requires a national exam after Japanese language training.

**Q: What are the career advancement paths from care work?**
A: After obtaining a Certified Care Worker license, there are paths to become a Care Manager, facility director, or specialist in care robot implementation.
