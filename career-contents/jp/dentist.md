# Dentist — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 122 (JSCO)**

Physicians who diagnose, treat, and prevent diseases and injuries of teeth and oral cavity. High specialization in caries treatment, periodontal disease, dentures, implants, pediatric orthodontics, with high self-employment rates.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Dental School (6-year program) | 6 years | $3,500,000~$5,000,000 (National university tuition approx. ¥2.5M; private ¥3-5M/year.) |
| Clinical Training | 1 year | $0~$0 (Salary provided as trainee (approx. ¥300,000/month).) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Dentist Examination | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Required | Eligibility after dental school graduation. Pass rate approx. 60-70% |
| Dentist License | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Required | Obtained by registration after passing national exam. Required for practice. |
| Dental Specialist (certified by respective societies) | Japan Dental Specialty Board | Optional | Optional specialty certifications in orthodontics, oral surgery, etc. Beneficial for career advancement. |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 105,000. Dental demand stable due to aging population. However, competition intensifying in urban areas with a slight oversupply of dentists. Supply-demand adjustment expected after 2025.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After graduating from a university dental school (6 years), pass the national exam, complete clinical training (1 year), then work as a dental practitioner. Subsequently, common paths include graduate school, specialty certification, or opening a private practice.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Interpreting simple X-ray images
- Automated cavity detection (AI-assisted)
- Partial treatment plan creation
- Creating patient education materials

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Designing and fabricating prosthetics with 3D scanning (CAD/CAM)
- Early detection via AI diagnostic support
- Robot-assisted implant surgery
- Preventive dental programs through patient data analysis

**Human moat:**

- Manual treatments (cutting, extraction, periodontal surgery)
- Patient psychological care and trust-building
- Comprehensive judgment in complex cases
- Emergency response

**Skills to build:**

- Digital dental technology (CAD/CAM, 3D printing)
- Operation and interpretation of AI diagnostic tools
- Cosmetic dental techniques
- Management & Leadership Aptitude (Entrepreneurial Orientation)

**Key growth areas:**

- Implants
- Cosmetic dentistry
- Home-visit dental care
- Dental-oral collaboration

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a dentist?**
A: Residents earn about ¥3.6-4.8 million, employed dentists ¥6-10 million, and clinic owners ¥12-25 million. There is a large gap depending on whether you own a clinic.

**Q: Can foreigners become dentists in Japan?**
A: Possible, but you must graduate from a Japanese dental school (6 years) and pass the national dental exam. Japanese proficiency (N1 level) is also required, making it extremely challenging.

**Q: How can a dentist advance their career?**
A: Generally, by pursuing a graduate degree to become a specialist, or by opening a private practice. Recently, specializing in cosmetic dentistry, implants, or home-visit dentistry has become important.
