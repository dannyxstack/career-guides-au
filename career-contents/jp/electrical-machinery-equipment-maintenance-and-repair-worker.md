# Electrical machinery equipment maintenance and repair worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 552 (JSCO)**

Technical role inspecting, maintaining, and repairing electrical machinery like motors, transformers, and generators. High demand in manufacturing and maintenance services.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

AI and IoT support fault prediction and diagnosis, but actual disassembly, repair, and adjustment rely on humans. AI complements and improves efficiency but cannot fully replace humans. Skilled labor remains in high demand.

Career progression to chief engineer or service engineer possible with skills. Options include internal promotion at manufacturers or starting own repair business.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated diagnostic report creation
- Recording of periodic inspections
- Simple anomaly detection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Fault cause identification (AI suggests candidates)
- Automated parts inventory management and ordering
- Remote support using AR

**Human moat:**

- Disassembly and reassembly of complex mechanisms
- On-site quick decision-making
- Human consideration for safety

**Skills to build:**

- Combined knowledge of mechanics and electricity
- Basics of AI and data analysis
- Customer communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Renewable energy
- Electric vehicles
- Smart grids
- Energy-saving equipment

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of electrical machinery equipment maintenance and repair workers?**
A: Depending on experience and qualifications, it ranges from about ¥3-4.2M for entry-level, ¥4.2-5.8M for mid-level, and ¥5.8-8M for advanced, including overtime.

**Q: Is it possible to immigrate to Japan for work in this occupation?**
A: Yes, via the Specified Skilled Worker (i) visa (manufacturing sector) or the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa. Specified Skilled Worker (ii) allows family accompaniment and permanent residency but requires advanced skills.

**Q: Can overseas qualifications be used in Japan?**
A: Some qualifications are recognized, but Japanese Electrician or Chief Electrical Engineer licenses require separate exams. For technical internships and Specified Skilled Worker, certain practical experience may be accepted.
