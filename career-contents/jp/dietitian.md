# Dietitian — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 151 (JSCO)**

A dietitian is a professional who manages nutrition and provides dietary guidance at cafeterias, hospitals, and schools to support healthy eating habits.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dietitian License | Prefectural Governor | Required | Obtainable upon graduation from an accredited school. Mandatory. |
| National Registered Dietitian Certification | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Optional | Obtained via national exam after work experience. Advantageous. |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 150,000. Demand is stable due to an aging population and rising health awareness, but competition is somewhat intense due to many qualified individuals. Be mindful of the increase in outsourcing of food service operations.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career paths are diverse: advancement to registered dietitian with experience, management roles in hospitals and welfare facilities, or independent practice as a freelance dietitian.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Partial automation of nutrition calculation and menu planning
- Data entry and form creation
- Checking meal records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Individual optimization of nutritional guidance
- Utilization of diet management apps
- Nutrition data analysis

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal counseling
- Handling individual preferences and medical conditions
- Understanding food culture

**Skills to build:**

- Nutritional guidance skills
- Management ability
- Digital tool proficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- Home medical care and nursing nutrition
- Sports nutrition
- Food service management
- Digital nutrition guidance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People interested in food and health
- People with strong communication skills
- Accurate and meticulous people

**Who is not suited**
- People not good at hygiene management
- People who dislike irregular working hours

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a nutritionist?**
A: Generally 3.5 million to 4.5 million yen, and with a registered dietitian qualification, it can be over 5 million yen.

**Q: How can foreigners work as nutritionists in Japan?**
A: A residence status of 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' is required. In addition to a nutritionist license, a university degree or more than 10 years of practical experience is required.
