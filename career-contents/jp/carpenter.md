# Carpenter — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 661 (JSCO)**

A craftsman who processes wood and assembles building frameworks and interiors. Active in both traditional techniques and modern architecture.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 340,000. The construction industry continues to face labor shortages, and demand for carpenters remains steady. Job openings are particularly abundant in the renovation and wooden housing sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Start as an apprentice, hone skills, advance to master or site supervisor. Self-employment possible. Construction career-up system gaining traction.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple marking
- Material cutting (increasing use of automated machinery)
- Inventory management

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimization of structural calculations
- Automatic generation of blueprints
- AI inspection of construction quality

**Human moat:**

- Advanced manual techniques and experience
- On-site adaptability
- Succession of traditional techniques

**Skills to build:**

- Digital design software (CAD)
- Building Information Modeling (BIM)
- Project management
- Latest tools and machinery operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Wooden housing
- Renovation
- Traditional architecture
- Skill succession

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a carpenter?**
A: Entry-level: 300-380万 yen, intermediate: 400-550万 yen, advanced: 600-900万 yen. Going independent can lead to higher income.

**Q: How can foreigners work as carpenters in Japan?**
A: Obtaining Specified Skilled Worker (i)/(ii) (construction field) is common. Routes include transitioning from technical intern training or being employed by a construction company via the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa.

**Q: What are the career advancement paths?**
A: Apprentice → Skilled worker → Master/foreman. Acquiring the Building Carpenter Skill Certification enhances evaluation and opens paths to independence.
