# Orthoptist, Speech-Language Pathologist — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 145 (JSCO)**

Orthoptists and speech-language pathologists are medical technologists specializing in rehabilitation for visual impairments and speech/hearing disorders, respectively.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 65,000. Demand is steadily increasing with aging. Speech-language pathologists' roles especially expand in community-based integrated care systems. Orthoptists see stable needs from children to the elderly.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Orthoptists gain expertise through clinical experience and certifications. Speech-language pathologists expand careers from hospitals to home-visit rehab. Both can pursue research via graduate school.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated visual field test analysis
- Partial automation of standard language training

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted visual and hearing tests
- Automatic recording and analysis of rehabilitation progress

**Human moat:**

- Building trust with patients
- Clinical judgment for individual adjustments
- Non-verbal communication

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI rehabilitation tools
- Data analysis and interpretation skills
- Multidisciplinary collaboration skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Community-based rehabilitation
- Home healthcare
- Pediatric developmental support
- Dementia prevention

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy interacting with others
- Patient people who can steadily build up rehabilitation

**Who is not suited**
- People who seek short-term results

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of orthoptists and speech-language therapists?**
A: Starting salary is about 3.2 to 3.8 million yen annually; with 10+ years of experience, about 4.8 to 6 million yen. Varies by hospital size and region.

**Q: How can foreigners work as orthoptists or speech-language therapists in Japan?**
A: Requires Japanese national certification. After studying at a training school and passing the national exam, work is possible with an Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa. However, Japanese language proficiency is mandatory.

**Q: Is it possible to obtain both orthoptist and speech-language-hearing therapist certifications?**
A: Possible, but each requires a 3–4 year training program, costing time and money. Few people hold both.
