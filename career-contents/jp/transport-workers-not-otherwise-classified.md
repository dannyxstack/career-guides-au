# Transport Workers (Not Otherwise Classified) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 639 (JSCO)**

Transportation jobs other than automobile or railway driving, such as operating ships, forklifts, or special vehicles.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 150,000. Amid ongoing labor shortages in the logistics industry, demand for forklift operators and construction machinery drivers remains strong. These jobs involve on-site work less affected by automation.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Due to advances in AI and autonomous driving technology, some tasks like long-haul truck driving face replacement risk, but on-site work with forklifts and construction machinery is hard to automate, and manual demand will continue for now.

Career advancement to site leader or operations manager is possible by obtaining large vehicle or forklift licenses and gaining experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Constant-speed driving on highways
- Simple in-warehouse transport

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Route optimization
- Vehicle diagnostics
- Safe driving assistance

**Human moat:**

- Off-road driving
- Heavy load handling
- Flexible decision-making

**Skills to build:**

- Driving licenses for multiple vehicle types
- Mechanical maintenance
- Team communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Forklift
- Construction machinery
- Logistics driver
- Coastal vessels

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of transport workers?**
A: Entry-level: 3-4 million yen, mid-level: 4-5.5 million yen, senior: 5.5-7 million yen. Varies with overtime pay and qualification allowances.

**Q: Can foreigners work in this occupation in Japan?**
A: Currently, it's difficult to obtain a residence status for simple driving jobs. For technical and knowledge-intensive roles like operations managers, the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa may apply.

**Q: What career advancement paths are available?**
A: By acquiring qualifications such as forklift or heavy vehicle licenses, there are career paths to become field leaders, operations managers, or sales staff.
