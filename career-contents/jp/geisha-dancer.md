# Geisha/Dancer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 406 (JSCO)**

Perform dances and arts on stage and at banquets, from traditional geisha to modern dance. Active in the entertainment and flower-and-willow world.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Geisha belong to okiya or ochaya, advancing from apprentice (maiko) to full-fledged. Dancers gain experience in troupes or clubs, and may advance to choreographer or instructor.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple routine dance
- Backup dancer
- Basic choreography creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

- Traditional performing arts

- Performance
- Cultivating successors for the flower and willow world

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much do geiko earn?**
A: Apprentices earn about ¥2-3.5M/year; full-fledged ¥6M+, top earners over ¥10M, but split with okiya and expenses apply.

**Q: Can foreigners become geiko?**
A: Possible, but requires obtaining an 'Artist' visa and demands prominence and achievements. The path to permanent residency is narrow.

**Q: How to become a dancer with no experience?**
A: Learn the basics at vocational schools or dance studios and audition. Long-term training is necessary.
