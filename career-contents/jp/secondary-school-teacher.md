# Secondary School Teacher — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 195 (JSCO)**

A professional responsible for subject instruction and student guidance in lower and upper secondary schools, supporting student growth through career counseling and club activities. Requires teaching practicum and teaching license.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower Secondary School Teaching License (Type 1, Subject-Specific) | Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) (via Prefectural Boards of Education) | Required | Obtained per subject after completing required credits at university. |
| Upper Secondary School Teaching License (Type 1, Subject-Specific) | MEXT (via Prefectural Boards of Education) | Required | Often obtained together with lower secondary license. |
| Teacher Recruitment Exam (Public Schools) | Prefectural and designated city boards of education | Optional | Mandatory for public school positions. Includes written exam, interview, practical skills. |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI greatly streamlines auxiliary tasks like material creation, grade management, and learning analysis for teachers, but human interaction in lessons and student guidance remains. Teachers can focus on higher-quality education by leveraging AI tools.

Career path: beginning teacher → teacher → head teacher → vice principal → principal. Options to specialize, pursue administration, or switch to education boards or university faculty.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Grading periodic tests (multiple choice)
- Grade management and aggregation
- Creating standard contact documents for parents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Planning individual learning plans
- Tracking learning progress during lessons
- Automated creation and customization of teaching materials

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face dialogue and empathy
- Perceiving students' psychological conditions
- Handling unstructured problems
- Collaborative team guidance

**Skills to build:**

- Data literacy
- AI tool operation
- Critical Thinking
- Interpersonal Communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Integrated middle and high school
- Support for school refusal
- ICT education
- Diversifying student guidance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a secondary school teacher?**
A: For public middle and high schools, about 200,000–220,000 yen per month for university graduates, which translates to an annual income of approximately 3.2–4.2 million yen (including allowances). Private schools vary more.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to become teachers at Japanese secondary schools?**
A: Typically, the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' status of residence is required. However, obtaining a Japanese teaching license and the ability to teach in Japanese are prerequisites, making the bar quite high.

**Q: What career path leads to a management position?**
A: Generally, after accumulating over 10 years of experience as a teacher and serving in positions such as lead teacher or mentor teacher, passing the management position exam is the norm.
