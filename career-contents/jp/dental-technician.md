# Dental Technician — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 147 (JSCO)**

Dental technicians fabricate dentures, crowns, and other dental prosthetics under dentists' prescriptions, combining precision handwork with cutting-edge digital technology.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 34,000. Demand remains stable due to aging-related dental needs, but job opportunities are slightly declining as fewer labs operate and CAD/CAM technology spreads.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

After training at a lab, you can open your own practice or contract directly with clinics. Specializing in digital or implant technology boosts career prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple crown fabrication such as full crowns
- Some steps in lost-wax casting
- Repetitive model-making tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- CAD/CAM design and fabrication
- Model fabrication using 3D printers
- Operation of digital scanners

**Human moat:**

- Fine adjustments and accommodation of individual differences
- Legal qualifications and responsibilities of dental technicians
- Craftsmanship based on the feel of the patient's oral cavity

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM operation skills
- Digital denture design
- Knowledge of materials science
- Communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital dental technology
- CAD/CAM
- Implant dental technology
- Geriatric dentistry

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who are dexterous and enjoy detailed work
- People who want to pursue mastery of a skill
- People who want to help others

**Who is not suited**
- People who like talking to others and prioritize customer service
- People who dislike monotonous work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a dental technician?**
A: The average annual income is about 4 million yen. It ranges from 2.5 to 7 million yen depending on experience, specialization, and workplace. Self-employment can lead to higher income.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a dental technician in Japan?**
A: A national dental technician license is required. Japanese proficiency (JLPT N2 or higher recommended) and either study at a training school or practical experience (10+ years for Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa) are necessary. Specified Skilled Worker visa is currently excluded, but the Engineer/Specialist visa allows employment.
