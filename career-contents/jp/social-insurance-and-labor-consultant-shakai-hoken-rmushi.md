# Social Insurance and Labor Consultant (Shakai Hoken Rōmushi) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 183 (JSCO)**

As an expert in social insurance and labor-related laws, this certified professional handles corporate labor management, procedural representation, and consultation. A specialist in HR and labor affairs.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 44,000. Due to the declining birthrate, aging population, and work style reforms, demand for social insurance and labor management remains steady. Consultation needs are increasing, especially among small and medium-sized enterprises.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI will streamline routine tasks (document creation, data entry), but expert advice and legal interpretation remain hard to replace; rather, AI tools will enable higher-level services.

A common career path involves working at an office before starting an independent practice. Career advancement includes becoming an HR/labor consultant or a partner at a Social Insurance and Labor Consultant firm.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Part of document creation and application procedures
- Data entry and verification

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Labor risk analysis
- Organizing and utilizing legal revisions
- Creating client materials

**Human moat:**

- Interpersonal consulting skills
- Complex legal interpretation
- Handling individual cases

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced legal knowledge
- Communication skills
- Proficiency in using AI tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Work style reform
- Social insurance procedures
- HR and labor consulting
- Electronic applications

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a Labor and Social Security Attorney?**
A: For employed attorneys, about 5–7 million yen; independent practitioners can earn over 10 million yen, but income varies.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a Labor and Social Security Attorney?**
A: No nationality restrictions for exam eligibility, but Japanese language ability and knowledge of Japanese law are essential. After qualification, work is typically under the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa.
