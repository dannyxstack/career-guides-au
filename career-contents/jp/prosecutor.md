# Prosecutor — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 172 (JSCO)**

A prosecutor is a legal professional responsible for investigating, indicting, and maintaining trials in criminal cases. As a national public servant, they belong to the Public Prosecutors Office of the Ministry of Justice and uphold fair criminal justice.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,500. Employment for prosecutors in Japan is stable as it supports the foundation of the criminal justice system, but the number of positions is limited. Due to the declining birthrate and judicial system reforms, new recruitment remains highly competitive with intense competition.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI cannot completely replace prosecutors' work, but it will be used as a tool to improve efficiency in evidence analysis and document review. Core decisions like prosecution remain essential for human judgment.

After passing the bar exam and completing legal training, one becomes a public prosecutor. The typical career path is from assistant prosecutor to prosecutor, and then to senior prosecutor (such as at the Tokyo District Public Prosecutors Office Special Investigation Department).

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Document organization
- Past precedent search
- Evidence classification

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Evidence analysis
- Crime pattern prediction
- Document review

**Human moat:**

- Ethical judgment
- Legal interpretation
- Courtroom argument
- Witness examination

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis
- AI tool utilization
- Criminology Knowledge
- International Law Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Criminal justice
- International crime response
- Cybercrime
- Organized crime countermeasures

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a prosecutor?**
A: The starting salary is about 6-8 million yen, but it increases with experience, and executive class can exceed 20 million yen. However, as public servants, the upper limit is lower than private lawyers.

**Q: Can foreigners become Japanese prosecutors?**
A: It is practically impossible. Prosecutors must have Japanese citizenship; foreign nationals would need to naturalize. Additionally, high requirements such as passing the bar exam and completing legal training are necessary.

**Q: What is the standard route to become a prosecutor?**
A: University (law faculty) → law school → pass bar exam → legal training (1 year) → appointment as prosecutor. The earliest appointment age is 25-26.
