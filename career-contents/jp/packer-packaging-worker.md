# Packer/Packaging Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 706 (JSCO)**

Occupation involving wrapping and packing products or packages. Manual tasks include boxing and labeling in warehouses or factories.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Compulsory Education | 9 years | $0~$0 (Junior High School Graduate) |
| Vocational Training School | 6 months | $300,000~$600,000 (Course for forklift and other qualifications) |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 300,000. Demand remains steady due to logistics needs from e-commerce growth, but employment is flat to declining due to automation and robotics.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Easy to start with no experience, but promotion paths are limited. Career advancement possible to team leader, quality control, or machine operator.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Packing into cardboard boxes
- Manual label application
- Slip confirmation and handwriting entry

**Where AI augments the role:**

- High-speed packaging using machinery
- Coordination work with inventory management system
- Automated warehouse monitoring

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of packers?**
A: Entry-level is about 2.8–3.5 million yen; with experience and management roles, it can rise to 4.2–5 million yen.

**Q: What visa is required for foreigners to work as packers?**
A: Specified Skilled Worker (i) (manufacturing sector) is the main route. Technical Intern Training is also possible, but Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services is not common.

**Q: Will this occupation be replaced by AI?**
A: Automated packaging machines and robots are being introduced, making simple manual tasks prone to replacement. However, machine monitoring and maintenance tasks will remain.
