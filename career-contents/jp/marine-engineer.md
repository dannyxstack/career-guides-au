# Marine Engineer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 634 (JSCO)**

This occupation involves operating, monitoring, and maintaining engine equipment such as main engines and generators on ships. Professionals work on domestic and international cargo ships, tankers, and passenger vessels.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 28,000. The domestic shipping industry faces chronic labor shortages, with particularly rapid aging among engine room crew. While new young entrants are expected, automated technology may also be introduced.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Typical promotion route: Second Engineer → First Engineer → Chief Engineer. Acquiring land-based certifications (e.g., Boiler Technician) is effective for career advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- periodic data recording
- simple anomaly monitoring
- routine inspections (sensor-replaced)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- fault prediction diagnosis
- energy efficiency optimization
- remote-assisted repairs

**Human moat:**

- judgment and response to sudden issues
- identifying complex anomalies
- human collaboration

**Skills to build:**

- automation system operation
- data analysis
- English proficiency (foreign routes)

**Key growth areas:**

- coastal shipping
- engineer shortage
- ship automation
- securing young workers

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who like tinkering with machinery
- People who don't get seasick
- People who can endure long voyages

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer life on land
- People with claustrophobia

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income for a ship engine operator?**
A: Entry-level: 3.5-4.5 million JPY, mid-level: 5-7 million JPY, senior: 7-10 million JPY. May be higher for international or large vessels.

**Q: Can foreigners work as ship engine operators?**
A: Possible. You can work under the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa, but a university degree or higher and work experience are required. Specified Skilled Worker is currently not applicable.
