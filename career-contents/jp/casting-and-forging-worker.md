# Casting and forging worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 522 (JSCO)**

A production process specialist who melts, casts, and forges metals to manufacture machine parts and molds. Requires high technical skill and expertise.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school (general or industrial course) | 3 years | $0~$150,000 (Public high school tuition is free) |
| Vocational training school (casting/forging) | 1–2 years | $100,000~$500,000 (Hello Training offers benefits) |

| Forging Skill Certification | Central Vocational Ability Development Association | Required | Levels 1, 2, 3; requires work experience |
| Metal Heat Treatment Skill Certification | Central Vocational Ability Development Association | Optional | Advantageous as a related qualification |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 65,000. Domestic production is declining due to reduced demand from the automotive industry. However, a severe shortage of successors due to aging skilled workers makes this a target field for Specified Skilled Worker visas.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Start as an apprentice and advance to mid-level and skilled worker by obtaining skill certifications. Mastering casting and forging techniques can lead to roles like site leader or quality control manager.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple casting work
- Forging press for standard products
- Manual product transport

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Casting design (simulation)
- Quality inspection (image recognition)
- Die maintenance and repair

**Human moat:**

- High-level casting design planning
- Forging die technology (including trials)
- Identifying root causes and countermeasures for defects

**Skills to build:**

- Use of casting simulation software
- CAD/CAE
- Robot teaching
- Knowledge of metal materials

**Key growth areas:**

- Casting technology
- Forging technology
- Die making
- Heat treatment

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for casting/forging?**
A: Depending on experience and qualifications, entry-level about 3-4 million JPY, skilled 5.5-7 million JPY. Higher at major companies or in management.

**Q: How can foreigners work in this occupation in Japan?**
A: The main route is Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Manufacturing 3 fields). Requires passing Skill Test Level 3 or evaluation exam. For Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, a university degree and relevant work experience are needed.

**Q: Can I become one even without experience?**
A: Yes, it's possible. You can acquire skills through vocational training schools or company apprenticeship programs. By gaining experience and passing skill tests, you can advance your career.
