# Personal Care Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 404 (JSCO)**

Supports daily life of elderly and disabled persons, assisting with meals, bathing, and mobility. Works in home care or facility care settings.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Initial Training for Care Workers | 2 months | $50,000~$100,000 (Correspondence or in-person courses available) |

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |

| Completion of Initial Training for Care Workers | Designated by prefectural governments | Optional | Acquire basic knowledge. No experience required |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★★).** National workforce approx. 2,150,000. Demand is rising due to aging population. Severe labor shortage, covered by Specified Skilled Worker visa. Care worker treatment improvements underway, but supply shortage expected to persist.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary for caregiving jobs?**
A: Starting salary is about 200,000-250,000 yen per month, annual income around 2.5-3.2 million yen. Becoming a certified care worker or facility manager can reach 4-5.5 million yen.

**Q: Can foreigners work in caregiving?**
A: Possible with Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Care) or care visa. Requires Japanese proficiency N4 or higher and completion of initial caregiver training.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: Obtaining the Certified Care Worker qualification is common. Accumulate experience to aim for care manager or facility manager.
