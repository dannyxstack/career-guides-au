# Fishery Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 481 (JSCO)**

Occupation involving boarding fishing vessels to catch, farm, and harvest fish and shellfish. It spans from coastal to pelagic fishing, supporting Japan's fishing industry.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 130,000. The aging of fishery workers and lack of successors are serious issues, leading to expanded acceptance of foreign workers under Specified Skilled Worker (ii).

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI/automation boosts efficiency in catch prediction, fish detection, and aquaculture management, but manual tasks like actual fishing and fish sorting remain labor-dependent, limiting full substitution.

Starting as a fishing apprentice, one gains experience to become a captain or fishery manager. Independence with one's own boat is possible, but requires funds and licenses.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Partial automation of fish detection
- Recording and aggregating catch data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Support for fish detection
- Automated feeding in aquaculture
- Catch prediction

**Human moat:**

- Expertise in vessel operation and net handling
- Sensory evaluation of fish quality
- Experienced judgment of weather and sea conditions

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis
- AI tool operation
- Aquaculture techniques
- English and Japanese language skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture
- Smart fishing
- High-end fish for export
- Fishing experience tourism

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income in fishing?**
A: Depending on experience and catch, beginners earn 250-350万 yen, intermediate 350-500万 yen, and captains/owners 500-1000万 yen. Deep-sea or high-end fish can yield higher income.

**Q: Can foreigners become fishers?**
A: Possible under Specified Skilled Worker (i)/(ii) or Technical Intern Training. Japanese ability and skill tests are required, but acceptance has expanded.

**Q: How to become a fisher without experience?**
A: Study at fisheries high schools or vocational schools, or be hired as an apprentice by fisheries cooperatives or companies. Obtaining a small boat license is essential.
