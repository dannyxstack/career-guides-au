# Electrical/Electronic/Telecommunications Engineer (excluding R&D) — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 082 (JSCO)**

Engineers responsible for design, construction, and maintenance of power equipment, electronic circuits, and communication networks. They work across a wide range, from factory and building electrical systems to public infrastructure.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Begins at the entry level with design assistance or site supervision. Progress to chief engineer or project leader through experience. Promotion is aided by acquiring national certifications such as the Electrical Chief Engineer license.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine circuit design and wiring diagram creation
- Standard inspection and measurement tasks
- Data organization and report creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-based failure prediction and diagnosis
- Design optimization via automation tools
- Inspection by drones and robots

**Human moat:**

- Flexible on-site troubleshooting
- Deep understanding of regulations and safety standards
- Coordination with diverse stakeholders

**Skills to build:**

- Basics of AI and machine learning
- Data analysis (Python/R)
- Programming (C/C++)
- Knowledge of renewable energy
- Project management

**Key growth areas:**

- Renewable energy
- Smart grids
- 5G/IoT
- Energy saving and electrification

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much is the annual income of electrical/electronic engineers?**
A: Starting salary around 3.5 million yen, mid-career 5–7 million yen, management or specialized qualifications can exceed 10 million yen. Including overtime, large companies tend to be higher.

**Q: Can foreigners work as electrical engineers?**
A: Yes, with a 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa requiring a university degree and related work. Also, from 2024, 'Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Electronics/Information Related Industries)' allows work with skills exam and JLPT N4.

**Q: Is an electrical chief engineer license mandatory?**
A: Not mandatory, but advantageous for promotion and salary increase. Especially Type 1 and Type 2 are in high demand, aiding career stability.
