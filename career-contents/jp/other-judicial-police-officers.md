# Other Judicial Police Officers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 449 (JSCO)**

A profession engaged in maintaining order and safety in judicial institutions, including guarding court-related facilities, escorting detainees, and managing evidence.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High School Diploma | 3 years | $0~$300,000 (Public high school tuition is free under the policy.) |
| University Degree (e.g., Faculty of Law) | 4 years | $2,500,000~$5,000,000 (About ¥500,000/year at national/public universities, about ¥1,000,000/year at private universities.) |
| Passed police officer recruitment exam | Less than 1 year | $50,000~$200,000 (Test preparation courses, etc.) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Police Officer Recruitment Exam | National Police Agency / Prefectural Police | Required | Training at a police academy is required after passing the patrol officer recruitment exam. |
| Court Staff Recruitment Exam | Supreme Court | Required | Required when hired as a court clerk or court security officer. |
| Security Guard Instructor and Education Supervisor Qualification | General Incorporated Foundation: Security Industry Promotion Foundation | Optional | Recommended to obtain when working as a security guard at a private security company. |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 25,000. Judicial and police-related work is a public sector with stable demand, but new hiring is limited due to staff reductions and the introduction of mechanical security.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career paths are based on a rank system, with promotion to sergeant, assistant inspector, etc., requiring exam success. Long-term stability can be expected as a Metropolitan Police Department or court employee.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patrol security
- Checking surveillance cameras
- Managing entry/exit records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Data analysis for patrol planning
- AI-based facial recognition and suspicious behavior detection
- Remote monitoring via drones

**Human moat:**

- Human judgment in emergencies
- Counseling and persuasion of detainees
- Complex interpersonal coordination within courts

**Skills to build:**

- Crisis management skills
- Psychology and counseling skills
- Legal knowledge
- Leadership

**Key growth areas:**

- Courthouse security
- Civil servant stability
- Police officer shortage
- Mechanical security substitution

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those who follow rules and value order
- Patient and stress-resistant individuals
- Those seeking stable careers as civil servants

**Who is not suited**
- Those preferring work with creativity and high autonomy
- Those wishing to avoid physical danger

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary?**
A: For civil servant hires, the starting salary is about ¥200,000–250,000 per month, or ¥3,500,000–4,500,000 annually, depending on region and education.

**Q: Is it possible for foreigners to work in this occupation in Japan?**
A: Judicial police officers in Japan are mainly civil servants, and Japanese nationality is usually required. It is extremely difficult for foreigners to work in this field, and there is no standard residence status route.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: You need to pass promotion exams to raise your rank as a police officer or take exams to increase your specialization as a court staff member.
