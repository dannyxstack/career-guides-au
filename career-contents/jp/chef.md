# Chef — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 391 (JSCO)**

A profession that involves cooking ingredients and providing dishes in restaurants, hotels, etc. It is divided into specialties such as Japanese, Western, and Chinese cuisine.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 850,000. Labor shortages are severe, with particularly high demand in rural areas and specialty restaurants. It is a target occupation for Specified Skilled Worker (i), promoting the acceptance of foreign workers.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a chef?**
A: Depending on experience and the establishment, the average is about ¥3.5–4.5 million. Head chefs can earn ¥5 million+, and at high-end restaurants, ¥8 million+.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a chef in Japan?**
A: Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Cooking) is most common. Requires N4 Japanese or above and passing a skills test. Experienced workers may also qualify for a Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa.

**Q: Is a cooking license required?**
A: Not mandatory, but advantageous for employment or independence. Eligibility for the exam requires graduation from a vocational school or 2 years of practical experience.
