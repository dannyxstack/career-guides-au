# Reforestation Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 471 (JSCO)**

A specialist in forest cultivation and management, maintaining healthy forests through planting, weeding, thinning, and pruning.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 43,000. Forestry workers are aging, with few new entrants and a chronic labor shortage. Increased demand for domestic timber and the need for forest maintenance are expected to sustain employment in the medium to long term.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression goes from field worker to team leader to site supervisor. Obtaining qualifications such as Forestry Worker or Forestry Technician is a benchmark for promotion, and self-employment as an independent contractor is also possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Weeding after planting (mechanized)
- Simple felling (mechanized)
- Surveying and measurement (replaced by drones)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Forest surveying and growth analysis using drones
- Efficiency improvement of thinning and logging by machinery
- AI-based pest risk prediction

**Human moat:**

- Judgment in complex terrain
- Consideration for ecosystems
- Know-how for safety management

**Skills to build:**

- Drone Operation
- GIS Analysis
- Machine Operation (Harvester, Forwarder)
- Safety Management

**Key growth areas:**

- Reforestation and thinning
- Forest conservation
- Biomass utilization
- Smart forestry

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a forestry worker?**
A: Starting salary is about 2.6 million JPY, rising to 4.5-6 million JPY with experience. Stable for forest unions or local government staff, but varies for general forestry companies.

**Q: Can foreigners work as forestry workers in Japan?**
A: Specified Skilled Worker (i) for forestry has been introduced; working is possible if you pass skill tests and meet Japanese language requirements. There are also records of acceptance under the Technical Intern Training Program.

**Q: How can I become a forestry worker without experience?**
A: It is common to use training programs at forest unions or forestry businesses. You can also study at high schools or vocational schools with forestry departments. Useful qualifications include special training for chainsaws and forestry machinery.
