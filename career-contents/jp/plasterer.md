# Plasterer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 664 (JSCO)**

A construction skilled worker who applies mortar, plaster, etc. to walls and floors. A profession where traditional techniques coexist with modern building demand.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| After high school, join a construction/plastering company as an apprentice | 3 years | $0~$500,000 (May require self-funding for tools at the start) |
| Vocational training school (Construction/Plastering course) | 1–2 years | $200,000~$600,000 (Public vocational training may be free) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plasterer Skill Test (Grade 1/2) | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (Prefectural Vocational Ability Development Association) | Optional | National qualification. Grade 1 is the highest level for skilled workers. |
| Construction Business Permit (Plastering Work) | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Prefectural government) | Optional | Required for independent business |
| Scaffolding Assembly Supervisor | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (Prefectural Labour Bureau) | Optional | May be required for high-rise work |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 120,000. Due to chronic labor shortages across the construction industry, demand for plasterers remains steady. As of 2024, it is a target field for Specified Skilled Worker (i), with increasing acceptance of foreign workers.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Starting as an apprentice, one can aim for Skill Test Grade 1 or recognition as an Excellent Skilled Worker (Modern Master). Career paths are diverse, including independent business or incorporation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple mortar mixing work
- Certain pattern spray painting
- Flat surface plastering feasible by automatic plastering robots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Automatic optimization of material blending
- AI quality checks of construction photos using smartphones
- Finish simulation using AR

**Human moat:**

- Craftsman's intuition for adjusting subtle textures of finishes
- Adaptability for hand plastering on uneven/irregular surfaces
- Situation-based judgment and flexible response at each site

**Skills to build:**

- Obtaining Grade 1 Plastering Skilled Worker certification
- Adaptability to new materials (plaster, diatomaceous earth, etc.)
- Basic understanding of construction IT (AI, drones, BIM)
- Business skills such as customer service and estimate creation

**Key growth areas:**

- Exterior wall renovation
- Plaster and diatomaceous earth
- Plastering skill inheritance
- Fireproof coating work

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who are dexterous and enjoy making things
- People who like physical work and are confident in their stamina
- People who want to learn traditional skills and work long-term as artisans

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike working at heights or with dust
- People who dislike weather-dependent work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a plasterer?**
A: Less than 3 years experience: 2.8–3.6 million yen; intermediate: 3.6–4.8 million yen; skilled: 4.8–6.5 million yen. Self-employment can yield higher income.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a plasterer in Japan?**
A: The main route is through Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Construction) or Specified Skilled Worker (ii) (Construction). You must pass a skills test. Specified Skilled Worker (ii) allows family accompaniment and permanent residence applications.

**Q: Are special qualifications needed to become a plasterer?**
A: No mandatory qualifications are required, but obtaining a skilled worker certification is advantageous for salary increases and independence. To start your own business, a construction permit (plastering work) is necessary.
