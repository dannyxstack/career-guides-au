# Craft Artist — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 223 (JSCO)**

Specialist creating traditional crafts or contemporary art works. Works in diverse fields including pottery, lacquer art, metalwork, and dyeing/weaving, contributing to Japanese cultural heritage and innovation.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a craft artist?**
A: It varies greatly by experience and reputation, but roughly 2.5–3.5 million JPY for entry-level, 3.5–5 million JPY for intermediate, and 5–8 million JPY for advanced. Sales depend on piece prices and exhibition frequency.

**Q: Can foreigners work as craft artists in Japan?**
A: Possible. However, the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa requires post-graduation work experience and an employment contract in the crafts field. Artistic activities may be permitted under a 'Designated Activities' visa.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: Holding solo exhibitions, winning prizes in open competitions, obtaining traditional craftsman certification, and participating in overseas exhibitions are effective. Starting your own business or collaborating with companies are also career options.
