# Natural Sciences Researcher — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 051 (JSCO)**

A specialized professional who explores new knowledge through theories and experiments in natural sciences such as physics, chemistry, and biology. Engages in basic and applied research at universities, research institutes, or corporate laboratories.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 175,000. The government is expanding science and technology budgets, driving steady demand for researchers in AI, life sciences, and environmental energy fields. However, postdoctoral positions remain a serious issue, and competition is intense.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After obtaining a PhD, gain experience as a postdoc, then advance from assistant professor to associate professor to professor. In companies, progress from senior researcher to research management. Ability to secure external funding is key to career advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine data collection and preprocessing
- Simple statistical analysis
- Comprehensive literature review

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Large-scale data analysis for hypothesis generation
- Complex simulation
- Drug candidate screening
- Optimization of experimental design

**Human moat:**

- Setting original research topics
- Design and interpretation of experimental systems
- Integration of knowledge through collaboration with other fields
- Ethical Judgment

**Skills to build:**

- Machine Learning/Deep Learning
- Programming (Python, R)
- Statistics
- Domain Knowledge (Physics, Chemistry, Biology)
- English Academic Writing

**Key growth areas:**

- AI and Machine Learning
- Life Science and Biotechnology
- Environment and Energy
- Quantum Technology

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those with strong curiosity and persistence in tackling unknown problems
- Those with logical thinking, experimental and data analysis skills
- Those who can communicate in English without difficulty in international joint research

**Who is not suited**
- For those seeking stable income immediately
- For those who prefer monotonous routine work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of natural science researchers?**
A: About 6 million JPY for assistant professors at universities, and about 10 million JPY for professors. Corporate researchers average around 7 million JPY, but it varies by field and performance.

**Q: What visa is necessary for foreign researchers to work in Japan?**
A: Mainly the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa or 'Highly Skilled Professional (i)' visa. The point system also opens a path to permanent residency.

**Q: Can I get a job as a researcher without a PhD?**
A: Corporate research positions may hire master's graduates, but at universities and public research institutions, a PhD is essentially required. It can be a career barrier.
