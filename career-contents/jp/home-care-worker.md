# Home Care Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 362 (JSCO)**

A home care specialist who visits the homes of the elderly and disabled to provide physical care (bathing, toileting, meals) and daily living support.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| Initial Training for Care Workers | 2–6 months | $30,000~$100,000 (Some municipalities offer grants) |
| Certified Care Worker (National Qualification) | 2–3 years | $100,000~$1,500,000 (Practical training + 3 years of experience or vocational school route) |

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★★).** National workforce approx. 410,000. Demand is steadily increasing due to the aging population. It is estimated that Japan will face a shortage of approximately 340,000 care workers by 2025, and job openings for home care workers are expected to remain high.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Start as a home helper. With experience, you can advance to service provider manager, care manager, or administrator. Qualifications can be obtained stepwise: initial training, practical training, and certified care worker.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Recording tasks
- Schedule management
- Handling simple inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Abnormality detection via monitoring sensors
- Care recording through voice input
- Transfer assistance using care robots

**Human moat:**

- Empathy
- Flexible response
- Physical care techniques
- Building trust with the elderly

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI care record systems
- ICT literacy
- Multidisciplinary collaboration skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Expanding demand for home care
- Utilization of care robots
- Acceptance of foreign care workers
- Strengthened collaboration with home-visit nursing

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of home care workers?**
A: Average annual income is about 3.5 million yen. Varies by experience, qualifications, and employment type (full-time/part-time). Night/early morning visits can increase income.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work in home care in Japan?**
A: Mainly 'Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Nursing Care)' or 'EPA Certified Care Worker Candidate'. Specified Skilled Worker (i) requires passing Japanese exam (N4 level) and nursing care skills evaluation exam.

**Q: How to advance your career?**
A: Career paths include service provider manager, care manager, and administrator. Obtaining a certified care worker qualification leads to salary increases and better job opportunities.
