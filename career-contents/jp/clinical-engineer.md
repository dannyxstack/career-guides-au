# Clinical Engineer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 142 (JSCO)**

A specialist who operates and manages life-support equipment such as ventilators and dialysis machines, ensuring safe operation and maintenance in medical settings. Collaborates with doctors and nurses to support treatment.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical Engineer National Exam | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare | Required | Eligible after completing training program. Pass rate is about 80%. |
| Certified Dialysis Technician | Japanese Society for Dialysis Therapy | Optional | A private certification to enhance expertise in dialysis. Not mandatory but advantageous. |
| Medical Device Information Communicator | Japanese Society of Medical Instrumentation | Optional | Specialist in safety management and information communication of medical devices. |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

After gaining experience, options include specializing in fields like dialysis centers or operating rooms, or switching to medical device manufacturers. Advancement to management or educational roles is also possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine device monitoring
- Simple alarm responses
- Manual data recording

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Early anomaly detection (AI analysis)
- Remote monitoring for patient management
- Preventive maintenance optimization for devices

**Human moat:**

- Deep knowledge of life-support devices and judgment
- Emergency response skills
- Communication skills in team healthcare
- Physical device inspection and repair skills

**Skills to build:**

- Basic knowledge of AI and machine learning
- Data analysis (Python/R)
- IT system management
- Advanced knowledge of medical device safety management

**Key growth areas:**

- Artificial dialysis
- Intensive care
- Medical device management
- Home healthcare

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People interested in machinery and electronic devices
- People who find fulfillment in supporting lives in medical settings
- Meticulous people who can accurately perform detailed and routine tasks

**Who is not suited**
- People who want to avoid sudden overtime or night shifts
- People who prefer working with machines over people (though patient interaction is also involved)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a clinical engineer?**
A: Approximately 3.5–4.2 million yen for new graduates, 4.5–5.5 million yen for mid-career, and 6–7 million yen for management positions. Varies by hospital size and region.

**Q: Can foreigners work as clinical engineers?**
A: Possible with an Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa, but the national exam is in Japanese, requiring advanced language skills. Also, practical experience in Japanese medical institutions is required, making it challenging.

**Q: How can I become a clinical engineer?**
A: After completing a 3-year vocational school or 4-year university training program, must pass a national exam, then gain practical experience in medical facilities.
