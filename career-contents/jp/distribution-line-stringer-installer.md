# Distribution line stringer/installer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 672 (JSCO)**

Installs and maintains power distribution lines on poles and steel towers. Mainly outdoor high-elevation work, including outage and live-line work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Start as an apprentice, gain experience and qualifications to become fully skilled. Advancement to team leader, supervisor, safety manager, or management roles at electrical contractors.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Assistance in simple cable laying
- Routine inspection record creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Overhead line inspection using drones
- Confirmation work for AI-based equipment diagnostics

**Human moat:**

- Precise manual work at heights
- Safety judgment during outage and live-line work
- Flexible response to site conditions

**Skills to build:**

- High-altitude work skills
- Electrician's license
- Safety management ability
- Basics of drone operation and AI analysis

**Key growth areas:**

- Distribution lines
- Renewable energy
- Aging infrastructure measures
- High-altitude work

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those who do not mind working at heights or outdoors
- Those who value teamwork
- Those who are physically fit and have a strong safety awareness

**Who is not suited**
- Those with a fear of heights
- Those who prefer desk work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for workers engaged in distribution line stringing and laying?**
A: For entry-level, it's about 3.5-4.5 million yen; intermediate, 4.8-6 million yen; advanced, 6-8 million yen. Varies with overtime and site allowances.

**Q: Can foreigners work in this occupation?**
A: It is a target occupation for Specified Skilled Worker (i) and technical intern training, requiring Japanese language proficiency and passing a skills test. An Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is also possible.

**Q: How can I start with no experience?**
A: Generally, obtain an electrician's license and get hired as an apprentice at an electrical construction company. Studying at a vocational school provides an advantage.
