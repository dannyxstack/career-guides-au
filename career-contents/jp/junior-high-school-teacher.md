# Junior High School Teacher — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 193 (JSCO)**

A professional who provides subject instruction, student guidance, and class management at junior high schools. Based on the national curriculum guidelines, they play a crucial role in supporting the growth of adolescent students.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junior High School Teaching Certificate (First Class) | Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (Prefectural Board of Education) | Required | Required per subject; completion of designated credits at university |
| Junior High School Teaching Certificate (Advanced Class) | Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (Prefectural Board of Education) | Optional | Master's level; advantageous for administrative roles |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 250,000. Demand for teachers is declining due to a decreasing student population from low birth rates, but hiring numbers are expected to remain high due to replenishing the retiring baby boomer generation and increased demand for special needs education.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Generally, career paths include moving into administrative roles such as vice principal and principal, or developing as subject specialists. The teacher license renewal system has been abolished, but ongoing training continues.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Lesson material creation
- Grading and data aggregation
- Routine Q&A handling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Individual learning plan design
- Visualizing student progress
- Data analysis for lesson improvement

**Human moat:**

- Empathy
- Interpersonal skills
- Conveying values
- Fostering non-cognitive skills

**Skills to build:**

- ICT teaching skills
- Data literacy
- Curriculum management
- Special needs education knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Special Needs Education
- English Education
- ICT Utilization Guidance
- Student Guidance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those who enjoy engaging with children
- Those capable of multitasking and handling multiple tasks simultaneously
- Those who are patient and can collaborate in a team

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking short-term results
- Those who want to avoid overtime and taking work home

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a junior high school teacher?**
A: It is said to be about 4 million yen in their 20s and about 7 million yen in their 40s, but varies by prefecture and allowances.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to become junior high school teachers?**
A: It is extremely difficult to be hired as a regular teacher. An education visa or Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is assumed, but Japanese proficiency and a teaching license are required.
