# Director — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 234 (JSCO)**

A professional who oversees the artistic direction of stage or film productions, guiding actors and staff to unify the overall expression of the work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Starting as an assistant, gaining experience, and becoming independent. Some belong to major production companies or theater troupes, while others freelance.

- Simple scene transition instructions
- Automatic script checking

**Where AI augments the role:**

- CG previsualization

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with creativity and leadership
- People with multitasking ability to manage multiple tasks simultaneously
- People comfortable giving instructions in front of others

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer a regular lifestyle
- People sensitive to criticism or rebuttal

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a director?**
A: It varies greatly by experience and track record, but typical ranges are 3-5 million yen for entry-level, 5-8 million yen for mid-level, and 8-15 million yen for senior. Successful commercial TV or film projects can earn even more.

**Q: Can foreigners work as directors in Japan?**
A: Yes, it is possible. You can apply with an Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa if you have specialized knowledge or experience in directing. Japanese language ability and industry networks are important.
