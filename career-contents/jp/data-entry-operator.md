# Data Entry Operator — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 312 (JSCO)**

An office job in companies or government agencies that accurately inputs and manages documents and data into systems. Primarily involves routine keyboard operations, requiring speed and accuracy.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Automation through AI and RPA is advancing rapidly, and simple data entry work is sure to decline. Especially routine tasks will be replaced by AI, limiting human roles to exception handling and data quality management.

Career paths are limited, and as clerical positions shrink, skill transition from input tasks to data analysis or system management is required.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Transcribing customer data
- Entering invoice data
- Tabulating survey results

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Checking and correcting OCR misrecognitions
- Detecting anomalies and generating reports
- Designing data cleansing rules

**Human moat:**

- Judging non-standard data
- Communicating with clients
- Proposing business process improvements

**Skills to build:**

- Excel VBA
- SQL
- Data Visualization (Tableau, etc.)
- Business Knowledge (Accounting, Logistics, etc.)

**Key growth areas:**

- AI Replacement
- RPA
- Data Quality Management
- Online Operations

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a data entry operator?**
A: It varies by experience level, but is generally lower compared to other occupations: 2.5–3.5 million yen for entry level, 3.5–4.5 million yen for intermediate, and up to 5.5 million yen for advanced.

**Q: Can foreigners work as data entry operators?**
A: Data entry work is often considered simple labor and is usually not eligible for the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa. However, if combined with data analysis or system management, it may be possible. Part-time work on a student visa is allowed.

**Q: How can I advance my career?**
A: It's important to expand your skills from data entry to data analysis and system management. Consider obtaining advanced qualifications in bookkeeping and Excel, and look into transitioning to accounting or sales administration.
