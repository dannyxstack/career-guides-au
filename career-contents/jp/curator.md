# Curator — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 242 (JSCO)**

Professional responsible for collection, preservation, exhibition, and educational activities in museums and art galleries. Plays a role in academic knowledge and cultural property protection.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career advancement: Assistant Curator → Curator → Senior Curator → Director. Performance and expertise are valued; master's or doctoral degree holders have an advantage.

- Basic search work

**Human moat:**

- Collaboration with local communities

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much does a curator earn?**
A: Starting salary is around 3-4 million yen annually, but it's low due to many non-regular positions. Regular staff can earn 5-7 million yen based on experience, but high income is not expected except for management.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a curator in Japan?**
A: Residence status 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' required. A degree equivalent to a curator qualification and practical experience in a museum are required. Japanese proficiency (N1 or above) is almost mandatory.

**Q: What routes are available to become a curator?**
A: The typical path is to take the necessary courses for a curator qualification at university, then after graduation, aim for a regular position through part-time work or internships at museums. A master's degree or higher gives an advantage in hiring.
