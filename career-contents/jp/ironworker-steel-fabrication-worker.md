# Ironworker / Steel Fabrication Worker — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 525 (JSCO)**

Cuts, bends, and welds steel to produce structures and tanks; a skilled trade in high demand in manufacturing and construction.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 85,000. Demand in construction and plant work remains stable, but early career turnover is causing labor shortages. Not covered by Specified Skilled Worker; automation impact is limited long-term.

**Competition: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Full automation with AI and robots is difficult, but NC cutting and automated welding robots are reducing simple cutting and welding processes. Advanced skills and on-site adaptability will remain human tasks.

Starts as apprentice, becomes independent in 3–5 years; further welding certifications lead to leadership roles. Factory management or self-employment options.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine straight-line cutting
- Simple fillet welding
- Marking and scribing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Simulation of complex bending
- Precision control with 3D measurement
- Education using datafied expert movements

**Human moat:**

- Adaptive on-site responses
- Experience-based judgment of distortion and deformation
- Beautiful finish by manual welding

**Skills to build:**

- NC machine tool operation
- CAD/CAM skills
- Welding robot teaching
- Non-destructive testing

**Key growth areas:**

- Steel frame fabrication
- Shipbuilding
- Plant piping
- Automotive bodywork

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of ironworkers and sheet metal workers?**
A: Depending on experience, starting around 3 million JPY, mid-level 4 to 5.5 million JPY, and skilled 5.5 to 7 million JPY. It varies based on certifications and workplace size.

**Q: Is it difficult for foreigners to obtain a Japanese visa for this occupation?**
A: Work under the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is possible but requires practical experience and expertise. The Specified Skilled Worker visa currently excludes this field, limiting transfer routes from technical intern training.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities in this occupation?**
A: By acquiring advanced welding certifications (e.g., Welding Management Engineer), one can become a site supervisor, quality control manager, or even start their own steel fabrication shop. There are also paths to management roles at construction companies or plant manufacturers.
