# Stone Cutter — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 692 (JSCO)**

Occupation of mining and processing stone in quarries. Requires traditional stonemasonry skills and heavy machinery operation.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school diploma | 0 years | $0~$0 (No educational requirements; OJT-centered) |
| Vocational training school (Stone Processing) | 1 year | $500,000~$800,000 (Prefectural vocational training schools) |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 5,000. Employment is declining due to decreased construction demand and alternative materials. Aging workforce and difficulty securing younger workers are challenges.

**Competition: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

From apprentice to skilled worker, advancing to quarry management or self-employment.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- simple quarry work
- heavy material handling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- precision cutting and processing (AI-controlled machinery)
- optimization of quarrying plans

**Human moat:**

- artisan's aesthetic sense
- knowledge of traditional techniques
- on-site adaptability

**Skills to build:**

- operation of digitally controlled machinery
- 3D measurement technology
- knowledge of stone materials

**Key growth areas:**

- Decrease in stone demand
- Alternative materials
- quarry closure
- automation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Confident in physical strength
- Dexterous hands
- Interested in traditional skills

**Who is not suited**
- Dislike physical labor
- Want stable income

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a stone cutter?**
A: Depends on experience; for mid-level, around 4-5 million yen.

**Q: Can foreigners work as stone cutters?**
A: It may be possible under Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa, but Japanese proficiency and skill certification are required.

**Q: Does this occupation have future prospects?**
A: Demand is declining, with few young entrants.
