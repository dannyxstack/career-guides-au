# Professional Athlete — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 245 (JSCO)**

Professional athletes compete in various sports using advanced skills, aiming for results in games and tournaments. Entertainment value is also required.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 85,000. While the declining birthrate and aging population raise concerns about a shrinking pool of athletes, demand remains stable due to increased international competitions and media exposure. Top athletes receive high compensation, but competition is extremely fierce.

**Competition: very high (★★★★★).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

The path from amateur to professional is narrow. After retirement, common career transitions include coaching, commentary, and sports-related businesses. An increasing number of athletes also hold second jobs.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of professional athletes?**
A: Varies greatly by sport and performance, but many earn under 5 million yen. Top athletes can earn hundreds of millions.

**Q: How can foreigners work as professional athletes in Japan?**
A: An entertainer visa is required. Contracts with Japanese clubs or organizations are essential, along with proven skills and achievements.

**Q: What career options exist after retirement?**
A: Various paths include coaching, commentary, running a sports gym, or working at a general company. Some use second-career support programs.
