# Actor — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 233 (JSCO)**

A profession that performs roles based on scripts in film, television, stage, etc., delivering emotion and empathy to audiences and viewers.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

AI can synthesize an actor's expressions and voice, but humans have an advantage in emotional expression and improvisation. However, extras and some voice acting roles may be replaced.

Career starts with theater company membership or auditions, progressing to lead roles or directing after gaining experience. Freelance is common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Extra
- Simple voice acting
- CG stunt doubles

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Facial capture
- CG avatar operation
- Acting assistance via speech synthesis

**Human moat:**

- Emotional expression
- Improv
- Audience rapport

**Skills to build:**

- Acting ability
- Improvisation
- Physical expression
- Understanding of digital technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Video Distribution
- Video Production
- Voice Actor Demand
- Performing Arts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of an actor?**
A: Beginner: around 1-3 million yen. Intermediate: 3-8 million yen. Advanced: over 8 million yen.

**Q: Can foreigners work as actors in Japan?**
A: Possible with an entertainment visa, but requires performance contracts and is very challenging.

**Q: Is a training school necessary to become an actor?**
A: Not mandatory, but it's common to learn basics at vocational schools or theater companies.
