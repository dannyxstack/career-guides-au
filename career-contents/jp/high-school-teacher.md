# High School Teacher — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 194 (JSCO)**

High school teachers are education professionals who provide subject instruction, career guidance, and life guidance to high school students. Based on the Courses of Study, they support student growth through classes, homeroom, and club activities.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (JPY) |
|---|---|---:|
| University (Teacher Training Course) | 4 years | $2,500,000~$4,000,000 (About ¥500,000/year at national universities, about ¥1,000,000/year at private universities) |
| Obtain a teaching certificate (including teaching practicum) | During enrollment | $50,000~$100,000 (Practicum costs and application fees) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| High School Teacher License (Class 1) | Each prefectural board of education | Required | Required per subject; complete designated credits at university |
| High School Teacher License (Advanced Class) | Each prefectural board of education | Optional | Advanced qualification such as a master's degree |
| Pass the teacher recruitment exam | Each prefectural or ordinance-designated city board of education | Optional | Required for public school employment |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 250,000. Due to the declining birth rate, the number of students is decreasing, leading to a shrinking demand for teachers. However, specialized subject knowledge and club activity guidance are still required, and competition continues in urban areas.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After obtaining a teaching license, public school teachers must pass recruitment exams, while private schools require applications. Career paths include promotion to head teacher, vice principal, principal, or positions on the board of education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Grading periodic tests
- Automatic generation of teaching materials
- Analysis of student learning data

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a high school teacher?**
A: For public schools, the average annual income is about 7 million yen (with about 10 years of experience). For private schools, it ranges from 6 to 8 million yen, depending on the school.

**Q: How can a foreigner teach at a Japanese high school?**
A: Must hold a Japanese teaching license and have Japanese language proficiency (N1 level). While the 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' visa status applies, hiring quotas are limited.

**Q: Is it easy to change careers from teaching to another occupation?**
A: It's not easy. With little work experience outside of education, switching to other industries is difficult. Main options include education-related companies or cram school instructors.
