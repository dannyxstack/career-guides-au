# Company Director — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 021 (JSCO)**

A managerial occupation that involves determining corporate management policies and overseeing business execution as a board director, auditor, or executive officer of a stock company.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,350,000. Demand is stable due to corporate governance enhancement and succession shortages, but new positions are limited. Business succession in SMEs is a challenge.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

AI supports management decisions with data analysis, but final decisions and leadership remain human. Risk of AI replacement is low.

Promotion typically requires long-term achievements and internal advancement. MBAs and specialized certifications are advantageous. External appointments are also increasing.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a company director?**
A: For SME executives: ¥8-12 million; for large companies: tens of millions to over ¥100 million. Varies greatly by company size and performance.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a company director in Japan?**
A: Yes. You need a Business Manager visa and must establish or participate in managing a company with capital of at least ¥5 million.

**Q: What is the career path to becoming a director?**
A: Typically, after years of experience and a department manager role, one is promoted to director. An MBA or professional qualifications (certified public accountant, lawyer) can be advantageous.
