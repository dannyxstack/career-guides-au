# Telecommunications Network Engineer — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 106 (JSCO)**

A specialist responsible for the design, construction, operation, and maintenance of communication networks, supporting information and communication infrastructure such as mobile phone networks and optical fiber networks.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

After joining, work begins with on-site tasks and maintenance, progressing to design and management with experience. Career advancement to project manager or consultant is possible through certification and specialization.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- routine network monitoring tasks
- automation-capable configuration change tasks
- initial fault isolation response

**Where AI augments the role:**

- network design optimization
- fault prediction and preventive maintenance
- security incident response

**Human moat:**

- advanced troubleshooting skills
- complex network design
- customer communication
- adaptability to new technologies

**Skills to build:**

- cloud networking (AWS, Azure)
- network automation (Ansible, Python)
- security (e.g., CCNP Security)
- SDN/NFV

**Key growth areas:**

- 5G
- IoT
- optical fiber
- telecommunications infrastructure

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the annual income of a telecommunications network engineer?**
A: Entry-level: 3.5-4.5 million yen, mid-career: 5-7 million yen, senior: 7-10 million yen. Major carriers and highly specialized positions may offer more.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a telecommunications network engineer in Japan?**
A: The residence status of 'Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services' is common. A university degree or practical experience is required. 'Specified Skilled Worker (i)' (Information and Communication field) will also be available from 2024, requiring passing an exam and Japanese proficiency N4 or higher.

**Q: Is there a future for telecommunications network engineers?**
A: Demand remains strong due to the expansion of 5G/6G and IoT. Although AI-driven automation is progressing, advanced decision-making in design and operations management still requires human input, so the future prospects are high.
