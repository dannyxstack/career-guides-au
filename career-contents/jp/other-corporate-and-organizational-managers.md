# Other Corporate and Organizational Managers — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 039 (JSCO)**

Top management of non-profit organizations and various groups, responsible for organizational management, strategic planning, and external negotiations.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 120,000. Due to the declining birthrate and aging population, consolidation of non-profit organizations is progressing, and management positions are on the decline. However, demand for highly specialized personnel remains due to the need for enhanced governance.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

While AI contributes to streamlining administrative tasks, human judgment and interpersonal skills are essential for top management. This field is expanded through AI utilization.

Internal promotion from general positions is typical. Obtaining qualifications such as an MBA or Master of Public Administration is advantageous for career advancement. Employment opportunities vary greatly by industry and organizational size.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine administrative tasks (schedule coordination, report writing)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Strategic decision-making, external negotiations, human resource development

**Human moat:**

- Leadership
- Interest coordination ability
- Understanding of organizational culture

**Skills to build:**

- Strategic thinking
- Facilitation
- Digital literacy

**Key growth areas:**

- Non-profit organization
- Organizational governance
- Organizational restructuring
- Public management

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for this occupation?**
A: It depends on experience and organization size, but typically ¥6-10 million for managerial positions. Senior executives may exceed ¥15 million.

**Q: Can foreigners work in this occupation in Japan?**
A: Yes, but the Business Manager visa is for company executives, and the Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services visa is for professionals. Without managerial experience at a Japanese corporation/organization, obtaining a visa is difficult.
