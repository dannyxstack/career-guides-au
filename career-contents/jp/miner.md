# Miner — career analysis · Japan

**Occupation code: 691 (JSCO)**

Occupation that excavates mineral resources in mines. Involves drilling, blasting, and hauling underground, supporting the subsurface resource industry.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blaster License | Prefectural Public Safety Commission | Required | National license for handling explosives. Requires work experience before exam. |
| Mine Safety Technical Officer Certificate | Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry | Optional | Required for safety management. |
| Specified Skills Evaluation Test (Mining) | Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare / Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry | Optional | Useful for transitioning from Technical Intern Training. |

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Japan issues status-of-residence work visas by field, and high-scoring professionals can use the Highly Skilled Professional route, which offers a faster path to permanent residence. Eligibility depends on your qualifications and employer, not on any single occupation.

**Official immigration source:** [Immigration Services Agency of Japan](https://www.isa.go.jp/en/)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income of a miner?**
A: Varies by experience/qualifications; entry-level 3-4 million yen, veteran 5.5-7.5 million yen. Overtime and underground allowances often paid separately.

**Q: Can foreigners work as miners?**
A: Yes, with Specified Skilled Worker (i) (Mining) status, passing skill test & JLPT N4+. However, domestic job openings are limited.

**Q: How to become a miner with no experience?**
A: After being hired by a mining company, on-site training is common. Obtaining qualifications such as blasting technician licenses is effective for career advancement. It is also possible to learn the basics at vocational schools.
