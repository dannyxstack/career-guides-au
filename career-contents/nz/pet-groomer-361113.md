# Pet Groomer — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 361113 (ANZSCO)**

Pet groomers provide services such as bathing, clipping, styling, and nail trimming for pets in pet shops, veterinary clinics, etc. This occupation can apply for an Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV), but it is not on the long-term shortage or green list, with limited skilled migration pathways, suitable for experienced individuals or entrepreneurs.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 250. With rising pet ownership in New Zealand (about 68% of households own pets), demand for pet grooming services is steadily growing. However, this occupation is small, with job opportunities mainly in major cities like Auckland and Christchurch, and competition is moderate. Employment growth is expected to be stable over the next 5 years, with a high proportion of part-time or self-employment.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior pet groomers can advance to senior groomers or store managers, and with experience can start their own studios. Some groomers become certified mentors or judges. The industry in New Zealand has high recognition, but advancement requires continuous learning of styling skills and customer service.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic bathing and drying (fully automatic equipment)
- Appointment scheduling and customer communication (chatbot)
- Photography and editing (AI imaging tools)
- Inventory and order management (automated systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI tools assist in designing pet styles (reference images and trend analysis)
- Smart camera system records before-and-after comparison to enhance presentation
- AI health monitoring devices warn about skin issues and guide care
- Automated marketing and social media content generation to expand customer base

**Human moat:**

- Creativity and precision in manual pruning and shaping
- Safe handling and calming of anxious/restless pets
- Building Trust and Personalized Service with Pet Owners
- On-site judgment for sudden health issues (e.g., wounds, allergies)

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced Trimming and Creative Styling (e.g., painting, complex carving)
- Pet behavior science and positive reinforcement techniques
- Basic veterinary knowledge (dermatology, first aid)
- Using AI tools (image generation, social media automation)
- Customer management and social media operations
- Safety and hygiene compliance (Australian standards)

**Key growth areas:**

- Steady demand
- Pet ownership growth
- Self-employment opportunities
- AEWV pathway

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa) | The most common work visa pathway requires a job offer from an approved employer, with matching skills, and is not a permanent residency pathway. |
| Subclass SMC (Skilled Migrant Category) | Skilled migration, but this occupation is not on the skills shortage list; requires employer support and a salary meeting a threshold (approximately above the median). Under the 6-point system, direct application is possible but approval is difficult. |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a pet groomer in New Zealand?**
A: Entry level approx. NZD 35,000-45,000/year, experienced up to NZD 58,000-75,000, but self-employed income varies significantly depending on client volume and pricing.

**Q: Can pet groomers settle in New Zealand through skilled migration?**
A: High difficulty. This occupation is not on the Green List, only eligible for AEWV work visa or SMC requiring employer support and salary threshold, but under the 6-point system, if salary reaches twice the median, it is possible to apply, but competition is fierce. Self-employed entrepreneurship immigration is another option.

**Q: What are the recognised pet grooming training courses in New Zealand?**
A: Mainly NZPGI Pet Grooming Certificate Level 3 (3-6 months, about NZD 3,000-8,000) and NZ Certificate in Animal Technology (1 year, about NZD 12,000-20,000). Internships or apprenticeships can also accumulate experience.
