# Aquaculture Farmer — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 121111 (ANZSCO)**

In New Zealand, manage fish, shellfish or aquatic plant farms, including feeding, water quality monitoring, disease control and harvesting. This occupation is on the Green List (Tier 1), eligible applicants can directly apply for residence with good immigration prospects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Start as a junior aquaculturist, can advance to farm supervisor, manager, or move into R&D, quality control, and other specialist roles. Experienced individuals can start their own business or become industry consultants, participating in sustainable aquaculture projects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding system replaces manual scattering
- Drone/underwater robot inspection of cages replaces manual patrols
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor, replacing manual sampling
- Automated sorting and counting systems replace manual sorting
- Robotic arms replacing manual net pulling in fishing operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Data analysis tools assist in optimizing breeding density and feeding strategies
- Remote sensing and GIS technology enhance sea area site selection and disaster warning
- Machine vision aids early disease identification, improving diagnostic accuracy
- Automated record-keeping systems improve production ledger management efficiency
- Predictive models assist in weather and market supply-demand forecasting.

**Human moat:**

- Equipment maintenance and troubleshooting in complex environments
- Empirical diagnosis of sudden illnesses or abnormal behaviors
- Government/community communication and compliance management
- On-site decision-making for biosafety and emergency response
- Fine manual operations such as parent selection and surgical tagging

**Skills to build:**

- Automated farming system operation and maintenance
- Basic troubleshooting of sensors and IoT devices
- Basic data analysis and visualization
- Drone/underwater robot operation
- Aquatic disease diagnosis and biosecurity knowledge
- Sustainable aquaculture regulations and certification standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Green List Tier 1
- Aquaculture Expansion
- Sustainability Demand
- Regional Skilled

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Have a strong interest in aquaculture and marine biology, willing to work outdoors/in remote areas.
- Possess endurance and adaptability to humid, cold, or offshore working environments.
- Those seeking fast-track migration to New Zealand with an occupation on the Green List

**Who is not suited**
- Cannot accept rural or island life, frequent travel, or seasonal work patterns.
- Aversion to high physical exertion, prolonged standing, or cold water work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for aquaculture farmers in New Zealand?**
A: Junior positions annual salary approx NZD 45,000-55,000; intermediate supervisors NZD 55,000-70,000; senior managers up to NZD 70,000-90,000. Some large companies or management roles may pay more.

**Q: How can an aquaculture farmer immigrate to New Zealand?**
A: This occupation is on Green List Tier 1; meeting the median wage (approx. NZD 29.66/hour) allows direct residence application. Also eligible via Skilled Migrant Category (6-point system) or AEWV work visa.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to work in aquaculture?**
A: Typically requires a New Zealand Certificate in Aquaculture (Level 4) or a related diploma/bachelor's degree. Practical experience is more important; relevant qualifications can increase competitiveness.
