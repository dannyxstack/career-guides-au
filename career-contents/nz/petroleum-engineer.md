# Petroleum Engineer — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 233612 (ANZSCO)**

Petroleum engineers design and optimize plans for oil and gas exploration, drilling, production, and transport. In New Zealand, this occupation is on the long-term skill shortage list and can lead to PR through the Green List direct residence or skilled migration, with good job prospects.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 300. New Zealand's oil and gas industry is small but stable, mainly concentrated in the Taranaki region. With the global energy transition, demand for petroleum engineers is expected to remain steady; domestic output is limited, but maintaining existing fields and integrating new energy technologies create a few positions. Long-term shortage list supports migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior engineers can start in drilling or production engineering and advance to senior engineer or project manager with experience. Some move into new energy (e.g. geothermal) or consulting. Continuous learning is required, and registration as a Chartered Professional Engineer (CPEng) can boost career prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated analysis of seismic data interpretation and reservoir modeling
- Drilling parameter optimization and real-time monitoring decisions.
- Production prediction and optimization algorithms
- Routine report generation and compliance document organization

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Multi-well sensor data fusion and anomaly warning using AI
- AI-assisted reservoir simulation for faster option screening
- Machine learning optimization for well completion design and stimulation measures
- AI-driven asset integrity management for predictive maintenance

**Human moat:**

- Emergency decision-making and handling of on-site drilling accidents
- Design decision-making under complex geological conditions
- Communication and coordination with stakeholders such as regulatory bodies and communities.
- Interdisciplinary team leadership and project management

**Skills to build:**

- Machine learning and data science fundamentals
- Python/R programming and automation scripts
- Advanced reservoir simulation software (e.g., CMG, Eclipse)
- Internet of Things and Sensor Data Analysis
- Project management and leadership
- Digital twin and visualization tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Green List Tier 1
- Long Term Skill Shortage List
- Skilled Migrant Category
- Taranaki region

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|

| Subclass SMC (Skilled Migrant Category (6 points)) | Accumulate 6 points for residency through qualifications/registration/salary etc., with bonus points for long-term shortage occupation. |

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Graduates in petroleum engineering or related engineering disciplines
- Those with oil and gas industry experience and willing to relocate to the Taranaki region of New Zealand
- People passionate about the energy industry and adaptable to oilfield work environments

**Who is not suited**
- People unwilling to work in regional areas (e.g., New Plymouth).
- For those seeking big city life and wide career options

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a petroleum engineer in New Zealand?**
A: Entry-level around 65,000-85,000, mid-level 90,000-130,000, senior 140,000-200,000 NZD annually, including bonus may be higher.

**Q: What are the pathways for petroleum engineers to immigrate to New Zealand?**
A: This occupation is on Tier 1 of the Green List; meeting salary and qualification requirements allows direct residence application; also eligible via Skilled Migrant Category (SMC 6-point system) or Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) pathway to residency.

**Q: What is the job market for petroleum engineers in New Zealand?**
A: Small market size, around 300 people, concentrated in the Taranaki region. Supported by long-term shortage list, but domestic production is limited, offering stable but slow growth opportunities.
