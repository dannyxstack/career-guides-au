# Electrical Engineering Technician — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 312312 (ANZSCO)**

Assist electrical engineers in designing, testing, installing, and repairing electrical systems and equipment, covering power distribution, control systems, and industrial automation. This occupation is eligible for residence under New Zealand's Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) and Green List (Tier 2), with stable employment prospects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior technicians can advance to senior technicians or supervisors by gaining experience and obtaining New Zealand electrical registration (e.g., EWRB), or transition to project management or design roles. Some later develop into electrical engineers or technical managers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use multimeter for routine circuit testing and troubleshooting.
- Draw electrical schematics and wiring diagrams according to standard templates.
- Conduct regular inspections of electrical equipment and complete standardised checklists
- Compile technical documentation and test reports
- Calculate basic circuit parameters such as current, voltage, and power

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizing industrial automation control systems using AI simulation software (e.g., ETAP, MATLAB)
- Quickly locate complex electrical faults through AI-assisted diagnostic tools
- Using machine learning algorithms to predict equipment maintenance cycles and failure probabilities
- Using natural language processing to quickly search technical manuals and new regulations
- Use digital twin technology to simulate system upgrade scenarios

**Human moat:**

- On-site handling of non-standard electrical faults (e.g., old equipment upgrades, environmental interference)
- Conduct compliance inspection and safety assessment for critical infrastructure (hospitals, data centers).
- Coordinate with engineers and construction teams to resolve installation issues
- Quick decision-making under high-pressure situations like power outages and emergency repairs
- Understand and comply with local electrical regulations and certification standards (e.g., AS/NZS 3000)

**Skills to build:**

- PLC programming and SCADA system integration.
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g., AutoCAD Electrical's intelligent wiring)
- Industrial IoT and sensor data analysis
- Power System Simulation Software (ETAP, DIgSILENT)
- New energy technologies (solar PV, energy storage system design)
- Project management and communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Green List Tier 2
- Skilled Migrant Category
- Infrastructure
- Renewable Energy

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much do electrical engineering technicians earn in New Zealand?**
A: Entry-level about NZD 50,000-65,000, mid-level 70,000-90,000, senior 95,000-120,000. Varies by region and industry (e.g., construction vs industrial), Auckland usually slightly higher.

**Q: how can electrical engineering technicians immigrate to New Zealand?**
A: Eligible via Skilled Migrant Category (SMC 6-point system) or Green List Tier 2 work-to-residence pathway. Requires accredited employer sponsorship, salary at median (approx. NZD 67,000), and meeting English requirements.

**Q: Are there many job opportunities for this occupation in New Zealand?**
A: Stable opportunities, especially in construction, infrastructure, and renewable energy. However, competition exists; those with local experience and recognized certifications have greater advantages.
