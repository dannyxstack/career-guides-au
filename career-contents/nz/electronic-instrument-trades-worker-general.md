# Electronic Instrument Trades Worker (General) — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 342314 (ANZSCO)**

Responsible for installing, testing, maintaining, and repairing electronic instruments and control systems, covering industrial automation, medical equipment, and communication systems. This occupation is on New Zealand's Green List for skilled migration and can lead to permanent residence via direct residence or work-to-residence pathways.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Start as an electrician or electronics technician, with potential to advance to senior technician, project manager, or automation engineer. Holding a New Zealand registered electrician license (EST) can boost salary and employment competitiveness.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate circuit schematics and PCB layouts
- Perform standard electrical calculations and simulation verification
- Writing and testing routine control logic code
- Automatically generate test reports and documentation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted rapid generation of multiple design options and performance comparison
- Using machine learning to predict equipment failures and perform preventive maintenance
- Quickly retrieving technical documentation and standards through natural language interaction
- AI optimizes power system load distribution and energy efficiency
- Automated checking of design compliance with safety standards

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard or highly customized system integration needs
- Innovative design and decision-making under uncertainty
- Take on legal and ethical responsibilities as project lead
- Cross-team communication coordination and client needs insight
- Experience-based fault diagnosis and on-site debugging

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool applications (e.g., Altair AI, Ansys AI)
- Data analytics and machine learning basics (Python/TensorFlow)
- Digital twin and simulation technology
- Renewable energy system design (e.g., photovoltaic, energy storage)
- Embedded systems and IoT development
- Project management and interdisciplinary communication

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level of electronic instrument workers?**
A: Entry-level annual salary approximately NZD 55,000-70,000, mid-level 70,000-90,000, senior 90,000-120,000. Salary increases significantly after obtaining a licensed electrician certification.

**Q: How does this occupation immigrate through the Green List?**
A: This occupation is on the Green List Tier 1, meeting the median wage ($29.66/hour) and qualification requirements, allowing direct application for a Straight to Residence Visa; or transition to residence after 2 years of work through Tier 2.

**Q: What qualifications are required?**
A: Typically requires an NZQF Level 4 Electronic Engineering certificate (approx. 2 years) or higher. Overseas qualifications need NZQA assessment, possibly requiring bridging courses.
