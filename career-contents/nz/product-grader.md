# Product Grader — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 839312 (ANZSCO)**

In New Zealand's mining, agriculture, and manufacturing sectors, Product Graders visually and mechanically inspect raw materials or finished products, grading them by size, quality, purity, and other standards to ensure compliance with export or processing requirements. This occupation is typically eligible for residence through an Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) or the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression: from junior grader to senior grader, quality control supervisor, or shift to quality management, supply chain coordination, etc. After gaining experience, obtain relevant industry certifications to advance to management or become an independent quality auditor.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use visual or measuring tools to inspect dimensions, quality, and purity of raw materials or finished products; AI machine vision can do it faster and more accurately.
- Record classification results and fill in paper or electronic forms; AI automatically collects data and generates reports
- Manually sorting defective products on a conveyor belt; robot arms with AI vision can automatically sort
- Classifying products based on simple criteria (e.g., color, weight); algorithm model can grade in real time.
- Regular calibration and inspection of tools (e.g., callipers, balances); AI auto-calibration systems are becoming common.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate and maintain AI-driven automatic grading equipment, requiring understanding of sensors and software interfaces
- Verify the accuracy of AI classification results, optimize model performance through sample rechecking
- Handle complex defects that AI struggles to judge (such as internal cracks, mixed stains); human experience is still necessary
- Participate in AI system deployment and debugging when upgrading production lines, turning domain knowledge into training data
- Manages workflow of multiple AI graders, coordinating maintenance and exception handling.

**Human moat:**

- In non-standardized products (e.g., natural ores, agricultural products), combine touch, smell, and other senses for comprehensive judgment
- Flexible decision-making in handling extreme abnormal situations (e.g., equipment failure, raw material batch mutations)
- Final release decisions involving safety responsibilities (e.g., signing off on export quality compliance)
- Understanding and adaptation to local industry implicit standards (e.g., specific client preferences)

**Skills to build:**

- Computer vision system operation and basic maintenance
- Data analysis and statistical process control (SPC)
- Python or simple programming for automated report generation
- Basic sensor and PLC knowledge
- Understanding of quality standards systems (e.g., ISO 9001)
- Equipment anomaly handling and troubleshooting

**Key growth areas:**

- Export-driven
- Quality Control
- AEWV
- Primary Industry

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for product graders in New Zealand?**
A: Junior annual salary approx NZD 45,000-55,000, mid to senior can reach NZD 68,000-80,000. Salary varies significantly by industry, region, and experience.

**Q: Is it easy for a product grader to immigrate to New Zealand?**
A: This occupation is not on the Green List, but you can apply for the Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) after gaining experience via an Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV). It must meet the salary threshold (currently about NZD 29.66/hour) and qualification requirements.

**Q: What is the employment outlook for product graders in New Zealand?**
A: Employment prospects stable; with continued exports of primary industries (dairy, meat, fruit, etc.), demand for qualified graders remains. However, automation may affect some positions.
