# Drycleaner — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 811512 (ANZSCO)**

Dry cleaners operate dry cleaning and washing machines in dry cleaning shops or laundries, responsible for cleaning, stain removal, ironing, and finishing of clothing and textiles. In New Zealand, this occupation can enter via the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV), but is not on the Green List or shortage list; Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) requires higher salary or points.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Common path: from cleaner/ironer to dry-cleaning technician, then to store manager; some gain experience and start their own dry-cleaning business. May also move into laundry factory management, textile care technical consultant, etc.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Sort clothes by procedure and clean using dry cleaning machines
- Standardised ironing using an ironing machine
- Routine chemical treatment for simple stains
- Packaging and labeling sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI vision inspection systems assist in identifying stubborn stains and recommend solutions.
- Intelligent devices monitor washing parameters in real time to optimize energy use and efficiency.
- IoT system automatically schedules maintenance, reducing downtime
- VR training modules accelerate novices' mastery of complex decontamination techniques

**Human moat:**

- Manual stain removal techniques for expensive or special fabrics
- Assessing client communication and customized service needs.
- Rapid diagnostics and manual repair of equipment faults
- Comprehensive experience-based judgment of garment history and materials

**Skills to build:**

- Smart washing equipment operation and basic programming
- Fabric science and advanced stain removal technology
- Data analysis (washing parameter optimization)
- Customer relationship management and customized service design
- Equipment maintenance and troubleshooting (mechanical/electronic)

**Key growth areas:**

- Steady
- Part-time
- Competition
- Low skill

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa) | Accredited Employer Work Visa: Requires a job offer from an accredited employer with a salary not below the median wage (NZD 29.66/hour, 2024 rate) or industry agreement wage. |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for dry cleaners in New Zealand?**
A: Junior salary approximately NZD 38,000-45,000/year, mid-level NZD 45,000-52,000, supervisor up to NZD 52,000-60,000. Salary is low, does not meet the 1.5x median for skilled migration (approx. NZD 44.49/hour) requirement.

**Q: Can a dry cleaner migrate to New Zealand?**
A: This occupation is not on the Green List or shortage list. Skilled migration requires a salary 1.5 times the median and meeting the 6-point system, which is usually difficult. The main pathway is the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV), but transitioning to residence is challenging.

**Q: In which cities are there more dry cleaner job opportunities?**
A: There are more job opportunities in dry cleaners and hotels in major cities like Auckland, Wellington, and Christchurch, as well as tourist towns, but overall positions are limited.
