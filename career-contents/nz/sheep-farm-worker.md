# Sheep Farm Worker — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 842317 (ANZSCO)**

Perform physical labor on sheep farms including feeding, shearing, marking, and fence maintenance; responsible for daily flock management and wool production. This occupation can lead to immigration in New Zealand via AEWV or SMC, but must meet salary or skill requirements.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Can progress from junior worker to senior shepherd, farm supervisor, or manager. With experience, can also move into livestock advisory roles or self-employed farming.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic shearing robots for wool harvesting
- Automated feeding systems complete daily feed dispensing
- Uses drones or automatic fencing systems to monitor flock positions
- Smart weighing and sorting equipment replaces manual sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes grazing routes using GPS navigation and electronic fence monitoring
- Use data analysis tools to track sheep health and growth trends
- Remote monitoring of wool quality and yield via sensor data.
- Using mobile apps to record and manage medical tasks such as vaccinations

**Human moat:**

- Handling emergency animal health issues (such as dystocia, rapid diagnosis of diseases)
- Manual pruning and wound treatment in complex environments
- Flexibly adjusting work plans in remote or harsh conditions
- Careful observation of sheep emotions and stress behavior
- Communicate and coordinate with farm manager and execute non-standard tasks.

**Skills to build:**

- Skilled in operating and maintaining automated shearing equipment
- Basic data analysis and use of digital farm management software
- Drone inspection and remote monitoring technology
- Basics of veterinary medicine (especially prevention and emergency treatment)
- Sustainable agriculture practices and precision agriculture technologies
- Basic mechanical maintenance and electronic system troubleshooting

**Key growth areas:**

- Stable
- AEWV
- Skilled Migrant Category
- Manual Labor

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa) | Applicable to workers with accredited employer sponsorship, requiring wages at the median rate (approx. NZD 29.66 per hour). |
| Subclass SMC (Skilled Migrant Category) | If the skilled migration salary threshold (1.5 times the median) is met, it may be eligible, but this occupation is generally not considered skilled |
| Subclass Green List T2 (Green List Tier 2 (Work to Residence)) | This occupation is not on the Green List unless with special skills or high salary. |
| Subclass Working Holiday (Working Holiday Visa) | Short-term working holiday visas are common for seasonal farm work |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sheep farm worker?**
A: Entry-level: NZ$20–25/hour, annual NZ$42,000–52,000; mid-level: NZ$52,000–62,000; senior: NZ$62,000–75,000, depending on experience and skills.

**Q: Can sheep farm workers immigrate to New Zealand?**
A: Usually cannot directly apply for skilled migration (not on the Green List or high-skilled category). However, you can gain experience through the AEWV work visa and then transfer to other pathways, such as reaching 1.5 times the median wage or having employer sponsorship.

**Q: What are the career progression paths for sheep farm workers?**
A: Starting as a junior worker, can advance to senior shepherd, farm supervisor, or even manager. Also can become a shearer, veterinary assistant, or agricultural advisor through training.
