# Nurseryperson — career analysis · New Zealand

**Occupation code: 362411 (ANZSCO)**

In New Zealand, a nursery worker (nursery) is involved in plant cultivation, care, and sales, and is a low-skilled occupation. Entry is possible via an Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV), but generally does not meet conditions for Skilled Migration or Green List direct residence unless promoted to horticulturist or farm manager.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 4,500. New Zealand's horticulture industry is driven by primary sector demand; nursery work is seasonal, with job opportunities mainly in Auckland, Waikato and other regions. Demand is expected to remain stable over the next five years, but automation may reduce some roles.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior horticultural workers can progress to senior horticulturalists or team supervisors through experience, and with a horticulture diploma, become horticulturists eligible for skilled migration. They can also move into nursery management or farm management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic irrigation system waters based on sensor data, replacing manual watering
- AI vision robots identify and remove weeds, replacing manual weeding labour
- Inventory management system automatically tracks plant quantities and sales, replacing manual counting
- Customer chatbot handles common inquiries (pricing, maintenance), replaces front desk reception

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted pest and disease identification: diagnose by photo, enhancing early detection
- Data analysis tools optimize planting plans: enhance decision-making quality based on historical climate and sales forecasts.
- Automated greenhouse environmental control: AI adjusts temperature and humidity for enhanced precision care
- Drones/robots monitor large-scale nursery conditions to enhance inspection efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Meticulous observation and tactile judgment of plant health and growth conditions
- Ability to communicate face-to-face with clients and provide personalised care recommendations
- Quick response to unexpected situations (e.g., sudden weather changes, pest outbreaks)
- Manual pruning, grafting and other skills requiring fine touch

**Skills to build:**

- Operate and perform basic maintenance on automated irrigation systems and sensors
- Use plant identification apps and AI diagnostic tools
- Learn greenhouse environmental control software (e.g., Priva, HortiMaX)
- Basic data analysis: using Excel or simple tools to track sales and growth data
- Digital Marketing: Using Social Media to Showcase and Sell Plants
- Sustainable practices: mastering green techniques such as composting and water conservation

**Key growth areas:**

- AEWV
- Seasonal Work
- Horticulture
- Plant Nursery

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa) | Accredited Employer Work Visa for nursery work with employer sponsorship, renewable, but must meet median wage (currently NZD 29.66/hour) |

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy outdoor physical work and have a passion for plant cultivation
- Willing to start from entry-level, gradually gain experience or pursue further training to enhance skills
- Able to adapt to seasonal work rhythms and climate changes

**Who is not suited**
- People who want to quickly obtain residency through skilled migration
- Dislike repetitive physical labor or outdoor work environment

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How are the wages for horticultural workers in New Zealand?**
A: Junior gardener annual salary about NZD 42,000-48,000, roughly minimum wage. Experienced mid-level NZD 48,000-55,000, senior or supervisor NZD 55,000-65,000. Salary below median immigration wage; tech migration requires promotion.

**Q: Can gardeners immigrate to New Zealand via skilled migration?**
A: Directly working as a gardener (ANZSCO Skill Level 4) does not meet skill migration or Green List requirements. However, obtaining a horticulture diploma and advancing to a Horticulturist (ANZSCO Skill Level 3) may allow application for skill migration or Green List work-to-residence.
