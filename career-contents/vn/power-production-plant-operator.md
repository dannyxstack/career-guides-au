**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Real-time monitoring of power generation parameters and alarm response
- Perform standardized equipment start-stop operations
- Record and report operational data.
- Adjust routine parameters such as boilers and turbines
- Pattern recognition steps in fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI predictive maintenance to optimize equipment repair schedules
- Conduct accident simulations and training using digital twin simulations
- Using automated systems to analyze large volumes of operational data to assist decision-making
- Remote operation and cross-station coordinated scheduling

**Human moat:**

- quick judgment and handling in emergency situations
- Cross-disciplinary comprehensive analysis of complex system failures
- Effective communication with maintenance teams and dispatch centers
- Social responsibility and safety compliance awareness
- Intuition and feel from on-site experience

**Skills to build:**

- Understand the application of AI and machine learning in predictive maintenance

- Obtain a higher-level professional qualification

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a power production plant operator in Vietnam?**
A: Entry-level operators earn around 8-12 million VND per month, while experienced operators at major plants can earn 15-25 million VND. Additional allowances and shift differentials are common. Salaries vary by plant type (thermal, hydro, renewable) and location, with higher pay in industrial zones.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence affecting power plant operators in Vietnam?**
A: AI is increasingly used for predictive maintenance and grid management, reducing the need for manual monitoring. Operators now require skills in data analysis and SCADA systems. However, AI augments rather than replaces operators, as human judgment remains critical for safety and unexpected events.

**Q: What is the career outlook for power plant operators in Vietnam?**
A: Demand is stable with Vietnam's growing energy needs, especially in renewable sectors like solar and wind. Operators with skills in modern control systems and digital tools have strong prospects. Government investment in power infrastructure ensures ongoing opportunities, though automation may slow hiring in traditional plants.
