**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual recording of crop growth logs and yield data, replaced by automatic sensors
- Manual pest and disease identification replaced by AI image recognition
- Manual calculation of fertilizer amounts replaced by precision agriculture algorithms
- Paper-based bookkeeping and tax calculations are being replaced by financial software

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI tools analyze soil moisture and meteorological data to optimize irrigation timing.
- Use satellite imagery and drones to monitor crop health and provide early warning of pests and diseases
- AI-assisted planting decisions based on historical data and market price forecasts
- Online platforms connecting local consumers with restaurants to expand direct sales channels
- Smart devices monitor storage conditions to reduce post-harvest loss

**Human moat:**

- Physical labour and on-site work (e.g., manual weeding, harvesting)
- Decisions based on local experience (e.g., traditional crop rotation, variety selection)
- Trust relationship with communities and consumers
- Flexible judgment in response to sudden weather and pests

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis and digital record-keeping

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly income for subsistence crop farmers in Malaysia?**
A: Subsistence crop farmers in Malaysia typically earn between RM 800 and RM 1,500 per month, depending on crop type, land size, and market prices. This income is often supplemented by own consumption and seasonal sales. Government subsidies and aid programs may provide additional support.

**Q: What skills are essential for subsistence crop farmers in Malaysia?**
A: Key skills include knowledge of crop cultivation (e.g., paddy, vegetables, fruits), soil management, pest control, and basic financial planning. Physical stamina, weather prediction, and traditional farming techniques are also important. Familiarity with sustainable practices and simple irrigation systems is increasingly valued.

**Q: What is the career outlook for subsistence crop farmers in Malaysia?**
A: The outlook is stable but challenging due to urbanization and climate change. Younger generations often move to cities, leading to an aging workforce. Government initiatives promote modern farming techniques and cooperative models to improve productivity and income. Technology adoption may create opportunities for efficiency gains.
