**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate 3D printing paths for sand moulds/cores using CAD models
- AI vision system detects casting defects in real-time and automatically sorts them
- Robots automatically complete sand mold assembly and pouring operations
- AI optimizes pouring parameters and adjusts cooling system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI simulation software assists in predicting casting shrinkage and porosity locations, guiding process improvements.
- AR headset displays sand core assembly sequence and position calibration
- AI-driven quality analysis system generates defect root cause reports
- The digital twin system monitors the entire casting process in real time and warns of anomalies

**Human moat:**

- Process innovation capability for handling non-standard, small-batch castings
- Skills in manual repair and fine-tuning of complex sand moulds/cores
- On-site decision-making ability for multi-variety quick mold changes.

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly salary for metal moulders and coremakers in Malaysia?**
A: Entry-level metal moulders and coremakers in Malaysia earn around RM 1,800 to RM 2,500 per month. Experienced professionals with specialized skills can earn RM 3,500 to RM 5,000. Salaries vary by location, industry (automotive, aerospace, general manufacturing), and employer. Overtime and shift allowances may increase take-home pay.

**Q: What skills are essential for metal moulders and coremakers in Malaysia?**
A: Key skills include proficiency in reading technical drawings, pattern making, and operating molding machines. Knowledge of casting processes (sand, die, investment) and materials like iron, steel, or aluminum is crucial. Attention to detail, physical stamina, and ability to use hand tools are important. Certification from a technical college or on-the-job training is common.

**Q: How is the job outlook for metal moulders and coremakers in Malaysia?**
A: The demand for metal moulders and coremakers in Malaysia remains steady, driven by manufacturing sectors like automotive and heavy machinery. However, automation and CNC machining may reduce demand for manual roles. Skilled workers who adapt to new technologies and maintain quality will have better opportunities. Job growth is projected to be moderate.
