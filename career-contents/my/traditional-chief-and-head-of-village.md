## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary or allowance for traditional chiefs and heads of villages in Malaysia?**
A: In Malaysia, traditional chiefs (e.g., Penghulu or Ketua Kampung) receive a monthly government allowance, typically ranging from RM 800 to RM 2,000, depending on the state and village size. Some also receive housing or transport perks. However, this is often a part-time stipend, and many hold other jobs concurrently.

**Q: What does a typical day look like for a traditional chief or head of a village in Malaysia?**
A: Daily tasks include mediating local disputes, organizing community events, liaising with government agencies on village needs, managing communal land or facilities, and preserving cultural practices. They also handle administrative matters like recording births and deaths, and issuing letters for residents. The role demands strong interpersonal and leadership skills.

**Q: How is AI impacting the role of traditional chiefs and heads of villages in Malaysia?**
A: AI has minimal direct impact on village leadership, as the role relies on human judgment, personal relationships, and cultural knowledge. Digital tools may aid in record-keeping or communication with authorities, but core duties like mediation and tradition preservation remain AI-resistant. Chiefs may need basic digital literacy to adopt administrative technologies.
