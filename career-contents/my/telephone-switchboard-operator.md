## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic Call Distribution (ACD) system takes over call routing
- Interactive Voice Response (IVR) handles simple queries and routing
- Speech recognition and natural language processing replace basic manual information consultation services
- Automated attendant software enables 24/7 unattended call handling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted real-time customer information and advice to improve service quality
- Natural language processing helps handle multilingual calls, expanding service scope
- Intelligently analyze call data to optimize call routing and staff scheduling
- Automatically record and summarize call content, reducing administrative work

**Human moat:**

- Handle complex, emotional, or contentious customer calls
- Personalized services requiring empathy and interpersonal communication
- Ability to adapt flexibly in emergency or unusual situations
- Multitasking ability (managing multiple lines and systems simultaneously)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis and reporting

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a telephone switchboard operator in Malaysia?**
A: In Malaysia, entry-level operators earn around RM1,500–RM2,000 monthly, while experienced operators in major cities like Kuala Lumpur can earn RM2,500–RM3,500. Pay often includes shift allowances, and senior roles or those handling multilingual calls may command higher rates.

**Q: What skills are essential for a telephone switchboard operator in Malaysia?**
A: Operators need fluency in Malay and English; Mandarin or Tamil is advantageous. Key skills include clear verbal communication, patience, multitasking, and basic computer literacy for using digital switchboard systems. Professional telephone etiquette and the ability to remain calm under pressure are critical.

**Q: How is the career outlook for telephone switchboard operators in Malaysia?**
A: The role is declining due to automation and IVR systems, but demand persists in hotels, hospitals, and large corporations requiring human touch. Opportunities remain in niche industries and as a stepping stone to administrative roles. Upskilling in customer service software can improve prospects.
