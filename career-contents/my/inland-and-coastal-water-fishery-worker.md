## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Malaysia?**
A: The monthly income typically ranges from RM 1,500 to RM 3,500, depending on experience, location, and catch volume. Earnings are often variable and may include profit-sharing arrangements, especially in small-scale fisheries. Government subsidies and seasonal bonuses can supplement base pay.

**Q: What key skills are required to work in Malaysia's inland and coastal fisheries?**
A: Essential skills include knowledge of fishing techniques and navigation, boat handling and maintenance, fish handling and storage, and basic weather forecasting. Physical stamina, teamwork, and familiarity with safety regulations are also crucial. In Malaysia, understanding local marine ecosystems and seasonal patterns is highly valued.

**Q: What is the career outlook for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Malaysia?**
A: The demand for fishery workers remains stable, driven by domestic consumption and aquaculture growth. However, overfishing and environmental changes pose challenges. Opportunities may arise in sustainable fishing practices and ecotourism. Technology adoption, such as GPS and fish finders, is increasing, requiring adaptability.
