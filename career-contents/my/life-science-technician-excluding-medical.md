## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine sample preparation and processing (e.g., pipetting, centrifugation)
- Standard biochemical tests (e.g., ELISA, PCR result interpretation)
- Record and organize experimental data
- Reagent inventory management and ordering
- Repetitive animal husbandry and observation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- High-throughput sequencing data analysis (AI-assisted variant identification)
- Experimental protocol optimization (machine learning predicts optimal conditions)
- Literature mining and hypothesis generation (Natural Language Processing)
- Automatic analysis of microscopic images (cell counting, phenotype recognition)
- Multi-omics data integration (transcriptome and proteome correlation analysis)

**Human moat:**

- Unstructured experimental troubleshooting (e.g., investigating unexpected contamination)
- Ethical Decision-Making and Animal Welfare Assessment
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication (with clinicians, engineers)
- Experimental innovation design and hypothesis generation.
- Regulatory compliance and quality control responsibilities

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of laboratory automation systems (e.g., liquid handling workstations).
- Data visualization and reporting (Tableau, Power BI)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for life science technicians in Malaysia?**
A: Entry-level life science technicians in Malaysia earn around MYR 2,500 to MYR 3,500 per month, while experienced technicians can earn MYR 4,500 to MYR 6,000. Salaries vary by industry, with pharmaceutical and biotechnology firms often paying higher than food testing or academic labs.

**Q: What technical skills are most important for life science technicians in Malaysia?**
A: Key skills include proficiency in laboratory techniques (e.g., PCR, chromatography, microscopy), knowledge of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP), and experience with data analysis software. Employers also value attention to detail, sterile technique, and the ability to document procedures accurately.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence affecting life science technicians in Malaysia?**
A: AI is automating routine data analysis and lab documentation, allowing technicians to focus on complex experiments and equipment maintenance. However, hands-on skills like sample preparation and quality control remain essential. Technicians need to adapt by learning basic programming or AI tools to stay relevant.
