**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)

- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garment patternmaker or cutter in Malaysia?**
A: Entry-level garment patternmakers and cutters in Malaysia earn around RM1,500 to RM1,800 monthly. Experienced workers typically earn RM2,500 to RM3,500, while senior or specialized roles (e.g., in high-fashion or industrial settings) can reach RM5,000. Salaries vary by company size, location (e.g., Kuala Lumpur, Penang), and skill level.

**Q: What key skills are required to work as a garment patternmaker or cutter in Malaysia?**
A: Proficiency in manual and digital pattern drafting, grading, and marker making is essential. Knowledge of cutting techniques for various fabrics, attention to detail, and the ability to interpret design sketches are crucial. Familiarity with CAD software like Gerber or Lectra is increasingly required. Sewing skills and fabric knowledge are also highly valued.

**Q: How is AI impacting garment patternmaking and cutting jobs in Malaysia?**
A: AI automates pattern grading and marker making, increasing efficiency and reducing manual drafting time. However, creative patternmaking and complex fabric adjustments still rely on human expertise. For cutters, AI-assisted systems optimize fabric layout but manual handling remains key. Workers are advised to upskill in digital tools to stay relevant as the industry evolves.
