## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for physicists and astronomers in Malaysia?**
A: Salaries vary by sector. In academia, junior lecturers earn RM 4,500-7,000 monthly; senior researchers in government agencies like MIGHT or ANGKASA earn RM 8,000-12,000. Private sector R&D roles range RM 5,000-10,000. Experience and specialization affect pay.

**Q: What key skills are essential for physicists and astronomers working in Malaysia?**
A: Strong analytical and mathematical skills for modeling and data analysis are crucial. Proficiency in computational tools (Python, MATLAB) and experimental design. For astronomers, knowledge of remote sensing or astrophysics is valued. Soft skills like problem-solving and teamwork are important for collaborative projects.

**Q: What is the career outlook for physicists and astronomers in Malaysia?**
A: Opportunities exist in academia, research institutes (e.g., National Planetarium), and industries like semiconductor, oil & gas, and data analytics. Government initiatives in space technology and renewable energy may create new roles. However, competition is moderate; advanced degrees often required for research positions.
