# Ships' Engineer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 3151 (ISCO08)**

Ships' engineers operate, maintain, and repair marine machinery and equipment on sea-going vessels, including propulsion systems, generators, and auxiliary machinery. In China, where the maritime industry is a key pillar of trade and logistics, ships' engineers are essential for ensuring safe and efficient vessel operations. The role demands strong technical skills, adherence to Chinese maritime regulations, and often involves long periods at sea. Certified engineers are required on all commercial vessels, with demand driven by China's extensive shipping fleet and growing maritime infrastructure.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as junior engineer, advancing to second engineer and then chief engineer. Chinese maritime qualifications (Class 1-3) are key. Many engineers work for Chinese state-owned shipping companies (COSCO, China Merchants) or private fleets. After sea service, transition to shore-based roles (surveyor, technical superintendent, port engineer) is common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine voyage data recording and report generation
- Basic meteorological route planning and optimization
- Marine machinery condition monitoring and early warning
- Cargo loading calculation and balance adjustment
- Communication relay and standard message forwarding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted navigation decisions to improve flight safety and fuel efficiency
- Real-time weather and sea condition forecasting, supporting dynamic adjustment of speed and route
- Automated fault diagnosis and predictive maintenance to reduce downtime
- Intelligent Cargo Monitoring Systems to Improve Stowage Accuracy and Safety
- Augmented reality (AR) assisted navigation at night and in adverse weather

**Human moat:**

- Ship handling and emergency response capabilities in crisis situations
- Team management and crew leadership (especially captain duties)
- Special cargo handling and complex port operations experience
- Regulatory compliance and maritime safety inspection responsibilities
- Flexible judgment in unpredictable marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI navigation and decision support systems
- Data analysis and interpretation of predictive models
- Remote operation and automated system monitoring
- Cybersecurity basics (ship system protection)
- STCW certification renewal (including E-navigation module)
- Cross-cultural communication and team management

**Key growth areas:**

- green shipping
- LNG carriers
- offshore
- automation

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a ships' engineer in China?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and experience. A junior engineer may earn 120,000–200,000 CNY/year, while a chief engineer on large vessels can earn 300,000–500,000 CNY/year. Additional allowances for sea service apply.

**Q: Can a foreign ships' engineer get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is employer-sponsored via the Z visa. Foreign engineers must hold relevant Chinese maritime certifications (or equivalent recognized by MSA) and have a job offer from a Chinese shipping company. Category C or B visa applies; language and regulatory requirements are challenging.

**Q: Is there a shortage of ships' engineers in China?**
A: Yes, China classifies marine engineers as a shortage occupation, particularly for senior roles and those with experience on specialized vessels (LNG, offshore). However, domestic supply is prioritized.
