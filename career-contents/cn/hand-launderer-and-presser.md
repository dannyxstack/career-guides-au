# Hand Launderers and Pressers — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9121 (ISCO08)**

Hand launderers and pressers manually clean, press, and finish garments and textiles in commercial laundries, dry cleaners, and hospitality settings. In China, the occupation is predominantly filled by domestic migrant workers and older laborers, with limited formal training requirements. The role is physically demanding and offers low wages, typically ranging from 30,000 to 60,000 CNY annually. Automation and self-service laundries are gradually reducing demand, particularly in urban areas, though niche high-end dry cleaning persists.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. —. Employment for hand launderers and pressers in China is expected to decline moderately due to automation, self-service laundry chains, and outsourcing to industrial laundries. Demand remains stable in luxury hotels and specialized fabric care, but overall job opportunities will shrink. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated adoption of contactless services, further reducing manual roles. Rural-to-urban migrants still fill positions, but younger workers avoid the sector, leading to an aging workforce.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most workers remain in entry-level roles or shift to machine operation. Advancement to supervisory positions requires several years of experience, but pay increases are modest. Some workers transition to dry cleaning shop managers or start small home-based businesses, but capital requirements are low. The occupation offers little upward mobility, and workers often leave for better-paying service or manufacturing jobs.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual washing operation: automatic washing machines can efficiently complete standard washing according to the program
- Regular ironing: Smart ironing machines batch-process flatwork items such as shirts and bed sheets.
- Manual sorting and folding: Image recognition and automatic sorting systems can identify clothing materials and fold them.
- Drying and sorting: automated hanging systems complete drying and sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Advanced stain removal: AI-assisted analysis of stain composition and recommendation of optimal cleaning solutions.
- Precision ironing: AI smart iron adjusts temperature and pressure in real time based on fabric.
- Customer preference management: system records customer laundry care preferences to enhance personalized service quality.
- Quality control: AI visual inspection for stains and wrinkles, assisting manual quality checks

**Human moat:**

- Experience in manual repair and care of delicate fabrics (e.g. lace, silk)
- Communicate with high-end clients, understand special cleaning needs and provide solutions
- Flexibility to handle unexpected situations (such as stains, damage)
- Shaping effects achieved by fine hand ironing (e.g., suits, gowns)

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart laundry equipment
- Knowledge of fabric properties and chemical stain removal
- Customer service and communication skills
- Basic data analysis (recording efficiency, costs)
- Advanced manual ironing technology (customized)
- Eco-friendly detergents and sustainable practices.

**Key growth areas:**

- High-end dry cleaning
- Hotel laundry services
- Automated pressing
- Fabric care chemicals

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a hand launderer in China?**
A: Annual pre-tax salary ranges from 30,000 to 60,000 CNY, with experienced workers in high-end hotels earning up to 80,000 CNY.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a hand launderer in China?**
A: It is possible under the C-tier work permit but challenging, as employers must prove no local candidate is available. Most foreign workers do not pursue this occupation.

**Q: Is there career progression for hand launderers in China?**
A: Limited; few move to supervisory roles or open small dry cleaning shops. Most remain in entry-level positions or leave the industry.
