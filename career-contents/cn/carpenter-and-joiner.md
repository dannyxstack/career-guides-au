# Carpenter and Joiner — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7115 (ISCO08)**

Carpenters and joiners in China are skilled tradespeople who construct, install, and repair wooden structures and fixtures, including frameworks, doors, windows, and furniture. They work in construction, manufacturing, and interior decoration sectors. The role is vital for China's urbanization and real estate development, with demand driven by infrastructure projects and growing demand for custom woodwork. Most workers obtain skills through vocational training or apprenticeships, often under the national vocational skill certification system. Career progression includes specialization in fine joinery, cabinetmaking, or site management.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to journeyman status. Experienced carpenters can become foremen, site supervisors, or start their own contracting businesses. Specialization in antique restoration, custom furniture, or high-end finishes offers higher earnings. Certification as a 'Senior Technician' or 'Technician' through the national vocational skill system enhances opportunities. Some workers transition into related fields like construction management or woodworking design. Ongoing automation in furniture factories may reduce demand for manual joiners but increase need for CNC operators.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for carpenters and joiners in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by location and skill level. In major cities like Beijing or Shanghai, a skilled carpenter can earn 60,000 to 120,000 CNY per year, while those in high-end custom furniture or finish carpentry may earn up to 180,000 CNY. Entry-level or apprentice wages are lower, around 30,000-50,000 CNY. Income often depends on piecework or project-based payment.

**Q: Can foreign carpenters and joiners get a work visa for China?**
A: It is very difficult. Carpentry is not a high-demand foreign occupation in China. Work visas are typically only available for Category A (high-level talent) or Category B (skilled professionals) under the Foreigner’s Work Permit system. Most carpenters fall into Category C (ordinary workers), which is rarely approved. Exceptions exist for niche roles like antique restoration if the employer can justify the need.

**Q: What certifications are needed to work as a carpenter in China?**
A: For Chinese workers, a Carpenter Vocational Skill Certificate is highly recommended, and a Safety Production Certificate is mandatory for construction sites. Foreign workers would need equivalency recognition or Chinese certifications, which is a long process. Most employers prefer local workers with established credentials.
