# Meteorologist — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2112 (ISCO08)**

Meteorologists in China primarily work for the China Meteorological Administration (CMA) and its provincial branches, providing weather forecasting, climate monitoring, and disaster early warning services. They are also employed in aviation, agriculture, renewable energy, and research institutions. The role requires strong analytical skills and knowledge of atmospheric science, with increasing emphasis on numerical modeling and AI applications. China's vast territory and frequent extreme weather events drive demand for accurate forecasting, making meteorology a stable public-sector career. Foreign meteorologists are rare but can find opportunities in research or international cooperation projects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a bachelor's degree and entry-level forecaster roles at provincial meteorological bureaus. With experience and advanced degrees, meteorologists can move into senior forecasting, research, or management positions. Specializations like satellite meteorology, radar technology, or climate risk assessment offer advancement paths. Many pursue master's or PhDs to join research institutes or the CMA's central office. Foreign experts may enter through high-level talent programs, but promotion can be limited without local credentials and network.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of routine weather forecast text
- Compilation and preliminary statistical analysis of historical meteorological data
- Standard warning content editing based on numerical model output
- Simple fault diagnosis and standardized reporting of meteorological observation equipment
- Write descriptive reports on basic climate trends

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Interpretation and Probability Calibration of Ensemble Forecasts Using AI
- Combine deep learning to predict local extreme weather (e.g., thunderstorms, heavy rain).
- AI-assisted climate change attribution analysis and scenario simulation
- Automatically generate customized industry weather reports (agriculture, energy).
- Using natural language processing to automatically extract meteorological research progress from literature

**Human moat:**

- Physical intuition and interdisciplinary understanding of complex weather systems
- Deliver authoritative advice to the public and decision-makers during high-impact weather events
- Designing novel observation experiments and scientific methods for integrating multi-source data
- Assume legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., consequences of failing to issue early warnings)
- Collaborative decision-making with related fields (hydrology, ecology)

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch)
- Principles and applications of weather AI models (e.g., FourCastNet, GraphCast)
- Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification techniques
- Climate big data visualization and interactive dashboard development
- Cross-disciplinary Skills (Economics, Risk Management, Public Policy)
- AI ethics and social impact assessment of weather warnings

**Key growth areas:**

- Numerical Weather Prediction
- Climate Change Adaptation
- AI in Meteorology
- Disaster Early Warning

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with a strong background in physics, mathematics, and computer science.
- Those interested in public service and working for government agencies.
- People comfortable with shift work and high-stakes decision-making during severe weather.

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking high private-sector salaries or rapid career progression outside government.
- Individuals unwilling to learn Mandarin or relocate to less-developed regions for provincial postings.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for meteorologists in China?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Entry-level meteorologists at provincial bureaus earn around 100,000–150,000 CNY annually, while senior researchers at CMA central can earn 300,000–500,000 CNY. Foreign experts with special skills may receive additional allowances.

**Q: Can foreign meteorologists obtain a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it requires employer sponsorship. Foreign meteorologists typically need at least a bachelor's degree and two years of experience to qualify for a Category B work permit. Those with PhDs or exceptional expertise may qualify for Category A (fast-track) or the R talent visa. Mandarin proficiency is often expected but not mandatory for initial visa.
