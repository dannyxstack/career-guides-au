# Librarian and Related Information Professional — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2622 (ISCO08)**

Librarians and related information professionals in China manage and organize library resources, provide information services, and support research and learning in public, academic, and special libraries. The role is evolving with digital transformation, requiring skills in digital curation, data management, and information literacy instruction. The profession is regulated by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, with a national professional qualification system. Employment is concentrated in universities, research institutes, public libraries, and government agencies. The field is stable but not high-growth, with competition in top-tier cities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for librarians in China typically follows a hierarchical path from assistant to director, tied to years of service and professional qualifications. Many start as library assistants or technical staff after a bachelor's degree, then pass the Librarian Professional Qualification Exam (助理馆员, 馆员, 副研究馆员, 研究馆员). Advanced degrees (MLIS) can accelerate promotion. Opportunities exist in digital library projects, metadata management, and information literacy education. Moving to management or specialized roles (e.g., rare books, digital archives) is common. Competition for senior roles is high, especially in prestigious institutions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cataloging and metadata generation (AI can automatically extract keywords and classification numbers from full text)
- Basic reference inquiries (chatbots answer common questions like opening hours, borrowing rules)
- document delivery and reservation processing (AI scheduling system automatically allocates resources)
- Collection inventory and shelf checking (RFID + AI vision automatic identification of misplaced books)
- Overdue reminder notices and fine calculations (automated email/SMS system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- In-depth targeted search (AI analyzes user needs, selects high-relevance literature from multiple databases).
- Digital Literacy Education (AI-powered interactive learning scenarios teaching database search strategies)
- Digitization of special collections and knowledge graph construction (AI-assisted recognition of manuscript text and linking historical events)
- Reader behavior analysis (AI mining borrowing data to optimize collection purchasing and space layout)
- Multilingual resource recommendations (AI translation matching non-English users' needs)

**Human moat:**

- Critically evaluating information quality (judging authority and bias of AI-provided results)
- Complex research consultation (interdisciplinary issues requiring understanding of user true intent and context)
- Community service and reading promotion (organizing activities, building emotional connections with readers)
- Digital rights and privacy management (deciding which data can be public and how to use it compliantly)
- Cross-institutional collaboration and resource sharing (negotiating license agreements, promoting open access)

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool application (e.g., querying databases with natural language, training small classification models)
- Data ethics and privacy protection (understanding algorithmic bias, personal information protection regulations)
- User experience design (designing AI-assisted search interfaces and self-service processes)
- Digital curation (managing digital collection lifecycle, including metadata standards)
- Project Management and Change Management (coordinating teams and processes when introducing AI systems)
- Basic programming (Python/API calls, automating repetitive tasks)

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital Libraries
- Data Management
- Information Literacy
- Academic Libraries

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for librarians in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by location and institution. In public libraries, entry-level librarians earn around 80,000-120,000 CNY annually, while experienced librarians in top universities can earn 150,000-250,000 CNY. Senior directors may exceed 300,000 CNY. Benefits often include housing subsidies and bonuses.

**Q: Can a foreign librarian get a work visa in China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Most positions require Chinese language proficiency and are not on the shortage list, so the visa is Category B or C. Employers must prove the position cannot be filled locally. Foreigners with a master's degree in library science and two years of relevant experience may qualify for a Category B Foreigner's Work Permit.
