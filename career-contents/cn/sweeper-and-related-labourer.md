# Sweeper and Related Labourer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9613 (ISCO08)**

Sweepers and related labourers in China perform manual cleaning of streets, public spaces, and waste collection. They are employed by municipal sanitation companies or private contractors, often under government oversight. The role requires physical stamina and tolerance for outdoor work in all weather. With China's rapid urbanization, demand for these workers remains steady, though automation and mechanization are gradually reducing the need for manual labour in some cities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for sweepers is limited. Advancement usually comes from moving to team leader or supervisor positions within sanitation companies. Some workers may transition to related roles like waste sorting or recycling plant work. Formal education or vocational training is rarely required. Long-term, most remain in entry-level positions due to lack of structured career ladders and low formal skill requirements.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Daily floor cleaning and garbage collection
- Surface wiping with fixed cleaning agents
- Repeatedly patrol and clean along predetermined routes
- Simple waste sorting and disposal

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sweeper in China?**
A: Salaries vary by city and employer. In first-tier cities like Beijing or Shanghai, monthly pre-tax earnings range from 3,000 to 5,000 CNY. In smaller cities, it may be 2,000 to 3,500 CNY. Annual pre-tax income is typically 30,000 to 60,000 CNY, often including social insurance and housing fund contributions.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a sweeper in China?**
A: Almost never. China's work visa system prioritizes skilled workers (Category A and B). Sweepers fall under Category C (low-skilled), which is severely restricted and only available for limited industries or bilateral agreements. Domestic labour supply is sufficient, so employer sponsorship for a foreign sweeper is extremely unlikely.

**Q: Do I need any qualifications to work as a sweeper in China?**
A: No formal qualifications are required. Some employers may ask for a health certificate. Basic literacy to understand work instructions can be helpful, but is not mandatory.
