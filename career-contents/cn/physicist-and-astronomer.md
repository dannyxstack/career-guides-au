# Physicist — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2111 (ISCO08)**

Physicists and astronomers in China conduct research to understand the fundamental principles of the universe, from subatomic particles to cosmic phenomena. They work in universities, research institutes like the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), national laboratories, and increasingly in technology companies focusing on quantum computing, space exploration, and advanced materials. The field is highly competitive and research-intensive, with a strong emphasis on publications and international collaboration. China invests heavily in basic sciences, offering opportunities for top-tier talent, especially in strategic areas like quantum science and aerospace.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a PhD and postdoctoral research, followed by assistant, associate, and full professor positions in academia. In industry, physicists can move into R&D roles in semiconductors, quantum computing, or finance. The Chinese Academy of Sciences and top universities offer tenure-track systems, but competition is fierce. Successful researchers often lead their own labs, attract national grants, and collaborate internationally. Entrepreneurship in deep-tech startups is an emerging path, supported by government innovation funds.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physicist in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely: postdocs earn around 200,000-400,000 CNY annually; assistant professors at universities earn 300,000-500,000 CNY; senior researchers at institutes like CAS can earn 500,000-1,000,000 CNY. Industry roles in tech may pay higher, especially in quantum computing.

**Q: Can foreign physicists easily obtain a work visa in China?**
A: Yes, if sponsored by a recognized institution and classified as Category A (high-level talent) under the Foreigner's Work Permit system, the process is streamlined. Category A includes Nobel laureates, academicians, and leading scientists, allowing for fast-tracked R visas.

**Q: What is the job market like for astronomers in China?**
A: Astronomy is a niche but growing field in China, with major projects like the FAST telescope and space missions. Positions are mainly at CAS observatories and universities. Competition is high, and foreign candidates need strong publication records and collaborations.
