# Vocational Education Teacher — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2320 (ISCO08)**

Vocational Education Teachers in China deliver specialized training and theoretical instruction in technical and vocational fields, aligning with the country's push to upgrade its workforce under 'Made in China 2025'. They work in secondary vocational schools, higher vocational colleges, and training centers, often requiring industry experience alongside teaching credentials. The role is critical for bridging skills gaps, with growing demand for dual-qualified teachers who combine technical expertise with pedagogical skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from teaching positions in vocational schools, advancing to senior lecturer, department head, or academic dean. Many teachers enhance prospects by obtaining higher degrees (e.g., Master's or Ph.D.) or vocational certifications. Dual-qualified teachers – those with both professional qualifications and teaching credentials – are highly valued. Opportunities also exist in educational administration, curriculum development, or transitioning to industry training roles. Some teachers move to higher education or vocational universities as student numbers rise.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for vocational education teachers in China?**
A: Salaries vary by institution and city. In vocational colleges, foreign teachers typically earn 150,000-300,000 CNY annually, with higher pay in tier-1 cities or for dual-qualified teachers. Industry professionals teaching part-time may earn on an hourly basis, around 300-800 CNY per class hour.

**Q: What visa requirements apply to foreign vocational education teachers?**
A: Foreign teachers need an employer-sponsored Z visa (Work Permit). Requirements include a bachelor's degree, at least 2 years of teaching or relevant work experience, and a clean criminal record. For vocational subjects, a relevant vocational qualification or industry certification is often required. The employer handles the application process.

**Q: Do I need to speak Chinese to teach vocational subjects in China?**
A: While some vocational programs offer English-taught courses, especially in international schools or cooperative projects, most positions require at least intermediate Chinese proficiency for classroom management and industry communication. For highly specialized technical fields, translation support may be available.
