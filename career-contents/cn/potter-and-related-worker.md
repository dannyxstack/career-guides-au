# Potter and Related Worker — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7314 (ISCO08)**

Potters and related workers in China create ceramic products using clay, operating wheels and kilns. The craft is concentrated in historic centers like Jingdezhen, where traditional techniques blend with modern design. Employment is small-scale, often in private studios or small factories. The occupation requires manual skill and artistic sensibility, with limited formal employment opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship, moving to journeyman potter, then master artisan or studio owner. Some workers transition to teaching or ceramic design. Formal recognition through the national vocational skill certification can enhance credibility. The path is traditional and slow, with limited corporate laddering.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use a potter's wheel or molds for batch forming of standardized vessels
- Mechanical reproduction of basic carving and patterns
- Automated monitoring and adjustment of kiln temperature and time
- Automatic calculation and mixing of glaze formulas
- Visual inspection of dimensions and defects in product quality inspection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Quickly design complex ceramic shapes using 3D modeling software
- Use AI to generate glaze formulas and pattern ideas
- Use AR technology to show customers virtual finished ceramic products
- Analyze market data to optimize product design and pricing

**Human moat:**

- Irreproducible personal style and artistic expression in handmade pottery
- Ability for manual adjustments to handle customized, non-standard requirements.
- Deep perception and control over clay material properties
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional craft culture

**Skills to build:**

- 3D modeling and digital sculpting (e.g., Blender, Rhino)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g., Midjourney for creative prototyping)
- Digital manufacturing equipment operation (3D ceramic printer)
- Social media marketing and online portfolio presentation
- Deepening of traditional handicraft skills (e.g., wheel throwing, painting)
- Customer communication and customised service skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Ceramic Art
- Traditional Craft
- Cultural Heritage
- Export

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a potter in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely. An entry-level artisan may earn 30,000-60,000 CNY/year, while a master potter in a high-end studio can exceed 200,000 CNY/year. Many work on commission or own account.

**Q: Can a foreign potter get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You need a job offer from a Chinese employer (e.g., ceramic studio, art school). The employer must apply for a Foreigner's Work Permit. Category B requires at least 2 years of relevant experience and a bachelor's degree (arts) or equivalent. Category R is for top talent with international awards.

**Q: Do I need formal education to work as a potter in China?**
A: Not necessarily. Apprenticeships are common. However, a vocational degree or certificate can help with employment at larger studios or teaching positions.
