# Policy Administration Professional — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2422 (ISCO08)**

Policy administration professionals in China analyze, develop, and implement public policies within government agencies, state-owned enterprises, and public institutions. They work on regulatory compliance, policy research, and program evaluation, often in collaboration with central or local government bodies. The role requires deep understanding of China's political and legal systems, as well as skills in data analysis and stakeholder management. Foreigners are rarely hired due to language and citizenship requirements, though positions in international organizations or think tanks may be accessible.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from policy assistant or researcher, advancing to senior policy officer or director within government departments. Many professionals transfer between ministries or local governments. Obtaining the 'Senior Policy Analyst' professional title or passing the civil service exam (公务员考试) is crucial. Lateral moves to state-owned enterprises or consulting firms are common. Top positions require political alignment and extensive internal networks.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a policy administration professional in China?**
A: Entry-level policy analysts in local governments earn around 100,000–200,000 CNY annually; senior officials in central ministries can exceed 400,000 CNY. State-owned enterprises may offer 200,000–500,000 CNY plus benefits. Foreign professionals in think tanks earn 300,000–600,000 CNY.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a policy administration professional in China?**
A: Very difficult. Most roles require Chinese citizenship due to security and language requirements. Exceptions exist for foreign experts in international organizations (e.g., UN agencies) or foreign-owned consultancies advising on China policy. Work permits are Category C, with strict quotas.

**Q: What is the career progression like?**
A: Typically starts as a policy research assistant, advancing to policy officer, senior policy officer, then director-level. Internal exams and political connections (guanxi) are key. Many move to think tanks or private sector consulting after gaining experience.
