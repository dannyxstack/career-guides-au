# Musical Instrument Maker and Tuner — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7312 (ISCO08)**

Musical instrument makers and tuners in China craft, repair, and tune a wide range of instruments, from traditional Chinese instruments like guzheng and erhu to Western pianos and violins. This occupation is concentrated in specialized workshops, music schools, and manufacturing hubs in regions like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong. The role requires a deep understanding of acoustics, materials, and fine motor skills, with many practitioners learning through apprenticeships or vocational programs. China's growing interest in music education and cultural preservation supports demand, though the profession remains niche with limited formal recognition.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a musical instrument maker/tuner in China?**
A: Entry-level tuners earn around 60,000-80,000 CNY annually, rising to 120,000-200,000+ CNY for experienced masters with a strong clientele. High-end concert tuners or antique restorers can earn upwards of 300,000 CNY. Self-employed income varies widely.

**Q: Can a foreign musical instrument maker/tuner get a work visa in China?**
A: Rare. Only possible if you have exceptional skills (e.g., master piano tuner for national orchestras) and are sponsored by a Chinese employer (e.g., conservatory or high-end manufacturer). The position must be classified under Tier B or A. Most foreigners in this field work under other visa categories or as teachers.

**Q: Do I need a degree to become a musical instrument tuner in China?**
A: No. Vocational training or apprenticeship is the norm. Many learn through family tradition or short courses. A degree is not required, but formal certification can help build trust with clients.
