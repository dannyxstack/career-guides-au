# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers perform manual tasks such as feeding, watering, and cleaning animals; maintaining enclosures; and assisting with basic health checks. In China, this occupation is predominantly filled by domestic rural migrant workers, often from less-developed inland provinces, who work on large-scale pig, poultry, cattle, or dairy farms. The sector is consolidating towards industrialised operations, reducing demand for traditional labourers but requiring more skilled workers for automated systems. Foreign workers are rarely employed in this role due to visa restrictions and ample local supply.

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 202,632. Employment in livestock farming is stable but slowly declining as mechanisation and large-scale corporate farms replace manual labour. The push for modernised agriculture under China's rural revitalisation policies may create some demand for technicians, but general labourers face competition from automation and an ageing rural workforce. Growth is limited to specialised roles like animal husbandry technicians or farm managers. Overall, the outlook for basic labourers is flat to negative.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; labourers may advance to team leader or shift supervisor after years of experience, or transition to more specialised roles such as veterinary assistant or breeding technician. Some move into farm management with additional training. However, most remain in manual positions. The occupation is a stepping stone for rural migrants seeking urban employment, with high turnover. Formal training programs through vocational schools exist but are not commonly pursued.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for livestock farm labourers in China?**
A: Salaries are low and vary by region, typically ranging from 30,000 to 50,000 CNY per year, plus basic accommodation and meals on larger farms. Earnings are often below the national average for migrant workers.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa for this occupation in China?**
A: Extremely unlikely. The Chinese government restricts C-tier work permits to special cases, and livestock labour is not listed as a shortage occupation. Employers would need to prove a unique need, which is nearly impossible given the abundant local workforce.

**Q: What are the typical career progression opportunities?**
A: Advancement is limited. Some labourers become shift supervisors or move into semi-skilled roles like artificial insemination technicians or farm equipment operators. Formal management roles require education or long experience, but mobility is low.
