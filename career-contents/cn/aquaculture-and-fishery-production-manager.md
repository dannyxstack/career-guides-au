# Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 1312 (ISCO08)**

Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers oversee fish farms, hatcheries, and capture fisheries operations in China, the world's largest producer of aquatic products. They manage breeding, feeding, harvesting, and processing, ensuring compliance with environmental regulations and food safety standards. With China's focus on sustainable aquaculture and marine ranching, managers increasingly adopt smart farming technologies and ecological practices. They coordinate teams of technicians and workers, optimize production cycles, and maintain water quality. The role blends biological knowledge with business acumen, requiring familiarity with domestic supply chains and export markets.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 51,600. China's aquaculture sector is positioned for moderate growth, driven by rising domestic seafood demand and government policies promoting sustainable fisheries and 'blue granaries.' The 2025-2035 period sees expansion in marine ranching, deep-sea aquaculture, and inland recirculating systems. However, strict environmental regulations and competition from imports may temper rapid expansion. Managers with expertise in eco-friendly farming, disease control, and automation are sought after, particularly in coastal provinces like Shandong, Fujian, Guangdong, and Hainan.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an aquaculture technician or assistant farm manager, advancing to production manager, regional operations director, or senior consultant. With China's push for large-scale modern fisheries, experienced managers can move into government advisory roles or research institutes. Specialization in high-value species (e.g., sea cucumber, shrimp) or technologies (e.g., IoT water monitoring) enhances prospects. Multinational firms and state-owned enterprises also offer pathways to higher management or cross-border expansion.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable aquaculture
- Marine ranching
- Smart fisheries
- Inland aquaculture

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on farm scale, location, and experience. Entry-level managers may earn 120,000–200,000 CNY annually, while senior managers at large farms or state-owned enterprises can earn 300,000–500,000 CNY or more.

**Q: Can foreign aquaculture managers get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, through an Employer-sponsored Z visa (Work Permit) typically Category B or C. The employer must demonstrate the position cannot be filled locally. High-level talent with exceptional skills or advanced degrees may qualify for Category A (R visa).

**Q: What skills are most in demand for this role in China?**
A: Knowledge of sustainable aquaculture practices, water quality management, disease prevention, and familiarity with Chinese regulatory frameworks. Increasingly, digital literacy (IoT, data analytics) and bilingual ability (Chinese-English) are advantageous.
