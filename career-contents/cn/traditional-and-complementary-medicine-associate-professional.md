# Traditional And Complementary Medicine Associate Professional — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 3230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine Associate Professionals in China practice therapies such as acupuncture, tuina massage, and herbal medicine within the nationally recognized Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) system. They work in hospitals, clinics, and wellness centers, often under the supervision of licensed TCM physicians. The field is deeply rooted in Chinese culture and government-supported healthcare reforms, with increasing integration into primary care. However, foreign practitioners face strict licensing requirements, limiting entry to those who have obtained official TCM qualifications from Chinese institutions or passed equivalency exams.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a bachelor's degree in TCM (5 years) or a related diploma, followed by a mandatory internship and licensing exam. Associate professionals can advance to full physician status after further education and clinical experience. Many specialize in acupuncture, orthopedics, or pediatric tuina. Government-funded training programs and the 'TCM Revitalization Plan' encourage skilled professionals to work in community health centers. Foreigners with Chinese TCM degrees may progress into academic or managerial roles.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a TCM associate professional in China?**
A: For entry-level positions, salaries range from 80,000 to 150,000 CNY. Experienced associate professionals in private clinics can earn 150,000 to 250,000 CNY. Salaries in top-tier hospitals may be higher but competition is intense.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a TCM associate professional in China without a Chinese TCM degree?**
A: Generally no. To obtain a Z visa for clinical TCM work, you need a recognized TCM degree from a Chinese university or pass an equivalency exam. Non-clinical roles (e.g., teaching, research) may have more flexibility but still require employer sponsorship and relevant qualifications.

**Q: Is there a pathway to full TCM physician status as a foreigner in China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. After working as an associate professional and gaining experience, you can take the national TCM physician licensing exam. However, this requires fluent Chinese and a deep understanding of TCM theory and practice, which most foreigners achieve by studying a full bachelor's program in China.
