# Police Inspector and Detective — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 3355 (ISCO08)**

Police inspectors and detectives in China are law enforcement professionals responsible for investigating serious crimes, gathering evidence, and leading criminal investigations. They work within the Ministry of Public Security or local public security bureaus, often specializing in areas such as economic crime, cybercrime, or narcotics. The role requires rigorous training, political reliability, and a deep understanding of Chinese criminal law. Foreigners are generally not eligible for these positions due to citizenship requirements and national security restrictions.

Career progression typically begins with entry-level police officer roles, followed by promotion to detective or inspector after several years of service and passing examinations. Advancement to senior inspector or command positions requires additional training, political education, and demonstrated leadership. Specialization in high-demand areas like economic crime or counter-terrorism can accelerate promotion. Lateral entry from other government agencies is possible but rare. Retirement age is generally 60 for men and 55 for women.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for police inspectors and detectives in China?**
A: Salaries vary by region and rank, but entry-level inspectors earn around 80,000-120,000 CNY annually, while senior detectives can earn 200,000-350,000 CNY. Higher salaries are found in first-tier cities like Beijing and Shanghai, often supplemented by housing and benefits.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a police inspector or detective in China?**
A: No. Police inspector and detective roles are strictly reserved for Chinese citizens due to national security concerns. Foreigners cannot obtain a work permit or visa for such positions. Exceptional cases may involve non-operational advisory roles under the R visa, but these are extremely rare.

**Q: What is the promotion path for police inspectors in China?**
A: Promotion from inspector to senior inspector requires passing internal exams, completing leadership training, and achieving performance targets. Further advancement to bureau-level positions typically requires a master's degree and political connections.
