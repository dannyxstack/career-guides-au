# Bricklayers and Related Workers — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7112 (ISCO08)**

Bricklayers and related workers in China are skilled tradespeople who lay bricks, stones, blocks, and other masonry units to construct or repair walls, partitions, arches, and other structures. They work primarily in the construction industry, which is a major driver of China's economy. With rapid urbanization and ongoing infrastructure projects, demand for these workers remains steady. However, wages are moderate compared to other skilled trades, and the work is physically demanding. Many workers are employed by large construction firms or work as self-employed contractors. The occupation is regulated by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS) and requires vocational training and certification.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for bricklayers in China typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, followed by on-the-job experience. Skilled bricklayers can advance to foreman, supervisor, or site manager roles. Some may specialize in decorative masonry or restoration. With additional training, they can move into related trades such as stonemasonry or tiling. Opportunities for self-employment or starting a small contracting business exist. However, career advancement is limited without higher education or management skills. The occupation is often physically demanding, leading to shorter careers unless workers transition to supervisory roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Urbanization
- Infrastructure
- Real Estate
- Renovation

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a bricklayer in China?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries for experienced bricklayers typically range from 60,000 to 120,000 CNY, depending on location, experience, and employer. In major cities like Beijing or Shanghai, wages may be higher, but so is the cost of living.

**Q: Can a foreign bricklayer get a work visa for China?**
A: It is very difficult. Bricklayers fall under Category C of the work permit system, which is for low-skilled workers. Foreign workers in this category are rarely approved unless there is a documented labor shortage and the employer can justify hiring a foreigner. Most bricklayers in China are local citizens.

**Q: What training is required to become a bricklayer in China?**
A: Typically, a vocational secondary school diploma in construction or a three-year apprenticeship. On-the-job training is common. Certificates from MOHRSS are required for legal employment.
