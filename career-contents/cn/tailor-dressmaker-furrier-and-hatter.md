# Tailors, Dressmakers, Furriers And Hatters — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors, dressmakers, furriers and hatters in China are skilled artisans who create, alter, and repair custom garments, including traditional Chinese attire like qipao and cheongsam. The occupation is concentrated in urban tailoring shops, high-end boutiques, and garment factories. While mass production dominates, bespoke tailoring persists among affluent consumers and for ceremonial wear. The workforce is aging, and younger generations show less interest, leading to a potential skills gap in niche markets.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary outlook for tailors in China?**
A: Income varies widely by skill level and location. Entry-level tailors earn modest wages, while master tailors in high-end boutiques can command premium earnings. In first-tier cities, experienced custom tailors often have stable, well-paying clientele.

**Q: Can a foreign tailor obtain a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The position must be sponsored by a Chinese employer, and the foreign worker typically falls under Category C of the Foreigner's Work Permit. Approval requires demonstrating unique skills that are not readily available locally, and the process is subject to strict quotas and local regulations.

**Q: What is the career progression for tailors in China?**
A: Most tailors start as apprentices or after vocational training. With experience, they become journeymen and then master tailors. Many eventually open their own custom shops. Specializing in traditional Chinese garments or high-end menswear can open additional opportunities.
