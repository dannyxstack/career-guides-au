# Stonemason, Stone Cutter, Splitter and Carver — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7113 (ISCO08)**

In China, stonemasons and stone carvers are skilled tradespeople who shape, cut, and carve natural stone for construction, monuments, and artistic works. The occupation is concentrated in provinces with strong stone industries, such as Fujian, Shandong, and Guangdong, and in regions with historic stone architecture. With rapid urbanization and a growing focus on cultural heritage restoration, demand for traditional stone skills remains steady, though mechanization is reducing the need for manual laborers. Most workers are domestic, with foreign workers rarely employed due to language and certification barriers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

The construction sector's slowdown and increased use of substitutes like concrete limit demand. However, niche areas such as heritage conservation, high-end landscaping, and luxury stone interiors offer opportunities. Automation may displace low-skill roles, but skilled carvers and restorers remain needed. Government infrastructure spending on public art and historical sites provides some support.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, moving to journeyman, then master stonemason. Master craftsmen may become supervisors or start their own workshops. Specialization in carving, restoration, or CNC stone machining can enhance prospects. In China, formal certification through the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS) is common, with levels from初级 to高级技师. Some pursue further education in sculpture or architecture.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for stone cutting and layout design
- Operating automatic stone cutting machines and CNC engraving machines
- Material quantity calculation and layout using BIM models
- Use robots for repetitive stone handling and laying

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using 3D scanning and digital twins for precise stone structure installation
- Use AR glasses to overlay design drawings on construction sites, reducing errors
- Using AI to optimize stone splicing plans, improving aesthetics and strength
- Conduct drone inspections of large stone structures and remotely monitor maintenance needs.

**Human moat:**

- Artistic judgment for complex stone carving and hand modeling
- Adaptive skills for integrated on-site stone and structure installation
- Empirical judgment of stone texture, color, and physical properties
- Ability to communicate customized design solutions with clients

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/BIM software operation (e.g., AutoCAD, Revit)
- CNC stone processing equipment programming and operation
- 3D scanning and digital modeling skills
- Engineering surveying and precision installation technology
- Drone operation and image analysis basics

**Key growth areas:**

- heritage restoration
- stone carving artistry
- urban landscaping
- infrastructure projects

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with manual dexterity and attention to detail who enjoy hands-on craftwork.
- Artisans interested in blending traditional techniques with modern machinery.
- Workers willing to work outdoors or in dusty environments, often in remote construction sites.

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking computer-based or office jobs.
- Individuals allergic to dust or with respiratory issues due to silica exposure.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do stonemasons earn in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by skill level and region. Entry-level workers earn around 5,000-8,000 CNY/month, while experienced carvers can earn 15,000-25,000 CNY/month in prosperous areas. Master artisans in heritage restoration may exceed 30,000 CNY/month. However, no exact official statistics are provided here.

**Q: Can a foreign stonemason get a work visa for China?**
A: It is very difficult. Stonemason is not listed on China's shortage occupation lists, and the government prioritizes local workers. Only exceptionally skilled carvers with international awards might qualify for a Z visa under Category B talent, but approval is uncertain.
