# Craft And Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers a diverse range of skilled craft workers in China whose roles do not fit into more specific ISCO categories, such as artisans producing traditional handicrafts, custom jewellery makers, musical instrument builders, and precision manufacturing workers. They are typically employed in small workshops, cultural heritage preservation units, or niche manufacturing sectors, and their work often requires dexterity, attention to detail, and years of apprenticeship. In China, many of these crafts are tied to local intangible cultural heritage, and demand is supported by tourism and export markets.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to journeyman status, and eventually master craftsman. In China, master artisans can attain recognition as 'intangible cultural heritage inheritors', which offers prestige and government subsidies. Some workers may open their own studios or transition to teaching at vocational schools. The path is highly skill-dependent, and specialisation in high-demand crafts (e.g., jade carving, silk embroidery) can yield greater opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised jewellery grinding and polishing
- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments
- Repeating pattern reproduction in hand carving
- Traditional mold making and casting processes
- Simple wood/stone cutting and shaping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Conceive personalized jewelry/musical instrument designs using AI design software
- Accelerated prototyping through 3D scanning and modeling
- Using AI quality inspection system to improve product precision
- Using AR/VR for complex assembly guidance
- Customized client communication and quoting via digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Highly customized artistic creation and aesthetic judgment
- Experience and tactile feel of handling rare materials
- On-site repair and adaptation of complex non-standard parts
- Cultural story interpretation through in-depth communication with clients
- Overall process control through multi-process integration

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted design tools (e.g. Midjourney, DALL-E for concept design)

- Digital customer management and custom platform operations

**Key growth areas:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in China?**
A: Salaries vary significantly by skill level, region, and employer. Entry-level apprentices may earn only a subsistence stipend, while master craftsmen with national recognition can command high incomes, especially in luxury goods or cultural heritage sectors. Earnings are project-based or commission-based for many.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a craft worker in China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Most foreign craft workers enter under a Category C work permit for roles that are hard to fill locally, such as restoration of valuable antiques or unique artisan skills. Strong industry connections and a sponsoring employer with a clear business case are essential. Category B is possible with advanced qualifications.

**Q: Is formal education required to start in this field in China?**
A: Not always. Many traditional crafts are learned through informal apprenticeships. However, for modern precision crafts (e.g., jewellery making), a vocational diploma is advantageous. Government vocational schools offer 2-3 year programs combining theory and practice.
