# Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and aquaculture labourers perform manual tasks in fishing and fish farming operations, including netting, harvesting, feeding, and maintaining ponds or cages. In China, this occupation is concentrated in coastal provinces (e.g., Shandong, Fujian, Zhejiang) and inland aquaculture regions. Labourers often work on family-owned farms or fishing vessels, with limited mechanization. The role supports China's position as the world's largest aquaculture producer, but faces challenges from environmental regulations and aging workforce.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; labourers may advance to team leader or shift supervisor after 5-10 years, or become self-employed by leasing small ponds. Some transition to seafood processing or logistics. Formal training is uncommon, but experienced workers can obtain a 'Fishery Worker' vocational certificate for better positions. The occupation offers little upward mobility without capital or management skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Coastal aquaculture
- Inland freshwater farming
- Sustainable practices
- Processing

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**

- Physically robust and resilient to harsh outdoor conditions

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking indoor or sedentary work environments
- Individuals with limited physical stamina or aversion to water and weather

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery and aquaculture labourer in China?**
A: Salaries range from 30,000 to 70,000 CNY per year, varying by region and season. Coastal provinces like Zhejiang and Shandong offer higher pay, while inland areas pay less. Income is often supplemented by in-kind benefits (e.g., housing, meals).

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a fishery labourer in China?**
A: It is highly difficult. The Z visa C tier theoretically allows low-skilled work, but fishery jobs are almost never approved for foreigners due to labour surplus. A foreigner would need a rare employer willing to sponsor and justify the hire, plus pass a labour market test.

**Q: Is there any career progression for fishery labourers?**
A: Limited. Experienced labourers may become team leaders or start their own small aquaculture operations, but requires capital and business knowledge. Some transition to seafood processing or sales. Vocational certificates can help but do not guarantee advancement.
