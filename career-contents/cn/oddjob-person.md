# Odd-Job Person — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons in China perform a wide range of temporary, informal manual tasks such as moving goods, cleaning, basic repairs, or assisting at construction sites. This occupation is largely unregulated, with workers often hired on a daily or piece-rate basis through informal networks or digital gig platforms. The role is common among low-skilled migrant workers and rural-to-urban laborers, especially in fast-growing cities. It requires minimal formal qualifications but involves physical stamina and adaptability.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for odd-job persons is limited. With experience, some may transition to semi-skilled roles like construction assistant, warehouse packer, or domestic helper. Few move into skilled trades or formal employment. The work is often precarious and seasonal, with no standardized path for advancement. Government recognition of vocational skills could create limited opportunities for upskilling.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for an odd-job person in China?**
A: Income is highly variable and depends on location, task complexity, and bargaining. It is typically paid daily or per task and often below the average wage threshold for formal employment. Wages are not standardized and are usually cash-based.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as an odd-job person in China?**
A: It is extremely difficult. China's work visa system is designed for skilled talent (Tiers A/B). Odd-job work is considered low-skilled and does not meet the criteria for an employer-sponsored Z visa. There are no general migration pathways for such roles.
