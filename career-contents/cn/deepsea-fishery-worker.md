# Deep-sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6223 (ISCO08)**

Deep-sea fishery workers in China operate large fishing vessels on extended voyages, primarily targeting distant-water fisheries. The sector is central to coastal provinces like Zhejiang, Fujian, and Shandong, but faces challenges from overfishing, international quotas, and ageing fleets. Labour demand is stable for experienced crew, though automation and sustainability policies are reshaping the industry.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level deckhands can advance to boatswain, fishing master, or navigator through on-the-job experience and mandatory certifications. With additional training, workers may move into fishery management, vessel supervision, or aquaculture. The path is linear but competitive, with senior roles often requiring 10+ years at sea and a strong safety record.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing vessel navigation and route planning (AIS + autopilot)
- Fish catch classification and initial weighing (computer vision)
- Fish school detection and net deployment position optimization (AI sonar analysis)
- Predictive maintenance for fishing vessel equipment (IoT + machine learning)
- Catch tracking and compliance report generation (automated data entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fish school prediction (combining meteorological and oceanographic data)
- Real-time deck safety monitoring (AI alert wearable)
- Automation of catch processing (robot-assisted)
- Resource management and sustainable fishing strategies (AI models)
- Market sales data analysis (providing optimal auction timing)

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making in complex maritime environments (storms, currents)
- Manual net repair and deck emergency handling
- Judging fish behavior based on experience (limited AI data)
- Communication and coordination with fleet and shore teams
- Flexible operations to adapt to seasonal and regulatory changes

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Computer Vision Applications in Fisheries
- Data analysis and prediction tools (Python, R)
- Drone operation and maintenance
- Marine sensor and IoT device management
- Sustainable fisheries regulations and certifications
- Deck automation system operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Distant-water fishing
- Sustainable fishing practices
- Aquaculture integration
- Vessel automation technology

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals comfortable with long periods at sea and harsh weather conditions
- Experienced fishermen seeking international opportunities
- Those with strong physical stamina and adaptability to cramped living quarters

**Who is not suited**
- Individuals prone to seasickness or preferring stable onshore work
- Those uncomfortable with hierarchical crew dynamics and long separation from family

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for deep-sea fishery workers in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, vessel type, and catch share. Foreign workers can typically expect between 70,000 and 120,000 CNY per year, plus bonuses.

**Q: Can foreigners work as deep-sea fishery workers in China?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. Employers must sponsor a Z visa under the Foreigner's Work Permit system, and the role usually falls under Category C (limited quota). The applicant must have relevant seafarer certifications and a clean criminal record.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: With experience and additional certifications, workers can advance to roles like fishing master, navigator, or vessel manager. Some transition to shore-based positions in fleet management or fishery administration.
