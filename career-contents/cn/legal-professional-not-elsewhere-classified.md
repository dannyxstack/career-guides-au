# Legal Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2619 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers legal professionals such as legal advisors, consultants, and in-house counsel who are not classified under other specific legal roles. In China, foreign legal professionals typically advise on international law, cross-border transactions, intellectual property, and compliance. They work in international law firms, multinational corporations, or as independent consultants. Due to strict licensing requirements, they cannot practice Chinese law unless they pass the Chinese bar exam. Employment is employer-sponsored, and demand is growing in areas like fintech, M&A, and IP protection.

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Legal professionals with expertise in international business law and cross-border transactions.
- Bilingual lawyers (English/Chinese) from common law jurisdictions seeking to advise on foreign law in China.
- In-house counsel experienced in compliance, intellectual property, or M&A within multinational corporations.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who expect to practice Chinese law without passing the local bar exam.
- Professionals unwilling to navigate complex visa and licensing processes.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a foreign legal professional in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and firm type. Entry-level foreign associates at international law firms in Shanghai or Beijing can expect CNY 400,000 to CNY 800,000 per year. Senior counsel with 5+ years may earn CNY 1,000,000 to CNY 2,000,000. In-house roles at multinationals often range from CNY 600,000 to CNY 1,200,000.

**Q: How do I obtain a work visa as a foreign legal professional in China?**
A: You need a job offer from a Chinese employer who will sponsor a Z visa. The employer applies for a Foreigner's Work Permit (typically Category B for professionals with a bachelor's and 2+ years experience). You must provide your qualifications, a health check, and a criminal record check. After approval, you apply for the Z visa at a Chinese embassy abroad.

**Q: Do I need to pass the Chinese bar exam to work as a legal professional in China?**
A: Only if you intend to practice Chinese law. Most foreign legal professionals work as foreign legal consultants advising on non-Chinese law, so they do not need the Chinese bar. However, having a foreign bar membership is essential. Some choose to take the Chinese bar to expand their scope, but it is extremely challenging and requires fluency in Chinese legal terminology.
