# Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2132 (ISCO08)**

In China, Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers play a key role in modernizing the agricultural sector, promoting sustainable practices, and boosting rural incomes. They work alongside farmers, forestry enterprises, and aquaculture operators to introduce advanced techniques, improve yields, and meet environmental regulations. With the government's Rural Revitalization Strategy and push for green agriculture, demand for these advisers is growing, especially in regions undergoing land consolidation and smart farming adoption. They often work for agricultural extension stations, research institutes, or private agribusinesses, and may be deployed to remote areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with field advisory work, then moves into project management, policy research, or leading extension teams. Experienced advisers may transition to agribusiness consulting, NGO roles, or academic positions. In China, holding a professional title (e.g., agronomist, forestry engineer) is important for advancement. Specialization in a niche area like organic farming, fishery disease control, or forest carbon accounting can open higher-level opportunities. Bilingual professionals may work with international organizations on cooperative projects.

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with a degree in agriculture, forestry, fisheries, or environmental science who are passionate about rural development and hands-on fieldwork.
- Professionals experienced in modern farming techniques, sustainability certification, or agricultural policy analysis.
- Candidates who are adaptable to living in rural areas and fluent in Chinese (or willing to learn) for effective communication with local farmers.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer urban office environments and lack interest in outdoor or remote assignments.
- Individuals without a background in agricultural sciences or who are unwilling to obtain China-specific professional certifications.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and region. Entry-level government advisers earn around 80,000–120,000 CNY annually, while senior consultants in private agribusiness can earn 200,000–400,000 CNY. Foreign experts with unique skills may command higher pay.

**Q: Can a foreign Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, via the employer-sponsored Z visa. The employer must hold a Foreigner's Work Permit, and the applicant needs at least a bachelor's degree and two years of relevant experience. High-level talent with publications or major achievements may qualify for the R visa.

**Q: What is the career progression like for this role in China?**
A: Progression typically follows a technical track from assistant adviser to senior engineer, or a management track leading extension programs. Obtaining professional titles (e.g., senior agronomist) and publishing research are key. Some advisers move into policymaking or agribusiness management.
