# Policy and Planning Manager — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 1213 (ISCO08)**

Policy and Planning Managers in China oversee the development, implementation, and evaluation of policies and strategic plans for government agencies, state-owned enterprises, and large private organizations. They analyze economic, social, and environmental data to guide decision-making, aligning with national priorities such as the 14th Five-Year Plan. This role is critical in sectors like urban development, industrial policy, and public administration, requiring deep understanding of China's regulatory environment and policy framework.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from public policy analyst or strategic planning specialist roles, advancing to manager and then senior director. Many professionals hold advanced degrees (Master's or PhD) in public administration, economics, or related fields. Networking through think tanks and government exchanges is common. Transition to consulting or academia is possible. Senior roles may require Party membership for government positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically collect and organise policy-related data (e.g., economic indicators, regulatory changes)
- Generate initial policy impact analysis report and summary
- Quantified results of simulated policy options (e.g., budget impact model)
- Monitoring news and social media trends and generating public opinion briefs
- Format and submit compliance documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI tools to quickly compare policy frameworks across countries and identify best practices
- Analyze public feedback using natural language processing tools to optimize policy communication strategies
- Use predictive models to assess long-term socio-economic benefits of policies
- Leverage collaborative AI platforms to efficiently manage cross-departmental policy-making processes
- Use AI to generate multiple versions of policy drafts for leadership review

**Human moat:**

- Making balanced and value-based judgments amidst conflicting interests of multiple parties
- Build trust and conduct political negotiations with senior officials, the public, and others.
- Deep understanding of local culture, history, and power structures
- Assume ultimate responsibility in situations of uncertainty and ethical grey areas
- Proactive policy framework for innovative design rather than optimizing existing solutions

**Skills to build:**

- AI data analysis tools (e.g. Python/R, SQL, and automated reporting platforms)
- Policy modeling and simulation software (e.g., System Dynamics)
- Stakeholder management and conflict mediation skills
- AI ethics and bias recognition capabilities
- Digital governance and e-government platform operation
- Agile Project Management and Cross-Departmental Collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- Smart cities
- Industrial policy
- Environmental planning
- Regional development

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Experienced policy analysts or strategic planners with a deep understanding of China's political and economic systems
- Individuals with strong research skills and a track record in government or large-scale project planning
- Those comfortable working within bureaucratic structures and possessing excellent Chinese language skills (reading and writing official documents)

**Who is not suited**
- Early-career professionals with little experience in policy formulation or high-level strategic planning
- Individuals who prefer independent, fast-paced environments or lack patience with lengthy approval processes

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Policy and Planning Manager in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and location. In government, annual pre-tax income ranges from 300,000 to 800,000 CNY for senior roles. In large state-owned enterprises or consulting firms, it can exceed 1,000,000 CNY, especially in Beijing or Shanghai. Bonuses and housing allowances are common.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a Policy and Planning Manager in China?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. Foreigners typically need a Category A (high-level talent) or Category B (professional) work permit, with at least 5 years of relevant experience and a master's degree. Jobs are mostly in foreign chambers of commerce, international organizations, or multinational corporations with government affairs roles. Direct government positions are generally closed to foreigners.

**Q: What is the career progression for a Policy and Planning Manager?**
A: Starting as a policy analyst or planner, one can advance to manager, then senior manager, director, and potentially vice president or deputy director. Many move into consulting, think tanks, or academia after building a network. In government, progression requires passing internal assessments and may involve rotations.
