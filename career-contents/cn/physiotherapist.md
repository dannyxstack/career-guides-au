# Physiotherapist — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in China assess, plan, and implement rehabilitation programs for patients with physical impairments due to injury, illness, or aging. The profession is growing rapidly due to an aging population, rising chronic diseases, and increased awareness of rehabilitation. Most physiotherapists work in hospitals, rehabilitation centers, sports clinics, or community health facilities. The field is regulated by the National Health Commission, and practitioners must hold a national license (康复治疗师资格证). Foreign physiotherapists face significant entry barriers, including recognition of qualifications and language requirements, but demand for high-skilled specialists in top-tier hospitals is rising.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior therapist in a hospital, advancing to senior therapist, then chief therapist or department head. Many physiotherapists specialise in areas such as orthopaedics, neurology, paediatrics, or sports medicine. With additional training, some move into teaching or research at universities. Private practice is legal but requires a business license and often partnership with a hospital. Foreigners with strong Mandarin skills and international credentials may progress faster in multinational clinics or medical tourism facilities. Continuous education is mandatory for license renewal.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient record organization and electronic health record entry
- Scoring and data analysis of standardized assessment scales.
- Generate routine reports on treatment progress
- Administrative tasks such as appointment scheduling and patient reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

- Clinical reasoning and real-time adjustments for complex cases
- Professional judgment in interdisciplinary team collaboration
- Flexibility in handling emergencies and patient individual differences

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a foreign physiotherapist in China?**
A: Salaries vary by city and experience. Entry-level foreign physiotherapists in first-tier cities (Beijing, Shanghai) can expect 150,000–250,000 CNY annually. Senior specialists in top hospitals may earn 300,000–500,000 CNY. Benefits often include housing allowance and health insurance.

**Q: Can a foreign physiotherapist get a work visa to China?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from a Chinese employer who sponsors the Foreigner's Work Permit. You need a recognized degree and at least 2 years of relevant experience. The position must align with the employer's business scope. The process takes 2–4 months and includes health check and criminal record check.

**Q: Do I need to retake exams to practice as a physiotherapist in China?**
A: Yes, all physiotherapists must pass the national 康复治疗师资格证 exam. Your foreign degree must first be verified by CSCSE. The exam is in Mandarin, so proficiency is essential. Some hospitals may require additional internal assessments.
