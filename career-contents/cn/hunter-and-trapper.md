# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

Hunters and trappers in China operate within a strict regulatory framework due to wildlife protection laws and conservation priorities. The occupation is rare and primarily limited to licensed pest control, scientific research support, or traditional subsistence in remote ethnic minority areas. Most wildlife hunting is prohibited, and trapping for fur or sport is nearly non-existent. Employment is largely through government forestry departments, research institutes, or licensed pest control companies. The role requires knowledge of local ecosystems and compliance with the Wildlife Protection Law.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is minimal. Hunters and trappers may advance to senior wildlife management roles in government agencies or become specialized researchers, but such moves typically require additional education in ecology or biology. The skill set is not transferable to other sectors. Most practitioners are older and from traditional backgrounds; younger generations rarely enter the field. Certification in wildlife management or pest control is necessary but does not guarantee stable employment.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for hunters and trappers in China?**
A: Salaries are very low, typically ranging from 30,000 to 60,000 CNY per year for licensed trappers or pest control workers in remote areas. Government-employed wildlife management staff may earn more, around 60,000 to 90,000 CNY annually.

**Q: Can foreigners work as hunters or trappers in China?**
A: No. Foreigners are not permitted to work as hunters or trappers in China due to strict wildlife protection laws and the sensitive nature of wildlife management. Employer-sponsored visas for this occupation are not available.

**Q: Is hunting a viable career in China?**
A: No. Legal hunting is extremely restricted and limited to specific purposes like research or population control. It is not a viable full-time career for most people.
