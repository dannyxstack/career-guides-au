# Mixed Crop and Animal Producer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6130 (ISCO08)**

Mixed Crop and Animal Producers in China manage farms combining crop cultivation and livestock rearing, often on small to medium family-run holdings. They oversee planting, harvesting, animal feeding, breeding, and basic farm maintenance. In China, this occupation is prevalent in rural areas, particularly under the government's rural revitalization strategy, which encourages integrated farming systems. Many producers are self-employed or belong to cooperatives, with increasing adoption of sustainable and organic practices. The role requires practical knowledge of agriculture, animal husbandry, and basic business management.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for Mixed Crop and Animal Producers in China is typically limited to expanding farm size, joining cooperatives, or diversifying into agritourism. Some transition to agricultural extension officers, farm managers for larger enterprises, or start their own agribusinesses. Formal qualifications in agricultural science or veterinary medicine can open opportunities in research or advisory roles. However, many remain self-employed with modest income growth. The aging workforce and outflow of rural youth pose challenges.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual income for a Mixed Crop and Animal Producer in China?**
A: Income varies greatly by region and farm size, but typical earnings range from 40,000 to 80,000 CNY pre-tax for small producers. Larger operations or those selling premium organic products can earn 100,000 CNY or more.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a Mixed Crop and Animal Producer in China?**
A: It is extremely difficult. Employers must prove no local candidate is available, and the role is usually not considered skilled. Only highly specialized experts in modern agriculture may qualify for an R visa.

**Q: What career advancement options are available for this occupation in China?**
A: Producers can scale up operations, join cooperatives, or become farm managers. With additional training, they may transition to agricultural extension workers or agribusiness consultants.
