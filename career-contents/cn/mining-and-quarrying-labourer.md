# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers in China perform manual tasks in the extraction of minerals, coal, stone, and other materials from mines and quarries. They work under the supervision of machine operators and engineers, handling activities such as drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials. In China, this occupation is concentrated in resource-rich provinces like Shanxi, Shaanxi, and Inner Mongolia. The industry is heavily regulated and is undergoing modernization with increased automation and safety standards. Labourers often work in remote areas and face physically demanding conditions. The workforce is predominantly domestic, with very limited foreign participation due to strict work permit categories.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for mining and quarrying labourers in China is limited, with most remaining in entry-level positions. However, experienced labourers may advance to supervisory roles or become machine operators after training. Alternatively, they can move into related fields such as construction or transportation. Formal vocational training and certifications can enhance prospects. The industry is increasingly demanding skilled operators rather than manual labourers, so upskilling is recommended for long-term career development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Safety compliance
- Green mining
- Remote operations

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining labourer in China?**
A: Salaries vary by region and company, but annual pre-tax earnings for a mining labourer in China typically range from 60,000 to 120,000 CNY. State-owned enterprises may offer higher pay and benefits. Foreign workers are rare and would require a premium due to visa complexities.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a mining labourer in China?**
A: It is very difficult. The position is classified under the C category (limited-skilled) of the Foreigner's Work Permit, which requires employers to demonstrate that no suitable domestic worker is available. In practice, such visas are rarely granted for labourer roles. High-skilled positions in mining (engineers, geologists) are more viable for migration.

**Q: How can a mining labourer advance in China?**
A: Career advancement typically involves gaining experience and obtaining certifications to operate machinery or supervise teams. Some labourers transition to roles in mining safety or logistics. Vocational training in mining technology can lead to higher-paying positions.
