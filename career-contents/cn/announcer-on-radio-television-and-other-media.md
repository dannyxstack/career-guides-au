# Announcer on Radio, Television and Other Media — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2656 (ISCO08)**

Announcers present news, music, talk shows, and other content on radio, television, and digital platforms. In China, most announcers are native Mandarin speakers due to strict linguistic requirements, and roles are concentrated in state-owned broadcasters like CCTV and provincial stations. Foreign announcers are rare and typically limited to English-language roles at international media outlets or specialized positions.

**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Experienced announcers may move into production, content management, or media training. The rise of livestreaming e-commerce and short-video platforms has created niche opportunities for bilingual talent, but formal career paths remain state-dominated.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an announcer in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by platform and experience. Entry-level local radio announcers earn around 100,000-200,000 CNY annually, while national TV hosts at CCTV can exceed 500,000 CNY. Foreign announcers in international media may earn 300,000-600,000 CNY plus benefits, but opportunities are limited.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as an announcer in China?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. Foreigners must have a bachelor's degree, at least 2 years of relevant experience, and high-level Mandarin proficiency (HSK 5 or above). Most visas are Tier B, requiring employer sponsorship. The role is not on the shortage list, and competition with locals is intense.

**Q: Do I need the Putonghua Proficiency Test as a foreigner?**
A: While not legally mandatory for foreigners, most employers require it to demonstrate language ability. Without it, visa approval is unlikely for announcer roles. Achieving Grade 1 Class B is recommended.
