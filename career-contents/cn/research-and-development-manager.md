# Research and Development Manager — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 1223 (ISCO08)**

Research and Development Managers plan, direct, and coordinate R&D activities to develop new products, processes, or knowledge. In China, this role is pivotal in the national innovation drive under the 14th Five-Year Plan, with strong government support for R&D spending and technology self-sufficiency. These managers typically work in high-tech industries, such as electronics, pharmaceuticals, and renewable energy, guiding teams of scientists and engineers. They are concentrated in innovation hubs like Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, and the Yangtze River Delta, where they link corporate strategy with scientific discovery, manage budgets, and ensure compliance with Chinese regulations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from senior technical roles (e.g., lead engineer, senior scientist) before moving into management. In China, a PhD or MBA is common at top levels. Many R&D Managers advance to Director of R&D, Chief Technology Officer, or VP of Innovation. Lateral moves between industries are possible, especially in cross-sector areas like digital health. Networking within China's innovation ecosystem (e.g., Zhongguancun, Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park) is crucial. Continuous learning in emerging fields is expected, with some managers pursuing executive education at top Chinese universities.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Research and Development Manager in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by industry, location, and company size. In top-tier cities like Shanghai and Beijing, annual pre-tax salaries for mid-level R&D managers range from 300,000 to 800,000 CNY, while senior roles can exceed 1,500,000 CNY, including bonuses and stock options. State-owned enterprises typically offer lower base pay but strong benefits.

**Q: How do I obtain a work visa for an R&D Manager position in China?**
A: You need a job offer from a Chinese employer, who will apply for a Foreigner's Work Permit on your behalf. Your qualifications (degree, experience, salary) determine the tier (A/B/C). Tier A or B is typical for R&D managers. After approval, you apply for a Z visa at a Chinese embassy/consulate. Once in China, you convert it to a residence permit.

**Q: What is the career progression for R&D Managers in China?**
A: Many start as senior engineers or scientists, then move into team lead or R&D manager roles. With success, they may become Director of R&D, VP of Technology, or CTO. Some transition to general management or entrepreneurship. Networking within industry clusters and obtaining professional certifications (e.g., PMP) can accelerate advancement.
