# Rigger and Cable Splicer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7215 (ISCO08)**

Riggers and cable splicers in China set up and maintain rigging equipment for hoisting and moving heavy objects, and splice cables for electrical, telecommunications, and fiber-optic networks. They work in construction, manufacturing, energy, and telecom sectors, often at heights or in challenging environments. The role demands physical strength, technical precision, and adherence to safety standards. In China, these workers are classified under skilled trades and require vocational qualifications from the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to junior rigger/splicer roles. With experience and advanced certifications (e.g., senior technician), workers can become team leaders, site supervisors, or safety inspectors. Some move into related fields like construction management or telecom network design. The Chinese government encourages upskilling through vocational training programs, and experienced workers may open their own contracting businesses.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Image recognition-based automatic detection of cable wear
- Use drones for overhead cable inspection
- Automatically generate rigging load calculations and configuration plans
- Installation path planning using digital twin simulation
- Automated report generation and compliance document filling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AR headset displays complex rigging connection steps and safety annotations
- AI real-time analysis of tension data and early warning of potential breakage risks
- Smart wearable devices monitor worker posture and fatigue to prevent occupational injuries
- machine learning-based defect identification and repair guidance for cable joints
- Digital twin platforms enable remote expert collaboration for complex lifting operations

**Human moat:**

- Improvise and erect scaffolding in adverse weather or confined spaces
- Manual slinging and adjustment of non-standard components
- Intuitive experience in judging local damage and repair of old cables
- On-site team safety communication and evacuation decision-making in emergencies
- Interpersonal skills for on-site negotiation of installation plans with clients and engineers

**Skills to build:**

- Skilled in operating drones and robots for high-altitude/underground inspections
- Use AI detection software to analyze cable images and sensor data.
- Interpret 3D models and digital twin platforms (such as BIM).
- Master smart wearables and IoT monitoring systems
- Learning automated rigging design software (e.g., OptiRig)
- Basic programming (Python/R) for data cleaning and report automation

**Key growth areas:**

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Experienced riggers with international certifications (e.g., NCCCO, IPAF) seeking to work on large-scale Chinese infrastructure projects.
- Cable splicers with specialized knowledge in fiber-optic or high-voltage cable joining, especially for telecom or renewable energy firms.
- Technical instructors or safety trainers in rigging/splicing who can transfer skills to Chinese workforces.

**Who is not suited**
- Those without physical stamina or comfort working at heights and in outdoor conditions.
- Individuals lacking Mandarin proficiency or willingness to adapt to Chinese safety regulations and work culture.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a rigger or cable splicer in China?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience. Entry-level workers earn around 60,000-80,000 CNY annually, while experienced technicians with certifications can earn 100,000-150,000 CNY. Senior roles in major cities or on complex projects may exceed 200,000 CNY.

**Q: Can a foreign rigger get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Foreign workers need a job offer from a Chinese employer who sponsors a Z visa (work permit). The role is typically classified as Category C (limited demand), requiring relevant qualifications and at least 2 years of experience. High-level talent (Category A) is rare for this occupation.

**Q: Do I need Chinese qualifications to work as a rigger in China?**
A: Yes, mandatory local certifications (e.g., special operation certificate from MOHRSS) are required. Foreign qualifications may be recognized if they meet equivalent standards, but retesting or additional training is often necessary.
