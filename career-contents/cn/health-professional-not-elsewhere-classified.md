# Health Professionals Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2269 (ISCO08)**

This ISCO-08 group encompasses a diverse range of health professionals not covered by other categories, such as audiologists, dietitians, and orthoptists. In China, these roles are increasingly recognized as essential for specialized patient care, particularly in urban hospitals and rehabilitation centers. The demand is rising due to an aging population, growing chronic disease burden, and expanding healthcare infrastructure. However, foreign professionals face significant regulatory hurdles, including mandatory Chinese licensing exams and language proficiency requirements. Work opportunities are concentrated in tier-1 cities and specialized public hospitals, with competition from domestically trained talent.

**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for these professionals in China typically starts in public hospitals or specialized clinics, with pathways to senior clinical roles or management positions. Many pursue advanced degrees or certifications to enhance mobility. The rise of private healthcare and medical tourism offers alternative career tracks. However, foreign professionals often hit a glass ceiling unless they obtain full Chinese licensure and achieve proficiency in medical Chinese. Those who adapt may transition into teaching, research, or healthcare consulting roles.

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Qualified health professionals with Chinese language skills and a willingness to navigate complex licensing.
- Specialists in niche areas like audiology or dietetics with advanced degrees and international certifications.
- Researchers or educators willing to work in Sino-foreign joint institutions.

**Who is not suited**
- Professionals unwilling to learn Chinese to a high level, as licensing and patient communication require it.
- Those seeking immediate high salaries without prior adaptation to the Chinese healthcare system.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What salary can I expect as a foreign health professional in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by city and specialization. In top-tier hospitals, experienced dietitians or audiologists might earn 200,000–400,000 CNY annually, while senior roles in private clinics can exceed 500,000 CNY. However, entry-level or publicly employed positions are significantly lower.

**Q: Do I need to pass the Chinese medical licensing exam to work?**
A: Yes, for most clinical roles. The exam is in Mandarin and covers Chinese medical standards. Some non-clinical roles (e.g., research) may waive this, but a work permit and Z visa are still mandatory.

**Q: Is there a pathway to permanent residence for health professionals?**
A: Permanent residence is rare and requires high-level talent status (A tier) or exceptional contributions. Most health professionals remain on renewable work visas.
