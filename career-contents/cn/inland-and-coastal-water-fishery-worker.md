# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and coastal waters fishery workers in China engage in fishing, aquaculture, and related activities in rivers, lakes, and coastal areas. The sector is dominated by small-scale family operations and larger state-owned enterprises, with significant regional variation. The workforce is aging, with many younger workers moving to urban areas, leading to a gradual decline in labor supply. The government promotes sustainable fisheries and aquaculture modernization, creating some demand for skilled workers in areas like fishery management and environmental monitoring.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for fishery workers typically starts as deckhands or fish farm laborers, advancing to crew leaders, boat captains, or farm managers. Certification in navigation, fishing technology, or aquaculture management can boost prospects. Larger enterprises and state-owned companies offer more structured paths, but many workers remain in informal employment. Transition to related fields like seafood processing or fishery inspection is possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable fishing
- Aquaculture expansion
- Inland fisheries
- Coastal management

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong physical endurance and comfort working outdoors in water environments.
- Workers seeking seasonal or coastal employment with low formal education requirements.
- People interested in traditional fishing livelihoods or small-scale aquaculture.

**Who is not suited**
- Those preferring office-based or urban jobs with regular hours.
- Individuals with limited swimming ability or aversion to remote locations.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery worker in China?**
A: Salaries vary by region and season, but typical annual earnings range from 40,000 to 80,000 CNY for crew members. Captains or experienced workers can earn upwards of 100,000 CNY. Income is often supplemented by bonuses from catches.

**Q: Can foreign nationals work as fishery workers in China?**
A: Foreign workers are rarely employed in China's fishery sector due to abundant local labor, language barriers, and stringent visa requirements. Exceptions exist for specialized roles in aquaculture technology or fisheries research, typically under Z visa (Foreigner's Work Permit C-tier).
