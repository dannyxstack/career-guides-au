# Miner and Quarrier — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers extract coal, ores, stone, sand, clay, and other minerals from underground and surface mines and quarries in China. The occupation is concentrated in resource-rich provinces such as Shanxi, Shaanxi, and Inner Mongolia, with large state-owned enterprises dominating the industry. Workers operate drilling, blasting, and cutting machinery, and perform manual extraction using hand tools. The sector is undergoing rapid mechanization and automation to improve safety and productivity, while also facing strict environmental regulations. Employment is declining overall as China transitions to cleaner energy, but demand remains for skilled operators in modernized mines.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for miners and quarriers in China typically starts with entry-level manual labor, moving to machine operation, then to supervisory roles such as shift foreman or safety inspector. Many workers obtain vocational certifications in mining engineering, mine safety, or equipment operation to advance. Some transition into mine planning, blasting engineering, or environmental management. The path is more structured in state-owned mines, which offer training and promotion opportunities. Experienced workers may also move into consulting or training roles as the industry modernizes.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Operating mining equipment such as drills and excavators (automated remote control replaces manual driving)
- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)
- Daily equipment inspection and simple maintenance (predictive maintenance system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)
- Production scheduling and resource allocation (AI optimization algorithm)

**Human moat:**

- On-site assessment and handling of emergencies (e.g., collapses, gas leaks)
- Adaptive operation for abnormal geological conditions (unstructured environment)
- Team safety management and communication coordination
- Mining regulation compliance and government reporting responsibilities
- Complex repairs and equipment modifications (requiring physical hands-on skills)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated mining systems and remote-control equipment
- Data analysis and use of mining software (e.g., Surpac, Datamine)
- Skills in drone and sensor maintenance
- Basic programming and automation scripting (Python)
- Safety regulations and emergency response training
- Hazardous environment work skills (e.g., confined space entry)

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Safety
- Deep Mining
- Green Mining

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals comfortable working in remote, rural locations and underground environments.
- Those with experience in heavy machinery operation and a strong safety mindset.

**Who is not suited**
- People with claustrophobia or health issues related to dust or confined spaces.
- Those seeking careers in fast-growing service or tech sectors with higher social status.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for miners and quarriers in China?**
A: Salaries vary by region, mine type, and experience. Entry-level workers earn around 60,000-100,000 CNY per year, while experienced operators can earn 120,000-180,000 CNY. State-owned mines often provide benefits and housing. Foreign workers may receive higher compensation but face visa restrictions.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a miner in China?**
A: It is very difficult. Foreign workers must obtain a Z work permit (Category C or B) sponsored by a Chinese employer. The employer must demonstrate no suitable Chinese candidate. Most mines prefer local workers due to language and certification requirements. Only specialized experts (e.g., in rare earths) may get approval.

**Q: What is the job outlook for miners in China?**
A: Overall employment is declining due to automation and a shift to cleaner energy. However, skilled operators of automated equipment and workers in strategic mineral mining (lithium, rare earths) have stable prospects. Job security is higher in large state-owned mines.
