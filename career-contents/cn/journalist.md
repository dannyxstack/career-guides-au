# Journalist — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

A journalist researches, writes, and reports news and current affairs for print, broadcast, or online media. In China, journalists work within a state-regulated media environment, primarily for state-owned outlets like Xinhua News Agency, CCTV, or People's Daily, as well as a growing number of digital platforms. The profession is subject to strict content controls and requires accreditation. With China's emphasis on international news coverage and soft power, demand remains steady for bilingual journalists, especially those covering business, technology, and international relations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts from junior reporter to senior correspondent, editor, or news anchor. Journalists may move into media management or public relations. Specialization in a beat (e.g., finance, tech) enhances prospects. Foreign journalists often work as correspondents for international outlets, but long-term roles require a work visa and can be limited due to regulations.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for journalists in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely. Entry-level journalists at state media earn around 150,000-250,000 CNY annually; experienced correspondents can earn 300,000-500,000 CNY. Foreign correspondents' salaries are often higher, paid by their home organization.

**Q: Can foreign journalists work in China on a Z work visa?**
A: Foreign journalists typically use the J-1 (Foreign Journalist) visa, not the Z visa, which is for other professionals. The J-1 visa is issued by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and requires sponsorship by a media organization in China.

**Q: What qualifications do I need to work as a journalist in China?**
A: Chinese journalists must hold a press card (记者证) and pass the national industry examination. For foreign journalists, accreditation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and a J-1 visa are mandatory, along with sponsorship from a recognized media outlet.
