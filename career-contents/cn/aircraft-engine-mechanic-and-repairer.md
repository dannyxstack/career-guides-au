# Aircraft Engine Mechanic And Repairer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7232 (ISCO08)**

Aircraft engine mechanics and repairers in China specialize in inspecting, maintaining, and overhauling aircraft engines and their components. They work primarily in civil aviation maintenance bases, military depots, and MRO (Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul) facilities. With China's rapidly expanding airline fleet and domestic aircraft manufacturing initiatives (e.g., COMAC C919), demand for certified mechanics is rising. Most positions require a Vocational School diploma and a CCAR-66 Aircraft Maintenance Licence (AML). The role is critical for flight safety and is regulated by the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a mechanic trainee, advancing to certified technician, team lead, and then shop supervisor or quality inspector. Chinese MRO firms often sponsor additional type ratings and send staff for overseas training. Experienced mechanics may move into engineering, technical instruction, or management roles. The path is linear and skill-based; senior roles require at least 5-8 years of hands-on experience and multiple engine-type endorsements. Self-employment is rare; most work for airlines, MRO providers, or the military.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an aircraft engine mechanic in China?**
A: Entry-level mechanics earn about 80,000-120,000 CNY per year; with 5+ years experience and CCAR-66 licence, 150,000-250,000 CNY; senior lead mechanics can earn 300,000+ CNY. Salaries vary by city and employer type (airlines vs MRO).

**Q: Can a foreign aircraft mechanic get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. You need an employer sponsor and a valid CCAR-66 licence or equivalent. Most applicants fall under Category B or C. The process takes 2-4 months and requires police clearance and medical checks.

**Q: Is it possible to switch to engineering or management after being a mechanic?**
A: Yes, with additional education or internal training. Many Chinese MROs promote experienced mechanics to team leaders or quality assurance roles. Some pursue part-time bachelor degrees to move into design or certification engineering.
