# Dancer and Choreographer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

In China, dancers and choreographers perform in diverse settings including traditional Chinese opera, ballet, contemporary dance troupes, commercial dance studios, and increasingly in online content creation and live entertainment. The occupation is regulated under the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, with career paths often tied to state-run performance troupes or private studios. Foreign professionals typically require employer sponsorship and may qualify for talent visas if exceptionally skilled.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for dancers in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and city. Professional dancers in tier-1 cities earn around 150,000-300,000 CNY annually, while freelance choreographers may earn more per project but with less stability. Government-sponsored troupes offer lower base pay but better benefits.

**Q: Can foreign dancers get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, foreign dancers can obtain a Z visa (Foreigner's Work Permit) if sponsored by a registered employer like a performance troupe or studio. The process requires a valid contract, qualifications, and a clean criminal record. High-level talent may qualify for an R visa.

**Q: What are the main career paths for a choreographer in China?**
A: Many choreographers start as dancers and move into creating works for troupes, TV shows, large events (e.g., Chinese New Year Gala), or commercial productions. Teaching at academies and opening private studios are common alternatives. Digital platforms also offer new avenues for online choreography content.
