# Environmental and Occupational Health Inspector — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 3257 (ISCO08)**

Environmental and Occupational Health Inspectors and Associates in China monitor workplace and environmental health hazards, enforce regulations, and promote public health. They work for government health departments, third-party inspection agencies, or large corporations, ensuring compliance with national standards on air quality, chemical exposure, noise, and sanitation. With China's increasing focus on occupational safety and environmental protection, demand for these professionals is growing in industrial zones, manufacturing hubs, and urban centers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an assistant inspector or technician, advancing to senior inspector or manager. Many move into EHS management roles in multinational corporations or consulting. Certification through MOHRSS vocational qualifications enhances prospects. The field offers opportunities to specialize in industrial hygiene, radiation protection, or environmental health.

- Basic data analysis and trend identification

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for Environmental and Occupational Health Inspectors in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and location. Government inspectors earn moderate but stable pay, while private sector EHS specialists in multinational companies can earn significantly higher. Expect annual pre-tax income ranging from 100,000 to 300,000 CNY for mid-level positions, depending on experience and certifications.

**Q: Can foreigners work as Environmental and Occupational Health Inspectors in China?**
A: Yes, but mainly in private sector roles such as EHS managers in foreign-invested enterprises, third-party inspection agencies, or consulting firms. Government positions are typically reserved for Chinese nationals. A valid employer-sponsored work visa (Z) is required, and the role must qualify under the Foreigner's Work Permit system, usually as Category B (skilled professional).
