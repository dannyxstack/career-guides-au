# Precision-Instrument Maker and Repairer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7311 (ISCO08)**

Precision-instrument makers and repairers in China manufacture, calibrate, and repair high-accuracy instruments such as gauges, watches, and scientific apparatus. This occupation is concentrated in China's advanced manufacturing hubs like Guangdong and Jiangsu, supporting industries from automotive to aerospace. Workers typically require vocational training and MOHRSS skill certificates. Demand is driven by quality control needs and the growth of precision engineering, but automation is reducing low-skilled roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational school diploma and a junior technician role. With experience and additional certifications (e.g., technician to senior technician), workers can move into supervisory, quality control, or R&D positions. Some become independent repair specialists. Continuing education in emerging technologies (e.g., CNC calibration, electronic instruments) is increasingly important. The path is stable but competitive; advancement often requires multi-skilling.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repeated part dimension measurement and recording (replaced by AI vision and automated instruments)
- Calibration process for standard instruments (taken over by AI automatic calibration algorithms)
- Remote diagnosis of common faults (AI knowledge base provides solutions)
- Inventory management and material procurement (optimised by AI prediction systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis (AI-generated fault trees and historical cases)
- Fine-tuning decisions in precision adjustment (AI provides real-time compensation suggestions)
- Customized instrument design (AI-assisted 3D modeling and tolerance analysis)
- Repair plan optimization (AI compares efficiency and cost of multiple approaches)
- Multi-domain knowledge quick retrieval (AI integrating optics, mechanics, electronics materials)

**Human moat:**

- Manual finishing and tactile feedback (e.g., micron-level polishing, balance tuning)
- Repair of non-standard/antique instruments (no blueprints, no spare parts)
- Communicate with clients to understand custom requirements and translate them into technical solutions
- On-site emergency repairs (decision-making in uncontrollable environments)

**Skills to build:**

- Using AI-assisted fault diagnosis tools (e.g., image-based inspection systems)
- CNC machine and 3D printing operation (for manufacturing replacement parts)
- Digital measurement and reverse engineering software (e.g., CAD, laser scanning).
- IoT sensor and smart instrument debugging.
- Python scripts (automating repetitive data collection)
- Client communication and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- precision manufacturing
- medical devices
- automation
- calibration services

## 7. FAQ

**Q: Can a foreign precision-instrument maker get a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Most roles fall under Tier C (ordinary skilled), requiring employer sponsorship, relevant qualifications, and at least 2 years of experience. Tier A or B is rare unless you have exceptional skills or awards.

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by location and skill level. Entry-level workers earn around 60,000-80,000 CNY annually, while experienced senior technicians can earn 150,000-200,000 CNY or more in industrial hubs.

**Q: What training is needed to qualify as a precision-instrument maker in China?**
A: At minimum, a vocational high school diploma (3 years) in precision machining or instrument technology. Many also attend technician colleges (3 more years) and obtain the national skill certificate (level 4 or 3 is typical for entry).
