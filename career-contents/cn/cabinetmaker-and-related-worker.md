# Cabinet-Maker — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7522 (ISCO08)**

Cabinet-makers and related workers in China design, construct, and install wooden cabinets, furniture, and fixtures, primarily in residential and commercial settings. The trade is rooted in China's long-standing woodworking tradition, with modern demand driven by rapid urbanization, real estate development, and a growing middle class seeking custom home interiors. Workers typically gain skills through vocational schools or apprenticeships, often in provinces like Guangdong, Zhejiang, and Jiangsu, which host major furniture manufacturing hubs. While automation has increased in large factories, bespoke cabinetry still requires manual craftsmanship, especially for high-end projects. The occupation is dominated by domestic labor, with limited foreign worker participation except for niche luxury or heritage work. Income varies significantly based on location, skill level, and studio vs. factory employment.

Employment for cabinet-makers is expected to remain stable in China, supported by sustained construction activity and home renovation demand. Conversely, skilled artisans in custom and luxury segments will enjoy steady demand, especially in first-tier cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Shenzhen, where high-end real estate and interior design flourish. The government's push for vocational education and 'Made in China 2025' may produce more trained workers, increasing competition. Overall, the outlook is moderate, with displacement risk for semi-skilled roles but opportunity for those mastering CAD/CAM or traditional joinery.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cabinet-makers in China typically moves from apprentice to journeyman then master craftsman or supervisor. Many start in workshop environments and later open their own studios or join design firms. With experience, some transition into carpentry management, quality control, or teaching at vocational colleges. Cross-training in CNC programming, wood finishing, or interior design can boost earning potential and independence. In luxury markets, collaboration with architects and interior designers is common. Older masters still train apprentices through traditional lineages, but formal certification via MOHRSS (e.g., Woodworker Skill Levels) is increasingly valued. Entrepreneurship is a common path, especially in smaller cities where local furniture brands thrive.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD/CAM software to automatically generate cutting paths and material lists
- Batch assembly and cutting of standardized cabinets (done by CNC robots)
- Finished product quality inspection via AI vision detection system
- BIM-based automatic measurement and adjustment planning for on-site installation dimensions
- Automated inventory management and replenishment system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI-driven custom design software to quickly generate client proposals (e.g. space fitting and style recommendations)
- AR/VR-assisted customer preview of finished product results, improving communication efficiency
- AI optimizes material usage and reduces waste
- Smart tools (e.g., electric screwdrivers with automatic torque adjustment) improve precision in manual installation
- Project management platform automates scheduling and progress tracking.

**Human moat:**

- Hand carving and antique finishing techniques for high-end custom cabinets
- Creativity and experience judgment for solving non-standard installation issues on site
- Ability to communicate aesthetic needs with clients and translate them into unique designs
- Machining and assembly techniques for complex curved and irregularly shaped parts
- Touch-dependent skills such as wood property identification and defect repair

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in CAD/CAM software (e.g., TopSolid, WoodWop)
- Learn AI-assisted design tools (e.g., Autodesk Fusion 360 generative design)
- Improve client communication and project management skills
- Operate CNC machining centers and basic robot maintenance.
- Learn BIM modeling (e.g., Revit) for building integration
- Digital manufacturing (3D printing molds, laser cutting)

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom Furniture
- Residential Renovation
- Interior Design
- Heritage Restoration

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What can a cabinet-maker earn in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely: junior workers in factories earn around 50,000-80,000 CNY/year; experienced craftsmen in custom studios earn 100,000-200,000 CNY/year; top masters in luxury segment can exceed 300,000 CNY/year. Self-employed earnings depend on client base.

**Q: Can a foreign cabinet-maker get a work visa for China?**
A: It's challenging. Most positions are filled locally. Exceptions exist for recognized master woodworkers or those hired by high-end furniture brands with a specific need. Usually, you must have at least 5 years of experience and a vocational certificate. Sponsored Z visa via Category C is possible but rare.

**Q: What are the main career advancement paths?**
A: Typical progression: apprentice → journeyman → master craftsman → workshop supervisor or studio owner. Some transition into furniture design, teaching, or quality inspection. Learning CNC and digital design can lead to higher-paying roles in modern factories.
