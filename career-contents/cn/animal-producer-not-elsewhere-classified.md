# Animal Producer Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6129 (ISCO08)**

Animal producers not elsewhere classified encompass a diverse range of occupations involved in the breeding, raising, and management of livestock, poultry, and other animals for food, fiber, or other products. In China, this sector is a vital part of the agricultural economy, with many workers employed on family farms, state-owned enterprises, or large-scale commercial operations. The industry is undergoing modernization, with increasing emphasis on biosecurity, animal welfare, and sustainable practices. Rural-to-urban migration has created labor shortages in some regions, while technological advancements are changing traditional roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for animal producers in China typically starts with hands-on farm work, advancing to supervisory or management positions on larger farms. Specialization is common, e.g., focusing on pig breeding, poultry health, or dairy herd management. Many workers gain experience through vocational training or apprenticeships, with some pursuing agricultural degrees for higher-level roles. The industry is consolidating, leading to fewer but larger farms, which may limit opportunities for small-scale producers but create pathways into agribusiness, veterinary services, or agricultural technology firms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Animal health monitoring and disease early warning report generation based on sensor data
- Automated control and optimization of feeding amounts and water intake
- Real-time monitoring and automatic adjustment of environmental parameters (temperature, humidity, ammonia)
- Breeding cycle records and mating time algorithm recommendations
- Automatic generation and preliminary analysis of farm production reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to predict optimal feed ratios and feeding strategies to improve production efficiency
- Using computer vision and sound analysis for early detection of abnormal animal behavior and diseases
- Optimise herd structure, culling time and breeding plans through data analysis
- Use digital twins to simulate the impact of different management plans on output and costs
- Use drones or robots to assist patrolling large farms and facilities

**Human moat:**

- Recognition of individual animal emotions and stress handling (e.g., birthing care, soothing injured animals)
- Flexible decision-making for on-site emergencies (e.g., crowd riots, complex trauma)
- Coordinate with veterinarians, nutritionists and other professionals to develop personalized management plans
- Follow and enforce strict animal welfare and biosecurity standards

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an animal producer in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region, farm size, and specialization. Entry-level workers may earn around 40,000-60,000 CNY annually, while experienced managers on large commercial farms can earn 100,000-150,000 CNY or more. Technicians with specialized skills in genetics or health management may command higher pay.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as an animal producer in China?**
A: It is very difficult. Foreigner's Work Permits (Z visa) for animal production are rarely granted because the job is not considered in shortage and can be filled locally. Only highly specialized roles (e.g., rare breed experts) might qualify for a Category B permit, but most falls under Category C (restricted).

**Q: Is there demand for foreign animal producers in China?**
A: Demand is minimal as China's animal production sector is largely self-sufficient. Foreign experts might be hired for short-term projects in biotechnology or by international joint ventures, but long-term employment is uncommon.
