# Livestock and Dairy Producer — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 6121 (ISCO08)**

Livestock and dairy producers in China manage the breeding, feeding, and health of cattle, sheep, pigs, and other animals for meat, milk, and other products. This role is critical in China's rapidly modernizing agricultural sector, which aims to increase efficiency and food safety through large-scale, technology-driven operations. Producers oversee daily operations, ensure animal welfare, and comply with strict biosecurity and environmental regulations. While the occupation is primarily domestic, opportunities exist for foreign experts with specialized skills in intensive farming systems, dairy science, or veterinary medicine, typically through employer-sponsored work permits for high-level talent.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 18,576,772. China's livestock sector is consolidating from smallholder farms to large, industrialized operations, driven by rising meat and dairy demand. This shift creates demand for producers skilled in automated feeding systems, genetic improvement, and disease management. Government policies promoting food security and sustainable agriculture will sustain employment. However, the overall workforce in primary agriculture is declining due to urbanization. Growth will be focused in specialized, high-productivity roles rather than general labor. Foreign experts may find niches in elite dairy farms or technology-driven livestock enterprises.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm hand or technician, advancing to supervisor, farm manager, or production specialist. In China, large-scale operations offer clear hierarchies, with roles like herd manager, nutritional consultant, or quality control lead. Entrepreneurship is possible by starting a specialized farm. Professional development is supported through vocational training and certifications from the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs. Opportunities also exist in agri-tech companies providing equipment or consulting. Advanced skills in data analytics and precision farming can lead to higher positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding systems replace manual feeding and water management.
- Smart sensors automatically monitor animal health data and send alerts
- Milking robots replace manual milking operations.
- Automation of data recording and analysis replaces manual ledgers
- Driverless tractors replace some field labor

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted early disease warning system improves veterinary diagnostic accuracy.
- IoT optimizes pasture environment control and feed ratios on farms
- Drone inspection of large-scale pastures to improve management efficiency
- Data analysis tools assist breeding plans and yield prediction
- Blockchain technology tracks dairy supply chains to enhance trust

**Human moat:**

- On-site judgment of animal welfare and emergency health conditions
- Unstructured problem-solving ability in complex environments
- Interpersonal communication and team management with veterinarians, staff, and others
- Trade-off decisions on macro factors such as ecology, market, and policy.
- Intuition and feel gained from long-term farming experience

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and sensor technology application
- Automated equipment operation and maintenance
- Use of AI diagnostic tools for animal health
- Sustainable agriculture and precision farming practices
- Digital marketing and supply chain tracking
- Change management and team digital leadership

**Key growth areas:**

- precision livestock farming
- dairy automation
- biosecurity
- sustainable meat production

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for a livestock producer in China?**
A: Salaries vary widely: a farm manager in a large-scale dairy can earn 150,000-300,000 CNY annually; entry-level workers may earn 40,000-80,000 CNY. Foreign experts with specialized skills could earn higher, but it's not typical.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as a livestock producer in China?**
A: It is challenging but possible if sponsored by a large farm with proven need for foreign expertise. The position must meet government criteria for foreign workers, typically requiring at least a bachelor's degree and two years of relevant experience. Most livestock producers are not eligible.

**Q: What is the career progression for a livestock producer in China?**
A: Progression often follows: farm technician → supervisor → farm manager → regional production director. Large operations may offer specialization in areas like genetics, nutrition, or animal health. Some move into agri-business consulting or start their own farms.
