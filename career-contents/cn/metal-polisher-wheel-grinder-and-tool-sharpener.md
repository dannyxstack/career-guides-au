# Metal Polisher, Wheel Grinder and Tool Sharpener — career analysis · China

**Occupation code: 7224 (ISCO08)**

Metal polishers, wheel grinders and tool sharpeners in China use abrasive tools to finish metal surfaces, sharpen cutting tools, and grind components to precise tolerances. They work in manufacturing sectors such as automotive, aerospace, machinery, and tool production, often in factories with advanced CNC grinding equipment. Demand is driven by China's role as a global manufacturing hub, but automation is slowly replacing manual polishing. The workforce is typically experienced, with skills passed through vocational training or apprenticeships. Employers value precision and safety compliance, as workplace injuries from grinding are common.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or helper, advancing to skilled polisher/grinder after 2–4 years. With experience, workers can become team leaders or quality inspectors. Some transition to CNC machine operation or tool design. Certification through China's Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (MOHRSS) occupational skill levels (初级/中级/高级/技师/高级技师) can improve pay and mobility. Independent shop ownership is rare due to capital requirements. The role is physically demanding and often leads to burnout or health issues.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive metal surface polishing and grinding operations
- Use automated grinding machines for batch workpiece processing.
- Grinding wheel dressing and balancing based on preset parameters
- dimensional inspection and pass/fail determination of simple workpieces
- Automatic grinding of standard tool edges.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted process parameter optimization: automatically recommends grinding parameters based on workpiece material and precision requirements
- Predictive maintenance: AI analyzes equipment vibration and temperature data to provide early fault warnings
- Visual inspection: AI system checks surface finish in real-time, reducing manual inspection
- Collaborative robots: human-robot collaboration to move heavy workpieces, reducing labor intensity
- Digital Twin: Simulate machining processes, shorten trial cutting cycles

**Human moat:**

- Manual polishing and finishing of non-standard workpieces
- Manual adjustment and grinding of complex-shaped molds and tools
- On-site judgment and process adjustment for material defects
- Manual repair and precision restoration of aging equipment
- Communicate with clients on customized surface treatment requirements

**Skills to build:**

- CNC grinding programming and operation
- CAD/CAM software (e.g., Mastercam)
- Fundamentals of robotics and automation system integration.
- Data analysis and quality control (SPC)
- Additive manufacturing post-processing techniques
- Electrical and mechanical maintenance skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision machining
- Automotive manufacturing
- Aerospace components
- Tool maintenance

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a metal polisher in China?**
A: Annual pre-tax salaries range from CNY 60,000 to CNY 120,000, depending on experience, location, and factory type. Master technicians in high-end aerospace workshops may earn up to CNY 180,000.

**Q: Can a foreign metal polisher obtain a work visa for China?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. The role falls under Category C (limited supply) of the Foreigner's Work Permit system. The employer must prove that the position cannot be filled by a local worker. Visa approval is not guaranteed, and the process requires extensive documentation. The Z visa is tied to the sponsoring employer.

**Q: Do foreign qualifications count in China?**
A: Foreign vocational certificates are not directly recognized. Workers must often obtain Chinese certifications, such as the Grinding Tool Worker certificate, through training and exams. Some bilateral agreements may simplify the process, but generally retraining is required.
