# Building Construction Labourer — career analysis · Ireland

**Occupation code: 9313 (ISCO08)**

Construction workers perform various physical tasks on building sites, such as moving materials, digging, and cleaning up. They provide basic support to the construction team and assist skilled workers in completing tasks.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Construction workers can advance to foreman or supervisor, or become skilled workers (e.g., carpentry, bricklaying) through apprenticeship training. Many workers acquire additional skills to enhance career development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Transporting construction materials (replaced by automated transport vehicles/drones)
- Basic excavation and backfilling (replaced by remote-controlled excavators)
- Cleaning construction site waste (replaced by automated cleaning robots)
- Simple bricklaying/plastering (replaced by bricklaying robots)
- Manual measurement and marking (being replaced by laser scanning + AI)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR headsets to view construction drawings and process instructions
- Operate remote-controlled/semi-autonomous construction machinery (e.g., drone inspections).
- Optimize scheduling by using AI to predict weather and material needs.
- Using digital twins to simulate construction processes and reduce rework
- Collaborate with AI for quality inspection (e.g., crack detection)

**Human moat:**

- Flexible judgment and problem-solving in complex environments.
- Fine hand-eye coordination (e.g., installation of non-standard parts)
- Team communication and safety supervision
- Adapting to sudden changes (e.g., weather, design changes)
- Process intuition and experience (e.g., concrete curing)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated construction equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, bricklaying robots)
- Use BIM/AR tools for digital construction
- Basic programming (e.g., robot path debugging)
- Data Analysis (e.g., understanding sensor feedback)
- Safety Compliance and Emergency Response
- Cross-occupation collaboration and communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Infrastructure
- Housing
- Green Building
- Renovation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass General Employment P (General Employment Permit) | Applies to non-occupations in demand, requires employer sponsorship, annual salary threshold of €30,000 or more. Construction workers are usually not on the shortage list, so this permit is required. |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are salaries for construction workers?**
A: Junior workers earn around €25,000-32,000 per year, rising to €50,000 with experience, especially with special skills (e.g., working at heights).

**Q: How can non-EU citizens immigrate to Ireland as construction workers?**
A: Requires employer-sponsored General Employment Permit with annual salary of at least €30,000. This occupation is not on the shortage list, making applications somewhat difficult.

**Q: Can construction workers switch to other trades?**
A: Yes, becoming a skilled worker like a carpenter or electrician is possible through apprenticeships, boosting income and stability.
