# Life Science Technician — career analysis · Ireland

**Occupation code: 3141 (ISCO08)**

Life science technicians assist scientists in laboratories with biological, chemical, and medical research, handling sample preparation, instrument operation, data recording, and maintenance. Widely used in pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, food testing, and environmental monitoring.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career path: from junior technician to senior technician or lab supervisor. Also possible to move into quality control, regulatory affairs, or sales support. Further study can lead to scientist or R&D specialist roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine sample preparation and processing procedures
- Basic instrument operation and data collection
- Experimental data collation and preliminary report generation
- Reagent inventory management and automatic recording
- Repetitive testing in standard processes

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize experimental design and high-throughput screening using AI
- Analyzing complex biological data (e.g., omics data) through machine learning.
- Use AI-assisted literature retrieval and knowledge management
- AI-assisted microscope image recognition and quantitative analysis
- Adopt automated laboratory systems to increase throughput and reproducibility

**Human moat:**

- Experimental design innovation and hypothesis generation
- Identification of abnormal data and root cause analysis
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication skills
- Regulatory compliance (e.g., GLP, GCP) and quality control
- Ethical judgment and scientific integrity

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and data science
- Machine learning basics (scikit-learn, TensorFlow)
- Operation of laboratory automation systems (e.g., LIMS, ELN)
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., BLAST, Galaxy, Python libraries)
- Critical thinking and complex problem solving
- Project management and cross-team collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- biopharma
- R&D expansion
- QC demand
- lab automation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass Critical Skills Empl (Critical Skills Employment Permit) | Applicable to occupations in demand; annual salary must be €32,000+ (bachelor's degree) or €64,000+ (no degree). Fast-track family reunification allowed. |
| Subclass General Employment P (General Employment Permit) | Applicable to positions with an annual salary of €30,000 or more, requiring labor market testing. Quota-based system; non-EU applicants need employer sponsorship. |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for life science technicians in Ireland?**
A: Entry-level annual salary around €28,000-€35,000, rising to €45,000-€60,000 with experience. Pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies often offer higher salaries, along with bonuses and benefits.

**Q: How can overseas individuals become life science technicians in Ireland through skilled migration?**
A: A work permit is usually required: if the occupation is on the Critical Skills list (e.g., biochemical technician), you can apply for a Critical Skills Employment Permit with an annual salary of €32,000 or more, without a labor market test. Alternatively, you can apply for a General Employment Permit, but a labor market test is required.

**Q: What qualifications do you need in Ireland to work as a life science technician?**
A: Usually requires a bachelor's degree in a relevant field (life sciences, biochemistry, etc.). Some technician roles also accept advanced diplomas or applied science degrees. Some employers require GMP training.
