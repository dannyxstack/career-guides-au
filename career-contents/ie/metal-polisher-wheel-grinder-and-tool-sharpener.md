## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Can progress from apprentice to senior technician, then to production supervisor or quality controller; can also transition to precision machining fields.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use polishing and grinding machines to mechanically grind and finish metal surfaces.
- Rely on visual inspection and experience to judge whether surface finish meets standards
- repetitive manual polishing operations, such as deburring and scratch removal
- Adjust polishing parameters based on simple drawings or verbal instructions

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI vision inspection systems to monitor polishing quality in real time and provide adjustment recommendations.
- Use robotic polishing arms to achieve high-precision, consistent polishing, with humans responsible for programming and monitoring
- Simulating polishing process parameters using digital twins to reduce trial-and-error costs.
- Using smart tools to record polishing data and optimize process flow

**Human moat:**

- High-precision manual polishing of complex curved surfaces or artwork (e.g. moulds, jewellery)
- On-site fault diagnosis and emergency handling, e.g., sudden abnormality in polishing machines
- Communicating with clients on customized polishing requirements and understanding aesthetic intent
- Flexible Judgment and Experiential Decision-Making in Multi-Process Collaborative Operations

**Skills to build:**

- Robot programming and debugging (e.g., FANUC, ABB)
- CAD/CAM software application, understanding of polishing path generation
- Quality inspection data analysis (SPC, AI vision software)
- Automated production line maintenance and troubleshooting
- Fundamentals of digital twin or simulation software (e.g., Ansys, COMSOL)
- Lean production and process optimization methods

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Engineering
- Manufacturing
- Automation
- Medical Devices
