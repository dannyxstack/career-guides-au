# Subsistence Mixed Crop and Livestock Farmer — career analysis · Ireland

**Occupation code: 6330 (ISCO08)**

Self-sufficient mixed crop and livestock farmers in Ireland engage in small-scale agriculture, growing crops and raising livestock to meet basic household needs, with occasional small market sales.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★☆☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,500. Overall employment in Irish agriculture is stable, but the number of self-sufficient farmers is slowly declining due to EU Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) support and agricultural modernisation trends. The occupation is expected to decrease by about 5% over the next decade, mainly due to larger-scale operations and youth outflow.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

This occupation has limited career progression; most practitioners run family farms. A few can increase income through expansion, organic farming, or agritourism, but this usually requires additional investment and skills training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically monitor soil moisture, crop growth and livestock health data (replacing manual inspection and recording)
- Using AI to analyze weather and market data to assist decision-making (replacing some experience-based judgment)
- Automated Feeding and Milking Systems (Replacing Repetitive Physical Labour)
- AI generates farm management reports and bookkeeping (replaces manual records)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI optimizes crop rotation and polyculture plans to improve land productivity
- Precise application of fertilizer and pesticides using drones and image recognition, reducing waste
- Market analysis tools help farmers choose the best selling times
- Smart sensors warn of animal diseases and crop pests.
- Digital platform connects small farmers, shares resources and knowledge

**Human moat:**

- Planting and livestock experience and intuition based on local climate and soil
- Subtle observation and assessment of livestock health and behaviour
- Interpersonal trust built with neighbors and local markets
- Flexibility in responding to extreme weather and sudden disasters
- Traditional farming knowledge, cultural heritage, and craftsmanship

**Skills to build:**

- Use agricultural IoT devices (sensors, drones)
- Basic data analysis and interpretation (e.g., Excel, simple AI tools).
- Sustainable agriculture and precision farming practices
- Digital market sales and social media marketing
- Ability to repair and modify small agricultural machinery
- Ecological diversity and natural resource management.

**Key growth areas:**

- declining
- small-scale
- organic transition
- agri-tourism

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the income of subsistence farmers?**
A: Income is very low, mainly from the value of self-produced food and CAP subsidies, typically between 10,000 and 25,000 euros per year.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens immigrate to Ireland through this occupation?**
A: No. This occupation does not meet any work visa requirements; only EU/EEA citizens can work freely.

**Q: What educational background is required?**
A: No formal education requirements, but short-term agricultural courses offered by Teagasc can help improve skills.
