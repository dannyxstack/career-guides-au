# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Ireland

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

Performing physical labour on mixed farms in Ireland, such as planting, harvesting, feeding animals, and cleaning enclosures, often seasonal work.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

This role has limited promotion opportunities; you can gain experience and move up to farm supervisor or shift to animal/crop technician, but additional training is needed.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated execution of crop sowing, fertilization, and irrigation
- Automated feeding, milking, and health monitoring of livestock
- Farm weed identification and targeted weeding
- Yield prediction and harvest scheduling optimization
- Robotisation of stable cleaning and waste management

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making: analyzing crop growth conditions from satellite imagery and sensor data to optimize fertilization and irrigation
- Smart livestock management: wearable devices monitor health, AI predicts diseases
- Precision agriculture planning: predict weather and market prices to formulate planting/breeding plans
- Automated recording and compliance: GPS tracking of pesticide use, generating environmental reports
- Remote farm monitoring: Real-time livestock status and equipment operation via mobile app

**Human moat:**

- Mechanical fault diagnosis and on-site repair in complex environments
- Identification of abnormal livestock behavior and emergency veterinary handling
- Adjust agricultural operations on the fly based on unstructured situations
- Interpersonal communication and negotiation with suppliers and regulatory bodies
- Long-term planning for land management and ecological diversity

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart agricultural machinery such as tractors/harvesters
- Precision agriculture software (e.g., FarmLogs, Climate FieldView)
- Basic data analysis (Excel/Google Sheets, basic statistics)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Knowledge of safety regulations (pesticide use, animal welfare laws).
- Basic English communication (equipment manuals, client interaction).

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal demand
- Mechanisation reducing unskilled labour
- Organic farming niche
- Brexit labour impact

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy outdoor physical work and don't mind seasonal unemployment
- Young people seeking temporary or short-term work to gain experience
- EU citizens seeking to utilize free movement rights to enter Irish agriculture.

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking stable high income and career advancement.
- People who dislike physical labor or are not suited to Ireland's rainy weather.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for mixed farm laborers in Ireland?**
A: Entry-level workers earn approximately €22,000–€28,000 per year, or €11–€14 per hour. Experienced workers can earn over €30,000, but still below the national average salary.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens immigrate to Ireland through this occupation?**
A: Very difficult. This occupation is not on the shortage list and salaries are usually below the general employment permit threshold of €30,000. Seasonal permits are limited to 9 months and do not lead to residency.

**Q: Does this career have growth prospects?**
A: Limited prospects. Mechanization reduces demand for unskilled labor, but organic farming and the local food movement may create a few new jobs. Promotion requires additional training.
