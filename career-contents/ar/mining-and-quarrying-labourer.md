**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for mining and quarrying labourers in Argentina?**
A: Monthly salaries for mining and quarrying labourers in Argentina typically range from 150,000 to 250,000 Argentine pesos (ARS), depending on region, employer, and experience. Higher pay may be offered in remote areas like San Juan or Santa Cruz. Overtime and productivity bonuses are common in larger operations.

**Q: What skills are needed to work as a mining and quarrying labourer in Argentina?**
A: Key skills include physical stamina, ability to operate hand tools and simple machinery, basic safety training, and teamwork. Many employers require a high school diploma and on-site safety certifications. Experience in heavy labour or construction is valued. Knowledge of Spanish is essential for communication.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence impacting mining and quarrying labourer jobs in Argentina?**
A: AI-driven automation affects some tasks like drilling and ore sorting, but manual labour remains crucial for maintenance, inspection, and safety monitoring. AI tools assist in predictive maintenance and surveillance, reducing risks. However, routine physical tasks are still primarily done by workers, limiting immediate job displacement.
