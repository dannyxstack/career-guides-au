- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Argentina?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on the region and type of fishing. In Argentina, entry-level workers earn around ARS 80,000–120,000 monthly, while experienced crew on coastal boats can earn ARS 150,000–250,000. Income is often seasonal and supplemented by profit-sharing in cooperative fisheries. Overtime during peak seasons can boost earnings significantly.

**Q: What key skills are required for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Argentina?**
A: Essential skills include knowledge of local fishing grounds, net mending, boat maintenance, and safe navigation on rivers or coastal waters. Physical stamina and ability to work in harsh weather are crucial. Familiarity with Argentine fishing regulations and sustainable practices is increasingly valued. Basic first aid and swimming skills are also recommended.

**Q: What is the career outlook for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Argentina?**
A: The outlook is stable but affected by overfishing and climate change. In Argentina, inland fisheries (e.g., Paraná River) face pressures from pollution and water management, while coastal fisheries (e.g., Mar del Plata) rely on species like hake. Employment is seasonal, with opportunities in both artisanal and small-scale commercial sectors. Demand for sustainable practices may create new roles in monitoring.
