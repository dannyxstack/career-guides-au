## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for garment patternmakers and cutters in Argentina?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, with entry-level positions earning around ARS 45,000–60,000 per month, while experienced workers can earn ARS 80,000–120,000. In Buenos Aires, pay tends to be higher due to the concentration of textile and fashion industries. Many positions also offer production bonuses.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence impacting garment patternmaking and cutting in Argentina?**
A: AI-powered software is increasingly used for automated pattern grading, marker making, and fabric cutting optimization, mainly in large factories. This reduces manual work but also requires patternmakers to develop digital skills. However, small workshops still rely on traditional techniques, and human oversight remains essential for complex designs and quality control.

**Q: What is the career outlook for garment patternmakers and cutters in Argentina?**
A: Employment opportunities are stable but competitive, driven by the local textile and fashion industry, including SMEs and export-oriented firms. Demand for skilled patternmakers persists, especially those proficient in both manual and CAD methods. Economic fluctuations affect hiring, but experienced workers with technical expertise remain valued.
