## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Argentina?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Argentina typically earn a very low and variable income, often just enough to cover basic needs. Annual earnings can range from USD 2,000 to 5,000 equivalent in Argentine pesos, depending on herd size and market prices. Most income comes from selling calves, milk, or wool locally, with little profit after expenses like feed and veterinary care.

**Q: What does a day-to-day work routine look like for a subsistence livestock farmer in Argentina?**
A: A typical day starts before dawn, caring for cattle, sheep, or goats. Tasks include feeding, watering, checking for illness, moving animals to fresh pasture, and mending fences. Farmers also manage land, repair equipment, and handle births. Seasonal tasks like branding, shearing, or haymaking add to the workload. The work is physically demanding and weather-dependent, with little to no mechanization in many areas.

**Q: How is AI impacting subsistence livestock farming in Argentina?**
A: AI has minimal direct impact on subsistence livestock farmers due to high costs and limited infrastructure in remote areas. While technologies like automated feeders or health monitoring exist, they are unaffordable for most. However, AI-driven market price apps and weather forecasting tools are slowly becoming accessible via basic smartphones, helping farmers make better decisions on selling livestock and planning grazing.
