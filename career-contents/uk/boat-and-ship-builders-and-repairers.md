# Boat and ship builders and repairers — career analysis · United Kingdom

**Occupation code: 5235 (SOC)**

Shipbuilder and repairer build, install, and repair wooden hull structures and fittings, and form, position, rivet, and seal metal plates and components to create metal structures and frameworks for ships.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression can start from an apprentice, and gradually advance to senior technician, team leader, or shipbuilding manager. Holding advanced welding or composite material certificates can lead to yacht construction or naval projects, with some moving into design or quality inspection roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Drawing-based sheet metal cutting and nesting (AI vision + plasma cutter automated execution)
- Standard Node Welding and Riveting (replaced by Collaborative Robot with Weld Seam Tracking System)
- Repeated positioning and clamping in hull block assembly (replaced by automated fixtures and AGVs)
- Dimensional measurement in quality inspection (3D scanning + AI deviation analysis replaces manual calipers)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- 3D modeling and digital twin simulation of complex hull structures (AI-assisted design optimization)
- Real-time optimization of welding parameters (machine learning automatically recommends parameters based on sheet thickness and environment)
- Repair solution diagnosis (AI compares historical fault database to assist in locating corrosion or fatigue cracks)
- Supply chain and material management (AI forecasts sheet demand to reduce inventory waste)

**Human moat:**

- Non-standard emergency repairs: assess structural damage on-site and design temporary reinforcement plans
- Experience in hand lay-up and joining of new composite materials (carbon fiber/aluminum alloy)
- Overall shipyard construction scheduling and multi-trade coordination capability.
- Interpreting client needs and communicating customized design solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Shipbuilding CAD/CAM (e.g., Rhino, ShipConstructor) and digital twin basics
- Robot welding/cutting programming and teaching (e.g., FANUC, KUKA)
- Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing Marine Spare Parts) Principles and Operation
- Nondestructive Testing (UT/PAUT) Data Analysis and AI-Assisted Interpretation
- Project management (PMP or lean shipbuilding methods)
- Basic Python or scripting for automated report generation

**Key growth areas:**

- Shipbuilding
- Marine repair
- Composite materials
- Welding

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Craftspeople who enjoy hands-on work and attention to detail
- Passionate about ships and navigation technology
- Willing to learn specialized skills such as welding or composite materials.

**Who is not suited**
- Dislike physical labor or outdoor work environments
- Severe fear of working at sea or in shipyards

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for a naval architect?**
A: Junior technician annual salary approx £22,000-28,000, mid-level skilled worker £28,000-38,000, senior or supervisor up to £38,000-50,000. Overtime and sea allowances can increase income.

**Q: How can overseas workers immigrate to the UK through the naval architect occupation?**
A: This occupation is on the shortage occupation list, eligible for Skilled Worker visa, requiring employer sponsorship and meeting salary requirements (currently £26,200/year). UK qualifications or work experience may increase chances.

**Q: What are the advancement paths for shipbuilders?**
A: Can progress from apprentice to tradesperson, then to senior tradesperson, supervisor, or quality inspector. Some can become ship designers or project managers with additional training.
