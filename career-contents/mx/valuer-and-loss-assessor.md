# Valuer and Loss Assessor — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 3315 (ISCO08)**

Valuers and loss assessors estimate the value of property, goods, and equipment, and assess insurance claims. They play a critical role in real estate transactions, insurance settlements, and financial reporting. In Mexico, these professionals commonly work for appraisal firms, insurance companies, banks, or as independent consultants. The role requires strong analytical skills, attention to detail, and knowledge of market trends and legal regulations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with assistant roles, advancing to independent valuers or senior assessors. Experienced professionals may become branch managers, consultants, or open their own firms. Specialization in certain asset types (e.g., machinery, fine art) or sectors (e.g., insurance) can lead to higher earnings. Continuous education and certification are important for advancement.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Valuer and Loss Assessor in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience, specialization, and sector. Entry-level valuers earn modest incomes, while senior professionals with certifications can earn competitive wages. Many also receive commissions or bonuses.

**Q: How can a foreigner work as a Valuer and Loss Assessor in Mexico?**
A: Foreigners typically need an employer-sponsored Temporary Resident Visa with a work permit. The employer must demonstrate that no qualified local worker is available. Holding a recognized certification can strengthen the application.

**Q: What advancement opportunities exist for Valuers in Mexico?**
A: Valuers can advance to senior or supervisory roles, specialize in high-value assets (e.g., commercial property), or become independent consultants. Some move into risk management or insurance underwriting.
