# Water and Firewood Collector — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 9624 (ISCO08)**

Water and firewood collectors gather natural resources for household use or local sale, primarily in rural and peri-urban areas of Mexico. They may fetch water from communal wells, rivers, or springs, and collect firewood from forests or scrubland, often using basic tools and manual transport. This occupation is largely informal, with no formal employment contracts or social benefits. Workers may be self-employed or engage in subsistence activities to support their families. The role demands physical stamina and knowledge of local terrain and resource availability. It is associated with communities lacking access to piped water and modern energy, making it more common in less developed regions.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 9,429. The employment outlook for water and firewood collectors in Mexico is declining due to urbanization, expansion of water infrastructure, and increased adoption of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and electricity for cooking and heating. Government programs such as 'Programa de Desarrollo Rural' aim to improve rural energy access, reducing dependence on firewood. However, in remote indigenous communities and areas with chronic water scarcity, demand for these services persists. Overall, formal employment opportunities are minimal; the occupation remains a subsistence activity rather than a career path. Climate change may exacerbate water shortages, potentially sustaining informal collection roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for water and firewood collectors is extremely limited. Workers typically remain in the same informal role or transition to other unskilled manual labor in agriculture, construction, or domestic work. Some may move into small-scale vending of firewood or water, but formal sector entry is rare. There are no structured training or certification pathways. With improved infrastructure, many workers exit the occupation entirely. The lack of social protections and irregular income make it a precarious livelihood with no upward mobility.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do water and firewood collectors earn in Mexico?**
A: Income is typically very low and irregular, often below the official minimum wage. Most work is informal without fixed pay; earnings vary by region and effort. Collectors may receive payment in kind or small cash amounts per load.

**Q: Can a water and firewood collector get a work visa for Mexico?**
A: No. This occupation does not qualify for employer-sponsored work visas as Mexican immigration authorities require skilled positions with formal contracts. The role is considered informal and low-skilled, with no migration pathway.
