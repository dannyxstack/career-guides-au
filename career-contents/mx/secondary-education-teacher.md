# Secondary Education Teacher — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2330 (ISCO08)**

Secondary education teachers in Mexico instruct students typically aged 12–15 in lower secondary (secundaria) or 15–18 in upper secondary (preparatoria/bachillerato). They specialize in subjects like math, science, language, or social studies, develop lesson plans, assess student progress, and often participate in school administration. The role requires strong communication, pedagogical skills, and adaptation to diverse classrooms. While most positions are in public schools (SEP-regulated), private and international schools also hire teachers, often requiring additional qualifications. The profession is stable but competitive, with opportunities in underserved regions and bilingual education.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 313,411. Mexico's secondary education sector is growing due to demographic trends and government efforts to increase coverage, especially in rural areas. The OECD recommends improving teacher training and retention. Demand for STEM and English teachers is rising, while enrollment in upper secondary has expanded under mandatory schooling reforms. However, budget constraints and temporary contracts limit job security. Overall, employment is expected to grow modestly (2–4% annually), with an emphasis on public school placements and bilingual programs. Economic downturns may slow hiring.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a classroom teaching role, advancing to academic coordinator, head of department, or school principal with experience and additional certifications. Many teachers move into curriculum development, educational consulting, or teacher training. Opportunities to work in private or international schools can increase salary. Some pursue graduate studies (master's in education or specialized subject) to qualify for higher-ranked positions or university teaching. Public school teachers may join the national teachers' union (SNTE) for tenure and benefits, but promotion is often seniority-based.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a secondary teacher in Mexico?**
A: Public school teachers earn 8,000–15,000 MXN/month (96,000–180,000 MXN/year) depending on level and experience. Private schools may pay 12,000–25,000 MXN/month. International schools can offer 20,000–40,000 MXN/month. Salaries vary by state and type of school.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa to teach secondary school in Mexico?**
A: Yes, but it is employer-sponsored. You need a job offer from a school, a valid bachelor's degree (apostilled and translated), and teaching experience. The employer applies for a Temporary Resident Visa with work permit. Bilingual (English) teachers have better chances, but the process can be lengthy and requires meeting SEP qualification equivalency.

**Q: Is teaching secondary school a regulated profession in Mexico?**
A: Yes. All teachers must have a bachelor's degree in education or a related field and obtain a Cédula Profesional from SEP. Public school teachers also need to pass competitive exams (e.g., for new hires). Private schools may have more flexibility but usually require certification.
