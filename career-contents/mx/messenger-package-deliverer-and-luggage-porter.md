# Messengers, Package Deliverers and Luggage Porters — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 9621 (ISCO08)**

Messengers, package deliverers and luggage porters transport items and luggage on foot or by bicycle, motorcycle or light vehicle. In Mexico, they are common in urban courier services, e-commerce last-mile delivery, hotels, airports and bus stations. Work is often informal or semi-formal, with many employed by small courier firms or on a commission basis. The job requires physical stamina, punctuality and basic navigation skills. Advancement is limited without additional training or capital to start a delivery business.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is limited without additional qualifications or moving into self-employment. A messenger/deliverer may become a team leader or dispatcher in a larger courier company. Others save capital to acquire their own vehicle and start a small delivery service. There is no formal ladder; most stay in the role or exit to similar manual jobs. Upskilling (e.g., logistics certification) can open doors to warehouse or transport coordination roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually delivering letters and small parcels by address
- Handle and store luggage at hotels or stations
- Operate simple material handling equipment (hand trucks, lifts)
- Read paper maps to plan delivery routes
- Manually sort packages and verify addresses

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for a messenger/deliverer in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely by employer and region. Most deliverers earn near the minimum wage, often supplemented by tips or commission. Annual pre-tax income typically ranges from MXN 60,000 to MXN 120,000, with some app-based gig workers earning less.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for this job in Mexico?**
A: It is theoretically possible through employer sponsorship for a Temporary Resident Visa with work permit, but it is extremely rare due to low wages and abundant local labor. The role does not qualify for any skilled migration route.

**Q: What is the career progression for a messenger/deliverer?**
A: Progression is limited. Some workers become dispatchers, supervisors, or start their own small delivery business. Others transition into warehouse or logistics roles with additional training.
