# Meteorologist — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2112 (ISCO08)**

Meteorologists study atmospheric phenomena to forecast weather and analyze climate patterns. In Mexico, they are primarily employed by the National Meteorological Service (SMN) under CONAGUA, as well as universities and private environmental consulting firms. Their work supports agriculture, aviation, disaster prevention, and water resource management. With climate change increasing extreme weather events, demand for meteorologists is growing, though competition for government positions remains high.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 12,197. The employment outlook for meteorologists in Mexico is stable to positive, driven by climate change adaptation needs and government investment in weather monitoring infrastructure. The SMN continues to expand its network of radars and stations. However, most openings are in the public sector, which offers job security but limited salary growth. Private sector opportunities exist in renewable energy and agricultural consulting, but are niche. Overall, moderate growth is expected over the next decade, with slight emphasis on hydrometeorology and climate modeling.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for meteorologists in Mexico typically starts with a role as a weather observer or forecaster at the SMN. With experience and additional certifications, one can move into specialized areas like climatology, hydrometeorology, or research. Senior positions include chief meteorologist or head of department. Academic career paths via universities offer roles as professors/researchers. Many professionals also transition into environmental consultancy or data analysis. Advancement often requires a master's or doctorate for research-oriented roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of routine weather forecast text
- Compilation and preliminary statistical analysis of historical meteorological data
- Standard warning content editing based on numerical model output
- Simple fault diagnosis and standardized reporting of meteorological observation equipment
- Write descriptive reports on basic climate trends

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Interpretation and Probability Calibration of Ensemble Forecasts Using AI
- Combine deep learning to predict local extreme weather (e.g., thunderstorms, heavy rain).
- AI-assisted climate change attribution analysis and scenario simulation
- Automatically generate customized industry weather reports (agriculture, energy).
- Using natural language processing to automatically extract meteorological research progress from literature

**Human moat:**

- Physical intuition and interdisciplinary understanding of complex weather systems
- Deliver authoritative advice to the public and decision-makers during high-impact weather events
- Designing novel observation experiments and scientific methods for integrating multi-source data
- Assume legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., consequences of failing to issue early warnings)
- Collaborative decision-making with related fields (hydrology, ecology)

**Skills to build:**

- Climate big data visualization and interactive dashboard development

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for meteorologists in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely; entry-level government meteorologists earn around 250,000-350,000 MXN annually, while senior specialists can earn up to 600,000 MXN. Private sector roles may offer higher pay but are scarce.

**Q: Can a foreign meteorologist work in Mexico?**
A: Yes, but you need a job offer from a Mexican employer who sponsors a Temporary Resident Visa with work permit. Certification and recognition of your degree are required. PR is possible after long-term residency.

**Q: What education is required to become a meteorologist in Mexico?**
A: A bachelor's degree in meteorology, atmospheric sciences, geophysics, or a related engineering field is required. A master's or doctorate is often needed for research positions. Degrees from accredited Mexican universities or recognized foreign institutions are accepted.
