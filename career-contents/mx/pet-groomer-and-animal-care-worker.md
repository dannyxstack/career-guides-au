# Pet Groomer and Animal Care Worker — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 5164 (ISCO08)**

Pet groomers and animal care workers provide grooming, bathing, and basic care for domestic animals such as dogs and cats, often in salons, kennels, or pet stores. They perform tasks like brushing, trimming nails, cleaning ears, and handling animals with care. Many also offer pet sitting or walking services. This occupation requires patience, animal handling skills, and attention to cleanliness. While some acquire formal training through short courses or apprenticeships, others learn on the job. In Mexico, employment is largely informal, with opportunities concentrated in urban areas where pet ownership is growing.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited for pet groomers. Many start as assistants and become independent groomers after gaining experience, potentially opening their own salons. Specialization in breeds or medical grooming can increase income. Some move into pet store management or animal care roles in shelters. Continuing education through workshops or certification programs can enhance skills, but formal career ladders are absent. The path is entrepreneurial for those seeking advancement.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for pet groomers in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely and are often informal. Entry-level groomers may earn modest hourly wages or commissions, with experienced independent groomers earning more. No precise official figures exist due to informal sector dominance.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a pet groomer in Mexico?**
A: It is extremely difficult. Pet grooming does not appear on shortage lists, and employers must demonstrate no suitable Mexican worker. Most foreign pet groomers work informally or as tourists, which is not legal for paid employment.

**Q: Does pet grooming have career growth in Mexico?**
A: Growth is limited but possible through self-employment, specialization (e.g., show grooming), or opening a salon. Formal employment with benefits is rare, and most remain independent or part-time.
