# Psychologist — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2634 (ISCO08)**

Psychologists study cognitive, emotional, and social processes and behavior by observing, interpreting, and recording how individuals relate to one another and to their environments. They apply their knowledge to promote personal, social, educational, or vocational adjustment and development. In Mexico, psychologists work in clinical, organizational, educational, and research settings, often requiring a cédula profesional to practice legally.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a bachelor's degree and licensing, followed by specialization through master's or doctoral studies. Early roles include assistant psychologist or intern, advancing to licensed clinical psychologist, senior therapist, or private practice owner. Organizational psychologists may move into HR leadership, while academics pursue research and teaching. Continuing education is key for career growth.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a psychologist in Mexico?**
A: Salaries range from 180,000 to 360,000 MXN annually for entry-level, increasing with experience and specialization. Private practice and corporate roles can exceed 600,000 MXN.

**Q: Can a foreign psychologist work in Mexico?**
A: Yes, with a job offer and a Temporary Resident Visa. The employer must sponsor the visa, and the psychologist must have their degree validated and obtain a cédula profesional.

**Q: What are the requirements to practice as a psychologist in Mexico?**
A: A bachelor's degree in psychology and registration with the Dirección General de Profesiones (Cédula). Specialization is not mandatory but recommended for clinical practice.
