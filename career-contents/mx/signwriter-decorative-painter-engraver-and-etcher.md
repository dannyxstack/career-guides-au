# Signwriter, Decorative Painter, Engraver or Etcher — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 7316 (ISCO08)**

Signwriters, decorative painters, engravers and etchers create and apply lettering, designs, and artwork on signs, buildings, glass, metal, and other surfaces. They use traditional hand tools as well as digital software and printing techniques. Their work ranges from hand-painted shop signs and murals to engraved plaques and etched glass. In Mexico, these craftspeople often work in small workshops, for sign companies, or as independent artisans, serving businesses, cultural institutions, and private clients.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do signwriters, decorative painters, engravers and etchers earn in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, location, and type of work. Entry-level workers may earn near minimum wage, while experienced master craftsmen can earn significantly more, especially in tourism-focused areas. The occupation tends to be on the lower end of skilled trades in Mexico.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a signwriter in Mexico?**
A: Yes, if you are a foreigner, you need a work visa. The most common route is the Temporary Resident Visa with a work permit, which requires a job offer from a Mexican employer who sponsors your application. Permanent residence is possible but rarely granted solely for this occupation.

**Q: What are the best regions in Mexico for this occupation?**
A: The best opportunities are in regions with strong tourism and cultural sectors, such as Mexico City, Guadalajara, Oaxaca, and the Yucatán Peninsula. These areas have demand for hand-painted signs, murals, and decorative work in hotels, restaurants, and heritage sites.
