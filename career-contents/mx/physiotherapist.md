# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Mexico are healthcare professionals who assess, diagnose, and treat physical impairments, disabilities, and pain through therapy, exercise, and manual techniques. They work in hospitals, clinics, rehabilitation centers, sports facilities, and private practice, often collaborating with doctors and other specialists. The profession is regulated by the Ministry of Public Education, requiring a university degree and professional certification (cédula profesional). With an aging population and growing awareness of rehabilitation, demand is steady, particularly in urban areas and private healthcare. However, public sector opportunities are limited and often lower-paid.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with general practice, then specialization in areas like orthopedics, neurology, or pediatrics. Advanced roles include clinical supervisor, rehabilitation director, or opening a private clinic. Some physiotherapists pursue further education in physiotherapy or related fields, such as sports science or occupational therapy. International certifications (e.g., from the US or Canada) can enhance mobility. In Mexico, formal recognition of specialties is limited, so experience and reputation drive advancement. Private practice offers higher income potential but requires business skills.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapist in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience. Entry-level in public hospitals may earn around 180,000 MXN annually, while experienced private practitioners can exceed 400,000 MXN. Specialization and location significantly affect income.

**Q: Can a foreign physiotherapist get a work visa in Mexico?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from a Mexican employer who sponsors a Temporary Resident Visa with a work permit. The employer must prove no suitable Mexican candidate is available, and the role must meet salary thresholds. Registration with the SEP (cédula profesional) is mandatory, which may involve additional validation of foreign degrees.
