# Midwifery Professional — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2222 (ISCO08)**

Midwifery professionals provide comprehensive care to women during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period, as well as neonatal care. In Mexico, they work in hospitals, clinics, and rural health centers, often serving underserved communities. The role requires clinical skills, empathy, and cultural sensitivity. Midwives may also offer family planning and gynecological services. The profession is regulated by the Secretaría de Salud and requires a professional license.

Government initiatives aim to expand community-based midwifery and integrate traditional midwives into the formal healthcare system. Employment is expected to grow with increased focus on reducing maternal mortality and improving access to reproductive health services. Urban hospital demand remains steady, but rural and public-sector roles offer the most opportunity.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a midwifery professional in Mexico?**
A: Entry-level midwives in public hospitals earn around 180,000-250,000 MXN annually. Experienced private-sector midwives can earn 300,000-500,000 MXN. Rural community health workers may receive lower base pay but often get housing and transportation allowances.

**Q: Can a foreign midwife get a work visa for Mexico?**
A: Yes, through an employer-sponsored Temporary Resident Visa with a work permit. The employer must prove no suitable Mexican candidate is available. The process involves submitting a job offer to the INM, followed by visa application at a Mexican consulate. It is restricted but possible for qualified midwives.

**Q: Is a Mexican midwifery degree recognized abroad?**
A: Recognition varies by country. Mexican public university degrees (e.g., UNAM) are generally well-regarded. However, practice in other countries usually requires additional exams or bridging courses. For migration to Mexico, foreign degrees must be revalidated by the SEP.
