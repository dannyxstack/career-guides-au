# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Workers catch fish, crustaceans, and mollusks in rivers, lakes, and coastal areas using nets, traps, lines, and small vessels. They operate and maintain fishing gear, sort and store the catch, and perform basic boat maintenance. In Mexico, this occupation is predominantly informal and artisanal, concentrated in coastal states like Veracruz, Tabasco, Campeche, and along the Pacific and Gulf coasts. Work is physically demanding, seasonal, and dependent on resource availability, with limited formal employment structures.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 92,638. Employment prospects for inland and coastal fishery workers in Mexico are weak due to overfishing, climate change, pollution, and stricter environmental regulations. Many traditional fishing grounds are depleted, leading to reduced catches and income. The sector is expected to shrink further, with some workers transitioning to aquaculture or tourism. Government sustainability programs may create limited demand for certified workers, but overall job growth is flat or negative.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is minimal. Workers typically start as crew members and may advance to boat captain or establish their own small fishing operation. Diversification into aquaculture, fish processing, or eco-tourism offers better opportunities. Formal career paths are rare due to high informality. Some join cooperatives that provide access to markets and credit, but upward mobility remains constrained by limited resources and declining fish stocks.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainability
- aquaculture
- eco-tourism
- fish processing

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for inland and coastal fishery workers in Mexico?**
A: Income is highly variable and often based on catch share. Most workers earn below the minimum wage, averaging around MXN 120,000 annually in good seasons, but many earn less. Formal salaried positions are rare.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a fishery worker in Mexico?**
A: It is extremely difficult. The occupation is not considered skilled, and employers rarely sponsor visas. The only possible route is a temporary resident visa based on a job offer, but it requires proving the position cannot be filled locally, which is unlikely.

**Q: How can a fishery worker improve their career prospects?**
A: Diversifying into aquaculture, obtaining a boat captain license, joining a cooperative, or moving into fish processing or eco-tourism can improve income and stability.
