# Information and Communications Technology Services Manager — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 1330 (ISCO08)**

Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Services Managers plan, direct, and coordinate the provision of ICT services within an organization or to external clients. They oversee teams that design, implement, and maintain IT systems, ensuring alignment with business goals and compliance with security standards. This role requires strong technical expertise, strategic thinking, and leadership skills to manage budgets, vendors, and service-level agreements (SLAs). In Mexico, ICT managers are critical in driving digital transformation across industries such as finance, manufacturing, and telecommunications.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Progression typically starts from roles like IT project manager or systems architect to ICT manager, then to director of IT or CIO. Many professionals complement experience with certifications (e.g., PMP, ITIL, CISSP). Lateral moves into consulting or entrepreneurship are common. The career demands adaptability due to rapid technological change. Networking within Mexico's IT community and bilingual Spanish-English skills significantly enhance prospects.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an ICT Services Manager in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Entry-level (5-7 years) earn about MXN 800,000–1,200,000 annually, while senior managers can earn over MXN 2,000,000. Bonuses and benefits are common.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as an ICT Services Manager in Mexico?**
A: Yes, via an employer-sponsored Temporary Resident Visa. The company must prove the role cannot be filled locally. A job offer and meeting salary thresholds (currently around MXN 500,000/year) are required. The process can take 2-4 months.

**Q: What skills are most in demand for this role?**
A: Cloud platforms (AWS, Azure), cybersecurity, agile/DevOps practices, and vendor management. Spanish proficiency is essential; English is mandatory for multinational firms.
