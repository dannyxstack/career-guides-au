# Door-to-door Salesperson — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 5243 (ISCO08)**

Door-to-door Salespersons travel to residential or commercial locations to sell products or services directly to customers, often working on commission. They demonstrate items, explain features, and close sales, relying on interpersonal skills and persistence. This role is common in industries like telecommunications, utilities, and consumer goods. It requires minimal formal education but strong communication and negotiation abilities.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 694,257. In Mexico, door-to-door sales remain a significant but informal segment of the retail sector. Employment outlook is stable, with demand fluctuating based on economic cycles and consumer spending. Growth is tied to expanding telecom and home services sectors. However, formal employment opportunities are limited, and most positions are commission-based with high turnover. Automation and online sales pose risks, but personal interaction remains valued in certain niches.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression is typically flat; however, top performers may advance to sales supervisor, trainer, or branch manager roles. Some transition into independent representation or start their own small businesses. The role often serves as an entry point into the sales profession, with skills transferable to retail or telesales. Formal career paths are rare without additional education or certifications.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a door-to-door salesperson in Mexico?**
A: Income is largely commission-based and highly variable. Experienced salespersons in high-demand sectors (e.g., telecom) may earn MXN 150,000–250,000 annually, but many earn below minimum wage due to low conversion rates.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa for door-to-door sales in Mexico?**
A: It is very difficult. The occupation is not considered skilled, and employer-sponsored work permits are rarely approved for such roles. Foreigners typically need to qualify under high-salary thresholds or have unique skills to obtain a temporary resident work permit.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist?**
A: Top performers may become sales team leaders, area managers, or move into independent sales representation. Some transition to related roles like retail store management or telesales. However, formal advancement is limited without further education.
