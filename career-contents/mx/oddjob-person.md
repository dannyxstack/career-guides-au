# Odd-Job Persons — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons perform a variety of simple, manual tasks on a temporary or casual basis, such as cleaning, moving furniture, painting, gardening, or minor repairs. They typically work for households, small businesses, or as self-employed individuals, often in the informal economy. This occupation requires basic physical fitness and a willingness to perform diverse duties, with no formal education or certification needed.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 457. The demand for odd-job persons in Mexico is stable, driven by the gig economy and the need for flexible, short-term labor in domestic and maintenance services. Growth is moderate as more households and small businesses outsource tasks. However, competition is high due to low barriers to entry, and wages remain near minimum levels. Informal employment dominates, limiting benefits and job security.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; odd-job persons may specialize in a trade (e.g., plumbing, painting) or start their own handyman business. Some move into formal employment in cleaning companies or maintenance departments. With additional skills (e.g., electrical, carpentry), they can transition to skilled trades. Most remain in casual work due to lack of formal qualifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- gig economy
- handyman services
- maintenance
- domestic help

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for an odd-job person in Mexico?**
A: Income varies widely but averages around MXN 3,000–6,000 per month for part-time work. Full-time, formal positions might reach MXN 8,000, but most earn less than minimum wage (approx. MXN 207 per day in 2024) due to informal employment.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as an odd-job person in Mexico?**
A: It is very difficult. Work visas require a formal job offer from an employer registered with the INM, and odd-job positions are rarely formalized. Most foreign workers in this field come on tourist visas and work illegally, which carries risks. Permanent residence is not viable through this occupation.

**Q: What are the long-term prospects for an odd-job person?**
A: Prospects are limited unless the person acquires skills in a trade (e.g., plumbing, electrician). With additional training, they can earn more and access formal employment. Otherwise, income and job security remain low.
