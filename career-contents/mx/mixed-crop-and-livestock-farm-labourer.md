# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

Mixed crop and livestock farm labourers perform a variety of manual tasks to assist in the production of crops and the raising of animals. Duties include preparing soil, planting, irrigating, weeding, harvesting crops, feeding and watering livestock, cleaning animal enclosures, and maintaining farm equipment and facilities. These roles are physically demanding and typically conducted outdoors in rural areas of Mexico.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited for farm labourers, though experienced workers may become supervisors or independent smallholders. Many transition to other agricultural roles or migrate to urban areas for better opportunities. Formal training is rare, and advancement often relies on on-the-job experience and networking within agricultural communities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using automated tractors for plowing, sowing, and harvesting
- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots
- Use drones to inspect farms and produce yield maps
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans based on historical data and weather forecasts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop comprehensive agricultural strategies to balance ecology and economy
- Collaborate with AI for precision agriculture management
- Train new employees in using digital tools

**Human moat:**

- Animal emergency medical care and behavioural intervention
- Compliance with organic certification and animal welfare standards.

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mixed crop and livestock farm labourer in Mexico?**
A: Salaries are low, typically around 120,000 to 150,000 MXN per year (pre-tax), varying by region and season. Many workers are paid daily or by task, and benefits are uncommon in informal settings.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a farm labourer in Mexico?**
A: It is very difficult. The employer-sponsored Temporary Resident Visa is intended for skilled roles; farm labour is considered low-skilled and rarely qualifies. Most foreign workers in this sector are either undocumented or participate in specific bilateral seasonal programs, which are limited.

**Q: What is the career progression for a farm labourer?**
A: Progression is limited. Some labourers become supervisors, tractor operators, or start their own small farms. Formal training in agricultural techniques can help, but many leave the sector for better-paying urban jobs.
