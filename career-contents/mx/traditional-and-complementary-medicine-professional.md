# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 2230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Mexico practice non-conventional health therapies such as acupuncture, naturopathy, and herbal medicine. They are regulated by the General Health Law and must hold a professional license (cédula profesional) issued by the Ministry of Public Education. The field is growing due to increasing interest in holistic health and wellness tourism, but remains niche compared to conventional medicine.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a university degree in Naturopathy or Traditional Medicine, followed by a professional license. Many practitioners specialize further in acupuncture or homeopathy. Experienced professionals may open their own clinics or work as consultants. Academic roles and research positions are limited.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely; employed practitioners in clinics earn between 180,000 and 300,000 MXN annually, while self-employed practitioners may earn less or more depending on client base.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a Traditional Medicine Professional in Mexico?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You need a job offer from a licensed clinic to apply for a Temporary Resident Visa with work permit. The employer must demonstrate that no Mexican candidate is available. Independent practice is not allowed without residency.

**Q: Is traditional medicine regulated in Mexico?**
A: Yes, it is regulated by the General Health Law. Practitioners must have a university degree in a recognized field and a cédula profesional from the SEP. Unlicensed practice can lead to fines or deportation.
