# Ship's Deck Crew — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ship's deck crew members perform critical duties on vessels such as cargo ships, fishing boats, and passenger ferries. Responsibilities include maintaining deck equipment, handling mooring lines, standing watch, and assisting with navigation. In Mexico, this occupation is concentrated in major ports like Veracruz, Manzanillo, and Altamira, as well as in the Gulf and Pacific fishing fleets. The work is physically demanding and often requires long periods at sea, but it offers opportunities for travel and career advancement through experience and certifications.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a deckhand or ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman (AB) after accumulating sea time and obtaining certifications. Further progress leads to roles like bosun (deck supervisor), then third officer, second officer, and ultimately captain. In Mexico, many deck crew transition to fishing or ferry operations due to easier entry. Some pursue additional qualifications to work on larger commercial vessels. The career is physically demanding but offers clear seniority-based advancement, with captaincies requiring a professional license (Patrón de Cabotaje).

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a ship's deck crew member in Mexico?**
A: A deckhand (marinero) in Mexico earns an average annual salary of MXN 120,000–180,000 gross. With experience and advancement to able seaman, income can rise to MXN 200,000–300,000 per year. Additional compensation may include bonuses for voyages, overtime, and meal allowances. Salaries vary by vessel type (cargo, fishing, passenger) and region.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as deck crew in Mexico without a visa?**
A: No, all foreign crew must have a valid work permit or visa. The standard route is a Temporary Resident Visa with Work Permit (employer-sponsored). This requires a job offer from a Mexican shipping company or port operator, and the employer must demonstrate that no Mexican worker is available. For short stays on foreign vessels, a Visitor Visa with Work Permission (FW-2) may be used, but it does not allow long-term employment in Mexico.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities for deck crew in Mexico?**
A: Deck crew can advance to bosun, third officer, and eventually captain (Patrón de Cabotaje) through accumulated sea time and certification exams. The Mexican Navy (Secretaría de Marina) and SCT regulate licensing. Obtaining higher certifications (e.g., STCW II/1) opens doors to larger vessels and better pay. Many experienced deckhands also move into port operations or maritime logistics.
