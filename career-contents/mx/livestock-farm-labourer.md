# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers assist with the daily care and management of farm animals such as cattle, pigs, sheep, and poultry. Their duties include feeding, watering, cleaning animal housing, monitoring health, and assisting with breeding or birthing. They often work outdoors in various weather conditions and perform physically demanding tasks like lifting feed sacks, mucking out stalls, and fencing. Entry-level positions typically require no formal education, with on-the-job training provided. The role is essential to Mexico's agricultural sector, particularly in states like Jalisco, Veracruz, and Chihuahua.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for livestock farm labourers is limited. With experience, workers may become senior labourers or supervisors overseeing small teams. Some transition to specialised roles like artificial insemination technicians or farm equipment operators. Advancement often requires additional training or certification. Many labourers remain in the same role long-term, as upward mobility is constrained by low formal education requirements and the informal nature of much agricultural employment.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Mexico?**
A: Monthly salaries range from MXN 4,500 to MXN 7,000 depending on region and hours. Annual earnings are typically between MXN 54,000 and MXN 84,000. Many workers are paid daily or weekly.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a livestock farm labourer in Mexico?**
A: Yes, but it requires a valid job offer from a Mexican employer who must obtain a work permit from the INM. The position is not considered skilled, so visa approval may be more difficult compared to professional roles. Seasonal programs via bilateral agreements are more common.

**Q: What are the opportunities for advancement in this role?**
A: Career progression is limited. With experience, labourers may become supervisors or move into specialized animal care. However, formal education or training is usually needed to advance beyond basic labouring roles.
