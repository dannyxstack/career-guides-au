# Carpenter and Joiner — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 7115 (ISCO08)**

Carpenters and joiners shape, cut, install, and repair wooden structures and fixtures in construction, furniture making, and renovation projects. In Mexico, they work on residential and commercial buildings, producing doors, windows, cabinets, and frameworks. The trade requires manual dexterity, knowledge of wood properties, and proficiency with hand and power tools. Many enter through apprenticeships or vocational training, with formal certification not always mandatory but advantageous for higher-paying roles.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 12,727. The outlook for carpenters in Mexico remains stable, driven by residential construction demand, especially in growing urban areas and tourism-led regions like Quintana Roo and Jalisco. However, the sector faces challenges from informality and cyclical economic fluctuations. Government infrastructure projects, such as the Tren Maya, create periodic demand. Skilled carpenters proficient in modern techniques and safety standards are expected to find consistent work, but overall growth is moderate.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts from apprentice or assistant, moving to journeyman after a few years. Experienced carpenters can become foremen, site supervisors, or start their own small businesses. Specialization in finish carpentry, cabinetry, or restoration work can lead to higher earnings and more stable employment. Some pursue advanced certifications in green building or project management to differentiate themselves in the market.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a carpenter in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, location, and formality. In formal employment, carpenters earn between the minimum wage (around 8,000–10,000 MXN monthly) and up to 15,000 MXN for skilled joiners in specialized workshops. Self-employed carpenters can earn more but face income irregularity.

**Q: What visa options exist for foreign carpenters to work in Mexico?**
A: The main route is the employer-sponsored Temporary Resident Visa with work permit. The employer must prove local labor shortage and cover immigration fees. Family-based permanent residency is another option if you have a qualifying relative. There is no explicit points system for trades.

**Q: Is formal certification required to work as a carpenter in Mexico?**
A: No, but many construction sites require safety training (STPS). Voluntary certifications from CONOCER can improve job prospects and wages, especially in formal companies or for specialized work like furniture making.
