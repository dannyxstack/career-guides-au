# Companion and Valet — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 5162 (ISCO08)**

Companions and valets provide personal assistance, companionship, and household support to individuals, often in private homes or luxury settings. In Mexico, this occupation is niche, serving affluent families, elderly persons, or high-net-worth individuals requiring discreet, personalized care. Work may include errands, scheduling, light housekeeping, and travel accompaniment. The role demands excellent interpersonal skills, trustworthiness, and adaptability. Employment is typically informal, with few formal contracts, and is concentrated in Mexico City, Guadalajara, and tourist destinations like Cancún. Opportunities arise through personal referrals or agencies specializing in domestic and caregiving staff.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression from companion/valet into higher-level household management, senior care coordination, or private concierge services is possible with experience and client references. ## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a companion or valet in Mexico?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region and employer. In formal arrangements, monthly pay ranges from 6,000 to 12,000 MXN (pre-tax), often plus room and board. Live-in positions may include meals and accommodation. The informal sector dominates, with cash payments common.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a companion or valet in Mexico?**
A: It is very difficult. The employer must sponsor a Temporary Resident Visa with a work permit, proving the role cannot be filled by a Mexican. The salary threshold (approx. 42,000 MXN/month) is far above typical pay, so almost no companion positions qualify. More realistic: obtain permanent residency via family or other means, then work informally.

**Q: What career growth opportunities exist for companions in Mexico?**
A: Experienced companions may move into household management, senior care coordination, or luxury concierge services. Formal training in gerontology or hospitality can help. Most progress through referrals and building trust with high-net-worth clients.
