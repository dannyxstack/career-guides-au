# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Mexico

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers not elsewhere classified (ISCO-08 7119) include carpenters, bricklayers, and other skilled construction workers who install, maintain, and repair building frames, structures, and related components. In Mexico, these trades are essential for residential, commercial, and industrial construction projects. Workers typically perform tasks such as measuring, cutting, assembling, and finishing wood, metal, or concrete materials. They often work on-site under varying conditions and collaborate with other construction professionals. The role requires physical stamina, precision, and knowledge of safety standards. Many workers gain skills through vocational training or on-the-job experience.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for building frame trades workers often starts as an apprentice or assistant, advancing to journeyman or lead tradesperson. Experienced workers may become supervisors, foremen, or independent contractors. Some specialize in areas like formwork, steel fixing, or restoration. In Mexico, formal certification through CONOCER or vocational schools enhances mobility. However, advancement can be limited without additional training in project management or technical skills. Union membership may provide better wages and job security.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- Residential Construction
- Commercial Development
- Infrastructure Projects
- Restoration Work

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a building frame trades worker in Mexico?**
A: Salaries typically range from 120,000 to 250,000 MXN annually, depending on experience, location, and specialization. Entry-level workers may earn less, while experienced supervisors can earn more.

**Q: Can a foreign worker get a Mexican work visa for this job?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from a Mexican employer who obtains a work permit from INM. The Temporary Resident Visa is available for skilled trades, though the process can be restrictive and employer-dependent.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: Advancement includes moving to foreman, supervisor, or independent contractor roles. Specializing in green building or restoration can open niche opportunities.
