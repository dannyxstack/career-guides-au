- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance

- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for building frame and related trades workers in Latvia?**
A: In Latvia, the gross monthly salary for building frame trades workers typically ranges from €900 to €1,400, depending on experience and employer. Entry-level positions may start at the national minimum wage (€700 per month in 2025), while skilled workers can earn higher. Actual pay varies by region and project.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU building trades workers to work in Latvia?**
A: Non-EU workers can apply for a Latvian work visa or EU Blue Card if they have a valid employment contract and meet salary thresholds (for Blue Card, at least 1.5x average gross salary). The employer typically sponsors the application. The work visa is issued for up to two years, renewable, and allows family reunification.

**Q: What are the main responsibilities of building frame trades workers in Latvia?**
A: These workers construct and repair building frames, including erecting steel or timber structures, installing roof trusses, and pouring concrete foundations. They read blueprints, operate tools like saws and drills, and ensure structures meet safety standards. Work often involves collaboration with other construction trades on residential, commercial, or industrial projects.
