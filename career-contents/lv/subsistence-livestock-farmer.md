## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Latvia?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Latvia generally earn low and irregular income, as production is primarily for personal consumption. Official statistics show average farm income around €10,000 per year, but subsistence farmers often earn less, sometimes below the minimum wage. Income varies significantly based on herd size and market conditions.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as subsistence livestock farmers in Latvia via an EU Blue Card or work visa?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers and does not apply to subsistence farming. Non-EU citizens may apply for a temporary residence permit for self-employment if they present a viable business plan and sufficient funds. Alternatively, they could seek employment with a registered farm, requiring a standard work visa and employer sponsorship.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a subsistence livestock farmer in Latvia?**
A: No formal qualifications are legally required, but practical experience in animal husbandry and knowledge of veterinary care are essential. Farmers must register with the Rural Support Service and comply with EU animal welfare and biosecurity standards. Training courses in organic farming and livestock management are available through agricultural advisory centers.
