**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using smartphones, GPS, simple apps)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Basic data analysis (capture records, weather patterns)
- Knowledge of sustainable resource management
- Wilderness first aid and survival skills
- Small solar/portable power maintenance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers and gatherers in Latvia?**
A: Income varies greatly and is often irregular, as this work is typically part of the informal economy or small-scale self-employment. There is no standard salary; earnings depend on catch or harvest, local demand, and season. Most practitioners supplement their income with other jobs or social benefits. Official statistics do not provide specific figures for this occupation.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for subsistence fishing or hunting in Latvia?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified workers and does not apply to this low-skilled occupation. Third-country nationals may theoretically obtain a work permit if an employer sponsors them, but such opportunities are extremely rare and typically limited to EU/EEA citizens. Self-employment as a subsistence fisher or hunter is possible but requires a residence permit for other purposes, not specifically for this work.

**Q: What are the career prospects for subsistence fishers and hunters in Latvia?**
A: This is a traditional livelihood, often passed down through families or practiced in rural areas near lakes, forests, or the Baltic coast. Career advancement is limited as it is not a structured profession. Many engage seasonally, combining it with farming or forestry. The work is physically demanding and depends on natural resources, which are regulated by state quotas and licenses.
