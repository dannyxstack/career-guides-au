## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for dancers and choreographers in Slovakia?**
A: Salaries vary widely. Entry-level dancers may earn around €800–€1,200 per month, while experienced choreographers in established institutions can earn €1,500–€2,500. Many work freelance or on project contracts. The minimum wage in Slovakia is €750 (2025), so income can be modest. State theatres and dance companies often offer fixed contracts with additional benefits.

**Q: What visa or EU Blue Card routes exist for dancers and choreographers to work in Slovakia?**
A: Non-EU dancers need an employer to obtain a work permit and temporary residence. The EU Blue Card is generally for highly skilled workers with a university degree and a salary 1.5 times the average wage (approx. €2,000+ per month in 2025), which most dance roles do not meet. Alternatively, a national employment visa may be granted for specific cultural projects. Official routes are available via Slovak embassies.

**Q: What qualifications or training are required to work as a dancer or choreographer in Slovakia?**
A: Most professional dancers and choreographers in Slovakia have completed formal training at a conservatory or university dance program, such as the Academy of Performing Arts in Bratislava. Many also gain experience through apprenticeships with state or independent dance companies. Continuous education in contemporary techniques is valued. Registration with the Slovak trade union for artists may be beneficial but is not mandatory.
