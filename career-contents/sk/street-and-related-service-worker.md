## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Slovakia?**
A: Pay for street vendors, door-to-door sellers, and similar roles is often based on daily or hourly rates, typically at or slightly above the national minimum wage. Earnings can vary significantly depending on location, volume of sales, and whether the worker is self-employed. Many workers are paid in cash, and income may be irregular.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as street and related services workers in Slovakia through a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: This occupation generally does not qualify for the EU Blue Card, which targets highly skilled workers. Non-EU nationals typically need a work permit and temporary residence for employment. The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled by a Slovak or EU worker. Self-employment is also possible but requires a trade license.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for street and related services workers in Slovakia?**
A: Career progression is limited, but workers may advance by starting their own small business or moving into wholesale or retail management. Some gain experience to become supervisors of street vendor teams or transition into formal sales roles. Language skills and customer service experience can open doors to other service sector jobs.
