**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Slovakia?**
A: Subsistence activities are primarily for personal consumption and not formally remunerated, so no official salary statistics exist. Those employed in related sectors like forestry or fishing earn an average gross monthly salary of €1,000–€1,200, but most subsistence workers operate outside formal employment.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain an EU Blue Card or work visa to work as a subsistence fisher, hunter, trapper, or gatherer in Slovakia?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified professionals and does not apply to subsistence occupations. Third-country nationals may obtain a Temporary Residence for Employment if offered a job in related fields like forestry or agriculture, requiring a work permit and proof that the position cannot be filled locally. Subsistence activity alone is not a valid basis for residence.

**Q: What career prospects exist for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Slovakia?**
A: This occupation is typically a traditional or lifestyle activity rather than a career path. In Slovakia, it is often practiced in rural areas, especially in regions with forests and rivers. Limited formal employment exists; some may transition to roles in conservation, wildlife management, or eco-tourism. Certification in hunting can lead to work with hunting associations or forest enterprises.
