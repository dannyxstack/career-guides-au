**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Real-time monitoring of power generation parameters and alarm response
- Perform standardized equipment start-stop operations
- Record and report operational data.
- Adjust routine parameters such as boilers and turbines
- Pattern recognition steps in fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI predictive maintenance to optimize equipment repair schedules
- Conduct accident simulations and training using digital twin simulations
- Using automated systems to analyze large volumes of operational data to assist decision-making
- Remote operation and cross-station coordinated scheduling

**Human moat:**

- quick judgment and handling in emergency situations
- Cross-disciplinary comprehensive analysis of complex system failures
- Effective communication with maintenance teams and dispatch centers
- Social responsibility and safety compliance awareness
- Intuition and feel from on-site experience

**Skills to build:**

- Obtain a higher-level professional qualification
- Learn new energy generation technologies (e.g., wind power, solar PV).

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Power Production Plant Operator in Slovakia?**
A: Salaries for power plant operators in Slovakia vary by experience and region. Entry-level positions start around €1,200–€1,500 gross per month, while experienced operators can earn €1,800–€2,500. Additional bonuses and shift allowances are common. The average gross salary is approximately €1,600 per month.

**Q: What work visa options exist for non-EU nationals to work as a Power Production Plant Operator in Slovakia?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a relevant university degree or 5 years of professional experience. Alternatively, a national work permit (temporary residence for employment) is available, requiring a job offer and employer sponsorship. Both routes need approval from the Slovak Ministry of Labour and Foreign Police.

**Q: What qualifications are required to become a Power Production Plant Operator in Slovakia?**
A: Typically, a secondary technical education in electrical engineering, energy, or a related field is required. Professional certification (e.g., from the Slovak Energy Board) is mandatory for operating high-voltage equipment. Many employers also provide on-the-job training and require knowledge of safety regulations and control systems.
