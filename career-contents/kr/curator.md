# Curator — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41311 (KECO)**

A professional who collects, preserves, researches, and exhibits artifacts and artworks in exhibition halls, museums, and art galleries, also referred to as a curator.


---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curator Certificate (Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism) | Korean Museums Association | Required | Levels 1, 2, 3 required |
| Proof of career for conversion to regular position | Employer | Optional | Preference for 2-3+ years of contract experience |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 12,000. Although the number of museums and galleries is increasing, public sector jobs remain stagnant, and demand is expected to arise mainly in private and small-scale facilities. Doctoral-level employment is very limited.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Promotion to a regular curator usually occurs after graduate school followed by internships and contract positions; advancement to senior curator or director is possible with experience, but competition is fierce.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Writing Docent and Exhibition Guide Scripts
- Exhibit Labeling and Basic Commentary
- Organizing and Inputting Collection Data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Personalized Exhibition Recommendations for Visitors
- Collection Preservation Condition Analysis and Prediction
- Creating Exhibition-Related Educational Content

**Human moat:**

- Exhibition Storytelling and Planning
- Cultural Heritage Preservation and Restoration Techniques
- Public and Private Networking and Negotiation Skills
- Deep Humanistic Insight

**Skills to build:**

- Digital Curation
- Data Analysis
- English and Foreign Language Proficiency
- Project Management

**Key growth areas:**

- Exhibition Planning
- Museum·Gallery Expansion
- Cultural Heritage Digitization
- Local Culture Specialization

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a curator?**
A: Contract-based: 24–30 million KRW annually (pre-tax); regular positions are higher but competitive.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a curator?**
A: E-7 (Specific Activities) visa is common; a curator certificate and experience in culture/arts are required; Korean language proficiency is also important.

**Q: Is a curator certificate mandatory?**
A: Yes, a curator certificate (Level 1/2/3) issued by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism is mandatory; it is usually obtained during a master's program.
