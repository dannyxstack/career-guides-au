# Sound and Recording Technician — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41651 (KECO)**

A professional who operates sound equipment and records, edits, and mixes audio in broadcasting, film, performances, and music production.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Typically starts at broadcasting stations, recording studios, or as a freelancer, with potential to advance to chief engineer or sound director based on experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic noise removal
- Automatic level adjustment
- Simple editing tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Auto-mixing assistance
- AI-based audio analysis
- Real-time feedback systems

**Human moat:**

- Artistic sensibility and auditory judgment
- On-site situation handling skills
- Collaboration with artists and directors

**Skills to build:**

- AI audio tool utilization
- Programming-based audio processing
- Spatial audio design

**Key growth areas:**

- Content industry
- OTT
- Live performances
- VR/AR audio

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Person with keen hearing and excellent musical sense
- Person who is meticulous, patient, and good at collaboration

**Who is not suited**
- Person who prefers regular working hours
- Person sensitive to noise or with weak hearing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a sound/recording engineer?**
A: Depending on experience, entry-level: 24-32 million KRW, mid-level: 35-50 million KRW, senior: 50-80 million KRW. Large difference between freelancers and permanent employees.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a sound/recording engineer in Korea?**
A: E-7 visa required for specialized fields, with important experience and degree proof. D-10 visa can be converted for job seeking after study.

**Q: What is the long-term outlook for this occupation?**
A: Demand is expected to persist due to growth in OTT and the live performance market, but AI automation and advancements in recording technology may replace some tasks. Creative mixing and on-site experience are crucial.
