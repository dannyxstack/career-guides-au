# Barber — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 51111 (KECO)**

A professional who trims customers' hair and provides beauty services such as shaving, dyeing, and perming, working at barbershops or hair salons.


---

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Common path: complete a beauty academy, obtain a barber license, get a job at a barbershop, gain experience, then open a shop or operate a franchise.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple cutting guide
- Reservation management
- Dye recipe calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Scalp diagnosis
- Style recommendation
- Customer management CRM

**Human moat:**

- Hand dexterity and aesthetic sense
- Trust-based relationships with customers
- Customized design ability

**Skills to build:**

- Hair design
- Scalp and hair care
- Customer consultation and marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging
- Single-person households
- Premium services
- K-beauty

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with good manual dexterity and attention to detail
- People who enjoy customer service and are interested in creative styling

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike conversation and find repetitive work difficult
- People who cannot adapt to irregular working hours

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a barber?**
A: Beginner level: 20–30 million KRW; with experience: 30–40 million KRW; self-employed: 40 million KRW or more.

**Q: Can foreigners work as barbers in Korea?**
A: Possible, but an E-7 visa is required, and Korean language skills and license acquisition are mandatory. Conditions are strict, so it is limited as an immigration route.

**Q: Is there a risk of barbers being replaced by AI?**
A: AI is unlikely to fully replace shaving or cutting, so the risk is low. Rather, automated devices can improve work efficiency.
