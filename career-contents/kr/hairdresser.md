# Hairdresser — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 51121 (KECO)**

A professional who manages customers' hair and skin, providing beauty services such as cutting, dyeing, perming, and styling.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

This is an occupation with limited impact from AI and automation. Tasks requiring creativity and hand skills, such as hair design, consultation, and cutting, are difficult to replace. However, AI can assist with some tasks like appointment management and product recommendations.

Junior hairdresser → Mid-level hairdresser → Salon manager → Franchise owner or independent business. Income varies significantly based on experience and skills, and entry barriers for starting a business are relatively low.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Appointment management
- Simple consultation chatbots
- Product inventory management

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Scalp diagnosis

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a beautician?**
A: Entry-level is about 22-30 million won, while experienced can reach 45-70 million won, but it varies greatly depending on individual skills and clientele.

**Q: Can foreigners work as beauticians in Korea?**
A: General employment visa (E-7) issuance is difficult, making it a limited immigration route. However, holders of F-2 and F-4 visas are eligible.

**Q: How long does it take to get a beautician license?**
A: National certification can be obtained through a 2-year college program or a 6-month academy course, with passing the practical exam being crucial.
