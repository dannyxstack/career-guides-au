# Bartender — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 53161 (KECO)**

A bartender is a food service professional who mixes alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks for customers and manages bar operations and atmosphere.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 45,000. Demand contracted after COVID-19, but with the recovery of the food service and bar industry and an increase in tourists, moderate recovery in manpower demand is expected through 2025. In a market dominated by self-employed businesses, frequent movement of experienced workers leads to steady entry of newcomers.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those interested in creative beverage development
- Those who enjoy communicating with and serving customers
- Those who can handle night shifts and flexible schedules

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer regular daytime work
- Those who are weak to physical or mental stress

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for bartenders?**
A: Entry-level salary is around 24–30 million KRW per year, and depending on experience and establishment, it can reach 40–60 million KRW. Tips and bonuses are separate.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work as a bartender in Korea?**
A: If professional skills are recognized, an E-7 visa is possible but very limited. Generally, an F-2 (Residence) visa or F-4 (Overseas Korean) visa is required.

**Q: What is the career progression for bartenders?**
A: Common paths are promotion to head bartender, bar manager, F&B director, or starting one's own business. Winning cocktail competitions or having a sommelier certificate helps.
