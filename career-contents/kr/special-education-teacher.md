# Special Education Teacher — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 21231 (KECO)**

A professional educator who designs and operates individualized curricula for students with disabilities, providing guidance in learning, daily living, and vocational skills.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| Bachelor’s degree in Special Education (teacher training program) | 4 years | $20,000,000~$50,000,000 (Varies significantly between national and private universities; scholarships possible.) |
| Completion of special education teaching certification (non-teacher training program) | 2 years | $10,000,000~$30,000,000 (Graduate School of Education (special education major) or double major) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level 2 Special School (Kindergarten, Elementary, Secondary) Teacher Certificate | Ministry of Education/Provincial Office of Education | Required | Pass the certification exam after completing teacher training program |
| Level 1 Special School Teacher Certificate | Ministry of Education/Provincial Office of Education | Optional | 3+ years of experience + completion of training |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career paths include private/national/public special schools, special classes in general schools, and special education support centers under district offices of education. Promotion follows: Teacher → Master Teacher → Vice Principal/Principal.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Grading simple repetitive worksheets
- Basic data recording (attendance, performance assessment)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Supporting IEP development
- Diagnosis and assessment through learning analytics
- Use of augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) tools

**Human moat:**

- Emotional bonding with students with disabilities
- Behavior intervention and crisis management
- Family counseling and collaboration

**Skills to build:**

- Use of special education technology
- Behavior analysis
- Interpretation of learning data
- Augmentative and alternative communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Integrated Education for Students with Disabilities
- Individualized Education Plan (IEP)
- Transition Education (Career/Vocational)
- Expansion of Special Education Support Personnel

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Someone with understanding and patience for people with disabilities
- Someone who wants to design personalized education
- Someone interested in treatment-education collaboration (e.g., speech therapy)

**Who is not suited**
- Someone who wants immediate academic achievement
- Someone who dislikes discrimination against existing teachers

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting annual salary for a special education teacher?**
A: For public schools, starting salary (level 1) is about 35-40 million KRW; private schools vary.

**Q: Can foreigners work as special education teachers in Korea?**
A: Very difficult. Korean language skills and a special education teaching license are required, and E-7 visas are rarely approved.

**Q: Do I have to attend a college of education to become a special education teacher?**
A: College of Education's special education department is most common, but you can also double major in teaching (special education) at a general university or obtain certification through a graduate school of education.
