# Korean Cuisine Chef — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 53121 (KECO)**

A professional who cooks traditional Korean food, developing and preparing Korean menu items at restaurants or hotels.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school graduation | 3 years | $0~$0 (Not required, but costs incurred if attending a culinary high school) |
| Culinary arts department at a junior college | 2 years | $4,000,000~$8,000,000 (Annual tuition standard) |

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Korean Cuisine Cook (National Technical Qualification) | Human Resources Development Service of Korea | Optional | National technical qualification; not required but advantageous for employment |
| Culinary Engineer (National Technical Qualification) | Human Resources Development Service of Korea | Optional | Advanced certification; requires degree |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 250,000. Employment is stable due to the globalization of Korean cuisine and growth of the food service industry, but there is a persistent labor shortage.

**Competition: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

After obtaining a cook certification, one can work in general Korean restaurants, hotels, or corporate cafeterias; with experience, can become a head chef or restaurant owner.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Order taking
- Inventory management
- Simple repetitive cooking

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Recipe development
- Flavor analysis
- Ingredient optimization

**Human moat:**

- Taste and creativity
- Dexterity and experience
- Customer communication skills

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis
- Food tech utilization
- Understanding of multicultural cuisines

**Key growth areas:**

- Globalization of Korean cuisine
- Dining trends
- Healthy food demand
- K-food craze

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those passionate about cooking and enjoy creating new flavors
- Those with good physical strength and can manage kitchen stress
- Those with deep interest in Korean food culture

**Who is not suited**
- Those who dislike repetitive tasks and struggle with fast pace
- Those who find it difficult to strictly follow hygiene and safety rules

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a Korean cuisine cook?**
A: 0-3 years: 26-32 million KRW, 4-7 years: 33-42 million KRW, 8+ years: 43-55 million KRW; head chefs may earn more.

**Q: Can foreigners work as Korean cuisine cooks?**
A: Yes, skilled cooks can work in Korean restaurants with an E-7 visa. A Korean cuisine cooking certificate and experience are required.
