# Childcare helper — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 55021 (KECO)**

A care service job responsible for childcare, meals, hygiene management of infants and toddlers at home or in facilities.

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| High school graduate | 0 years | $0~$0 (No specific educational requirements) |

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 180,000. Although the infant population is declining due to low birth rates, demand for workers is expected to continue due to the increase in dual-income households and expanded government childcare support (childcare service).

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Loves children and is patient
- Able to work flexibly, including home visits
- Meticulous about hygiene and safety

**Who is not suited**
- Weak to physical/mental stress
- Difficulty coping with irregular hours

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a child caregiver?**
A: Before tax, about 24-30 million KRW annually for entry level; income varies greatly due to part-time work.

**Q: Can foreigners work as child caregivers?**
A: Yes, but limited. E-9 visas allow some overseas Koreans; F-2 and F-4 holders have more freedom.

**Q: Is there career growth potential?**
A: Yes, gaining experience and certification as a child care specialist can lead to roles in public childcare services or starting a private business.
