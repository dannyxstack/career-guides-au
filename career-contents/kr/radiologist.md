# Radiologist — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 30119 (KECO)**

A specialist who interprets medical images to diagnose diseases and plans/executes radiation treatments.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

General practitioner → Radiology resident (4 years) → Specialist → Subspecialist (internship) or career advancement to university hospital professor/private practitioner.

- Simple image reading
- Repetitive quantitative measurements

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Early disease detection
- Image post-processing and 3D reconstruction

**Human moat:**

- Legal responsibility and ethical decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Utilizing AI image analysis tools
- Data interpretation and statistics
- Interventional radiology procedures
- Patient counseling

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population,
- Health checkups,
- Radiology,
- AI reading

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with excellent medical knowledge and judgment
- People who can focus on image analysis and detailed observation
- People skilled in patient consultation

**Who is not suited**
- People who find it difficult to endure high stress and long training periods
- People who prefer working alone rather than with others

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a radiologist?**
A: Starting salary for a specialist is about 60 million to 100 million KRW, and can exceed 500 million KRW with experience.

**Q: What visa is required for a foreigner to work as a radiologist in Korea?**
A: Requires E-7 (Specific Activities) visa, with Ministry of Health and Welfare certification and employment at a medical institution mandatory. For long-term residence, can switch to F-2 (Residence) or F-5 (Permanent Residence) visa.

**Q: How long does it take to study to become a radiologist?**
A: 6 years of medical school (including graduate medical school) plus 4 years of residency, totaling over 10 years.
