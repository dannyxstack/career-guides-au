# Novelist — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41112 (KECO)**

A novelist is an artist who creates and publishes novels based on imagination and literary quality.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Initially, build a career through short stories/web novels, then grow into a stable writer through literary awards or bestsellers. Afterwards, it is possible to sign a contract with a publisher or self-publish.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- cliché-based story generation
- simple romance/fantasy web novels
- formatted episode structures

**Where AI augments the role:**

- plot idea discovery
- customized reader analysis
- draft review and sentence polishing

**Human moat:**

- unique worldviews
- deep human understanding and emotional expression
- literary style and wordplay

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool proficiency
- data-driven trend analysis
- diversified storytelling

**Key growth areas:**

- web novels
- digital publishing
- literary awards
- agencies

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- A person with rich imagination and creativity
- A person who enjoys reading and writing and can write consistently
- A person who can endure criticism and frustration

**Who is not suited**
- A person seeking immediate profits
- A person who prefers a regular work environment

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average income of a novelist?**
A: Entry-level writers earn very low at 12~30 million KRW per year, bestsellers can earn over 100 million KRW annually, but the average is much lower.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a novelist in Korea?**
A: E-7 visa is possible but strict, and offers fewer benefits compared to F-4 or F-2. Usually, they stay and work under other visa purposes rather than literary activities.

**Q: Is a university degree necessary to become a novelist?**
A: It's not mandatory, but graduating in creative writing or winning a literary award helps with debut. There are also cases of success through self-study.
