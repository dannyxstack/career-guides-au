# Fisheries Research Scientist — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 12214 (KECO)**

A professional who studies the management of aquatic resources, development of aquaculture technologies, and conservation of marine ecosystems, contributing to improved fishery productivity and sustainable fisheries development.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- A person with deep interest and curiosity in marine life
- A person who enjoys long-term research and fieldwork
- A person who values environmental conservation and sustainability

**Who is not suited**
- A person who prioritizes short-term results and wants fast outcomes
- A person who dislikes outdoor activities or fieldwork

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a fisheries research scientist?**
A: For entry-level (0-3 years), the annual salary is about 35-45 million KRW, and it may be slightly lower but stable at national/public research institutes.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a fisheries research scientist?**
A: As a specialized research field, the E-7 (Specific Activities) visa is common; if you have a master's or doctoral degree from a domestic university, you can switch from D-2 to F-2.

**Q: What is the career progression for this job?**
A: Promotion follows the path of Researcher → Senior Researcher → Principal Researcher, and advancement to government agencies or university professorships is possible.
