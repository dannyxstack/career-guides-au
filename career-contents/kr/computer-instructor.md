# Computer Instructor — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 21421 (KECO)**

A profession that teaches computer literacy (OA, programming, etc.) at academies, lifelong education centers, and corporate settings, with steady educational demand.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teaching Certificate (Information & Computer) | Ministry of Education | Required | Mandatory for regular school instructors |
| Information Technology Qualification (ITQ) | Korea Productivity Center | Optional | Advantageous for OA lectures |
| Computer Literacy Level 1 | Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry | Optional | Preferred for public institution lectures |
| Information Processing Engineer | Human Resources Development Service of Korea | Optional | Advantageous for programming lectures |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Starting as a junior instructor, one can advance to full-time and lead instructor roles through experience; obtaining IT certifications (e.g., Information Processing Engineer) enables advancement to managerial or educational consultant positions in training institutions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic OA (Word, Excel) lectures
- Repetitive practice guidance
- Standardized ITQ exam preparation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time Q&A and feedback
- Team project coaching
- Customized learning path design

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face communication skills
- Learner-customized teaching methods
- Case sharing based on practical experience
- Emotional support and mentoring

**Skills to build:**

- AI Educational Tool Proficiency
- Basic Data Analysis
- Educational Content Planning
- Online-Offline Blended Class Design

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital literacy
- Coding education
- Lifelong education
- Vocational training

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who find fulfillment in teaching others
- People who enjoy imparting computer skills to others
- People who continuously strive to learn educational trends

**Who is not suited**
- People who want high salary
- People who find repetitive lectures boring

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a computer instructor?**
A: It varies widely within the profession: beginner 2,200~3,000, intermediate 3,000~4,200, advanced 4,200~5,500 (in KRW 10,000).

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work as computer instructors?**
A: Specialized instructors can apply for an E-7 visa, while overseas Koreans can work with an F-4 visa.

**Q: Is there a possibility that AI will replace computer instructors?**
A: Basic OA training may be replaced by AI tutors, but advanced programming and problem-solving courses still require human instructors, leading to a mixed landscape.
