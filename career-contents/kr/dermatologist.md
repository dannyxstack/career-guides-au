# Dermatologist — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 301112 (KECO)**

A doctor who diagnoses and treats skin diseases and also performs cosmetic skin procedures. Due to the limited number of specialists, they receive high salaries, and the demand for cosmetic dermatology is increasing.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| Graduate School of Medicine (pre-med + medical school) | 6 years | $60,000,000~$99,999,999 (Higher at national universities; private universities are more expensive) |
| Dermatology residency training | 4 years | $30,000,000~$50,000,000 (Salary during training is low, but training costs are free; living expenses must be considered) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medical license | Ministry of Health and Welfare | Required | Obtained after passing the Korean Medical Licensing Examination |
| Dermatology Specialist Certificate | Korean Medical Association (Korean Dermatological Association) | Required | Obtained after completing residency and passing the specialist exam |

---

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 4,200. With an aging population, the increase in skin diseases such as skin cancer and rising demand for cosmetic procedures make the employment outlook bright. However, competition is fierce due to limited physician quotas, and there is a concentration in the Seoul metropolitan area.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Pass the Korean Medical Licensing Examination → Complete 4 years of dermatology residency → Obtain a specialist certificate, then open a private practice or work at a university hospital/clinic. Income increases significantly upon successful practice opening, but requires initial capital and competition.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Reading simple skin disease diagnoses
- Standardized laser procedure settings

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Early diagnosis of skin cancer
- Patient education and personalized treatment planning

**Human moat:**

- Direct face-to-face patient care
- Hand skills for complex skin procedures (surgery, laser)
- Medical judgment and ethical decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Utilization of AI-based diagnostic tools
- Improving proficiency in cosmetic skin procedures
- Patient communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Cosmetic dermatology
- Geriatric dermatology
- Skin cancer treatment
- Laser procedures

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with excellent medical knowledge and hand skills
- People with customer service skills and aesthetic sense
- People with patience to endure long-term study and training

**Who is not suited**
- People who want quick income
- People who want to avoid night shifts or on-call duties

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a dermatologist?**
A: After obtaining board certification, the starting salary for a salaried doctor is about 100-200 million KRW, and income can increase significantly when opening a private practice.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a dermatologist in Korea?**
A: Yes, but it is very limited. The medical license certification process is strict, and to obtain an E-7 visa, Korean language proficiency and professional expertise must be demonstrated.

**Q: How many years does it take to become a dermatologist?**
A: 6 years of medical school + 4 years of residency, taking at least 10 years.
