# TV/radio/online broadcast director — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41611 (KECO)**

A professional who oversees the planning, production, editing, and direction of programs for television, radio, and online broadcasting. A career requiring creativity and leadership.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

AI assists some tasks like planning, editing, and data analysis in broadcasting direction, but creative planning and director roles are hard to replace, leading to mixed impacts.

Typically start as a station PD, then advance to senior PD, team leader, or department head based on experience. Founding an independent production company or transitioning to freelance is also common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple editing tasks
- Subtitle generation
- Data collection and analysis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Scenario analysis
- Viewer data-based planning
- Auto-editing assistance

**Human moat:**

- Creative ideas
- Understanding human emotions
- Leadership and decision-making
- Networking

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool utilization
- Data analysis
- Digital Content Production
- Project Management

**Key growth areas:**

- OTT content
- Digital production
- Web entertainment
- Short-form content

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary of a broadcasting director?**
A: Entry-level PD (0-3 years) earns 30-45 million KRW, mid-level 45-70 million, and senior head PD 70-150 million KRW. Freelancers have variable income per project.

**Q: Can foreigners work as broadcasting directors in Korea?**
A: Possible with an E-7 visa, but Korean language skills and broadcasting experience are essential, and competition is fierce. Realistically, it's difficult unless exceptional expertise is recognized by K-content production companies.

**Q: What preparation is needed to become a broadcasting director?**
A: A broadcasting-related degree, along with station internships and assistant PD experience, is important. A creative portfolio and continuous self-development are essential.
