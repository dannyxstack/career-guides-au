# Sports Referee — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 42031 (KECO)**

A professional responsible for applying rules and making decisions in sports games, ensuring fair play and operating across various sports disciplines.


---

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Typically affiliated with sports associations; promotion to international referee is possible based on experience and qualifications. After retirement, can transition to referee educator or administrator.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive offside decisions
- Ball position tracking
- Simple out/save decisions (tennis, etc.)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- VAR review decisions
- Real-time statistics provision
- Training and education analysis

**Human moat:**

- On-site atmosphere control
- Ambiguous situation interpretation
- Referee-player communication
- Emotional judgment

**Skills to build:**

- AI analysis tool proficiency
- Video review collaboration skills
- Deepened rule interpretation

**Key growth areas:**

- Professional sports
- Community sports
- Esports
- Female referees

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those with fairness and quick reflexes
- Those with deep understanding and passion for sports rules
- Those who stay calm under stress

**Who is not suited**
- Those sensitive to audience criticism
- Those who struggle with immediate decision-making

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a sports referee?**
A: For beginners about 24-35 million KRW, senior international referees can earn over 100 million KRW. However, full-time referee positions are rare, most work part-time.

**Q: Can foreigners work as sports referees in Korea?**
A: With an E-7 visa, invitations for professional sports referees are possible but extremely rare, requiring Korean language proficiency and qualifications in the sport.

**Q: What training is needed to become a sports referee?**
A: A degree in physical education is advantageous, and you must obtain referee training and certification from each sport's association. Typically, training takes 6 months to 2 years.
