# Visual Designer — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41544 (KECO)**

A professional who designs the image of brands, products, and services through visual communication, working in diverse fields such as graphics, editorial, and UI/UX.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career is typically built on a portfolio; freelancers, agencies, or in-house design teams are options. Mid-level can advance to art director or design team leader, or specialize in areas like UX/UI or motion graphics.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple logo variations
- Image correction and retouching
- Template-based design
- General banner creation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Idea sketching and prototyping
- Color and font selection recommendations
- User testing simulation
- Design automation workflows

**Human moat:**

- Creative problem-solving skills
- Brand strategy development
- Emotional storytelling
- Client collaboration and communication

**Skills to build:**

- AI design tools (e.g., DALL-E, Midjourney)
- UX/UI Design
- Data visualization
- Programming Basics (HTML/CSS)
- Project Management

**Key growth areas:**

- UX/UI Design
- Motion Graphics
- Brand Design
- Digital Publishing

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Creative and aesthetically sensitive people

- People who value teamwork and communication

**Who is not suited**
- Those who prefer repetitive simple tasks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a visual designer?**
A: Starting annual salary before tax is 24~32 million KRW, varies greatly by experience and portfolio.

**Q: What visa does a foreigner need to work as a visual designer in Korea?**
A: An E-7 visa is common, requiring professional design experience and employer sponsorship. Alternatively, you can enter on a D-10 job seeker visa and switch upon employment.

**Q: What is the main career path for a visual designer?**
A: Junior designers can advance to senior, art director, design team lead, or specialize in fields like UX/UI.
