# University Part-time Lecturer — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 21121 (KECO)**

A non-tenure-track faculty member who takes charge of lectures instead of full-time professors at universities, receiving hourly lecture fees and contracting on a semester basis.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★½☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 80,000. Due to the decline in the school-age population and worsening university finances, hiring of part-time lecturers is decreasing. The government's enforcement of the Lecturer Act is improving treatment, but job instability persists.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI can assist with tasks such as generating lecture materials and automating assessments, but face-to-face lectures and human interaction are difficult to replace. Therefore, the overall impact is limited, and innovation in teaching methods using AI can actually be a competitive advantage.

Starting with a master's degree, one accumulates teaching experience, but opportunities to transition to full-time professor are extremely rare. Even after obtaining a doctoral degree, many remain as part-time lecturers, making it difficult to see as a lifelong career.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple knowledge-delivery lectures
- Objective test creation and grading
- Standardized lecture note writing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Customized learning material production
- Individual student feedback
- Lecture quality analysis

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face interaction
- Discussion guidance
- Mentoring
- Educational intuition

**Skills to build:**

- Utilizing AI educational tools
- Data-driven lesson design
- Online lecture operation
- Learning analytics

**Key growth areas:**

- Job insecurity
- Lecturer Act
- Declining school-age population
- Specialization of lectures

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a university part-time lecturer?**
A: Hourly lecture fees range from KRW 40,000 to 100,000, and with 6-12 hours per week, annual salary ranges from KRW 15 million to 60 million, significantly lower than full-time faculty.

**Q: Can foreigners work as part-time lecturers in Korea?**
A: Generally, the E-1 professor visa is for full-time faculty, so it is difficult to obtain for part-time lecturers. Only those with F-2 residence visas are eligible.

**Q: Can a part-time lecturer become a full-time professor?**
A: Realistically, it is very difficult. Most full-time faculty recruitment is through open competition, and part-time lecturer experience does not significantly help.
