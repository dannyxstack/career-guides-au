# Street stall and mobile vendors — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 61551 (KECO)**

This profession involves selling food, miscellaneous goods, etc., on the street using stalls or vehicles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Although AI does not directly run street stalls, the introduction of kiosks and mobile ordering systems may reduce some simple sales tasks. However, human interaction and on-the-spot cooking are difficult to automate, so the impact is limited.

Starting as an individual business without store rental, there is no specific promotion path. Expanding the business typically involves operating multiple stalls or combining online sales.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple repetitive sales
- Calculation and change handling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Menu recommendation and customized sales
- Automated inventory management

**Human moat:**

- On-the-spot cooking skills
- Customer relationship building
- On-site response ability

**Skills to build:**

- Basic cooking skills
- Small-scale accounting management
- SNS marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Street Food
- Tourism demand
- Regulatory changes
- Online substitution

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly income of a street vendor?**
A: The average is 1 to 3 million KRW per month, but it varies greatly by location, item, and season.

**Q: Can foreigners work as street vendors?**
A: It is difficult with an employment visa (E-7, etc.), but possible with a permanent residence (F-5) or overseas Korean (F-4) visa.

**Q: Do street vendors need permits?**
A: They need to complete hygiene training, report to local government, and obtain a street vendor permit. Unauthorized vending can be subject to crackdowns.
