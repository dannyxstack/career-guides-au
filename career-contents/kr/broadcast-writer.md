# Broadcast Writer — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41114 (KECO)**

A broadcast professional responsible for planning, organizing, and writing scripts for TV programs, specializing in genres such as entertainment, educational, and drama.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 15,000. With the growth of new media like YouTube and OTT, content demand is increasing, but competition is fierce due to the reduction of regular broadcast station hires and a high proportion of freelancers.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Typically start as freelancers, build experience and portfolios, and advance to main writers or head writers, or transition to broadcast PDs or content planners.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- News Breaking Scripts
- Pattern-based Entertainment Subtitles
- Simple Segment Proposals
- Drafts for Broadcast Scripts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Idea Generation
- Script Editing and Polishing
- Trend Analysis-based Planning
- Viewership Data Interpretation

**Human moat:**

- Original Creativity
- Improvising at Entertainment Sites
- Personal Networks with Celebrities and PDs
- Understanding Cultural Context

**Skills to build:**

- Utilizing AI-based Script Tools
- Data Analysis (Viewership, Trends)
- Advanced Storytelling
- Collaboration and Communication

**Key growth areas:**

- OTT content
- Web entertainment
- Short-form Content
- One-person Media

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with excellent writing skills
- People with abundant ideas
- People who are strong under stress

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer regular work hours
- People who prefer fixed frameworks over creative thinking

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a broadcast writer?**
A: Early career: 20-30 million KRW; 10+ years: 50 million to 100 million KRW; income varies greatly as many are freelancers.

**Q: Can foreigners work as broadcast writers?**
A: E-7 visa allows professional work, but Korean proficiency and broadcasting experience are required, and competition is fierce.

**Q: Is a degree necessary to become a broadcast writer?**
A: Not mandatory, but graduating from a broadcasting-related department or completing a broadcast writing institute is advantageous; portfolio and specs are important.
