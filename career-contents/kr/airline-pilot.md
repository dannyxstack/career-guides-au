# Airline Pilot — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 62111 (KECO)**

A professional who operates civil and military aircraft to safely transport passengers and cargo, requiring rigorous qualifications and training.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| Bachelor's degree in Aeronautical Science or Aviation | 4 years | $30,000,000~$60,000,000 (3 million KRW at national universities, up to 6 million KRW at private universities) |
| Flight training program | 1.5 years | $50,000,000~$99,999,999 (Cost varies significantly depending on flight hours acquired) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Airline Transport Pilot License (ATPL) | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport | Required | Required for civil airline captain; minimum 1,500 flight hours needed |
| Instrument Rating (IR) | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport | Required | Certifies instrument flight proficiency |
| First-Class Medical Certificate | Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport | Required | Requires annual renewal |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Promotion path: flight training school → First Officer → Captain → Senior Captain or training/management roles. Reaching captain at a major airline typically requires 10–15+ years of experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Regular altitude maintenance and automatic flight
- Fuel optimization calculation
- Basic communication procedures

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Emergency situation judgment and decision-making
- Weather diversion route planning
- Passenger and crew management

**Human moat:**

- Crisis response skills
- Legal and ethical responsibility
- Building passenger trust

**Skills to build:**

- System monitoring
- Data analysis
- Crew resource management

**Key growth areas:**

- Growth of low-cost carriers
- Recovery of international routes
- Early retirement of pilots
- Introduction of next-generation aircraft

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with high stress tolerance and concentration
- People who like following systematic procedures
- People willing to invest in continuous self-care and learning

**Who is not suited**
- People who struggle with irregular lifestyle and lengthy isolated work
- People negligent in physical and mental health care

---

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of an aircraft pilot?**
A: First officer starting salary 40–70 million KRW, captain 1.5–300 million KRW, varying greatly by experience and airline size.

**Q: What visa do foreigners need to work as an aircraft pilot in Korea?**
A: E-7 (Specific Activities) visa is required; aviation industry experience and licenses must be proven.

**Q: What is the cost of training to become a pilot?**
A: The flight training course costs 50 million to 150 million KRW, and including a university degree, it can reach up to 200 million KRW or more.
