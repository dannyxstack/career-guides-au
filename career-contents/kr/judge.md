# Judge — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 22111 (KECO)**

A judicial officer who presides over court proceedings and resolves legal disputes. A profession that realizes social justice through fair trials in accordance with the constitution and laws.


---

## 1. Education path / duration / cost

| Stage | Duration | Cost (KRW) |
|---|---|---:|
| Law School | 3 years | $99,999,999~$99,999,999 (Includes tuition and living expenses. National universities are relatively affordable.) |
| Judicial Research and Training Institute (JRTI) | 2 years | $0~$0 (Government-funded education; stipend provided upon completion.) |

---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lawyer's License | Ministry of Justice, Republic of Korea | Required | Required to pass the bar exam after graduating from law school. |
| Appointment as a Judge | Supreme Court of Korea | Required | Requires more than 10 years of lawyer experience or completion of JRTI and passing the appointment screening. |

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,200. Due to population aging and an increase in legal disputes, demand for judges is stable, but employment growth is expected to be modest as the number of appointments is limited.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much is a judge's annual salary?**
A: Starting judges earn about 60-80 million KRW pre-tax annually, rising to 140-200 million KRW after 15+ years.

**Q: Can foreigners become judges in Korea?**
A: Korean courts are for locals only; it is very difficult for foreigners to be appointed as judges. E-7 visa allows legal advisory activities but not judge duties.

**Q: What is the process to become a judge?**
A: Must graduate from law school (3 years), pass the bar exam, have 10+ years of attorney experience or complete Judicial Research and Training Institute, then pass judge appointment screening.
