# Firefighter — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 24021 (KECO)**

A specialized profession that protects lives and property from fires, disasters, and emergencies by performing rescue, suppression, and emergency medical activities.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.


---

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passing the firefighter recruitment exam | National firefighter appointment exam | Required | Includes written, physical, and interview tests |
| Level 1 Emergency Medical Technician | Korea Health Personnel Licensing Examination Institute | Optional | Mandatory for ambulance crew, optional certification |
| Class 1 Large Vehicle Driver's License | National Police Agency | Optional | Allows driving fire trucks, required by each fire station |

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very high (★★★★½).** National workforce approx. 72,000. Due to climate change, large wildfires and natural disasters are increasing, and with an aging population, demand for emergency medical services is rising, which is expected to maintain stable employment.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★★).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

While AI and robots can replace fire monitoring and information analysis tasks, on-site rescue, suppression, and emergency treatment, which require human judgment and physical ability, cannot be replaced, so the impact is limited.

After passing the firefighter exam and completing training at a fire academy, recruits are assigned to the field. Through work experience and promotion exams, they can advance to positions such as Fire Sergeant, Fire Lieutenant, and Fire Captain.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI partially replaces fire call receipt and classification in control centers.
- Drones assist in external building fire suppression.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI fire prediction systems support strategic resource deployment.
- Wearable AI analyzes rescuer biometric signals.

**Human moat:**

- Immediate on-site situation assessment.
- Heuristic decision-making ability during rescue.
- Building trust relationships with citizens.

**Skills to build:**

- AI-based disaster information analysis capability.
- Operation of drone and robot firefighting equipment.
- Understanding of data-driven safety management.

**Key growth areas:**

- Disaster Response
- Emergency Medical Services
- Wildfire Suppression
- Safety Management

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those with excellent physical and mental strength
- Those who remain calm in crisis situations and find reward in helping others
- Those who value teamwork and follow rules well

**Who is not suited**
- Those with acrophobia or claustrophobia
- Those who find it difficult to handle irregular shifts and risks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a firefighter?**
A: The starting salary is around 32-38 million KRW, and after 10+ years, it can rise to 55-75 million KRW. This includes hazard pay and overtime allowances.

**Q: Can foreigners become firefighters in Korea?**
A: Realistically, it is very difficult. Most firefighters are Korean nationals, and there are no recruitment exams for foreigners. There are almost no cases of issuing E-7 visas for this.

**Q: What preparations are needed to become a firefighter?**
A: You must pass the fire service recruitment exam (written, physical fitness, interview). Written subjects include English, Korean history, fire science, and administrative law, with physical and interview tests also important.
