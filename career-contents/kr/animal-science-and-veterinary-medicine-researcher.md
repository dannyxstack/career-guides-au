# Animal Science and Veterinary Medicine Researcher — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 12216 (KECO)**

A professional specializing in livestock disease prevention, productivity improvement, and food safety, conducting laboratory and field research.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veterinary License | Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs | Optional | Requires graduation from veterinary school and passing the national exam |
| Livestock Engineer | Human Resources Development Service of Korea | Optional | Requires university major or experience |
| Research Note Certification | National Research Foundation of Korea | Optional | Required for government research projects |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

With a master's or doctoral degree, researchers work in institutes, universities, or government agencies, advancing to senior researcher or lab director based on experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Data cleaning and statistical analysis
- Basic image interpretation
- Literature search and summarization

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Disease prediction model development
- Genomic data analysis
- Experiment automation

**Human moat:**

- Animal experiment design and ethical judgment
- On-site problem-solving skills
- Veterinary clinical diagnosis

**Skills to build:**

- AI/ML basics
- Data visualization
- Animal welfare and ethics

**Key growth areas:**

- Livestock disease prevention
- Livestock product safety
- Animal welfare
- Smart livestock farming

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

---

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Someone with a deep interest in life sciences and animals
- Someone with systematic and meticulous analytical skills
- Someone who excels at both collaboration and independent research

**Who is not suited**
- Someone who dislikes outdoor fieldwork
- Someone who lacks patience for repetitive experimental tasks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an animal science/veterinary researcher?**
A: Entry-level: 30–45 million KRW; with experience, it can rise to 90 million KRW.

**Q: Can foreigners get a work visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes. You can work at research institutes or universities on an E-7 visa; a master's degree or higher is advantageous.

**Q: What degree is required?**
A: Generally a master's degree or higher is required; veterinary research requires a veterinary license.
