# Electrical Measurement and Control Engineer — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 15312 (KECO)**

As a specialized technical professional who designs, develops, and maintains electrical/electronic measurement and control systems, they play a key role in smart factories and automation fields.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Junior Technician → Mid-level Design/Commissioning → Senior Project Manager/System Engineer, with faster growth possible through master's/doctoral degrees or certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Simple repetitive measurement data recording
- Basic PID controller tuning
- Standardized report writing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Advanced control algorithm optimization
- Anomaly diagnosis and predictive maintenance
- Complex system integration and simulation
- Real-time data analysis-based decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Field Experience & Intuition
- Unstructured Problem-Solving
- Safety & Ethical Judgment
- Multidisciplinary Collaboration

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Machine Learning Basics
- Big Data Analysis
- IoT Platform
- Cloud-Based Control Systems
- Programming (Python, C++)

**Key growth areas:**

- Smart factory
- Industrial automation
- Semiconductor equipment
- Renewable energy control

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of an electrical measurement and control engineer?**
A: Junior level: 30-40 million KRW, mid-level: 45-60 million KRW, senior level: 60-90 million KRW. Higher for large companies or specialized fields.

**Q: What visa is needed for foreigners to work in this occupation in Korea?**
A: E-7 (Specific Activities) visa is most common, for skilled technicians. E-9 (Non-professional Employment) is limited; long-term residents can switch to F-2 or F-5.

**Q: What is the future outlook for this occupation?**
A: Demand is expected to increase with growth in smart factories, automation, semiconductors, and advanced industries. Low risk of AI replacement, making it stable.
