# Secretary — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 02941 (KECO)**

A profession that manages the boss's work schedule, documents, calls, and performs office support tasks, responsible for efficient communication and administrative processing within the organization.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Starting as a junior secretary, one can be promoted to middle manager secretary or executive secretary with experience, and some may transition to office managers or administrative specialists.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Scheduling
- Phone handling
- Document sorting and organizing
- Basic data search

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Meeting minutes and analysis
- Data organization and report writing
- Communication coordination
- Providing business insights

**Human moat:**

- Trust relationship with superiors
- Situational judgment
- Organizational political understanding
- Emotional intelligence

**Skills to build:**

- AI tool proficiency
- Data analysis
- Project management
- Foreign language skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Office automation
- Executive secretary
- Secretary at foreign companies
- Digital collaboration tools

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Korea's E-7 skilled-worker visa is granted to qualifying professionals with a local job offer, and long-term holders may move to F-2/F-5 residence. Requirements are set by the Korea Immigration Service and change over time.

**Official immigration source:** [Korea Immigration Service](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/index.do)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is not suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a secretary?**
A: For entry-level, it ranges from 24 to 30 million KRW, and can rise to 60 million KRW with experience. Executive secretaries at large corporations earn more.

**Q: Can foreigners work as secretaries in Korea?**
A: Yes, it's possible. Through an E-7 visa, they can work at foreign companies or in specialized fields, and Korean language proficiency is essential.

**Q: Can secretaries transition to other roles?**
A: Yes, they can move to office management, HR, marketing, etc., and experience and certifications help.
