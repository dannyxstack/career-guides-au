# Reporter — career analysis · South Korea

**Occupation code: 41632 (KECO)**

A broadcast specialist who collects and reports information through on-site coverage and interviews in fields such as news, sports, and entertainment.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 12,000. Content demand rises due to digital media expansion, but hiring at traditional broadcasters is stagnant. Competition from freelancers, YouTube, and other alternative channels intensifies, so the employment outlook is flat.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

AI is used as an assistive tool rather than fully replacing reporters' work. AI automatic captioning, data analysis assistance, and video editing automation increase productivity, but core tasks requiring human judgment, such as on-site reporting and interviews, are difficult to replace.

Typically, after joining terrestrial/general/cable broadcasters, progress from reporter to special correspondent to professional anchor. Career changes and freelance transitions are common, digital content production skills are important.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI Voice Reading
- Automatic Captioning
- Basic Article Writing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Video Editing Automation
- Data Analysis
- AI Reporting Assistance

**Human moat:**

- On-Site Reporting
- Conducting Interviews
- Original Content Planning

**Skills to build:**

- Digital Media Production
- Data Journalism
- Using AI Tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Reporter
- Broadcast journalist
- Video journalist
- Digital Media

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much is a reporter's salary?**
A: Entry-level: 28-35 million KRW; 8+ years experience: 60 million to 100 million KRW. Freelancers vary greatly by negotiation.

**Q: Can foreigners work as reporters in Korea?**
A: Possible with an E-7 visa (Specific Activities), but Korean language ability and relevant experience are mandatory, and opportunities are very limited.

**Q: Do I need to graduate from a broadcasting university to become a reporter?**
A: Not mandatory, but a related major is advantageous. Practical skills and portfolio are more important than credentials.
