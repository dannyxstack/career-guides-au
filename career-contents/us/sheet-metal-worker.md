# Sheet Metal Workers — career analysis · United States

**Occupation code: 47-2211 (SOC)**

Manufacturing, assembling, installing, and repairing sheet metal products and equipment such as ducts, control boxes, drain pipes, and furnace casings. Involves operating cutting, bending, and straightening machines; welding or brazing parts; and hand-forming metal from drawings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

After apprenticeship, becomes a skilled sheet metal worker, can advance to supervisor, project manager, or self-employed contractor. Some move into related fields like HVAC or metal fabrication. Certifications such as HVAC Excellence can enhance career development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic nesting and CNC cutting of metal sheets
- Simple defect identification and classification through AI visual inspection
- Repeated bending and punching in bulk forming of standard ventilation ducts
- Automatic unfolding and blanking calculation based on 3D models
- AI-driven automatic inventory counting and replenishment in warehouses

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted unfolding and forming process optimization for complex geometric shapes
- AR glasses project installation guides to improve on-site positioning and assembly accuracy
- AI load calculation optimizes material utilization and reduces waste
- Digital twin simulation of sheet metal part stress performance in assemblies
- AI-Driven Robots Collaborate on Repetitive Welding and Handling

**Human moat:**

- On-site measurement and manual finishing of non-standard, irregular metal parts
- Installation and sealing of ventilation ducts and roofs in confined spaces or at heights
- Experienced judgment of hidden defects such as metal fatigue and weld quality
- Ability to communicate on-site with clients and designers and adjust plans
- Safety assurance: handling hazardous environments like heat, sharp edges, toxic coatings

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/CAM software (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD Inventor)
- Master CNC punching and laser cutting machine programming and operation
- Understanding collaborative programming of industrial robots with sheet metal bending and welding cells
- Apply digital measurement tools (e.g., 3D scanners) for reverse engineering
- Basic data analysis skills to optimise material layout and process parameters
- Learning connection techniques for lightweight materials (aluminum-magnesium alloys, composites)

**Key growth areas:**

- HVAC Installation
- Green Construction
- Automation
- Welding

## 6. Migration pathways / PR


| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass H-1B (H-1B Specialty Occupations) | Requires bachelor's degree or equivalent experience, but sheet metal workers typically do not meet professional position requirements; very few cases may apply for advanced welding or management roles. |
| Subclass EB-3 (EB-3 Skilled Workers/Professionals) | Employer-sponsored green card, requires PERM labor certification. Usually requires at least 2 years of training or experience, suitable for experienced sheet metal workers. Limited quotas, long waiting times. |
| Subclass TN (TN NAFTA Professional) | Only applicable to Canadian and Mexican citizens, but sheet metal worker is not a professional occupation on the TN list, so not eligible. |

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for sheet metal workers in the U.S.?**
A: According to BLS 2023 data, median annual salary is about $55,000, hourly about $26.4. Entry-level around $35,000-$50,000, senior up to $95,000.

**Q: How can a sheet metal worker immigrate to the US?**
A: Mainly through the EB-3 green card pathway, requiring employer sponsorship and PERM labor certification. Minimum 2 years of experience or apprenticeship completion. H-1B is generally not applicable.
