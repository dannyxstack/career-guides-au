## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Turkey?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Turkey primarily produce for their own consumption, but any surplus sold provides cash income. Monthly income varies widely, often ranging from TRY 5,000 to TRY 15,000 depending on herd size and product prices. This income is irregular and supplemented by other farm activities.

**Q: What does a typical day look like for a subsistence livestock farmer in Turkey?**
A: A typical day starts early with feeding, watering, and milking animals like cattle, sheep, or goats. Farmers also clean shelters, check for health issues, and manage grazing. Afternoon tasks include repairing fences, preparing feed, and processing milk or wool. Work is physically demanding and varies with seasons.

**Q: What skills are essential for subsistence livestock farming in Turkey?**
A: Key skills include animal husbandry knowledge, basic veterinary care, and pasture management. Farmers need physical stamina, problem-solving abilities, and traditional breeding techniques. Financial literacy for managing costs and sales is also important. Many skills are passed down through generations or learned through experience.
