## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Turkey?**
A: Income is largely non-cash from home consumption and barter. Cash earnings vary but often range from a few thousand to 15,000 Turkish Lira annually, depending on surplus sales of products like milk, eggs, and vegetables. Many farmers rely on off-farm income and government subsidies.

**Q: What does a typical day involve for a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Turkey?**
A: A typical day starts early with feeding and milking livestock, then working on crops according to season—planting, weeding, harvesting. Farmers perform maintenance on tools, fences, and water systems. The work is physically intensive, often with family members, and tasks vary greatly between summer and winter.

**Q: What is the career outlook for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Turkey?**
A: The outlook is mixed. While subsistence farming provides food security, younger generations often migrate to cities for work. Government support programs and organic farming trends may offer some opportunities, but overall the number of subsistence farms is declining. Farmers who adapt to market-oriented production or agritourism have better prospects.
