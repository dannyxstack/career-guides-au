- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical monthly income for street services workers in Turkey?**
A: Earnings vary widely by location and season. In major cities like Istanbul, street vendors and related workers can earn between 5,000 and 15,000 Turkish Lira per month, though income is often irregular and depends on foot traffic, product demand, and weather conditions. Many workers are not formally employed, so taxes and social security contributions are minimal.

**Q: What essential skills are needed to succeed as a street services worker in Turkey?**
A: Key skills include basic numeracy for handling cash and making change, good communication and bargaining abilities, and knowledge of local regulations regarding vending. Physical stamina for long hours on foot, adaptability to weather, and basic Turkish language proficiency (or relevant local language) are crucial. Familiarity with digital payment apps is increasingly important.

**Q: How is automation or AI affecting street services jobs in Turkey?**
A: AI and automation have limited direct impact on street services workers due to the informal, personal nature of the work. However, the rise of e-commerce and food delivery apps is shifting consumer habits, potentially reducing foot traffic for some vendors. Conversely, technology can help workers reach customers via social media or mobile payment systems, creating new opportunities.
