## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate tool paths using CAM software
- Operating CNC machine tools for blank processing
- Manual grinding and polishing of mold surfaces
- Measure and record mold dimensional accuracy
- Maintain and refurbish used moulds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI mold design software to quickly generate solutions
- Use simulation software to predict casting defects and optimize processes
- Mold assembly and debugging assisted by AR
- Using data analysis platforms to monitor mold life and quality
- Use 3D scanning and reverse engineering to quickly replicate molds.

**Human moat:**

- Experience in designing and commissioning gating systems for complex castings
- On-site diagnosis of mold failure causes (e.g., thermal cracking, deformation)
- Collaborate with foundry engineers to solve process problems
- Flexibility to handle non-standard, small-batch urgent orders
- Deep understanding of material properties (shrinkage, thermal expansion)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM 3D modeling and programming (e.g., SolidWorks, Mastercam)
- Operation and setup of multi-axis CNC machining centers
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Mold flow simulation analysis (e.g., ProCAST, Moldflow)
- Industrial robot-assisted grinding and assembly
- Data-driven preventive equipment maintenance

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for garment patternmakers and cutters in Turkey?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Entry-level workers earn around 8,000-10,000 TRY per month, while experienced patternmakers in Istanbul or large textile factories can earn 15,000-20,000 TRY. Overtime and piece-rate pay are common. Benefits may include social security, meals, and transport, but many positions are informal.

**Q: How is AI affecting garment patternmaking and cutting jobs in Turkey?**
A: AI and CAD software are increasingly used for digital patternmaking and automated cutting, especially in large factories. This reduces demand for manual marking and scissor cutting but creates need for tech-savvy workers who can operate computerized systems. Traditional hand skills remain valued in small ateliers and custom tailoring. Upskilling is recommended.

**Q: What is the career outlook for garment patternmakers and cutters in Turkey?**
A: Turkey's strong textile and apparel industry offers steady demand, especially in manufacturing hubs like Istanbul, Bursa, and Denizli. The shift to fast fashion and export markets sustains employment, but automation may reduce routine jobs. Skilled patternmakers with CAD knowledge have better prospects. The job is physically demanding with high turnover.
