## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Turkey?**
A: In Turkey, subsistence fishers and hunters generally earn modest incomes, often ranging from 500 to 1,500 TL per week, depending on season, location, and market access. Many rely on catches or harvests for personal consumption, and excess is sold locally. Income is irregular and varies greatly with resource availability.

**Q: What skills are essential for subsistence fishing, hunting, and gathering in Turkey?**
A: Essential skills include deep knowledge of local ecosystems, wildlife behavior, and seasonal patterns; proficiency with traditional tools like nets, traps, and firearms; navigation in rural or coastal areas; and food preservation techniques. Physical endurance, patience, and adaptability to weather changes are also crucial for success.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence impacting subsistence fishing and hunting in Turkey?**
A: AI has minimal direct impact on subsistence activities in Turkey. Some fishers use mobile apps for weather or fish location, but traditional knowledge remains paramount. AI-driven tools like satellite imaging or automated traps are rarely affordable or practical. The role is unlikely to be automated due to its reliance on personal experience and remote environments.
