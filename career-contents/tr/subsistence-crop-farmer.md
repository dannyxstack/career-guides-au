**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual recording of crop growth logs and yield data, replaced by automatic sensors
- Manual pest and disease identification replaced by AI image recognition
- Manual calculation of fertilizer amounts replaced by precision agriculture algorithms
- Paper-based bookkeeping and tax calculations are being replaced by financial software

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI tools analyze soil moisture and meteorological data to optimize irrigation timing.
- Use satellite imagery and drones to monitor crop health and provide early warning of pests and diseases
- AI-assisted planting decisions based on historical data and market price forecasts
- Online platforms connecting local consumers with restaurants to expand direct sales channels
- Smart devices monitor storage conditions to reduce post-harvest loss

**Human moat:**

- Physical labour and on-site work (e.g., manual weeding, harvesting)
- Decisions based on local experience (e.g., traditional crop rotation, variety selection)
- Trust relationship with communities and consumers
- Flexible judgment in response to sudden weather and pests

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average income for subsistence crop farmers in Turkey?**
A: Subsistence crop farmers in Turkey typically earn between 8,000 and 12,000 TL per month, but income varies widely by crop type, region, and market prices. Many farmers consume part of their produce, reducing cash income. Government subsidies and cooperative membership can provide additional financial support.

**Q: What skills are essential for subsistence crop farming in Turkey?**
A: Essential skills include knowledge of soil preparation, planting, irrigation, pest control, and harvesting. Familiarity with local climate and crop rotation is crucial. Basic financial management, use of hand tools and small machinery, and awareness of organic farming methods are increasingly valued.

**Q: How is AI affecting subsistence crop farming in Turkey?**
A: AI adoption is low but growing. Precision agriculture tools like drones and sensors help monitor crop health and optimize inputs. Mobile apps provide weather forecasts, pest alerts, and market info. However, high costs and limited internet in rural areas slow adoption, so traditional knowledge remains primary.
