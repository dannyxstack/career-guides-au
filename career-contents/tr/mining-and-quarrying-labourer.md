**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly wage for mining and quarrying labourers in Turkey?**
A: The average monthly wage ranges from 8,000 to 14,000 Turkish Lira, depending on experience, location, and mine type. Entry-level positions may start near the minimum wage, while experienced workers in large mines can earn higher. Overtime and hazard pay are common in remote or high-risk sites.

**Q: What does a typical workday look like for a mining labourer in Turkey?**
A: A typical workday involves manual tasks such as loading/unloading materials, operating basic machinery, and assisting with drilling or blasting. Shifts are often long (8–12 hours) and physically demanding, with work in underground or open-pit mines. Strict safety protocols, including wearing protective gear, are mandatory due to dust, noise, and heavy equipment.

**Q: How is automation affecting mining labourers in Turkey, and what is the career outlook?**
A: Automation is gradually being adopted in larger Turkish mines, with automated drills and monitoring systems reducing some manual tasks. However, many smaller quarries still rely heavily on labour. Career prospects remain stable in resource-rich regions like Zonguldak (coal) and Elazığ (chrome), but workers may need to adapt to new technologies over time.
