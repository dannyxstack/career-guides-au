**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Turkey?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, but inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Turkey typically earn between the minimum wage (around 11,000 TRY monthly in 2024) and 20,000 TRY, depending on experience, catch volume, and type of fishery. Seasonal work may offer higher daily rates but less stability.

**Q: What skills are essential for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Turkey?**
A: Key skills include physical stamina, knowledge of local fishing grounds and species, boat handling and net repair, basic navigation, and safety at sea. Familiarity with Turkish fishing regulations and sustainability practices is increasingly important, as is the ability to work in harsh weather conditions.

**Q: What is the career outlook for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Turkey?**
A: The outlook is stable but challenging due to overfishing and stricter quotas. Small-scale fisheries remain vital to coastal communities, and demand for fresh, locally caught fish supports continued employment. However, younger workers may seek other opportunities, leading to an aging workforce. Adaptation to sustainable practices is key for long-term viability.
