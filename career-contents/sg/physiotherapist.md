# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Singapore assess, plan, and deliver treatment programs to restore movement and function in patients affected by injury, illness, or disability. They work in hospitals, clinics, sports medicine centers, nursing homes, and community care settings. The profession is regulated by the Allied Health Professions Council (AHPC) and is in high demand due to an aging population and increased focus on rehabilitative care. Singapore's healthcare sector continues to expand, offering stable employment opportunities for both local and foreign-trained physiotherapists.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression in Singapore typically follows a path from junior physiotherapist to senior clinician, then to specialist roles (e.g., in orthopedics, neurology, cardiopulmonary) or management positions such as head of department. Further advancement may involve research, teaching, or private practice ownership. Many physiotherapists pursue postgraduate diplomas or master's degrees to specialize. The career offers a clear ladder in both public and private sectors, with leadership tracks in hospital administration and community care organizations.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapist in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and employer. According to the Ministry of Manpower's Occupational Wage Survey, median monthly wages for physiotherapists are competitive within the healthcare industry. Entry-level positions start lower, while senior specialists command higher pay. For precise figures, refer to the latest MOM report.

**Q: What work visa is needed for foreign physiotherapists in Singapore?**
A: Most foreign physiotherapists apply for an Employment Pass under the COMPASS framework, which requires a job offer from a Singapore employer and meeting a points-based system (including salary, qualifications, and diversity). The S Pass is an alternative for those with slightly lower qualifications but is subject to quotas and levies.

**Q: Do physiotherapists need to register with a local board?**
A: Yes, all practising physiotherapists must register with the Allied Health Professions Council (AHPC) under the Ministry of Health. Foreign qualifications are assessed individually, and a recognition exam may be required. Registration is mandatory before starting work.
