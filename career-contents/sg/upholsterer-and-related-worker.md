# Upholsterer and Related Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7534 (ISCO08)**

Upholsterers in Singapore are skilled tradespeople who repair, re-stuff, and re-cover furniture, seats, and other padded items. They work in furniture manufacturing, renovation, automotive, and marine industries, with demand arising from commercial refurbishments and custom furniture orders. The role requires manual dexterity, attention to detail, and knowledge of fabrics and foams.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 558. Employment outlook for upholsterers is stable but limited due to automation in mass furniture production and competition from low-cost imports. However, demand remains for custom and high-end upholstery in hospitality, residential renovation, and marine sectors. Workers must adapt to new eco-friendly materials and ergonomic designs. The government promotes SkillsFuture training to upskill tradespeople.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or assistant, advancing to senior upholsterer, supervisor, or workshop manager. Experienced workers may specialise in automotive or marine upholstery, or move into furniture design, restoration, or quality control. Self-employment is possible for those with client networks. Continuous learning through courses on modern techniques and materials is beneficial.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD software for standardized layout and cutting
- Sewing machine operation for simple straight stitching
- Mechanical cutting and forming of foam and filling materials
- Automated recording of inventory management and material ordering
- Repetitive filling and fastening tasks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates and modifies soft package patterns
- AR/VR tools visualize final results, reducing rework
- Machine vision detects fabric defects, improving quality
- Digital cutting beds improve material utilization rates
- Smart supply chain systems optimize material procurement

**Human moat:**

- Customized complex patterns and hand-sewing techniques
- The ability to communicate with clients and understand personalized needs
- Experience handling special fabrics (e.g., antique textiles, leather)
- Flexibility for on-site installation, adjustment, and repair
- Comprehensive assessment of furniture structure and comfort

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/3D modeling software (e.g., Rhino, SketchUp)
- Proficient in operating digital cutting and sewing equipment
- Familiar with AR/VR presentation tools
- Develop project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and eco-design trends
- Learn basic programming to customize automated workflows

**Key growth areas:**

- Custom furniture
- Marine upholstery
- Automotive interior
- Restoration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an upholsterer in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level upholsterers earn around SGD 1,800–2,200 per month; experienced workers can earn SGD 2,500–3,500 per month. Salaries vary by sector and skill level.

**Q: Can an upholsterer obtain an Employment Pass (EP) to work in Singapore?**
A: No, upholsterers typically do not qualify for an Employment Pass as it requires professional or managerial roles. Most foreign upholsterers come on a Work Permit (WP) or occasionally an S Pass if they have advanced skills and higher salary.
