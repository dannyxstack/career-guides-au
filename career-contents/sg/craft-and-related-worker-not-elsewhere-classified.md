# Craft and Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

Craft and Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified (ISCO-08 7549) encompass a diverse range of skilled tradespeople in Singapore, including boat builders, musical instrument makers, and precision artisans. These workers are essential to niche manufacturing and repair industries, often employed in small to medium enterprises (SMEs) and supporting sectors like marine, aerospace, and high-end manufacturing. The role requires manual dexterity, attention to detail, and specialised training through the Institute of Technical Education (ITE) or apprenticeship programmes. Employers typically sponsor work passes for foreign talent when local supply is insufficient.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for craft workers in Singapore typically starts as a trainee or apprentice, advancing to a skilled craftsman, then supervisor or workshop manager. Some specialise further (e.g., master craftsman) or shift to quality assurance or training. The ITE provides formal certifications (Nitec, Higher Nitec) that facilitate upward mobility. With experience, workers may start their own small business. The field values practical expertise over academic qualifications, so continuous upskilling through SkillsFuture credits can enhance prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised jewellery grinding and polishing
- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments
- Repeating pattern reproduction in hand carving
- Traditional mold making and casting processes
- Simple wood/stone cutting and shaping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Conceive personalized jewelry/musical instrument designs using AI design software
- Accelerated prototyping through 3D scanning and modeling
- Using AI quality inspection system to improve product precision
- Using AR/VR for complex assembly guidance
- Customized client communication and quoting via digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Highly customized artistic creation and aesthetic judgment
- Experience and tactile feel of handling rare materials
- On-site repair and adaptation of complex non-standard parts
- Cultural story interpretation through in-depth communication with clients
- Overall process control through multi-process integration

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM and 3D modeling (e.g., Rhino, Fusion 360)
- Digital fabrication equipment operator (3D printing, CNC engraving)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g. Midjourney, DALL-E for concept design)
- Materials science and sustainable process knowledge
- Digital customer management and custom platform operations
- Cross-sector collaboration and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Engineering
- Marine & Offshore
- Aerospace Maintenance
- Custom Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a craft worker in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary widely by specialisation and experience, but entry-level craft workers typically earn between SGD 18,000 and SGD 30,000 annually, progressing to SGD 35,000–50,000 for experienced craftsmen. Supervisors may earn SGD 50,000–70,000.

**Q: Can foreign craft workers obtain an Employment Pass?**
A: Generally no, as craft roles are considered mid- or semi-skilled. Foreign workers typically require an S Pass (for those with diploma/experience) or a Work Permit (for semi-skilled). The Employment Pass is reserved for professionals with degree qualifications and higher salaries.

**Q: Is there progression from craft worker to technician or engineer?**
A: Yes, with additional training and certifications (e.g., Higher Nitec, diploma), craft workers can advance to technician roles or even pursue engineering degrees part-time. However, this requires a significant commitment to academic study.
