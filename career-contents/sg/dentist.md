# Dentist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2261 (ISCO08)**

Dentists in Singapore are licensed oral health professionals who diagnose, prevent, and treat diseases and conditions of the oral cavity. With a strong public healthcare system and a growing private sector, the demand for dental services is rising due to an ageing population and greater awareness of oral health. The profession is regulated by the Singapore Dental Council, and both local and foreign-trained dentists must meet strict registration requirements. The occupation is recognised as a shortage role, offering stable employment and competitive remuneration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for dentists typically begins with general practice, followed by specialisation in fields such as orthodontics, oral surgery, or periodontics. In Singapore, many dentists pursue postgraduate qualifications locally (e.g., at the National University of Singapore) or abroad. Opportunities exist in public healthcare institutions, private clinics, and academic settings. Experienced dentists may also open their own practices or take on leadership roles in hospital dental departments. Continuous professional development is mandatory for licence renewal.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening and anomaly marking of X-rays and CT scans (AI reading)
- Administrative paperwork such as patient appointment, billing, and medical record organization
- Standardized preventive procedures like teeth cleaning and fluoride application (automated dental chair)
- Basic intraoral scanning and digital model generation
- Automated review and filling of insurance claim documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems provide treatment suggestions, improving diagnosis accuracy
- Simulate surgical paths via digital twin to optimize implant/orthodontic planning
- Real-time voice recording and medical record generation, freeing doctor's attention
- Personalized prevention plan recommendations based on patient historical data
- Virtual reality training systems enhance complex surgical skills

**Human moat:**

- Complex Surgical Decisions and Manual Operations (e.g., Root Canals, Tooth Extractions)
- Patient communication, empathy, and behaviour management (especially for children/anxious patients)
- Interdisciplinary Comprehensive Diagnosis (e.g., Oral-Systemic Disease Links)
- Emergency management and clinical judgement for sudden conditions
- Professional licensing and legal liability

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted Diagnostic Tool Operation and Result Interpretation
- Digital oral scanning and 3D printing design
- Patient interaction via telehealth platforms
- Basic data analysis and clinical research
- Patient communication and digital health education
- Continuous learning of automated workflow integration

**Key growth areas:**

- Cosmetic dentistry
- Geriatric dentistry
- Digital dentistry
- Implantology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for dentists in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for dentists in Singapore vary widely by experience, specialisation, and sector (public vs private). Entry-level salaries for fresh graduates in the public sector start around SGD 5,000–6,000 per month, while experienced specialists can earn significantly more. For precise figures, refer to the MOM Occupational Wage Survey.

**Q: Can foreign-trained dentists work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, foreign-trained dentists can work in Singapore if they pass the SDC Qualifying Examination (or hold a recognised degree) and obtain an Employment Pass. The occupation is on the Shortage Occupation List, which facilitates COMPASS points and visa approval.

**Q: What are the educational steps to become a dentist in Singapore?**
A: One must complete a Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) from a recognised university (e.g., NUS) or an equivalent foreign degree. After registration with the SDC, dentists must hold an Annual Practising Certificate and may pursue specialisation through postgraduate programmes.
