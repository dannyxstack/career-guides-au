# Odd-Job Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job workers in Singapore perform a variety of manual, unskilled or semi-skilled tasks across industries such as construction, cleaning, and maintenance. They are typically employed on a casual or temporary basis, handling duties like loading/unloading, basic repairs, or site cleaning. This role is common among foreign workers holding Work Permits, as it requires minimal formal education and attracts low wages. The occupation is not a primary migration pathway due to its low skill nature, and it is not listed on Singapore's shortage lists.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for odd-job workers is limited. Few advance to supervisory roles like foreman or site supervisor without additional training or certification. Local workers may move into other trades via SkillsFuture courses, while foreign workers often remain in similar roles or switch employers. Upskilling in areas like forklift operation or cleaning chemicals can lead to higher-paying positions, but the occupation typically offers low upward mobility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Cleaning services
- Maintenance
- Logistics support
- Construction labour

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Singapore's main professional work pass is the Employment Pass, granted to qualifying professionals with a job offer above a salary threshold; long-term holders may apply for Permanent Residence. Criteria are set by the Ministry of Manpower and updated regularly.

**Official immigration source:** [Ministry of Manpower (MOM)](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job worker in Singapore?**
A: The average monthly salary ranges from SGD 1,200 to SGD 1,800, depending on sector and experience. Annual pre-tax income is approximately SGD 14,400 to SGD 21,600.

**Q: Can I get a work visa as an odd-job worker in Singapore?**
A: Yes, mainly through a Work Permit for semi-skilled workers. Employers must sponsor and meet quota/levy requirements. No direct migration path; status is tied to employment.

**Q: Are there opportunities for career progression as an odd-job worker?**
A: Limited. Upskilling in areas like forklift operation or WSQ certifications can lead to roles like warehouse assistant or site supervisor, but overall upward mobility is low.
