# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

In Singapore, hunting and trapping is not a common occupation due to strict laws banning recreational hunting and the city-state's urban environment. Licensed trappers may work for government agencies like the National Parks Board (NParks) or private pest control firms to manage invasive species, nuisance animals, or for wildlife conservation research. The role involves setting traps, monitoring, humane euthanasia, and data collection. Given Singapore's limited natural habitats, demand is very niche and primarily linked to pest control and wildlife management operations in parks, reserves, and urban greenery.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited, typically from trapper to senior trapper or supervisor within a pest control firm. Some may move into wildlife management or conservation roles if they gain additional qualifications. Self-employment is possible but rare. The occupation does not offer a structured career ladder, and most workers remain in operational roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Wildlife population monitoring and counting (via AI image recognition and drone automation)
- Capture data recording and report generation (AI automatic sorting and visualization).
- Predicting animal activity patterns (machine learning models replacing empirical judgment)
- License application and compliance document processing (automated document system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze satellite imagery and infrared camera data to optimize monitoring efficiency
- Improves success rate of precise capture and release through real-time tracking with drones and AI.
- Develop science-based wildlife management strategies using data analysis tools
- Using VR to train on trap setting and safe operation in complex environments

**Human moat:**

- Physical and manual skills for setting and maintaining field traps
- Quick decision-making for unexpected situations (e.g., animal injuries, bad weather)
- Interpersonal communication and negotiation with local communities and landowners.
- Responsibility for ecological ethics judgement and compliance operations

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a hunter/trapper in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are not commonly published due to the niche nature. For pest control trappers, monthly salaries range from SGD 1,800 to SGD 2,800. Government wildlife trappers may earn SGD 2,200 to SGD 3,500. Annual figures are approx. SGD 21,600 to SGD 42,000.

**Q: Can a foreign hunter/trapper obtain a work visa for Singapore?**
A: It is extremely difficult. The occupation is not in demand for foreign workers. A Work Permit might be considered if the employer can demonstrate a local shortage, but approval is unlikely. Singapore prioritizes local talent for such niche roles.

**Q: Is recreational hunting allowed in Singapore?**
A: No. Recreational hunting is illegal. All trapping activities are strictly for pest control or wildlife management under license from NParks or NEA.
