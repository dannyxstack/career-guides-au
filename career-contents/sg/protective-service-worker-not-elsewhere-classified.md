# Protective Services Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective Services Workers Not Elsewhere Classified in Singapore primarily include security officers and guards who safeguard premises, personnel, and property. They are employed across private security firms, government agencies, commercial buildings, and events. The role requires vigilance, physical fitness, and the ability to handle emergencies. Licensing by the Police Licensing & Regulatory Department is mandatory, and ongoing training in security procedures, first aid, and crowd management is typical. The occupation is a mid-level entry point into the security sector, with progression to senior or supervisory roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from a Security Officer role, advancing to Senior Security Officer, then Supervisor, and potentially to Security Manager. Specializations in areas like K9 handling, CCTV monitoring, or executive protection are possible. Many workers pursue additional certifications in security management or cybersecurity to move into higher-level roles. The Singapore government provides training grants through the Security Industry Transformation Programme (SITP) to upskill workers, and there are pathways to roles in auxiliary police or corporate security.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a protective services worker in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level security officers earn around SGD 2,000 to SGD 3,000 per month, while supervisors or those with specialized skills can earn up to SGD 4,500. Overtime and shift allowances may increase take-home pay.

**Q: Can foreign workers get a work pass as a protective services worker?**
A: Yes, typically via a Work Permit for semi-skilled roles (e.g., security guard) or an S Pass for supervisory positions. Employers must sponsor the pass and comply with quota and levy requirements.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities in security?**
A: Progression paths include senior officer, supervisor, manager, or specialized roles like K9 handler or CCTV operator. Further education in security management can lead to corporate security positions.
