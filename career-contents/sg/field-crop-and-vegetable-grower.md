# Field Crop and Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Singapore cultivate a variety of crops, including leafy vegetables, fruits, and herbs, often using intensive farming methods. Due to Singapore's limited land, growers increasingly adopt vertical farming, hydroponics, and other agritech solutions. The sector is small but strategic for food security, with the government promoting local production through grants and land tenders. Most growers work on licensed farms or in agro-technology parks, with a focus on high-yield, sustainable practices.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,060. The outlook for field crop and vegetable growers is stable but niche, with gradual growth driven by Singapore's '30 by 30' goal to produce 30% of nutritional needs locally by 2030. Demand is rising for skilled growers proficient in controlled environment agriculture (CEA) and urban farming technologies. However, traditional open-field farming is declining due to land constraints. The occupation offers moderate employment opportunities, with entry-level roles available for local and foreign workers (Work Permit).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a general farm worker or assistant grower, advancing to lead grower or farm manager. Experienced growers may specialize in hydroponics, aquaponics, or crop science. Some move into consultancy, agritech R&D, or farm ownership. Government initiatives like the Agri-Food Tech Innovation hub provide upskilling pathways. However, opportunities are limited compared to other sectors, and advancement often requires formal training in agricultural science or horticulture.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban Farming
- Agritech
- Hydroponics
- Food Security

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a field crop and vegetable grower in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level growers on Work Permit earn around $1,500 to $2,200 per month. Experienced growers or supervisors on S Pass may earn $2,500 to $3,500. Managers or agritech specialists on Employment Pass can earn $4,000 to $6,000+.

**Q: Can foreigners work as field crop and vegetable growers in Singapore?**
A: Yes, typically via Work Permit (for semi-skilled) or S Pass (for mid-skill). Employers must meet quota and levy. The role is eligible for foreign workers, but most Work Permit holders come from approved source countries (e.g., Malaysia, China, India).
