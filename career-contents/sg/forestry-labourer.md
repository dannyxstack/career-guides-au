# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers in Singapore perform manual tasks related to the maintenance and conservation of green spaces, including nature reserves, parks, and roadside trees. Duties involve planting, pruning, clearing undergrowth, and ensuring tree health. Given Singapore's limited natural forests, work primarily occurs in managed landscapes. The role is physically demanding and typically filled by low-skilled migrant workers on Work Permits. Local Singaporeans rarely take up this occupation, which is part of the landscape maintenance sector. Employment is tied to contractors under the National Parks Board or private estates.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for forestry labourers is minimal. Most workers remain in manual roles or move into supervisory positions after years of experience. Upskilling in arboriculture (e.g., certified tree climbing, chainsaw operation) can lead to roles like Arborist or Landscape Supervisor, but requires certification and English proficiency. Local workers may transition to enforcement or park management roles via diplomas. Migration pathways do not exist beyond Work Permit renewal.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban Greenery
- Tree Conservation
- Habitat Restoration
- Arboriculture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry labourer in Singapore?**
A: Monthly salaries range from SGD 1,200 to SGD 1,800 for Work Permit holders, with basic accommodation and transport often provided by employers. Overtime pay may apply.

**Q: Can a forestry labourer obtain an Employment Pass or S Pass?**
A: No. Forestry labourer is classified as semi-skilled, eligible only for a Work Permit. Minimum salary requirements for S Pass (SGD 3,000) and Employment Pass (SGD 5,000) are far above typical wages.

**Q: Is there any pathway to permanent residence (PR) for forestry labourers?**
A: Work Permit holders generally cannot apply for PR directly. They must upgrade to S Pass or Employment Pass, which is unlikely due to low salary. Long-term stay is limited to work permit renewals.
