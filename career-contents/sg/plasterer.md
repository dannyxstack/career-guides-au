# Plasterer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers in Singapore apply plaster, stucco, and other materials to walls, ceilings, and partitions to create finished surfaces. They are essential in the construction and renovation industry, working on residential, commercial, and institutional projects. The role requires precision and physical stamina, with most work carried out on construction sites or during building refurbishments. In Singapore, plasterers are typically employed by construction firms or subcontractors and may be involved in both new builds and upgrading works under the Housing & Development Board (HDB) or private sector. The occupation demands trade-specific skills, often acquired through the Institute of Technical Education (ITE) or on-the-job training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for plasterers typically starts as a general labourer or apprentice, advancing to skilled plasterer, then supervisor or foreman. Some may specialise in decorative or restoration plastering. With experience, plasterers can become self-employed contractors or start their own subcontracting businesses. Continuous upgrading through WSQ certifications or higher Nitec in building services can open routes to construction management roles. The Singapore government promotes skills upgrading through the SkillsFuture programme.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Heritage Restoration
- Green Building Finishes
- HDB Upgrading

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How much can a plasterer earn in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience and skill level. Entry-level plasterers may earn around $1,800–$2,500 per month, while experienced plasterers or supervisors can earn $3,000–$4,500. Foreign workers on Work Permits typically receive a fixed monthly salary as per MOM regulations.

**Q: What work pass do I need to work as a plasterer in Singapore?**
A: Most foreign plasterers come on a Work Permit for the construction sector, which is employer-sponsored and has restrictions on changing employers. Some experienced plasterers may qualify for an S Pass if they meet the minimum salary and pass the COMPASS assessment.

**Q: Is there career progression for plasterers in Singapore?**
A: Yes, experienced plasterers can advance to foreman, site supervisor, or project coordinator roles. With further training, they can become construction managers or start their own subcontracting business.
