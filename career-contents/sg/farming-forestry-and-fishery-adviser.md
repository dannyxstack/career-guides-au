# Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2132 (ISCO08)**

Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers in Singapore provide expert guidance on sustainable agricultural practices, urban farming techniques, and resource management to optimize food production and environmental conservation. With land scarcity and a national '30 by 30' goal to boost local food production, these advisers play a key role in transforming Singapore's agri-food sector through technology adoption and policy support.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression in Singapore typically starts from junior advisory roles in government agencies or R&D labs, advancing to senior consultants or programme managers overseeing agri-tech projects. Specialising in smart farming, data analytics, or aquaculture certification can lead to leadership positions in policy planning or agribusiness. Cross-sector mobility into sustainability roles in MNCs is also growing.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate standardized soil test reports and crop growth cycle analyses
- Automated fertiliser recommendation based on historical data and satellite imagery
- Draft basic sustainable forest management plans
- Automatically aggregate fishing catch data and generate compliance reports
- Answer common pest and disease control questions (knowledge base chatbot)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize precision agriculture decision-making recommendations using AI remote sensing analysis and big data
- Simulate long-term environmental impacts of different planting/breeding strategies using AI models
- Real-time monitoring of weather and market data, dynamically adjusting advisor recommendations
- Automates data collection and initial analysis, allowing consultants to focus on farmer communication and customized solutions
- AI-assisted generation of visual reports to improve communication efficiency with farmers

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and long-term relationships with farmers
- On-site problem diagnosis based on local experience and intuition
- Handling complex, unique sustainability conflicts involving multiple stakeholders
- Adaptability to unstructured, low-information-density environments (e.g., remote farms)
- Cross-disciplinary integration (agriculture, ecology, economics) providing customized solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI tools (e.g., remote sensing analysis platforms, agricultural decision support systems)

- Precision agriculture technology (drones, sensors, IoT data analysis)

- Communication and change management skills (to promote adoption of new technologies among farmers)
- Continuous learning and industry knowledge updates (policy, market, biotechnology).

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are competitive and vary by experience and sector. Entry-level advisers may start around SGD 3,500–4,500 per month, while senior consultants or those in specialised agri-tech roles can earn significantly more. Precise figures depend on qualifications and employer.

**Q: Can a foreign Farming Adviser obtain an Employment Pass in Singapore?**
A: Yes, if they have a recognised degree and at least the minimum qualifying salary (SGD 5,000/month for fresh graduates). The COMPASS framework evaluates points for qualifications, salary, and other criteria. A job offer from a Singapore employer is mandatory.

**Q: What are the main career progression opportunities for this role in Singapore?**
A: Advisers can advance to senior policy roles in government (e.g., Singapore Food Agency), become R&D managers in agri-tech firms, or start independent consultancy. Specialisation in aquaculture or vertical farming can open doors to international projects.
