# Journalist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

Journalists in Singapore research, write, and report news across print, broadcast, and digital platforms. The industry is concentrated in mainstream media (Mediacorp, SPH Media) and digital-native outlets. Roles range from general reporters to specialist correspondents (business, politics, tech). The sector is competitive, with demand for multilingual and multimedia skills. Press freedom is constitutionally guaranteed but subject to legal constraints. Journalists must adhere to self-regulatory codes and licensing requirements for certain media activities.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,281. The outlook for journalists in Singapore is stable but evolving. Traditional media roles are declining due to digital disruption, while demand grows for content creators, digital journalists, and data-driven reporters. The government supports media talent development through initiatives like the Media Industry Digital Plan. However, competition is intense, and job growth is modest. Roles in corporate communications and content marketing may offer more opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior reporter or writer, advancing to senior reporter, editor, or specialist correspondent. Many move into digital content strategy, media management, or public relations. Freelancing and portfolio careers are common. Mid-career journalists may transition to corporate communications or academia. Continuous upskilling in data journalism, video production, and SEO is essential for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standard news briefs (e.g., financial reports, sports scores)
- Speech-to-text and automatic subtitle generation
- Fact-checking and data reconciliation (e.g., verifying statistical data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted data analysis and visualization (quickly identify trends)
- AI-recommended topics and audience preference prediction
- AI compiles multilingual information summaries

**Human moat:**

- Exclusive interviews and in-depth investigations (interpersonal insight and trust)
- Ethical judgment and sensitive issue gatekeeping
- Creative storytelling (features, commentary)

**Skills to build:**

- Data journalism (Python/R/data visualization)
- Multimedia production (video/podcast editing)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT-assisted writing)
- Fact-checking and information verification
- Audience Analysis and Digital Marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital Journalism
- Content Marketing
- Data Journalism
- Multimedia Storytelling

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for journalists in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level journalists can expect around SGD 30,000–45,000 annually, while experienced journalists earn SGD 50,000–80,000. Senior editors or specialist correspondents may earn over SGD 100,000. Actual salaries vary by outlet and experience.

**Q: What visa do foreign journalists need to work in Singapore?**
A: Foreign journalists typically require an Employment Pass (EP) if they earn above SGD 5,000/month and have a recognised degree. The employer sponsors the visa. Some mid-level roles may use an S Pass. Work permits are not typically used for journalism.

**Q: Is journalism a good career choice in Singapore?**
A: Journalism offers a dynamic career with opportunities to influence public discourse. However, the industry is competitive and transforming. Those with multimedia and digital skills have better prospects. Many journalists move into related fields like corporate communications.
