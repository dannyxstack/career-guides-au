# Policy and Planning Manager — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 1213 (ISCO08)**

Policy and planning managers oversee the development and implementation of strategic policies and plans within government agencies, statutory boards, or large corporations in Singapore. They analyze socio-economic trends, coordinate with stakeholders, and ensure alignment with national objectives such as smart nation initiatives, sustainable urban development, and economic competitiveness. This role requires strong analytical, leadership, and communication skills to manage complex projects and influence decision-making. Typically found in the public sector, these managers also operate in private firms dealing with regulatory affairs or corporate strategy.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a policy analyst or planner, advancing to senior or principal specialist roles, then to managerial positions. Many professionals pursue master's degrees in public policy, urban planning, or business administration to accelerate growth. Cross-sector moves between government and private consulting are common. Leadership pathways include deputy director, director, or chief policy officer in public service, or vice-president of corporate strategy in private firms. Continuous learning in data analytics and stakeholder engagement is essential for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically collect and organise policy-related data (e.g., economic indicators, regulatory changes)
- Generate initial policy impact analysis report and summary
- Quantified results of simulated policy options (e.g., budget impact model)
- Monitoring news and social media trends and generating public opinion briefs
- Format and submit compliance documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI tools to quickly compare policy frameworks across countries and identify best practices
- Analyze public feedback using natural language processing tools to optimize policy communication strategies
- Use predictive models to assess long-term socio-economic benefits of policies
- Leverage collaborative AI platforms to efficiently manage cross-departmental policy-making processes
- Use AI to generate multiple versions of policy drafts for leadership review

**Human moat:**

- Making balanced and value-based judgments amidst conflicting interests of multiple parties
- Build trust and conduct political negotiations with senior officials, the public, and others.
- Deep understanding of local culture, history, and power structures
- Assume ultimate responsibility in situations of uncertainty and ethical grey areas
- Proactive policy framework for innovative design rather than optimizing existing solutions

**Skills to build:**

- AI data analysis tools (e.g. Python/R, SQL, and automated reporting platforms)
- Policy modeling and simulation software (e.g., System Dynamics)
- Stakeholder management and conflict mediation skills
- AI ethics and bias recognition capabilities
- Digital governance and e-government platform operation
- Agile Project Management and Cross-Departmental Collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- urban planning
- sustainable development
- economic policy
- digital transformation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Policy and Planning Manager in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector (public vs private) and experience. For mid-career roles, annual compensation typically ranges from SGD 80,000 to 150,000, with senior positions exceeding SGD 200,000. Public sector salaries are generally lower but come with bonuses and benefits.

**Q: Can I obtain an Employment Pass (EP) for this role without employer sponsorship?**
A: No. The EP is employer-sponsored. You must first secure a job offer from a Singapore-based employer who will apply for the EP on your behalf. The application involves a points-based assessment (COMPASS) evaluating your qualifications, salary, and firm's diversity.

**Q: Do I need a master's degree to become a Policy and Planning Manager?**
A: Not strictly, but a master's in public policy, urban planning, or related field is highly advantageous, especially for competitive roles in government. Many professionals complete a master's after gaining some work experience to accelerate career progression.
