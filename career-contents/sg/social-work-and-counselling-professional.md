# Social Work and Counselling Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2635 (ISCO08)**

Social work and counselling professionals in Singapore support individuals, families, and communities in addressing social, emotional, and mental health challenges. They work in diverse settings such as government agencies, hospitals, schools, and voluntary welfare organisations (VWOs). With Singapore's ageing population and growing awareness of mental health, demand for these professionals is rising. They provide case management, counselling, crisis intervention, and community outreach. Professionals must be empathetic, resilient, and culturally sensitive, often requiring accreditation from local bodies like the Singapore Association of Social Workers (SASW) or the Singapore Association for Counselling (SAC).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a bachelor's degree in social work or counselling, followed by entry-level roles such as Assistant Social Worker or Counsellor. With experience, professionals can advance to Senior Social Worker, Therapist, or Team Leader. Specialisations include medical social work, school counselling, and rehabilitation. Further education (e.g., Master's) or accreditation (e.g., Registered Social Worker) enables upward mobility. Managerial roles in VWOs or government agencies offer long-term career paths.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a social worker or counsellor in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience, sector (public vs private), and qualifications. Entry-level positions at VWOs may start lower than government roles, but overall compensation is competitive and includes benefits. Specific figures are not provided here as they are subject to official surveys and market conditions.

**Q: Do I need employer sponsorship to work as a social worker or counsellor in Singapore?**
A: Yes, unless you are a Singapore citizen or permanent resident. Foreign professionals typically require an Employment Pass (EP) or S Pass, which must be sponsored by a registered employer. The employer applies on your behalf, and approval depends on meeting salary thresholds and COMPASS criteria.

**Q: What qualifications are required for this role in Singapore?**
A: A bachelor's degree in social work or counselling is typically required. For social workers, registration with the Social Work Accreditation Board (SWAB) is mandatory for many roles. Counsellors may pursue voluntary certification from the Singapore Association for Counselling (SAC).
