# Signwriter, Decorative Painter, Engraver and Etcher — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7316 (ISCO08)**

Signwriters, decorative painters, engravers and etchers create and apply lettering, designs, and decorative finishes on various surfaces such as glass, metal, wood, and walls. In Singapore, this trade is often found in small to medium enterprises specializing in signage, custom engraving, and interior decoration. These artisans work closely with clients in retail, hospitality, and events, blending traditional handcraft with modern digital techniques like CNC engraving and UV printing. The role requires precision, creativity, and knowledge of materials and safety procedures. While digital technology has automated some aspects, demand remains for bespoke, high-quality work in luxury retail, heritage buildings, and corporate events.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or junior in a signage or engraving workshop, gaining proficiency in hand lettering, stencil cutting, and machine operation. After 2-4 years, one can become a skilled craftsperson, taking on complex projects. Further advancement includes senior artisan or workshop supervisor. Many experienced practitioners eventually start their own business, often combining traditional skills with digital services. Additional certifications in areas like CNC operation or advanced painting techniques enhance prospects. Continuous learning and a strong portfolio are key to moving up.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a signwriter or engraver in Singapore?**
A: Salary varies based on experience, skills, and employer. Entry-level positions may start around $2,000 per month, while experienced artisans can earn $3,500 to $5,000. Those with specialized techniques or running their own business may earn more.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a signwriter in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but typically under an S Pass or Work Permit. The employer must sponsor the pass and meet MOM requirements including salary thresholds and quotas. Self-employment via an Employment Pass is rare as it requires higher qualifications and capital.
