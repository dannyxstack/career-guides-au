# Community Health Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 3253 (ISCO08)**

Community Health Workers (CHWs) in Singapore are frontline public health personnel who promote wellness, prevent disease, and provide basic health education in community settings such as senior activity centres, resident committees, and workplaces. They work under the supervision of healthcare professionals, conducting health screenings, counselling, and referral services. CHWs play a vital role in Singapore's preventive health strategy, especially in reaching underserved populations and supporting the national Healthier SG initiative. Their work bridges gaps between formal healthcare and community needs, focusing on chronic disease management, mental health awareness, and healthy lifestyle promotion.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for Community Health Workers typically starts with entry-level roles in outreach or health promotion. With experience and further training (e.g., WSQ certificate or diploma in community health), they can advance to Senior CHW, Community Health Coordinator, or Programme Executive. Some move into specialised areas like mental health, chronic disease management, or maternal and child health. Further education (e.g., degree in public health or social work) can lead to managerial or policy roles in government agencies or NGOs. The role is often used as a stepping stone to nursing, social work, or healthcare administration.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate health promotion materials and FAQ content
- using chatbots for initial health screening and appointment reminders
- Automatically record and organize customer follow-up records and basic data entry
- Generate personalized health action plan drafts based on standard protocols
- Automatically identify community health trends and generate reports via algorithms

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalized health advice: generate customized intervention plans based on resident health data
- Smart customer relationship management system: automatically flag high-risk families and remind follow-up
- Multilingual real-time translation: facilitating seamless communication with residents from different language backgrounds
- VR/AR simulation training: for health literacy education and behavior change skill practice
- Predictive analytics tools: identify potential health crises in communities and plan resources in advance.

**Human moat:**

- Build deep trust and empathetic communication
- Creative problem-solving in complex social situations.
- Cultural sensitivity assessment and localized intervention design
- Multi-agency coordination and advocacy for community resource integration
- Intuitive judgment and ethical decision-making in unstructured information

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use AI health tools (e.g., ChatGPT, AI assessment assistants)
- Enhance cross-cultural communication and community fundraising skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Ageing population

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with empathy and strong communication skills who enjoy interacting with diverse communities
- Those interested in public health, preventive care, and health education without needing clinical responsibilities
- People who are comfortable working in outreach settings such as community centres, homes, and workplaces

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking a clinical role with direct patient treatment or medical procedures
- Individuals who prefer a desk-based, administrative or non-people-facing job

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Community Health Worker in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level Community Health Workers earn around SGD 28,000 to SGD 36,000 per year. With experience and certification, salaries can rise to SGD 40,000 to SGD 50,000. Senior coordinators or those in managerial roles may earn SGD 55,000 or more. Salaries vary by employer (public vs. private) and qualifications.

**Q: Can foreigners work as Community Health Workers in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but it is not a highly migration-friendly occupation. Foreigners typically require an S Pass or Work Permit, sponsored by an employer. The S Pass has a minimum salary threshold, and Work Permits are restricted to certain source countries. Local candidates are usually preferred, so visa approval may be challenging unless the role is in a shortage area.
