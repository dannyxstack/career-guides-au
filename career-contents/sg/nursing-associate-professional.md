# Nursing Associate Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 3221 (ISCO08)**

Nursing associate professionals, commonly known as enrolled nurses in Singapore, provide basic nursing care under the supervision of registered nurses. They work in hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, and community care settings, supporting patient hygiene, monitoring vital signs, and administering medications. With an ageing population and expanding healthcare infrastructure, demand for these professionals remains strong. Entry typically requires a Nitec or diploma in nursing, followed by registration with the Singapore Nursing Board.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an enrolled nurse, with opportunities to advance to senior enrolled nurse roles after experience. Many pursue bridging programs to become registered nurses, leading to higher responsibilities and pay. Specialisation in areas like gerontology, community nursing, or mental health is also possible. Continuing education is encouraged, and skills upgrading is supported by employers and government grants.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a nursing associate professional in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by employer, experience, and qualifications, but they are generally competitive and include shift allowances. Entry-level enrolled nurses can expect a monthly salary that meets the S Pass minimum. Specific figures are not disclosed here.

**Q: Can foreign nursing associate professionals work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but they must obtain an S Pass or Work Permit sponsored by an employer. They also need to register with the Singapore Nursing Board, which may require passing a competency test or completing bridging training.

**Q: How can I progress from enrolled nurse to registered nurse?**
A: Enrolled nurses can upgrade through part-time diploma or degree programs in nursing, often supported by employers. After completing additional training and passing the SNB exam, they become Registered Nurses (RNs), unlocking higher pay and leadership roles.
