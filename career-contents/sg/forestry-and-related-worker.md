# Forestry and Related Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry and related workers in Singapore are primarily involved in arboriculture, tree planting, and maintenance of urban greenery, as the country has limited natural forests. They work for the National Parks Board (NParks), landscape companies, and private estates, performing tasks such as pruning, tree risk assessment, and pest management. The role is essential for Singapore's 'City in a Garden' vision, with a growing focus on biodiversity and climate resilience. While the occupation is small in scale, it supports the management of over 2 million trees in urban areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a general worker, with advancement to senior arborist or team leader after gaining certifications like the WSQ Advanced Certificate in Arboriculture. Experienced workers may move into supervisory roles or specialize in tree risk assessment or conservation. The field offers limited upward mobility due to Singapore's small land area, but skilled arborists are valued. Further training can lead to roles in landscape management or environmental consulting.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- urban arboriculture
- tree conservation
- biodiversity management
- climate resilience

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a forestry worker in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level workers can earn around $1,800 to $2,500 per month, while experienced arborists with certification may earn $3,000 to $4,000. Supervisors can earn up to $5,000.

**Q: Can a foreign forestry worker obtain an Employment Pass?**
A: Employment Pass is typically not issued for this occupation as it does not meet the professional or managerial criteria. The S Pass or Work Permit is more appropriate.

**Q: Is there a career progression path for forestry workers in Singapore?**
A: Yes, with experience and certifications like ISA Certified Arborist, workers can move into senior arborist roles, team leadership, or specialist positions in tree risk assessment.
