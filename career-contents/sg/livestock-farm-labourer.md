# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers in Singapore perform manual tasks such as feeding, watering, and cleaning livestock, as well as maintaining farm facilities. Due to land constraints, Singapore's agriculture sector is small but includes pig, poultry, and fish farms. Most livestock farms are located in Lim Chu Kang and Sungei Tengah. The role typically requires physical stamina and basic animal husbandry knowledge. Foreign workers may be employed under the Work Permit scheme, with quotas and levies imposed by the Ministry of Manpower. Local workers are limited, and the sector relies heavily on migrant labour.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for livestock farm labourers is typically limited. Some may advance to senior labourer or team leader roles with experience. Further training in farm management or agricultural technology could lead to positions such as farm supervisor or farm manager. However, upward mobility is constrained by the industry's small scale and reliance on manual work. Workers often remain in the same role for extended periods.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for livestock farm labourers are typically modest, often at or slightly above the minimum wage for foreign workers. Exact figures depend on the employer and include allowances. For more precise data, refer to the Ministry of Manpower's Occupational Wage Survey.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a livestock farm labourer in Singapore?**
A: Yes, under the Work Permit for Foreign Worker scheme. Employers must apply and meet quota and levy requirements. The role is low-skilled and does not lead to permanent residency directly.

**Q: Is there career advancement for livestock farm labourers?**
A: Limited advancement to senior labourer or supervisor roles. With additional training, some may move into farm management or agricultural technology. However, most labourers remain in their roles long-term.
