# Child Care Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 5311 (ISCO08)**

In Singapore, child care workers are employed in preschools and childcare centres regulated by the Early Childhood Development Agency (ECDA). They provide care, supervision, and early education for children from infancy to age 6, fostering developmental milestones through structured play and activities. The sector is expanding due to government initiatives to increase preschool capacity and quality, creating steady demand for trained professionals. Workers typically hold a Diploma in Early Childhood Care and Education or equivalent, with ongoing training required.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as an assistant teacher or child care worker, advancing to lead teacher, senior teacher, or centre supervisor with experience and further qualifications. Opportunities exist in curriculum development, early intervention, or centre management. The government offers scholarships and training grants to upgrade skills, but the career path may plateau without a degree or specialized certification. Many workers pursue part-time study to enhance their prospects.

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Singapore's main professional work pass is the Employment Pass, granted to qualifying professionals with a job offer above a salary threshold; long-term holders may apply for Permanent Residence. Criteria are set by the Ministry of Manpower and updated regularly.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a child care worker in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are moderate and vary by qualifications and experience. Entry-level workers with a diploma can expect a base salary typically between SGD 2,000 and SGD 3,500 per month, with annual increments and bonuses. Government subsidies and training grants may supplement income.

**Q: Can foreign child care workers obtain a work visa in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but options are limited. Most foreign workers enter on an S Pass or Work Permit, subject to quotas and levies. The employer must sponsor the visa, and the worker must meet minimum salary thresholds. Child care work is not on the skilled shortage list, so migration is considered restricted.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for child care workers?**
A: With experience and further education, child care workers can progress to lead teacher, centre supervisor, or curriculum specialist. Some transition into early intervention or educational consultancy. Government scholarships are available for degree programs.
