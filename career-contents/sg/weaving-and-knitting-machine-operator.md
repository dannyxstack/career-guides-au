# Weaving and Knitting Machine Operator — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 8152 (ISCO08)**

Weaving and knitting machine operators set up, operate, and monitor machines that weave or knit textile fabrics in Singapore's manufacturing sector. While the country's textile industry has largely shifted offshore, a niche remains for specialised high-value fabrics, technical textiles, and machine maintenance. Operators are employed mainly in smaller factories or in companies focused on industrial textiles, and the role requires attention to detail, mechanical aptitude, and the ability to work shifts. Due to automation, the demand for manual operators is declining, but opportunities exist for those with multi-skilling in machine programming and quality control.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for weaving and knitting machine operators typically starts with on-the-job training or a Certificate from the Institute of Technical Education (ITE). Experienced operators can advance to senior operator, team leader, or supervisor. With additional courses in textile technology or quality assurance, they may move into roles such as textile technician, production planner, or quality control inspector. Some operators transition to maintenance or engineering technician positions after acquiring relevant skills. The path is linear and requires continuous learning to adapt to new machinery.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically detects fabric defects and adjusts tension
- Automatically switch patterns and parameters based on production orders
- Remote diagnosis and automatic repair prompts for machine faults
- Automatic recording of production data and generation of summary reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-Assisted Optimization of Weaving Process Parameters to Improve Yield and Quality
- Using digital twin simulations to predict equipment wear and maintenance cycles
- Predictive maintenance using smart sensors and AI
- Use machine learning to analyze historical data and improve production planning

**Human moat:**

- Manual diagnosis and on-site repair of complex faults.
- Development and trial production of new fabrics or patterns
- Flexible scheduling for small-batch production of multiple varieties
- Communication with designers and clients, and understanding of custom requirements

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of industrial robots
- Basic programming (PLC, Python, C#)
- Data analysis and visualization tools (Tableau, Power BI)
- Digital twin and simulation software (AnyLogic, Simul8)
- Debugging smart sensors and IoT devices
- Basic English or Italian to read technical documentation

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Technical Textiles
- Quality Control
- Smart Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How is salary determined for weaving and knitting machine operators in Singapore?**
A: Salary is influenced by experience, company size, and sector (e.g., industrial textiles vs. apparel). Typically, entry-level operators earn lower base pay, but shift allowances and overtime can supplement income. Those with multi-skilling in machine programming or quality control may command higher wages.

**Q: What visa is typically required for foreign weaving and knitting machine operators?**
A: Most foreign operators come under the Work Permit (WP) scheme for semi-skilled workers in manufacturing. Employers must apply for quota and pay a levy. For those with a diploma or higher, the S Pass (SP) is possible if the salary threshold is met.

**Q: Is there career growth for machine operators in Singapore?**
A: Yes, operators can progress to senior operator, team leader, or supervisor with experience and further training. Some move into technical roles like machine programmer or quality assurance officer, especially after completing a WSQ Diploma in Textile Technology.
