# Optometrists and Ophthalmic Opticians — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2267 (ISCO08)**

Optometrists and ophthalmic opticians in Singapore deliver primary eye care, including vision assessments, prescription of corrective lenses, and detection of eye diseases. They work in optical retail chains, private clinics, hospitals, and community health settings. The profession is regulated by the Optometrists and Opticians Board (OOB), requiring registration and annual practising certificates. With high prevalence of myopia and an aging population, demand for eye care professionals remains strong. Technological advancements such as tele-optometry and advanced diagnostic tools are shaping the role.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Entry-level optometrists typically start in retail optical chains or hospital clinics, gaining experience in refraction and dispensing. With experience, they progress to senior roles, clinical specialists, or management positions. Some pursue further studies to open private practices or engage in research. Continuing professional development is mandatory for licence renewal. Specialisations in myopia management, geriatric eye care, or contact lenses are growing. Career progression may also include roles in academia or industry for optical product development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement
- AI analyzes corneal topography data to generate preliminary lens fitting plans.
- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Learning to operate digital optometric equipment and AI-assisted diagnostic systems
- Master the use of remote eye testing platforms
- Improve communication and customer management skills
- Learn basic data analysis to interpret AI reports
- Track new eye health technologies (e.g., OCT interpretation).
- Strengthen personalized eyewear fitting and problem-solving skills.

**Key growth areas:**

- myopia management
- elderly eye care
- tele-optometry
- contact lens specialization

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an optometrist in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and specialisation. Entry-level optometrists in retail chains earn competitive starting pay, while those in hospitals or with specialisations command higher incomes. For detailed figures, refer to the Ministry of Manpower's Occupational Wage Survey 2025.

**Q: Can foreign-trained optometrists work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, if they secure employer sponsorship for an Employment Pass. They must hold a recognized degree in optometry and meet the COMPASS framework criteria. The employer applies for the pass, and the candidate must be registered with the OOB.

**Q: What is the career progression for an optometrist in Singapore?**
A: Starting as an associate optometrist, one can advance to senior optometrist, clinic manager, or specialist (e.g., pediatric, contact lens). Some open private practices or transition into research or teaching. Continuous learning is key for advancement.
