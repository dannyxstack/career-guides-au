# Psychologist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2634 (ISCO08)**

Psychologists in Singapore assess, diagnose, and treat mental health conditions using evidence-based therapies, and also work in applied fields like organisational, educational, and forensic psychology. The profession is regulated by the Singapore Psychological Society (SPS) and the Singapore Register of Psychologists (SRP), with mandatory registration for clinical practice. Demand is rising due to growing mental health awareness and government initiatives, particularly in public healthcare and education sectors. Psychologists may work in hospitals, clinics, schools, private practice, or corporate settings. The role requires at least a recognised Master's or Doctoral degree, and supervised clinical experience is essential for registration. Foreign-trained psychologists need to meet SRP requirements and typically apply for an Employment Pass.

|---|---|---|---|
| Registered Psychologist with the Singapore Register of Psychologists (SRP) | Singapore Psychological Society (SPS) | Required | Mandatory for clinical practice. | Psychologist Registration under the Allied Health Professions Act (AHPA) | Ministry of Health (MOH) / Allied Health Professions Council | Optional | From 2024, psychologists in healthcare settings will need to register under AHPA. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically follows a path from associate psychologist or research assistant to fully registered psychologist, then senior psychologist, lead / clinical supervisor, and eventually head of department or private practice owner. Many psychologists specialise in areas such as clinical, educational, organisational, or forensic psychology. Continuing professional development is mandatory for registration renewal. Opportunities to move into academia, training, or consultancy exist. With experience, some psychologists transition into management roles in healthcare institutions or start their own private practice. The field is highly respected but requires significant self-funding of postgraduate education and supervision hours.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong empathy, patience, and cultural sensitivity to work with diverse populations in Singapore's multi-ethnic society.
- Those who enjoy analytical thinking, problem-solving, and ongoing learning to keep up with clinical research and interventions.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Do psychologists in Singapore earn high salaries?**
A: Salaries vary significantly by sector and experience. Early-career psychologists in public healthcare start around SGD 60,000–80,000 per year, while those in private practice or senior roles can earn more. However, high competition and self-funded training mean financial returns are not immediate. The profession is more attractive for its stability and societal impact.

**Q: Can foreign psychologists get an Employment Pass to work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, foreign psychologists can apply for an Employment Pass if they have a recognised degree and a job offer from a Singapore employer. The Ministry of Manpower assesses eligibility via the COMPASS framework, which awards bonus points for shortage occupations like psychologists. Registration with the Singapore Psychological Society and meeting SRP requirements are necessary for clinical roles.

**Q: What is the career progression for a psychologist in Singapore?**
A: Typically, after completing a Master's or Doctorate and gaining registration, psychologists start as associate psychologists. With 2–5 years of experience, they become senior psychologists, then lead or principal psychologists. Some move into management, private practice, or academia. Specialisation (e.g., clinical, educational, forensic) opens up niche opportunities.
