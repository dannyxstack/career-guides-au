# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners in Singapore maintain the cleanliness and appearance of windows on residential, commercial, and high-rise buildings. The role requires working at heights, using specialized equipment like safety harnesses, ladders, and water-fed poles. In Singapore's dense urban environment, window cleaning is a vital part of building maintenance, often contracted by property management firms. Jobs are typically held by local workers or foreign workers on Work Permits. Training focuses on workplace safety and handling of cleaning chemicals. The work is physically demanding and weather-dependent, but offers steady demand due to the city-state's numerous skyscrapers and emphasis on building aesthetics.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who are comfortable working at heights and have no fear of heights.
- Physically fit persons able to handle repetitive tasks and work outdoors in hot weather.
- Detail-oriented workers who take pride in a clean, streak-free finish.

**Who is not suited**
- People with a fear of heights or vertigo.
- Those who cannot tolerate prolonged physical activity or extreme weather conditions.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a window cleaner in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer. Local workers may earn a monthly salary between $2,000 and $3,000, while foreign workers on Work Permits typically receive lower wages. No exact figures are publicly standardized.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a window cleaner in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but only under a Work Permit (for semi-skilled workers) or occasionally an S Pass for higher-skilled roles. The employer must sponsor the visa, meet quota requirements, and pay a levy. No direct route to permanent residence exists through this occupation.

**Q: What are the main risks in this job?**
A: The primary risks include falls from heights, exposure to harsh chemicals, and heat stress. Proper safety equipment, training, and adherence to regulations are essential.
