# Tailor — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors in Singapore specialise in bespoke garment creation, alterations, and traditional ethnic wear such as cheongsam and kebaya. The trade serves a niche market of high-end clients, weddings, and corporate uniforms. With a strong heritage in textile crafts, Singapore's tailoring sector is small but resilient, supported by a demand for quality alterations and custom designs. However, mass-produced clothing dominates the retail market, limiting large-scale employment. Tailoring is a skilled trade that requires precision, creativity, and an understanding of fabrics and customer preferences.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for tailors in Singapore typically starts as an apprentice or sewing machinist, advancing to senior tailor, master tailor, or shop owner. With experience, tailors can specialise in bridal wear, luxury bespoke, or costume design for theatre and film. Some transition into fashion design or teaching at institutes like ITE or NAFA. The field values hands-on skills and a strong portfolio, but formal qualifications can accelerate advancement. Entrepreneurship is common, with many tailors eventually opening their own ateliers.

- Uses AI clothing design software to automatically generate basic patterns

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design tools to quickly generate personalized patterns and styles
- Use 3D modeling software for virtual sample display and client communication

**Human moat:**

- Creative design and impromptu modification skills in handmade haute couture
- Fine handling and restoration of complex fabrics (e.g., leather, fur)
- Build trust with clients and understand personalized needs
- Artistry of traditional handicraft techniques (e.g., embroidery, beadwork)
- Deep understanding and ability to reconstruct clothing history and culture.

**Skills to build:**

- Knowledge of sustainable fashion and eco-friendly materials

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tailor in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for tailors in Singapore vary widely by experience and specialization. Entry-level sewing machinists earn modest wages, while experienced master tailors in bespoke ateliers can earn a significant premium. Alterations workers typically receive lower pay than custom designers. Overall, the field offers moderate earning potential, with top professionals commanding higher rates.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign tailors in Singapore?**
A: Most foreign tailors enter on a Work Permit for lower-skilled roles like sewing or alterations. Skilled tailors with relevant qualifications (e.g., diploma) may qualify for an S Pass. Employment Pass is rare and reserved for highly specialised positions, such as master tailors for luxury brands, evaluated under the COMPASS framework.

**Q: Is tailoring a viable career in Singapore?**
A: Tailoring can be a viable niche career, especially for those specializing in bespoke or ethnic wear. The market is small but has a loyal clientele. Success often depends on building a reputation and client base. While formal training is not mandatory, it can help. Career growth is limited but stable, with opportunities for entrepreneurship.
