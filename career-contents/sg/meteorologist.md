# Meteorologist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2112 (ISCO08)**

Meteorologists in Singapore analyze atmospheric data to forecast weather and study climate patterns, primarily employed by the Meteorological Service Singapore (MSS) under the government, as well as in aviation, maritime, and research sectors. The field is highly specialized, with a small but stable labour market driven by public safety, climate change adaptation, and digital weather modelling demands.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a junior forecaster, advancing to senior meteorologist or research scientist roles. Those with postgraduate qualifications (MSc/PhD) can move into specialized areas such as climate modelling or environmental consulting. Management positions in weather service departments or academia are possible. Cross-sector mobility into data analytics or environmental policy is increasingly common, driven by digitalization and sustainability trends.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of routine weather forecast text
- Compilation and preliminary statistical analysis of historical meteorological data
- Standard warning content editing based on numerical model output
- Simple fault diagnosis and standardized reporting of meteorological observation equipment
- Write descriptive reports on basic climate trends

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Interpretation and Probability Calibration of Ensemble Forecasts Using AI
- Combine deep learning to predict local extreme weather (e.g., thunderstorms, heavy rain).
- AI-assisted climate change attribution analysis and scenario simulation
- Automatically generate customized industry weather reports (agriculture, energy).
- Using natural language processing to automatically extract meteorological research progress from literature

**Human moat:**

- Physical intuition and interdisciplinary understanding of complex weather systems
- Deliver authoritative advice to the public and decision-makers during high-impact weather events
- Designing novel observation experiments and scientific methods for integrating multi-source data
- Assume legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., consequences of failing to issue early warnings)
- Collaborative decision-making with related fields (hydrology, ecology)

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch)
- Principles and applications of weather AI models (e.g., FourCastNet, GraphCast)
- Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification techniques
- Climate big data visualization and interactive dashboard development
- Cross-disciplinary Skills (Economics, Risk Management, Public Policy)
- AI ethics and social impact assessment of weather warnings

**Key growth areas:**

- Climate Resilience
- Digital Weather Modelling
- Urban Weather Monitoring
- Aviation Meteorology

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong analytical and mathematical skills, passionate about weather and climate.
- Those comfortable working with large datasets and computer models in a methodical environment.
- Career seekers interested in government or research settings, contributing to public safety and environmental policy.

**Who is not suited**
- People who dislike repetitive data analysis or working under time pressure for forecasts.
- Those seeking fast-paced commercial roles with rapid career advancement; meteorology is a slow-moving field.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What factors influence a meteorologist's salary in Singapore?**
A: Salaries depend on qualifications (degree vs. postgraduate), experience, and employer (government vs. private). Government roles at MSS offer competitive pay with bonuses, while private aviation or consulting sectors may vary. Specialist skills in climate modelling or data science can command higher pay. Note that entry-level positions typically start lower and increase with seniority.

**Q: Can a foreign meteorologist get a work visa in Singapore?**
A: Yes, typically via an Employment Pass sponsored by an employer like MSS or a private company. The role is considered skilled, so EP is the most common route. Applicants must meet the COMPASS framework's points (based on salary, qualifications, and diversity). S Pass is possible for less experienced candidates but subject to quotas and levies.
