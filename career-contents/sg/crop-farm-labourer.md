# Crop Farm Labourer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9211 (ISCO08)**

Crop farm labourers in Singapore perform manual agricultural tasks such as planting, cultivating, harvesting, and packing crops. This occupation is predominantly filled by foreign workers on Work Permits, as local workforce participation is minimal due to low wages and physically demanding conditions. The role is concentrated in Singapore's remaining farmland areas and increasingly in high-tech vertical farms. Labourers work under supervisors, using basic tools and machinery. The work is seasonal and weather-dependent, with typical hours including early starts and occasional overtime. Employers must adhere to MOM regulations for accommodation and rest days. The sector faces challenges from urban development and import reliance, yet agri-tech initiatives aim to boost local production.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for crop farm labourers in Singapore is limited. Most workers remain at the same level, with rare advancement to supervisory roles such as team leader or farm supervisor. To move up, workers may need to gain skills in machinery operation, basic agronomy, or farm management, but formal training is not widely available. Some foreign workers may transition to other manual jobs in construction or cleaning after completing contracts. Local workers typically do not enter this role; those who do may find pathways into agri-tech positions, such as hydroponic technicians, through upskilling. Overall, it is not a career-oriented occupation in Singapore.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual seeding and transplanting: replaced by automated seeders and transplanting robots
- Weed control and pest monitoring: AI visual recognition combined with precision spraying robots.
- Routine irrigation operations: sensors and smart irrigation systems automatically regulate
- Crop maturity detection and harvesting: computer vision and robotic arms automatically assess and pick
- Field data recording: automatic collection and upload by IoT sensors

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining complex agricultural machinery: AI-assisted diagnostics and remote guidance improve efficiency
- Precision agriculture data analysis: using AI to optimize planting density and fertilization timing
- Crop health monitoring: AI-assisted early disease detection and intervention suggestions
- Quality grading and packaging: AI vision systems assist manual sorting with high speed and precision

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making ability for sudden anomalies (e.g., extreme weather, equipment failure)
- Flexible operation in complex terrain and multi-crop mixed planting environments
- Interpersonal collaboration and communication with farmers and technology suppliers
- Quality assessment based on local experience (e.g., optimal harvest window)

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural machinery operation and basic maintenance.
- Use of precision agriculture tools (e.g., GPS navigation, drones)
- Data analysis and agricultural software application
- Basic programming and automation system configuration
- Read technical documentation in English or Italian
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban Farming
- Agri-Tech
- Hydroponics
- Sustainability

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a crop farm labourer in Singapore?**
A: Crop farm labourers in Singapore earn a basic salary of approximately SGD 1,200 to SGD 1,800 per month, depending on experience and employer. This may include overtime pay and accommodation allowances. Wages are among the lowest in Singapore due to reliance on foreign labour.

**Q: Can a crop farm labourer get permanent residency in Singapore?**
A: No, the Work Permit route does not lead to permanent residency. Crop farm labourers are not eligible for Employment Pass or S Pass, which are required for PR applications. They must return to their home country after their work permit expires.

**Q: What is the typical work schedule for a crop farm labourer?**
A: Work usually starts early morning (e.g., 7am) and ends in the afternoon, with breaks. Hours can vary seasonally, and additional overtime may be required during harvest periods. Six-day work weeks are common, with one rest day per week.
