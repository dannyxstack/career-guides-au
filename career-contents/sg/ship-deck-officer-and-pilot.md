# Ship's Deck Officer and Pilot — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 3152 (ISCO08)**

Ships' Deck Officers and Pilots in Singapore are central to one of the world's busiest ports, responsible for safe navigation, mooring, and cargo operations. The role demands rigorous STCW certification from the Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA). With 90% of global trade passing through Singaporean waters, demand remains robust. Officers progress through ranks from cadet to master, while pilots specialize in bringing vessels into port. Local training at Singapore Maritime Academy and the Nautical Institute provides a strong foundation. The industry is adapting to digital navigation, autonomous shipping, and green maritime practices, offering evolving career opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression follows a structured path: Deck Cadet → Third Officer → Second Officer → Chief Officer → Master. Harbour pilots undergo additional rigorous training and examinations. Many officers transition to shore-based roles in maritime administration, training, or marine insurance after years at sea. Continuous upgrading of STCW certificates is mandatory, and specialization in areas like liquefied natural gas (LNG) tankers or offshore support vessels can accelerate advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data integration and collision avoidance computation
- Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS) automatic route planning and updates
- Repetitive data collection and analysis in weather route optimization
- Automated recording and report generation of routine navigation logs
- Automatic interpretation of sensor data in ship performance monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Combine AI weather models and real-time ocean data to make more accurate route adjustment decisions
- Use digital twins and predictive maintenance systems to identify equipment issues early
- Optimizes workforce and fatigue management through AI-assisted crew scheduling systems
- Computer vision-based berthing and anchoring operation assistance
- AI-generated emergency scenario simulations for training and crisis decision-making rehearsals

**Human moat:**

- Take ultimate decision-making responsibility in emergencies (e.g., engine failure, man overboard)
- Statutory qualifications and experience required by international maritime regulations (SOLAS, STCW)
- Cross-cultural crew management and interpersonal communication (multi-national team collaboration on board)
- Manual control and situational judgment in complex port environments (e.g., tugboat coordination)
- Long-term experience (intuitive judgment) with hulls, machinery, and marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced configuration and troubleshooting of electronic charts and autopilot systems
- Basic data analysis (Python/R) for interpreting ship sensor data
- Digital twin and predictive maintenance software operation
- Maritime cybersecurity knowledge (to counter onboard system attacks)
- Advanced English Communication and Cross-Cultural Leadership
- Use of fatigue management and compliance software (e.g., rest time monitoring)

**Key growth areas:**

- autonomous shipping
- green maritime
- digital navigation
- offshore wind

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deck officer in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and experience. Entry-level deck officers (Third Officer) earn around SGD 4,000-6,000 per month, while Chief Officers or Masters can earn SGD 8,000-12,000 per month. Harbour pilots may earn significantly higher due to specialized skills.

**Q: Can foreign deck officers work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, via employer-sponsored Employment Pass for experienced officers with a valid COC. COMPASS points system applies, and job must be advertised to locals first.

**Q: What is the career progression like?**
A: Typically: Deck Cadet → Third Officer → Second Officer → Chief Officer → Master. Harbour pilots require additional exams. Many move to shore-based roles in maritime services.
