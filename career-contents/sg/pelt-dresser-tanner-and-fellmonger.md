# Pelt Dresser, Tanner and Fellmonger — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7535 (ISCO08)**

Pelt dressers, tanners and fellmongers process animal hides and skins into leather or fur. In Singapore, this occupation is extremely niche due to the absence of a large-scale tanning industry; most leather is imported. A handful of artisanal tanneries and leather craft workshops exist, but employment opportunities are very limited. Workers typically handle raw hides, use chemical or vegetable tanning methods, and produce finished leather for small-scale boutique products. The role requires manual skill and knowledge of traditional tanning techniques.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression is extremely narrow in Singapore. Most practitioners work as sole artisans or in micro-enterprises. There are no structured career ladders; advancement typically means mastering advanced techniques (e.g., vegetable tanning, dyeing) or diversifying into leather product making (bags, shoes). Some may teach at craft schools or supply high-end custom leather to luxury brands. Formal training is limited to short courses at institutions like ITE or private craft schools. The path is entrepreneurial rather than corporate, with most earnings coming from bespoke orders.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Hide sorting and initial cleaning: automated sorting based on visual AI and robotic arms
- Tanning solution ratio and timing control: automated system precise dosing and pH adjustment
- Mechanical shaving and buffing: CNC equipment automatically performs standard thickness processing.
- Humidity and temperature monitoring: sensors and AI continuously optimize the drying process
- Surface defect detection: computer vision replaces manual visual inspection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex skin texture assessment and restoration decisions: AI-assisted identification of imperfections and suggestion of refinements.
- Optimization of eco-friendly tanning formulas: AI simulates the environmental impact and effectiveness of different formulas
- Predictive maintenance of equipment: IoT+AI predicts failures, reducing downtime
- Customized order scheduling: AI automatically schedules based on inventory, delivery dates, and process parameters.
- Training and remote guidance: AR+AI provides real-time operational instructions for beginners

**Human moat:**

- Manual assessment of precious hides and special texture treatments
- Traditional tanning techniques and unique recipe inheritance
- Creative realization and communication of customized customer needs
- Cross-process coordination and quality management decision-making
- Ability to handle non-standard exceptional items with manual finishing

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and programming of industrial robots and automation control systems
- Use of data analytics and AI-assisted quality management systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and green tanning processes
- Basics of leather material science and understanding of new bio-based tanning agents
- Application of digital twin and production line simulation software
- Cross-cultural communication and supply chain management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Artisanal Leathercraft
- Sustainable Tanning
- Small-Batch Production
- Heritage Crafts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tanner in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are low due to the niche market. Entry-level artisans may earn around SGD 24,000–36,000 annually. Experienced master tanners with a client base can earn SGD 48,000–60,000, but such positions are rare.

**Q: Can foreigners work as tanners in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but only via the Work Permit for semi-skilled workers, subject to quota and levy. The occupation is not on the list for Employment Pass or S Pass, so skilled professionals would need a different job title. Employer sponsorship is required.
