# Motor Vehicle Mechanic and Repairer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7231 (ISCO08)**

Motor Vehicle Mechanics and Repairers in Singapore are skilled tradespeople responsible for diagnosing, repairing, and maintaining cars, vans, and light commercial vehicles. With over 600,000 vehicles on the road, the demand for mechanics is steady, though the industry is evolving towards hybrid and electric vehicles (EVs). Most mechanics work in authorized dealerships, independent workshops, or fleet maintenance centers. The role requires physical dexterity, problem-solving skills, and increasingly, digital diagnostic proficiency. Singapore's Ministry of Manpower regulates foreign worker access via Work Permits for semi-skilled roles, with local training through the Institute of Technical Education (ITE) providing a pipeline of certified mechanics.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 2,528. The employment outlook for motor vehicle mechanics in Singapore is stable but shifting. While traditional combustion engine repairs will remain in demand for years, the growth of electric vehicles (EVs) – targeted to constitute 50% of new car registrations by 2030 – is reshaping skill requirements. Mechanics will need upskilling in high-voltage systems and electronic diagnostics. The government's push for green transport may increase demand for specialized EV technicians, but overall headcount growth may be moderate due to automation in diagnostics. Job security remains good for those who adapt, with retrenchment risks low in the essential mobility sector.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from an apprentice or junior mechanic, advancing to senior mechanic, then team leader or workshop supervisor. Further specialization can occur in areas like air-conditioning, transmission, or engine overhaul. With additional certification, mechanics can move into EV servicing, diagnostic specialist roles, or technical training. Some pursue diplomas or degrees in automotive engineering to move into management positions in dealerships or service centers. The Singapore Workforce Skills Qualifications (WSQ) framework offers modular training for continuous upgrading. Entrepreneurship via owning a workshop is also a common long-term goal for experienced mechanics.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use diagnostic tools to read standard fault codes and provide common solutions
- Automatically generate standard maintenance work orders and parts lists from repair database.
- Automatically generate routine maintenance reminder reports from vehicle sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI diagnostic systems to quickly locate complex electrical or mechanical faults
- Overlay maintenance steps and component disassembly instructions via AR glasses
- Use predictive maintenance algorithms to detect potential vehicle issues early
- Using natural language processing tools to automatically record maintenance logs

**Human moat:**

- Creative troubleshooting and on-site debugging of non-standard faults
- Fine disassembly and calibration requiring tactile feedback
- Comprehensive vehicle condition assessment and repair plan decision-making
- Customer trust communication and personalized service.

**Skills to build:**

- Mastery of AI diagnostic tools (e.g., Dealer-Fix, TEXA IDC5)
- Learning hybrid/electric vehicle high-voltage system repair
- Familiar with in-vehicle network diagnostics and software update processes
- Practice using AR-assisted maintenance equipment
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn the basics of data analysis and predictive maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- Electric Vehicles
- Hybrid Vehicles
- Diagnostic Technology
- Aftermarket Services

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a motor vehicle mechanic in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, qualifications, and employer. Entry-level mechanics may earn around SGD 2,000–2,500 per month, while experienced senior mechanics can earn SGD 3,500–4,500. Specialized EV technicians often command a premium. Refer to the Ministry of Manpower's Occupational Wage Survey for the latest figures.

**Q: Can a foreign mechanic work in Singapore? What visa do they need?**
A: Yes, foreign mechanics can work in Singapore if sponsored by an employer. Most enter under a Work Permit, which is for semi-skilled workers from approved source countries and is subject to quota and levy. For those with higher qualifications, an S Pass may be possible. There is no direct path to permanent residency from these permits, but long-term employment can lead to eligibility for PR application.

**Q: What are the prospects for advancement in this career in Singapore?**
A: Mechanics can progress to senior positions, supervisory roles, or specialize in areas like diagnostics or EVs. With further education, some move into engineering or management. The shift to EVs offers new opportunities, but ongoing training is essential.
