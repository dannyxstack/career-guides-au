# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Singapore primarily refer to practitioners of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), including physicians and acupuncturists. They diagnose, treat, and prevent illnesses using holistic approaches such as herbal medicine, acupuncture, and therapeutic massage. In Singapore, TCM is regulated by the Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners Board under the Ministry of Health, requiring practitioners to be registered. The profession enjoys growing recognition as a complementary healthcare option, with increasing integration into mainstream healthcare settings. Professionals may work in TCM clinics, hospitals, or private practice, and are expected to adhere to strict ethical and safety standards.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Traditional Chinese Medicine Physician in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level TCM physicians can expect an annual salary of around SGD 40,000 to SGD 60,000, while experienced practitioners may earn SGD 70,000 to SGD 100,000 or more, especially if they own a clinic or have a strong patient base. Salaries vary by employer, location, and specialization.

**Q: Can foreign TCM professionals obtain a work visa in Singapore?**
A: Yes, foreign TCM professionals can apply for an Employment Pass if they have a recognized degree and meet the salary threshold (at least SGD 5,000 monthly). They must also be registered with the Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners Board. The COMPASS framework assesses points for skills, diversity, and support for local employment.

**Q: Is TCM practice regulated in Singapore?**
A: Yes, TCM practice is regulated by the Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners Board (TCMPB) under the Ministry of Health. All TCM physicians and acupuncturists must be registered and adhere to the TCM Practitioners Act. The board oversees qualifications, ethics, and continuing education.
