# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers in Singapore are essential for constructing and repairing the structural framework of buildings and other structures. This includes tasks such as formwork carpentry, steel fixing, concreting, and scaffolding. The role is physically demanding and requires technical skills and adherence to safety standards. Workers are often employed by construction firms and contractors, and the demand is driven by ongoing infrastructure and residential projects. Due to labour shortages, many workers are foreign nationals holding Work Permits or S Passes, with opportunities for upskilling through certifications in areas like green building and digital construction.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from a general construction labourer or trade assistant, advancing to a skilled tradesperson (e.g., formwork carpenter) after gaining experience and certifications. With supervisory skills and safety training, workers can become foremen or site supervisors. Further education (e.g., construction management diplomas) opens paths to project coordinator or site manager roles. Singapore's SkillsFuture initiative supports upskilling in areas like green building and digital construction, which are increasingly valued. Many workers also transition into specialised trades like precast installation or scaffolding inspection.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- Prefabrication
- Green Building
- Digital Construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a building frame trades worker in Singapore?**
A: Semi-skilled workers (Work Permit) earn between SGD 1,500 and SGD 2,500 per month, while experienced or supervisory roles (S Pass) can range from SGD 3,000 to SGD 4,500. Overtime and bonuses can add 10–30%.

**Q: Can a foreign worker get a Work Permit for construction trades, and are there age limits?**
A: Yes, Work Permits are available from approved source countries (e.g., Malaysia, China, India). Age limit: 18-50 (up to 58 for some nationalities). Employer must apply. No direct route to PR, but S Pass holders may eventually apply for PR after 6 months.

**Q: What mandatory training is required before starting work?**
A: All construction workers must complete the Safety Orientation Course (SOC) and obtain a Green Card (valid 2 years). For certain trades, additional certifications like scaffolding or formwork are required. Training costs are often borne by employers.
