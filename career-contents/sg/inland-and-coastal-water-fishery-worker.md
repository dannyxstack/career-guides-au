# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Singapore are primarily employed in aquaculture farms, offshore fish farms, and coastal fishing operations. The industry is small-scale due to limited natural fishing grounds and heavy reliance on imports. Workers engage in fish breeding, feeding, harvesting, and maintenance of nets and equipment. With the government's push for food security and self-sufficiency under the '30 by 30' goal, there is growing emphasis on aquaculture technology and sustainable practices, though overall employment in traditional fishing remains modest.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Singapore's fisheries sector is limited for entry-level workers; they may advance to senior farm hands or supervisors with experience. However, the sector is not a typical long-term career path due to its size and low wages. Those with training in aquaculture technology or marine biology can move into management or R&D roles in larger fish farms. Many workers exit to other manual trades after a few years. Skills training through Workforce Singapore (WSG) is available but not widely pursued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture
- Sustainable Fishing
- Fish Processing
- Food Security

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery worker in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for entry-level fishery workers are generally low, typically ranging from SGD 1,500 to SGD 2,000 per month for local workers. Foreign workers on Work Permits earn less, often around SGD 1,200–1,800 per month, including basic accommodation. The occupation is not high-paying compared to other manual trades.

**Q: Can foreign workers get an Employment Pass as a fishery worker?**
A: No, the Employment Pass is not applicable for this occupation due to the low salary and skill level. Foreigners are hired via Work Permit (semi-skilled) or, rarely, an S Pass if they have a diploma and earn at least SGD 3,000 per month, though such cases are uncommon.

**Q: Is there a career path for fishery workers in Singapore?**
A: Career progression is limited; workers may become senior farm hands or shift into aquaculture technician roles. The industry is small and not a typical long-term career. Many workers move to other sectors after a few years.
