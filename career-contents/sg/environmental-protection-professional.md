# Environmental Protection Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2133 (ISCO08)**

Environmental protection professionals in Singapore develop and implement policies, programmes, and systems to prevent or remediate harm to the environment. They work across government agencies (e.g., National Environment Agency), consultancy firms, and corporate sustainability departments. The role includes environmental impact assessments, pollution control, waste management, and climate adaptation planning. Singapore's Green Plan 2030 and net-zero targets drive strong demand for these professionals, particularly in carbon management, circular economy, and environmental compliance. The occupation falls under SSOC 2024 code 2133 and is typically held by degree holders in environmental science, engineering, or related fields.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an environmental protection professional in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for environmental protection professionals vary by experience and sector. Entry-level roles (0-3 years) range from SGD 36,000 to SGD 50,000 annually. Mid-career (5-10 years) can expect SGD 55,000 to SGD 80,000, while senior roles (10+ years) may exceed SGD 100,000. Government and consultancy roles may have additional benefits and bonuses. These figures are pre-tax and based on MOM data; actual offers depend on qualifications and COMPASS outcomes.

**Q: Can I get an Employment Pass as an environmental protection professional?**
A: Yes, provided you meet the base salary threshold (SGD 5,000/month) and score sufficient points under the COMPASS framework. Points are awarded for qualifications (e.g., bachelor's degree), skills (shortage occupations earn extra points), diversity (nationality), and support for strategic economic priorities (e.g., green economy). If you have a relevant degree and at least 2-3 years of experience, an EP is feasible. If your salary is lower, an S Pass might be considered, but quotas apply.

**Q: What are the key growth areas for this role in Singapore?**
A: Key growth areas include carbon management (carbon footprint assessment, offsetting), circular economy (waste-to-resource, recycling technology), environmental data analytics (AI for climate modelling), and ESG compliance (reporting frameworks like GRI, TCFD). The Green Plan 2030 also drives demand in green building certification and renewable energy consultancy.
