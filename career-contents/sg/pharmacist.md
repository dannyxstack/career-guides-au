# Pharmacist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2262 (ISCO08)**

Pharmacists in Singapore are healthcare professionals responsible for dispensing medications, advising on drug therapies, and ensuring safe and effective use of pharmaceuticals. They work in hospitals, community pharmacies, retail chains, and the pharmaceutical industry. Registration with the Singapore Pharmacy Council (SPC) is mandatory, requiring a recognized degree and passing the Qualifying Examination. Singapore's ageing population and high prevalence of chronic diseases drive steady demand.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very high (★★★★½).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a staff pharmacist in a hospital or community pharmacy, advancing to senior pharmacist, clinical specialist, or pharmacy manager. With additional qualifications, pharmacists can move into regulatory roles, academia, or the pharmaceutical industry (e.g., drug safety, medical affairs). The Singapore government supports upskilling through initiatives like the Pharmacist Professional Development Framework. Specialisations in geriatrics, oncology, or infectious diseases are increasingly valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a pharmacist in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Fresh graduates in public hospitals earn about SGD 4,000–5,000/month, while experienced pharmacists in retail or industry can earn SGD 6,000–10,000/month or more. Refer to MOM's Occupational Wage Survey for precise figures.

**Q: Can a foreign pharmacist work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but must obtain an Employment Pass (EP) sponsored by a Singapore employer. Pharmacists on the Shortage Occupation List may receive additional COMPASS points. Registration with SPC is mandatory and requires a recognised degree and passing the Qualifying Examination.

**Q: Is it possible to become a permanent resident (PR) as a pharmacist?**
A: Yes, pharmacists on an EP can apply for PR through the Professionals/Technical Personnel & Skilled Workers Scheme. However, PR is not points-based; it is assessed on factors like salary, duration of stay, and integration. There is no direct migration route without employer sponsorship.
