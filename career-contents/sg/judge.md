# Judge — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2612 (ISCO08)**

Judges preside over court proceedings, interpret laws, and deliver verdicts in Singapore's judiciary. They are appointed from among senior legal professionals with extensive experience, typically requiring at least 10-15 years of practice and admission to the Singapore Bar. The role demands impartiality, legal expertise, and strong ethical standards. Judges handle civil, criminal, and family cases in the Supreme Court, State Courts, or Family Justice Courts. Appointments are made by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister and the Chief Justice.

The judiciary has modernised with e-litigation and AI-assisted case management, but the number of judges is fixed by statute. Opportunities are rare and highly competitive, typically reserved for senior lawyers or judicial officers. No significant growth is expected, but retiring judges create openings. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a Judicial Commissioner or District Judge, with advancement to High Court Judge, then Court of Appeal Judge. Candidates usually have a distinguished legal career and may serve as senior counsel or in the Legal Service. Specialisation in specific areas like family, commercial, or constitutional law is common. Leadership roles include Chief Justice or Supreme Court Justices. The path is non-linear and merit-based, with lifetime appointments until retirement age of 65 or 70.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for a judge in Singapore?**
A: Judges' salaries are not publicly disclosed but are known to be among the highest in the civil service. A High Court judge earns approximately SGD 300,000 to SGD 500,000 annually, while the Chief Justice's salary is higher and set by Parliament.

**Q: Can a foreign lawyer become a judge in Singapore?**
A: No, judges must be Singapore citizens. Foreign legal professionals cannot be appointed as judges. The role is reserved for citizens or, in rare cases, permanent residents with exceptional qualifications.

**Q: How long does it take to become a judge in Singapore?**
A: Typically, candidates require at least 10-15 years of post-qualification legal experience. Many judges are appointed in their 40s or 50s after a distinguished career as lawyers or judicial commissioners.
