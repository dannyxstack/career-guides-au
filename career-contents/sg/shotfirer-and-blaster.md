# Shotfirer and Blaster — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters handle the controlled use of explosives in construction, demolition, quarrying, and tunnelling projects. In Singapore, this occupation is highly regulated by the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) and the Explosives and Precursors Unit. Professionals must obtain a valid blaster's licence and complete mandatory safety training. The role demands meticulous planning, adherence to strict safety protocols, and coordination with engineers and site supervisors. Due to Singapore's limited natural mining, work primarily involves urban infrastructure development, such as underground MRT tunnels and building demolitions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a blaster assistant or trainee under a licensed mentor. After obtaining a blaster's licence (valid for 1–3 years, renewable), individuals can advance to senior blaster or blasting supervisor roles. Additional certifications in explosives storage, transport, or safety management open paths to consultancy or training. Some transition into demolition project management or civil engineering. Singapore's strict licensing ensures that experienced blasters are valued, but the career ladder is short; few reach executive levels without cross-training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- underground tunnelling
- infrastructure

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for shotfirers and blasters in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, certification, and project scale. Entry-level assistants may earn around $2,000–$3,000 per month, while licensed blasters with several years of experience can earn $4,000–$6,000 per month. Senior blasters or supervisors on major infrastructure projects may command higher pay, but official salary data is not publicly detailed due to the niche nature of the occupation.

**Q: Can a foreign shotfirer or blaster obtain a work visa for Singapore?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Foreign blasters typically require an S Pass (minimum salary ~$3,000/month) or Work Permit (construction sector) sponsored by a Singapore employer. Additionally, they must obtain a local Blaster's Licence, which may require passing exams and meeting competency standards set by the Ministry of Home Affairs. Employers often prefer to hire locally licensed candidates, so migration is possible but not straightforward.

**Q: What qualifications are mandatory to work as a blaster in Singapore?**
A: The key mandatory qualification is a valid Blaster's Licence issued by the Singapore Police Force (Explosives and Precursors Unit). This requires completing a recognised blasting safety course and passing a written and practical exam. Additionally, employers may require a Workplace Safety and Health (WSH) certificate or BCA safety induction. All licences are renewable and subject to background checks.
