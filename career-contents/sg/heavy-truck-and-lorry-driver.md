# Heavy Truck And Lorry Driver — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 8332 (ISCO08)**

Heavy truck and lorry drivers in Singapore operate large goods vehicles (Class 4 or 5) to transport materials, goods, and equipment across the island. They are essential to the logistics, construction, and manufacturing sectors, with demand driven by port activities, e-commerce, and urban development. Most drivers are local or hold Work Permits, as the role is classified as semi-skilled under MOM guidelines. The job requires a valid vocational driving license and often involves long hours, including night or weekend shifts. While automation and autonomous trucks pose future challenges, current demand remains steady for experienced drivers in last-mile and heavy haulage.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 15,640. Employment outlook for heavy truck drivers in Singapore is stable but slowly evolving. E-commerce growth and construction projects sustain demand, while port and warehouse logistics provide steady opportunities. However, the sector faces increasing automation (e.g., autonomous truck trials) and stricter emission regulations, which may reduce demand for traditional drivers over the next decade. The shift toward electric and smarter fleets will require retraining, but driver shortages persist due to aging workforce and low recruitment of younger locals. Foreign workers under Work Permits remain common, though quotas and levies curb reliance on them. Overall, the occupation is not expected to grow rapidly, but replacement demand will provide openings.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for heavy truck drivers typically starts with a Class 3 license then upgrading to Class 4 or 5. Experienced drivers can move into supervisory roles (e.g., fleet supervisor, logistics coordinator) or specialized transport (e.g., hazardous materials, oversized loads). Some become owner-operators with their own vehicles, contracting to logistics companies. Further advancement may require diplomas in logistics or supply chain management, leading to management positions. However, the career ladder is relatively flat for drivers, and many remain in driving roles long-term. On-the-job training and license upgrades are common, but formal education beyond secondary school is rare.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

- Autonomous driving system operation and monitoring
- Use of logistics scheduling software and multimodal transport platforms
- Basic Maintenance of New Energy Trucks (Electric/Hydrogen)
- Safety compliance and digital document management
- Customer communication and on-site problem solving
- Basic data analysis (fuel efficiency, route optimization)

**Key growth areas:**

- E-commerce Logistics
- Construction Delivery
- Port Operations
- Last-Mile Delivery

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who enjoy independent, hands-on work and prefer being on the road to office environments.
- Those with good spatial awareness and ability to handle long hours, including night shifts.
- People comfortable with physical demands like loading/unloading and vehicle maintenance.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who dislike traffic congestion and tight delivery schedules.
- Individuals seeking a career with rapid promotions or high-status professional roles.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a heavy truck driver in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, vehicle class, and employer. Generally, median monthly wages for heavy truck drivers are around SGD 2,500–3,500, with experienced drivers earning up to SGD 4,500. Overtime and allowances can increase take-home pay. Note that exact figures change annually; refer to MOM's Occupational Wage Survey for the latest data.

**Q: Can heavy truck drivers qualify for an Employment Pass?**
A: No, heavy truck drivers are typically classified as semi-skilled and do not meet the minimum salary threshold (≥ SGD 5,000) or qualification requirements for an Employment Pass. They are eligible only for Work Permits or, in some cases, S Passes if they have higher qualifications and a higher salary.
