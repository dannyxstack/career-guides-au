# Midwifery Associate Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 3222 (ISCO08)**

In Singapore, midwifery associate professionals support registered midwives in antenatal, intrapartum, and postnatal care. They work in public hospitals, private clinics, and community health settings, assisting with childbirth, newborn care, and parental education. The role is regulated by the Singapore Nursing Board (SNB) and requires a relevant diploma and SNB registration. Demand is steady due to Singapore's focus on maternal and child health, with opportunities in both hospital and community care sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically involves gaining experience as an enrolled midwife, then pursuing advanced diplomas or degrees to become a registered midwife or nurse. Some move into specialist roles such as neonatal care or lactation consulting. With additional qualifications, associates can transition to educator or managerial positions in hospital maternity wards or community health programs. The career path is structured, with opportunities for professional development through continuing education and SNB-accredited courses.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for a midwifery associate professional in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level positions in public hospitals typically start around SGD 2,500 to SGD 3,500 per month. With experience, monthly pay can rise to SGD 4,500 or more. Private clinics and foreign-owned hospitals may offer higher salaries. Additional allowances for shift work and on-call duties apply.

**Q: Can a foreign midwifery associate professional get a work visa in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You must first secure SNB registration, which requires a recognised diploma and may require passing an exam. The employer must then apply for an Employment Pass (EP) or S Pass. EP is more likely for those earning above SGD 5,000/month. Quotas for foreign healthcare workers are strict, and local candidates are prioritised.
