# Handicraft Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7319 (ISCO08)**

Handicraft workers in Singapore produce a wide range of handmade items including pottery, woodcarving, textiles, and jewellery, often for the tourism and cultural sectors. They typically operate as sole proprietors or within small workshops, selling at local markets, craft fairs, or online platforms. The occupation is small-scale and niche, with limited formal employment opportunities. Demand is driven by cultural tourism and interest in unique, artisanal products. Workers need creativity, manual dexterity, and business acumen to market their creations.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a handicraft worker in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on skill level, product type, and sales success. Most handicraft workers are self-employed, so income is project-based. Full-time employees in craft workshops may earn between S$1,800 and S$3,000 per month, but this is not common.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a handicraft worker in Singapore?**
A: It is difficult. Handicraft work is not a recognised skilled occupation for Employment Pass or S Pass. A Work Permit may be possible if employed by a local crafts company, but quotas and levy apply. Most foreign artisans enter on other passes (e.g., Dependent Pass) and work independently.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for a handicraft worker?**
A: Progression often involves becoming a master artisan, opening a studio, or teaching. Some move into product design or retail. Government grants and incubators like the Singapore Craft Council provide support. Without formal structures, advancement is entrepreneurial.
