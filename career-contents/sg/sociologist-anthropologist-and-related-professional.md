# Sociologist, Anthropologist and Related Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2632 (ISCO08)**

In Singapore, sociologists, anthropologists and related professionals work primarily in academic institutions, government research units, think tanks, and social service agencies. They conduct social research, analyse demographic trends, evaluate public policies, and provide cultural insights for business or community engagement. The role is highly specialised, with limited direct industry demand but steady opportunities in the public sector and universities. Given Singapore's multi-ethnic society and focus on evidence-based policymaking, these professionals contribute to social cohesion studies, ageing population research, and migration impact assessments.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a research assistant or associate, advancing to research fellow or lecturer in academia, or senior analyst in government agencies. Tenure-track academic positions are rare and competitive. Some professionals move into related fields like social policy, market research, or user experience research, leveraging their analytical skills. Mid-career opportunities include consultancy roles in public engagement or corporate social responsibility. Leadership paths lead to heads of research departments or directors of social research institutes.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a sociologist in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level research assistants with a bachelor's degree earn around SGD 36,000–45,000 annually. Mid-level researchers with a master's degree earn SGD 50,000–70,000, while senior academics or lead researchers with a PhD can earn SGD 80,000–120,000 per year.

**Q: Can a foreign sociologist obtain an Employment Pass to work in Singapore?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Singapore employer and meet the minimum salary threshold (SGD 5,000/month in 2025). The COMPASS points system requires a bachelor's degree and relevant skills. PhD and experience in high-demand research areas strengthen the application.

**Q: What is the career progression for a sociologist in Singapore?**
A: Many start as research assistants, then advance to research associates or policy analysts with a master's. Academics can progress from lecturer to associate professor. Alternative paths include market research, UX research, or social consultancy for government agencies.
