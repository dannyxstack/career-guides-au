# Physicists and Astronomers — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2111 (ISCO08)**

Physicists and astronomers in Singapore are primarily engaged in research and development within universities, research institutes such as A*STAR, and high-tech industries including semiconductor manufacturing, quantum computing, and photonics. They apply principles of physics to solve complex problems, develop new technologies, and advance scientific knowledge. The role demands strong analytical and mathematical skills, with a focus on experimental or theoretical work. While opportunities exist in academia, industry positions are growing in fields like data science and artificial intelligence, requiring interdisciplinary collaboration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression for physicists and astronomers typically begins with postdoctoral research or entry-level R&D roles, advancing to senior scientist or principal investigator positions in academia. In industry, physicists can move into R&D management, data science, or engineering leadership. Continuous learning and specialisation in emerging areas like quantum computing or nanomaterials enhance prospects. Transitioning to interdisciplinary fields such as fintech or healthcare analytics is also common, leveraging quantitative skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Data processing and statistical analysis
- Routine physical simulation calculations
- Literature search and review writing
- Automated collection and preliminary processing of experimental data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Multi-scale simulation of complex physical systems
- Efficient analysis of large-scale experimental data
- Automated Verification and Optimization of Theoretical Models
- Pattern recognition in interdisciplinary research
- Assisted generation and polishing of academic papers

**Human moat:**

- Propose original theories and hypotheses
- Design novel experimental plans
- Interdisciplinary innovative thinking
- Research ethics judgment and responsibility
- Teaching and knowledge transfer

**Skills to build:**

- Proficiency in Python/R and physics-specific simulation libraries
- Application of machine learning in physical analysis
- High-performance computing and cloud platform usage
- Data visualization and interactive display
- application of AI tools in academic research
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Quantum Computing

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals passionate about fundamental research and scientific discovery.
- Analytical problem-solvers comfortable with advanced mathematics and computational modelling.
- Those seeking interdisciplinary roles in high-tech industries like quantum computing or clean energy.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for physicists in Singapore?**
A: Salary varies widely by sector and experience. Entry-level PhD holders in R&D roles earn around SGD 60,000–80,000 annually, while senior scientists can exceed SGD 120,000. Industry positions in finance or tech may offer higher pay.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign physicists to work in Singapore?**
A: The primary route is the Employment Pass (EP), which requires a job offer from a Singapore employer and a minimum salary of SGD 5,000/month (higher for experienced candidates). The COMPASS framework grants points for qualifications and salary. S Pass is possible for lower-skilled roles but less relevant.
