# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Associate Professional — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 3230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine (TCM) Associate Professionals in Singapore include practitioners such as acupuncturists, TCM physicians, and herbalists who provide holistic healthcare services. Regulated by the Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners Board, they work in clinics, hospitals, and wellness centres. The sector is growing due to increasing interest in integrative medicine and an ageing population seeking alternative therapies. Most practitioners are Singaporeans or permanent residents, with foreign professionals limited to those with recognised qualifications and local registration. The occupation supports Singapore's push for preventive health and wellness tourism.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a diploma or degree in TCM, followed by registration as a TCM physician or acupuncturist. Experienced practitioners can become senior physicians, clinic managers, or lecturers at local universities. Some specialise in areas like paediatric TCM or sports acupuncture. With additional training, they may move into research or regulatory roles. The field offers limited vertical mobility but rewards specialised skills. Many practitioners also run their own clinics or join multidisciplinary health teams. Continuous professional development is mandatory to maintain registration.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for TCM associate professionals in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience and qualifications. Entry-level practitioners earn around S$2,500–S$3,500/month, while senior practitioners can earn S$5,000–S$8,000/month. Those in private practice may earn more.

**Q: Can foreign TCM practitioners get a work visa in Singapore?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Foreign practitioners must have a recognised TCM degree and pass the TCMPB registration exam. They typically need an Employment Pass (EP) or S Pass, sponsored by an employer. Local registration is a prerequisite for work authorization.

**Q: Do I need to be registered with the TCMPB to practise TCM in Singapore?**
A: Yes, it is mandatory. All TCM physicians and acupuncturists must be registered with the Traditional Chinese Medicine Practitioners Board. Registration requires an approved qualification and passing the board's examination.
