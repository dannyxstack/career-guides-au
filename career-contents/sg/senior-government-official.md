# Senior Government Official — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 1112 (ISCO08)**

Senior Government Officials in Singapore are top-level public servants, including Permanent Secretaries, Deputy Secretaries, and Directors, who formulate and implement national policies, manage ministries, and oversee public service delivery. They work closely with political office holders and are responsible for strategic planning, resource allocation, and ensuring efficient governance. This role requires extensive experience in the civil service, typically achieved through a career in the Singapore Public Service, and is restricted to Singapore citizens.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression to senior official status is a long-term path within the Singapore civil service. Officers typically start as administrative officers or policy analysts, progressing through grades such as Assistant Director, Deputy Director, Director, and then to Senior Director or Permanent Secretary. Successful candidates undergo rigorous training, including postings across ministries, leadership programmes, and often postgraduate education. The path is highly competitive and merit-based.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Senior Government Official in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are not publicly disclosed individually, but they are benchmarked to private sector top management. Annual salaries for Permanent Secretaries can exceed SGD 500,000, while Deputy Secretaries earn around SGD 300,000-400,000. These figures include performance bonuses and are subject to annual reviews.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a Senior Government Official in Singapore?**
A: No. Senior Government Official positions are constitutionally reserved for Singapore citizens. No work pass (Employment Pass, S Pass, or Work Permit) is issued for such roles.

**Q: What is the typical career path to become a Senior Government Official?**
A: Most start as Administrative Officers in the Civil Service, undergo structured postings and leadership programmes, and progress through director-level positions. The journey typically takes 15-25 years, with selections based on merit and performance.
