# Glass Maker, Cutter, Grinder and Finisher — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7315 (ISCO08)**

Glass makers, cutters, grinders and finishers in Singapore are skilled tradespeople who shape, cut, polish, and finish glass for construction, automotive, and decorative industries. They work in factories, workshops, and on construction sites, using hand tools and machinery to produce items like windows, mirrors, glass panels, and precision components. The occupation is vital for the building and renovation sector, with steady demand from commercial and residential projects. Workers must adhere to strict safety standards due to the handling of sharp materials and chemicals. Training is typically through the Institute of Technical Education (ITE) or on-the-job apprenticeships, with opportunities for specialization in areas like laminated or tempered glass.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or general worker, advancing to senior finisher or team lead with experience and certifications. Some move into quality control, estimator, or supervisor roles. Entrepreneurial individuals can start their own glass installation or repair businesses. Further training in building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) or architectural glass design opens doors to specialist positions. With Singapore's focus on sustainable construction, glass workers with knowledge of solar-control and low-emissivity coatings may find niche opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated cutting and grinding: CNC machines and robots perform precise glass cutting and polishing, reducing manual work
- Quality inspection: AI vision systems can quickly detect defects such as bubbles and scratches, replacing manual inspection
- Dimensional measurement and calibration: laser scanners automatically complete workpiece measurement and parameter adjustment
- Batch polishing: robotic arms with grinding heads can perform standard polishing tasks continuously
- Material handling and sorting: Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and robotic arms handle and classify glass panels

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Customized design and processing: AI-aided design software (CAD/CAM) helps workers quickly create complex geometric patterns
- Equipment maintenance and programming: workers can acquire robot programming and preventive maintenance skills to improve production efficiency
- Process optimization: AI analyzes historical data to optimize parameters such as furnace temperature and cutting path, reducing scrap
- Virtual training: AR/VR simulation training systems allow novices to quickly master high-risk operations
- Collaborative work: Workers and cobots cooperate to handle and position irregular glass

**Human moat:**

- Art carving and manual finishing: unique patterns, engraving, etc. rely on creativity and manual dexterity, difficult to automate
- On-site installation and repair: architectural glass must adapt to site conditions, requiring flexible adjustments and judgment
- Non-standard parts handling: glass with irregular shapes or special sizes requires manual intervention and decision-making
- Quality anomaly handling: identification and manual correction of complex defects (e.g. internal stress).
- Customer communication and custom solutions: understand client needs and translate them into feasible process plans

**Skills to build:**

- CNC programming and operation (G-code)
- Robot maintenance and collaborative robot application basics
- AI vision system debugging and maintenance
- CAD/CAM software applications (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD)
- Data analysis and process optimization (Python/R)
- Safety regulations and equipment operation certifications (e.g., ISO standards)

**Key growth areas:**

- Facade Installation
- Interior Fit-Out
- Architectural Glass
- Solar Panel Glass

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a glass maker in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and skill level. Entry-level workers may earn around S$1,500–S$2,200 per month, while experienced finishers and specialists can earn up to S$3,500 or more. Overtime and bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can foreign glass makers obtain a work visa for Singapore?**
A: Yes, but typically under the Work Permit for semi-skilled roles or S Pass for those with higher skills and salary. Employers must sponsor the visa and meet quota requirements. Skilled migrant pathways (Employment Pass) are uncommon for this occupation.

**Q: Where can I receive training as a glass maker in Singapore?**
A: The Institute of Technical Education (ITE) offers Nitec and Higher Nitec courses related to precision engineering and manufacturing. On-the-job training is also common. SkillsFuture courses provide additional certifications for workers.
