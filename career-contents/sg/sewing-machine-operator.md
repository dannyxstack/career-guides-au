# Sewing Machine Operator — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 8153 (ISCO08)**

In Singapore, sewing machine operators are primarily employed in the garment manufacturing, textile, and upholstery sectors, as well as in alteration and tailoring services. The industry is small due to high labor costs and competition from low-cost countries, with most operators being Work Permit holders from regional source countries. The role requires manual dexterity, attention to detail, and ability to work with various fabrics. Automation is gradually impacting mass production, but demand persists for custom alterations, industrial sewing (e.g., for furniture), and technical textiles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for sewing machine operators typically includes moving to senior operator, quality control, or supervisory roles. With additional training, operators may transition into pattern making, garment design, or sample machining. Entrepreneurial operators can start their own tailoring or alteration businesses. SkillsFuture courses in advanced sewing techniques or industrial automation can enhance prospects, but upward mobility is generally limited without formal education or shift into related trades.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sewing machine operator in Singapore?**
A: Salaries are generally low, often at or slightly above the minimum wage for Work Permit holders. Exact figures depend on experience, employer, and sector. For the most current data, refer to the Ministry of Manpower's Occupational Wage Survey.

**Q: Can a sewing machine operator obtain an Employment Pass or PR?**
A: No, this occupation does not meet the skill threshold for an Employment Pass (which requires professional-level qualifications). It is typically filled by Work Permit holders, which is employer-tied and offers no direct path to permanent residency.

**Q: Will automation replace sewing machine operators?**
A: Automation is increasing in mass garment production, but operators are still needed for complex tasks like alterations, custom work, and high-end upholstery. Demand will shift toward higher-skilled roles that involve machine setup, maintenance, or quality control.
