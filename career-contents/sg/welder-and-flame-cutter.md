# Welder and Flame Cutter — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7212 (ISCO08)**

Welders and flame cutters in Singapore are skilled tradespeople who join or cut metal parts using various welding and cutting equipment. They are essential in the construction, shipbuilding, marine, oil and gas, and precision engineering sectors. With Singapore's ongoing infrastructure projects and maritime industry demand, these roles offer stable employment, though often on a contract basis. Workers typically require technical education (ITE Nitec or Higher Nitec) and trade certifications. The job involves physical work and adherence to strict safety standards.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 940. Singapore's construction and marine industries continue to drive demand for welders and flame cutters, especially with the expansion of the Tuas Port and upcoming mega-infrastructure projects. However, automation and pre-fabrication may moderate long-term growth. The role is often filled by foreign workers under Work Permits, and local uptake remains low due to physical demands. Shortages persist in specialized welding (e.g., underwater, pipeline). Overall, the outlook is steady for experienced welders, with opportunities in supervision and inspection.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Typically, welders start as apprentices or junior welders after completing ITE training and gain certifications in specific processes (e.g., SMAW, GMAW). Career progression leads to senior welder, welding inspector, or supervisor roles. Some specialize in advanced techniques like orbital welding or robotic welding programming. With additional training, welders can move into quality control, project management, or start their own workshops. The Singapore Workforce Skills Qualifications (WSQ) system provides structured pathways for upskilling.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch standardised metal parts automatic welding (e.g., car body welding, pipe joints)
- Robot programming and path planning based on CAD models replace manual teaching
- Online weld quality inspection (visual inspection + AI defect detection)
- Automatic adjustment of welding parameters and welding process monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted welding parameter optimization (real-time recommendation of current/voltage based on material thickness and ambient temperature)
- Augmented reality (AR) welding guidance system improves novice training efficiency
- Drones/crawler robots equipped with welding modules for high-altitude or hazardous welding
- Digital twin welding simulation reduces material waste from test pieces
- AI-driven multi-robot collaborative welding group control

**Human moat:**

- On-site manual welding of non-standard workpieces and confined spaces (e.g., on ships, inside pipes)
- Welding of pressure vessels and load-bearing structures with high stress and fatigue life requirements
- Welding process development for dissimilar metals and special alloys (titanium, aluminum-magnesium alloys)
- Welding quality responsibility determination and repair decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Programming and debugging of welding robots (e.g., FANUC, KUKA, ABB)
- Welding process simulation software (e.g., Simufact Welding, SYSWELD)
- Automated welding quality control and testing methods
- Digital manufacturing basics (CAD/CAM, BOM management)
- Advanced certifications such as International Welding Engineer (IWE)
- Operation of AR/VR welding simulation training systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Engineering
- Oil & Gas
- Marine & Offshore
- Construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a welder in Singapore?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and certification. Entry-level welders earn around $24,000 per year, while experienced certified welders can earn $40,000 or more. Specialized welders (e.g., underwater) command higher pay.

**Q: Can a foreign welder get an Employment Pass (EP) in Singapore?**
A: Rarely. Most welders qualify for Work Permit (WP) or S Pass (if salary meets threshold). EP is reserved for professionals/managers; it is not a typical route for welders unless they have a degree in engineering and senior supervisory role.

**Q: Is welding a good career in Singapore?**
A: Yes, for those who enjoy practical work. There is consistent demand in construction and marine sectors. With upskilling, welders can become inspectors or supervisors. However, the work is physically demanding and may involve contract-based employment.
