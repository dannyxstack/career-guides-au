# Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and Aquaculture Labourers in Singapore perform manual tasks in fish farms, seafood processing plants, and fishing vessels, including feeding fish, maintaining nets, harvesting seafood, and cleaning equipment. The sector is small but evolving, with a shift towards land-based recirculating aquaculture systems (RAS) and offshore fish farms under the Singapore Food Agency's '30 by 30' goal. Most workers are foreign on Work Permits, as locals tend to avoid this physically demanding work. Automation is slowly entering, but labour remains central for tasks like sorting, grading, and water quality monitoring. The occupation is classified as semi-skilled.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 462. Singapore's aquaculture industry is targeted to produce 30% of local nutritional needs by 2030, driving demand for labourers in new coastal and land-based farms. However, automation (e.g., feeding systems, water sensors) may reduce growth in manual roles. Employment outlook is stable but limited, with a focus on replacing departing foreign workers. The sector faces competition from imported seafood, and labour shortages persist due to physically demanding conditions. Growth will be concentrated in licensed fish farms (e.g., in Lim Chu Kang) and seafood processing hubs.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression from labourer to senior farm hand or team leader is possible with experience and certifications (e.g., WSQ in Aquaculture). Some move into inspection or hatchery roles. However, the path is narrow; most remain in the same grade. Upskilling via courses (e.g., at Singapore Polytechnic or Temasek Polytechnic) can lead to technician roles in water quality or biosecurity. The sector offers few local advancement opportunities, but experienced workers may transition to farm management in nearby countries.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable Aquaculture
- Urban Vertical Farming
- Seafood Processing
- Cold Chain Logistics

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Fishery and Aquaculture Labourer in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from SGD 1,800 to SGD 2,500 per month, depending on employer and experience. Overtime can raise earnings.

**Q: Can I get an Employment Pass (EP) as a fishery labourer?**
A: No. This occupation does not qualify for an EP under the COMPASS framework. Only Work Permit for foreign workers is available, with quota and levy.

**Q: Is there demand for aquaculture labourers in Singapore?**
A: Demand is stable but limited due to automation and small sector. New farms under '30 by 30' create openings, but competition exists from experienced foreign workers.
