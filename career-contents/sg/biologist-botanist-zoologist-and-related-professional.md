# Biologist — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Biologists in Singapore study living organisms and their ecosystems, contributing to fields such as environmental conservation, biomedical research, and agritech. They work in research institutes (e.g., A*STAR), universities, government agencies (e.g., National Parks Board), and private sector companies focusing on biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and food security. Singapore's '30 by 30' goal to produce 30% of nutritional needs locally drives demand for biologists in agritech and sustainable food production. The role requires strong analytical, laboratory, and fieldwork skills, and often involves collaboration with multidisciplinary teams on projects related to biodiversity, climate change, and healthcare.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a research assistant or junior biologist, advancing to senior scientist, principal investigator, or project manager. Biologists may move into regulatory affairs, environmental consulting, or quality control in pharmaceuticals. Singapore's emphasis on translational research and industry partnerships enables pathways into biotech startups or corporate R&D. Further specialisation (e.g., in genetic engineering, marine biology) or obtaining a PhD can lead to leadership roles in academia or research institutions. Some biologists transition to policy roles in environmental agencies or science communication.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)
- Manual sorting of ecological monitoring data (replaced by IoT sensors + AI).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Creativity in experiment design, hypothesis formulation and validation
- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)
- Responsibility attribution and professional certification under the regulatory framework.

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning applications
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., AlphaFold, BLAST)
- Data visualization and interactive reporting (e.g., R Shiny, Tableau)
- Automated laboratory equipment operation and process optimization
- Science communication and policy advocacy skills
- AI ethics and biosecurity regulations knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- biotechnology
- environmental sustainability
- food security
- biomedical research

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

Singapore's main professional work pass is the Employment Pass, granted to qualifying professionals with a job offer above a salary threshold; long-term holders may apply for Permanent Residence. Criteria are set by the Ministry of Manpower and updated regularly.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a biologist in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level biologists with a bachelor's degree can expect a gross monthly salary of S$3,500 to S$4,500. Mid-career professionals earn around S$5,000 to S$7,000, while senior scientists or principal investigators may earn S$8,000 to S$12,000 or more, depending on experience and sector (public vs private).

**Q: Can foreign biologists work in Singapore easily?**
A: Yes, if they secure a job offer from a Singapore employer. Most biologists qualify for an Employment Pass (EP), which is employer-sponsored and subject to the COMPASS framework. The Singapore government does not have a general points-based immigration route for permanent residence; PR is possible but assessed individually.

**Q: What industries employ biologists in Singapore?**
A: Key industries include biomedical sciences (e.g., A*STAR, pharmaceutical companies), environmental consulting, agritech (e.g., vertical farming companies), government agencies (NParks, PUB), and education (universities, polytechnics).
