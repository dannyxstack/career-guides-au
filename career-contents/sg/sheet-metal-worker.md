# Sheet Metal Worker — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 7213 (ISCO08)**

Sheet metal workers in Singapore fabricate, assemble, install, and repair sheet metal products used in construction, manufacturing, and marine industries. They work with materials like steel, aluminum, and copper to create ductwork, roofing, cladding, and custom metal parts. The occupation is common in the built environment sector, including commercial building projects and shipyard maintenance. Workers must be skilled in reading blueprints, operating metalworking machinery, and ensuring precise measurements. With Singapore's ongoing investments in infrastructure and green buildings, demand for sheet metal workers remains steady, though local preference for higher-skilled roles creates reliance on foreign workers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or Nitec qualification, advancing to senior worker or supervisor roles with experience. Many sheet metal workers obtain WSQ certifications or trade tests to enhance employability. Some move into specialized areas like marine sheet metal work or HVAC system fabrication. With additional training, workers can become project coordinators or workshop managers. However, the occupation generally has limited vertical mobility unless workers acquire broader skills in engineering or project management. Local workers may find opportunities in government-linked construction projects that prioritize Singaporean employment.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD/CAM software to generate CNC cutting paths for automatic metal sheet cutting
- Repetitive bending and punching operations done by robots
- Automated welding systems take over standard joint welding
- AI vision inspection systems replace manual surface defect inspection
- Nesting optimization algorithms automatically calculate the most material-efficient cutting layout.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complete complex installations with remote technical expert guidance using AR headsets
- AI-assisted design tools quickly generate ventilation duct layout plans
- Digital twin simulates installation outcomes to reduce onsite rework
- Real-time feedback on fastening torque and angle accuracy via smart handheld tools
- Predictive maintenance systems based on historical data optimize repair schedules.

**Human moat:**

- Adaptability to handle non-standard, irregular structures during on-site installation
- Expertise in diagnosing and manually repairing complex system faults
- Ability to communicate with clients to clarify needs and provide customized solutions
- Physical skills for flexible operation in confined or hazardous environments
- Intuitive understanding of material properties (e.g., ductility of different metals)

**Skills to build:**

- CNC machine programming (e.g., CNC press brakes, laser cutters)
- 3D CAD/CAM software (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD)
- Industrial robot operation and maintenance basics
- Post-processing Techniques for Additive Manufacturing (Metal 3D Printing)
- Data analysis and digital twin tool application.
- Green building standards (e.g., LEED) and knowledge of energy-efficient materials

**Key growth areas:**

- Green Building
- Prefabrication
- Marine Engineering
- Precision Manufacturing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a sheet metal worker in Singapore?**
A: Salary varies by experience and sector. Entry-level workers can expect around SGD 1,800–2,500 per month, while experienced workers with certifications may earn SGD 3,000–4,000. Offshore or marine roles often pay higher.

**Q: Can a foreign sheet metal worker obtain an Employment Pass?**
A: Employment Pass is unlikely as it requires a degree and high salary (minimum SGD 5,600). Most foreign sheet metal workers come on S Pass or Work Permit, depending on skill level.

**Q: How can I advance from sheet metal work to a supervisory role?**
A: Gaining experience and certifications like WSQ Advanced Certificate in Workplace Safety or Supervisor roles can help. Some workers also pursue part-time diplomas at polytechnics.
