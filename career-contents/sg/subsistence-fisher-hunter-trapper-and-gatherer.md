# Subsistence Fisher — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 6340 (ISCO08)**

Subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Singapore engage in small-scale fishing, collecting shellfish, and harvesting wild plants primarily for personal consumption or local barter. Due to Singapore's urbanized landscape, this occupation is extremely rare and largely limited to a few coastal communities or offshore islands. Most local food sourcing is through commercial imports or aquaculture. The Ministry of Manpower does not recognize this as a formal employment category, and such activities are typically unregulated or part of traditional lifestyles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is nonexistent for subsistence fishers in Singapore. There are no formal training programs or career paths. Individuals may transition to commercial fishing or aquaculture if they seek formal employment, but such opportunities are limited. Most subsistence fishers are elderly or part of indigenous communities. Young Singaporeans are not entering this field.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)

- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence fisher in Singapore?**
A: Subsistence fishing yields no regular salary; income is from sale of catch or barter. Most engage in it as a secondary activity. Official wage data is not available for this occupation.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a subsistence fisher in Singapore?**
A: No. Singapore does not issue work passes for subsistence fishing. Commercial fishing may qualify for Work Permits, but self-employed subsistence is not a recognized employment category for foreigners.

**Q: Is subsistence fishing in demand in Singapore?**
A: No, it is not in demand and has no official shortage designation. The occupation is virtually nonexistent in the formal labour market.
