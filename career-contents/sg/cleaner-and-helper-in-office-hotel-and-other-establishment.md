# Cleaner — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 9112 (ISCO08)**

Cleaners and helpers in offices, hotels, and other establishments are responsible for maintaining cleanliness and hygiene in various settings, including commercial buildings, hospitality venues, and public spaces. In Singapore, this occupation is predominantly held by migrant workers from countries such as Malaysia, China, India, and Myanmar, often employed under Work Permit arrangements. The role involves tasks like sweeping, mopping, vacuuming, dusting, and waste disposal. Work is typically performed in shifts, including early mornings, evenings, and weekends. The sector is regulated by the Ministry of Manpower, with employers required to ensure basic accommodation and medical insurance for foreign workers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cleaners in Singapore is limited but possible. With experience and additional training, one can advance to supervisory roles like cleaning team leader or operations manager. Some may specialize in areas such as industrial cleaning or pest control. The Progressive Wage Model aims to provide clearer career pathways and wage increments for local cleaners, but foreign workers on Work Permits typically have fewer advancement opportunities. Upskilling in cleaning technology and management can enhance prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Clean floors using smart vacuum and mop robots
- Operation monitoring of automatic window cleaning robots
- Dust removal in standard areas such as corridors and halls following preset routes.
- Use automatic disinfection robots to disinfect bathrooms and kitchens.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling system to optimize multi-site cleaning routes and schedules
- Use smart sensors to monitor dirt levels and precisely schedule cleaning frequency
- Receive health and safety alerts via wearable devices to enhance personal protection
- Adjusts service details using a customer review AI system to improve satisfaction
- Use digital tools to manage cleaning supplies, reducing waste

**Human moat:**

- Identification and targeted treatment of complex stubborn stains (e.g., carpet stains, oil stains).
- Communicate with clients to understand specific needs and flexibly adjust cleaning methods
- Ability to handle unexpected situations (e.g., breaking items, pet accidents)
- Fine cleaning and care of valuable items (e.g., antiques, silk)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic AI device operation and simple troubleshooting
- Use cleaning management software for scheduling and customer relationship management
- Learn specialized cleaning techniques (e.g., stone care, disinfection)
- Improve communication and customer service skills
- Basic English proficiency to interact with smart devices or serve foreign clients
- Learn about personal protective equipment and eco-friendly cleaning agents

**Key growth areas:**

- Green cleaning
- Hospitality sector growth
- Outsourced cleaning services
- Automation integration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaner in Singapore?**
A: Salaries for cleaners in Singapore range from SGD 1,200 to SGD 1,800 per month for full-time work, depending on experience and employer. Under the Progressive Wage Model, local cleaners earn higher wages (about SGD 1,400–2,000), but foreign workers on Work Permits often receive lower base pay.

**Q: Can a cleaner apply for an Employment Pass or S Pass in Singapore?**
A: No. Cleaners are classified as semi-skilled workers and are only eligible for a Work Permit. S Pass and Employment Pass require higher qualifications and salaries (minimum SGD 3,000 and SGD 5,600 respectively). Work Permit holders have no pathway to permanent residency.

**Q: Are there opportunities for career progression as a cleaner?**
A: Yes, but limited. With experience and training, a cleaner can become a team leader, supervisor, or specialize in areas like industrial cleaning or pest control. Local cleaners have better prospects under the Progressive Wage Model, while Work Permit holders typically stay in entry-level roles.
