# Veterinarian — career analysis · Singapore

**Occupation code: 2250 (ISCO08)**

Veterinarians diagnose and treat illnesses, injuries, and health conditions in animals, encompassing pets, livestock, wildlife, and zoo animals. In Singapore, they work in private clinical practices, government agencies (e.g., Animal & Veterinary Service, AVS), research institutions, and the agri-food sector. The role requires a recognized veterinary degree and compulsory registration with AVS. With rising pet ownership, food safety concerns, and zoonotic disease management, demand for veterinarians is stable and growing. The occupation is on Singapore's Shortage Occupation List, facilitating foreign hiring under the Employment Pass. Skills in biosecurity, public health, and advanced surgery are increasingly valued.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 3,188. Employment outlook for veterinarians in Singapore is positive, driven by increasing pet humanization, stricter biosecurity standards, and a growing focus on One Health. The AVS and research bodies expand roles in disease surveillance and food safety. The shortage persists, especially for specialists in exotic animals, pathology, and epidemiology. The Ministry of Manpower projects modest growth, with opportunities in both private and public sectors. Foreign veterinarians benefit from the Shortage Occupation List, which adds COMPASS points for Employment Pass applications. However, competition for positions in desirable clinics remains moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for veterinarians in Singapore typically starts as an associate veterinarian in private practice, moving to senior or lead veterinarian, and eventually to clinic director or partner. Government roles offer paths from veterinary officer to senior specialist or managerial positions in AVS. Academia requires a PhD for research or teaching. Specializations (e.g., surgery, dermatology, zoo medicine) via internships and residencies enhance earning potential. Many pursue continuous education to keep abreast of advances. Entrepreneurship (opening a clinic) is common among experienced vets. Overall, the field rewards clinical excellence and business acumen.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening of imaging (X-ray/ultrasound) assisted by AI
- Standardized medical record entry and prescription generation
- Automated appointment management for routine vaccinations and deworming
- Automated interpretation of lab sample analysis (blood count/chemistry)
- Simple symptom consultation in telemedicine

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems provide reference for rare case identification
- Robot-assisted surgery improves precision in minimally invasive procedures
- Continuous monitoring of patient rehabilitation data via smart wearable devices
- Big data analytics to predict disease outbreaks and medication trends
- Personalized medical plan generation (based on genetics and medical history)

**Human moat:**

- Manual dexterity and adaptability in complex surgeries
- Empathetic communication and trust-building with pet owners.
- Cross-species clinical judgment (especially for large animals)
- Ethical decisions (e.g., timing of euthanasia)
- On-site handling ability in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Operating AI diagnostic imaging tools and verifying results
- Telemedicine platform usage and data analysis
- Genomics and precision medicine knowledge
- Basics of animal behavior and psychology
- Advanced management of digital health records (EHR)
- Surgical Robot Collaboration Skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Animal Health
- Biosecurity
- Food Safety
- Zoonotic Diseases

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a veterinarian in Singapore?**
A: Entry-level veterinarians earn around S$48,000 to S$60,000 annually. With 5-10 years' experience, salaries range from S$72,000 to S$100,000. Specialists or clinic directors can earn S$120,000 or more.

**Q: Can foreign veterinarians work in Singapore easily?**
A: Yes, veterinarians are on the Shortage Occupation List, which adds 20 COMPASS points for EP applications. You need a recognized veterinary degree and AVS registration. Employers must advertise to locals first, but the process is relatively straightforward for qualified candidates.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities for veterinarians in Singapore?**
A: Veterinarians can specialize through internships/residencies (e.g., surgery, dermatology), move into research or academia, or open their own clinics. Government roles at AVS offer management pathways. The profession allows both clinical and non-clinical career growth.
