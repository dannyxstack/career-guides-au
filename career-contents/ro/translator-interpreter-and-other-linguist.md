## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard conference interpreting (e.g., business negotiations)
- Simple telephone interpreting or community interpreting
- Basic subtitling/transcription proofreading
- Standardized oral exam scoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time captioning assisting interpretation (AI providing background information)
- Term base management (AI automatically extracts terms)
- AI speech-to-text note-taking assistance during simultaneous interpretation
- Remote interpreting platforms (AI optimizes audio/video).
- Quality self-check (AI playback comparison against standard translation)

**Human moat:**

- Cultural context understanding and emotional conveyance
- Rapid decision-making under high pressure
- Confidentiality and ethical judgment
- Interpretation of Non-Verbal Signals (Tone, Body Language)
- Transcreation and rhetorical refinement

**Skills to build:**

- Specialized domain knowledge (legal, medical, financial)
- AI-assisted translation tools (e.g., Otter.ai, SDL Trados)
- Remote interpreting platform operations
- Terminology management and data annotation
- On-site equipment commissioning and troubleshooting
- Advanced cross-cultural communication skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for translators and interpreters in Romania?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on language pair, specialization, and experience. For full-time in-house positions, monthly net salaries typically range from 3,500 to 7,000 RON. Freelancers and specialized translators (e.g., legal, medical) can earn higher rates, often between 200 and 400 RON per hour.

**Q: How can a foreign translator obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work in Romania?**
A: To work as a translator in Romania, a non-EU citizen needs a job offer from a Romanian employer. The employer applies for a work permit, which then allows the employee to apply for a long-stay visa. For the EU Blue Card, the applicant must have a higher education degree and a job offer with a salary exceeding the national threshold. The employer handles most of the paperwork.

**Q: What are the career prospects for translators in Romania?**
A: Demand remains steady for translators working with English, German, French, and niche languages. Many professionals work freelance, while some are employed by translation agencies or multinational corporations. Specialization in fields like legal, medical, or technical translation offers better opportunities. AI tools are increasingly used, but human expertise is still required for quality assurance and creative tasks.
