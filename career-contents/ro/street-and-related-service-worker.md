## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street services workers in Romania?**
A: Salary for street and related services workers in Romania varies widely based on sales, hours, and location. Many earn around the national minimum wage, set annually by the government. However, income can be irregular, especially in informal arrangements. Legal employment guarantees at least the minimum wage, which as of 2025 is approximately 3,700 RON gross per month.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as street services workers in Romania through the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card is for highly skilled professionals with a university degree and a high salary threshold, not for street services workers. Non-EU nationals must instead obtain a work permit and a long-stay visa for employment. The employer must prove a labor shortage, and the process is subject to annual quotas set by the Romanian government.

**Q: What skills are needed to become a street services worker in Romania?**
A: Street and related services workers need good communication and customer service skills, basic math for payments, and physical stamina for standing or walking. Knowledge of Romanian is important. No formal education is required, but experience in sales or vending helps. Work is often informal, so self-discipline and reliability are key.
