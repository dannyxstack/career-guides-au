**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.
- Personalized treatment plans: recommend optimized herbal combinations or acupuncture plans based on big data.
- Knowledge retrieval: quickly obtain the latest research literature and classic cases
- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

- Manual skills (e.g., acupuncture techniques, massage force)
- Interpersonal communication skills to build trust and empathy with patients
- Holistic differentiation and individualized adjustment for complex cases.
- Ethical Judgment and Treatment Responsibility in Cross-Cultural Contexts

**Skills to build:**

- Big data analysis of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and AI tool operation

- Application of telemedicine platforms and patient management
- Continuously learn the latest developments in AI-assisted diagnosis and treatment.
- Multimedia production for patient communication and health education

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for traditional and complementary medicine professionals in Romania?**
A: Salaries vary by specialization and experience. Acupuncturists or naturopaths in private practice may earn between 3,000 and 6,000 RON per month (approx. €600-€1,200). Hospital-based professionals might have lower base pay but additional benefits. Private clinics in larger cities tend to offer higher compensation.

**Q: How can non-EU traditional medicine professionals obtain a work visa to practice in Romania?**
A: Non-EU professionals typically need a work permit sponsored by a Romanian employer. The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified workers with a recognised degree and meeting salary thresholds. Alternatively, the Single Permit combines residence and work authorization. Recognition of foreign qualifications by Romanian medical authorities is required.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to practice traditional medicine in Romania?**
A: Practitioners must hold a recognised diploma in a specific traditional medicine field (e.g., acupuncture, homeopathy). The Romanian College of Physicians registers complementary medicine specialists. Continuing education is mandatory. Some disciplines may require additional certification from the Ministry of Health or professional bodies.
