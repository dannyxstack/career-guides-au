## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence livestock farmer in Romania?**
A: Income for subsistence livestock farmers in Romania is highly variable, as production is mainly for household consumption. Most earn below the national average wage, often supplemented by other activities or EU agricultural subsidies. Official statistics do not provide specific figures for subsistence farming, but net monthly income can range from RON 1,000 to RON 2,500, depending on herd size and market sales.

**Q: What are the visa options for a non-EU citizen to work as a subsistence livestock farmer in Romania?**
A: Non-EU citizens can apply for a long-stay visa for employment (work permit) through a Romanian employer, but subsistence farming is self-employment. For self-employment, one may apply for a business visa or a residence permit for economic activities, the latter requiring a minimum investment. The EU Blue Card is not applicable as it targets highly qualified workers, not subsistence farmers.

**Q: What are the main tasks and challenges of a subsistence livestock farmer in Romania?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers raise animals like cattle, sheep, goats, or poultry for meat, milk, or eggs, primarily for family consumption. Tasks include feeding, grazing, milking, and health checks. They often own small plots and use traditional methods. Challenges include low income, access to veterinary services, compliance with EU sanitary standards, and climate risks. Many supplement income through direct sales at local markets.
