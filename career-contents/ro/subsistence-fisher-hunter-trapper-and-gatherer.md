## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Romania?**
A: Income in this informal occupation is highly variable and generally low. Subsistence fishers in the Danube Delta may earn around 500–1000 RON per month from occasional surpluses, while gatherers of mushrooms or herbs earn seasonally. Official salary data is scarce because most operate outside formal employment, often below the minimum wage. Earnings are not sufficient for full-time livelihood without additional support.

**Q: What is the work visa or EU Blue Card route for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers to work in Romania?**
A: Non-EU nationals cannot obtain an EU Blue Card for this occupation as it requires a highly skilled position with a university degree. A standard long-stay work visa (Type D) is theoretically possible if a Romanian employer sponsors a formal job, but such positions are extremely rare due to the subsistence nature. The Romanian Immigration Inspectorate issues work permits only after a labor market test; however, this occupation is not typically registered for formal work migration.

**Q: What are the main challenges facing subsistence fishers in Romania today?**
A: Subsistence fishers in the Danube Delta face overfishing, climate change altering fish migration, water pollution, and stricter fishing quotas. Competition from tourism and fish farms also reduces available stocks. These challenges make it difficult to rely solely on fishing for income, forcing many to seek alternative informal work or migrate to cities. Access to sustainable livelihoods remains a pressing issue.
