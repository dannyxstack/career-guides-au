## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for dancers and choreographers in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on employment type and experience. Full-time choreographers in public institutions earn around €1200–€2000 gross monthly, while dancers in private companies or project-based work may earn €800–€1500. Freelancers' income fluctuates significantly. The minimum monthly wage is currently €924 gross.

**Q: What work visa or EU Blue Card options are available for dancers and choreographers moving to Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for a National Visa (D) or a temporary residence permit for employment. The EU Blue Card requires a higher education degree and a job offer with salary at least 1.5 times the average Lithuanian wage (approx. €2,500 gross monthly in 2024). Many dance positions may not meet this threshold, making a standard work permit a more common route.

**Q: What are the main career opportunities for dancers and choreographers in Lithuania?**
A: Dancers and choreographers can work in state-funded ballet and contemporary dance companies (e.g., Lithuanian National Opera and Ballet Theatre), private troupes, or as freelancers. Teaching at dance schools or universities is another path. The field is competitive with limited permanent contracts; many combine performance with choreography or instruction.
