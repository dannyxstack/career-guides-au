**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making
- Creative integration in overall curriculum design

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for university teachers in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and institution. According to official data from 2023, the average gross monthly salary for higher education teachers was around €2,000-€3,000. Professors earn higher, while assistant lecturers and junior researchers earn lower. Public universities follow state-regulated pay scales, and additional income can come from research projects or grants.

**Q: What are the official work visa and EU Blue Card routes for foreign university teachers to work in Lithuania?**
A: EU/EEA nationals can work freely. Non-EU nationals need a work permit or EU Blue Card. The Blue Card requires a higher education degree and a job offer with salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (approx. €33,000 in 2023). Application is through the Migration Department, and the employer typically sponsors the process.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for university teachers in Lithuania?**
A: Career progression follows academic ranks: assistant lecturer, associate professor, professor. Advancement requires research publications, teaching experience, and usually a doctoral degree. Many universities have habilitation procedures for full professorship. Tenure-track positions are becoming more common, and international experience is valued.
