## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated sewing machines perform standardized straight stitching and overlocking.
- AI layout systems automatically arrange embroidery patterns and generate machine instructions.
- Computer vision detects stitch defects and automatically adjusts parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates embroidery patterns and previews them.
- Smart sewing machine automatically adjusts tension based on fabric elasticity
- Using AR technology to overlay virtual samples to assist hand sewing
- Data analysis optimizes production scheduling to reduce changeover time

**Human moat:**

- Aesthetic and tactile qualities of complex techniques such as hand embroidery and seed stitch
- Expertise in three-dimensional tailoring and creative design for custom clothing.
- Exclusive handling experience with non-standard fabrics (silk, leather)
- Communicate face-to-face with clients to understand personalized needs

**Skills to build:**

- Digital embroidery design software (e.g., Wilcom, Tajima)
- Industrial sewing machine programming and automated tuning
- Fabric properties and sustainable materials knowledge
- Basic CAD pattern making and 3D garment simulation
- Customer communication and customized service skills
- Lean production and quality control methods

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for sewing, embroidery and related workers in Lithuania?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for sewing, embroidery and related workers in Lithuania ranges from approximately €800 to €1,200, depending on experience and employer. Many earn minimum wage (€924 gross in 2025) or slightly above. Overtime and piece-rate pay can increase earnings, but wages are generally modest in this sector.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU sewing, embroidery and related workers to work in Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU workers must obtain a national work visa (D visa) and a temporary residence permit for employment. The employer must obtain a work permit after demonstrating that no suitable local or EU worker is available (labor market test). The EU Blue Card is not typically applicable, as it requires a higher education degree and a salary threshold significantly above this occupation's average.

**Q: Are there opportunities for career advancement for sewing and embroidery workers in Lithuania?**
A: Advancement opportunities include moving into supervisory roles, quality control, or pattern-making. Some workers become self-employed or start small tailoring businesses. Lithuanian vocational schools offer further training in garment technology or fashion design, which can lead to higher-skilled positions. However, career progression is limited compared to other sectors.
