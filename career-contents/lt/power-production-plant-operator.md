## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Real-time monitoring of power generation parameters and alarm response
- Perform standardized equipment start-stop operations
- Record and report operational data.
- Adjust routine parameters such as boilers and turbines
- Pattern recognition steps in fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI predictive maintenance to optimize equipment repair schedules
- Conduct accident simulations and training using digital twin simulations
- Using automated systems to analyze large volumes of operational data to assist decision-making
- Remote operation and cross-station coordinated scheduling

**Human moat:**

- quick judgment and handling in emergency situations
- Cross-disciplinary comprehensive analysis of complex system failures
- Effective communication with maintenance teams and dispatch centers
- Social responsibility and safety compliance awareness
- Intuition and feel from on-site experience

**Skills to build:**

- Learn advanced operation of systems such as SCADA and DCS
- Master basic data analysis (Python/SQL)
- Understand the application of AI and machine learning in predictive maintenance
- Enhance problem-solving and interdisciplinary collaboration skills
- Obtain a higher-level professional qualification
- Learn new energy generation technologies (e.g., wind power, solar PV).

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for Power Production Plant Operators in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries for Power Production Plant Operators in Lithuania vary by experience and employer but generally align with national energy sector averages. According to official data from Sodra, the average monthly gross salary in the energy supply sector is approximately €2,300, with entry-level positions starting around €1,500 and experienced operators earning up to €3,000.

**Q: What are the work visa or EU Blue Card requirements for Power Production Plant Operators to work in Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU citizens need a work permit and residence permit. The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified workers with a higher education degree or five years of professional experience, and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross wage (about €2,512 per month in 2023). Applications are processed by the Migration Department under the Ministry of the Interior.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for Power Production Plant Operators in Lithuania?**
A: With experience, operators can advance to shift supervisor, plant manager, or specialist roles in renewable energy. Lithuania's growing focus on green energy creates opportunities in wind, solar, and biomass plants. Continuous training in automation and digital control systems enhances career prospects. Many operators also pursue additional certifications to move into maintenance or engineering roles.
