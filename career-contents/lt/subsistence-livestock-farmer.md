## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average income for subsistence livestock farmers in Lithuania?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Lithuania typically earn between €500 and €800 per month, though income varies greatly with herd size and market prices. Many rely on self-consumption and local sales, and formal wage data is scarce. EU agricultural subsidies often supplement income, but profitability remains low due to small scale and high input costs.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as subsistence livestock farmers in Lithuania via the EU Blue Card or other visa routes?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified professionals and does not apply to subsistence livestock farming. Non-EU citizens may obtain a temporary residence permit for employment if hired by a Lithuanian employer with a proven labor shortage. Seasonal work visas (D visa) are also available for short-term agricultural work. Self-employment requires a business visa and registration as a sole proprietor.

**Q: What are the main challenges for subsistence livestock farming in Lithuania?**
A: Key challenges include small land holdings, aging infrastructure, and competition from industrial farms. Climate change affects pasture and feed availability. Many younger farmers migrate to cities, causing succession issues. Access to markets and fair prices is limited. However, opportunities exist in organic production and local food networks, supported by EU rural development programs.
