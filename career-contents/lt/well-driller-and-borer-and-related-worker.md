## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual adjustment of drilling speed and pressure is taken over by AI adaptive control systems
- Experience-based judgment of strata changes is replaced by real-time data analysis
- Record drilling logs and parameters, replaced by automated sensors and IoT.
- Repetitive maintenance checks (e.g., lubrication, bolt tightening) performed by robots

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes geological data to assist in optimizing drilling paths and depths
- Drone and camera AI identification of well site safety risks
- Machine learning predicts equipment failures, reducing unplanned downtime
- Augmented Reality (AR) remote guidance for complex operations

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency decision-making and troubleshooting ability
- Adaptability assessment for complex terrain and abnormal geology
- Team coordination and safety leadership responsibilities
- Fine manual skills in equipment repair

**Skills to build:**

- Geological data analysis software (e.g., Leapfrog)
- PLC and automation control system operation
- Drone operation and AI image analysis
- Equipment health monitoring and predictive maintenance
- Basic programming (Python/R) for customized reports
- Safety and compliance management (e.g., ISO 45001)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for well drillers and borers in Lithuania?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for well drillers and borers in Lithuania ranges from approximately €1,200 to €1,800, depending on experience, location, and employer. Entry-level positions start around €1,000, while experienced workers in specialized drilling can earn upwards of €2,200. Overtime and remote site work may increase total compensation.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for foreign well drillers to work in Lithuania?**
A: Foreign well drillers can work in Lithuania via a national work visa (D visa) or an EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers. The D visa requires a job offer and employer-sponsored labor market test. The EU Blue Card is available for roles requiring higher education or specialized skills with a salary at least 1.5 times the average Lithuanian salary. Both routes involve applying at a Lithuanian embassy abroad.

**Q: What qualifications are required to become a well driller in Lithuania?**
A: To work as a well driller in Lithuania, you typically need a secondary vocational education in drilling, mining, or a related field. Practical training under supervision is essential. Some employers require a certificate of competency for operating drilling rigs. Proficiency in Lithuanian or English may be needed, and knowledge of local geological conditions is advantageous. Health and safety training is mandatory.
