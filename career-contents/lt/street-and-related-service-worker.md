**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries for street and related services workers in Lithuania vary widely depending on location and sales volume, but generally align with or slightly exceed the national minimum wage, which is €924 per month as of 2025. Earnings may be supplemented by tips or commissions, resulting in average monthly incomes between €800 and €1,200.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as street and related services workers in Lithuania under the EU Blue Card or other visa routes?**
A: The EU Blue Card is not available for this occupation as it requires a high-skilled position with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average. Non-EU citizens seeking this work must obtain a standard employment-based work permit, which requires employer sponsorship and a labor market test proving no suitable EU candidate is available. Self-employment is also possible with a temporary residence permit for business purposes.

**Q: What are the main duties and career prospects for street and related services workers in Lithuania?**
A: Street workers sell goods or services directly to the public, such as newspapers, flowers, food, or small items. Duties include setting up stalls, handling cash, and promoting products. Career prospects are limited; advancement usually involves starting a small business or moving into retail sales. Licensing may be required for certain products, and workers must comply with local municipal regulations.
