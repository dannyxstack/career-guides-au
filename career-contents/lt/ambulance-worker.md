- Automatic Generation of Electronic Medical Records and Reports
- Optimal route planning and real-time traffic scheduling

- Automatic monitoring and alerting of basic vital signs

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic decision support (e.g., ECG interpretation, allergy alerts)
- Remote medical collaboration (experts guide on-site first aid via AR glasses)

- Patient data-driven personalized emergency care recommendations

**Human moat:**

- Rapid assessment and decision-making for complex on-site injuries
- Emotional communication and psychological support with patients and families
- Team coordination and leadership in chaotic environments.
- Creative problem-solving in unforeseen situations
- Legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., decision to terminate resuscitation)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for ambulance workers in Lithuania?**
A: The average monthly gross salary for ambulance workers in Lithuania ranges from €1,200 to €1,600, depending on experience and location. Additional shift allowances and overtime pay may apply. Wages are typically higher in major cities like Vilnius.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as ambulance workers in Lithuania via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work as ambulance workers with a work permit. The EU Blue Card is an option for highly qualified workers, requiring a higher education diploma and a job offer meeting a salary threshold. Ambulance workers with relevant qualifications may apply through this route, subject to recognition of their professional credentials by Lithuanian authorities.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become an ambulance worker in Lithuania?**
A: To become an ambulance worker in Lithuania, you typically need a secondary education degree and a specialized training program (e.g., paramedic course) approved by the Ministry of Health. Registration with the State Healthcare Accreditation Service is mandatory. Continuous professional development is required to maintain certification.
