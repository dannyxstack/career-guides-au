**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.
- Personalized treatment plans: recommend optimized herbal combinations or acupuncture plans based on big data.
- Knowledge retrieval: quickly obtain the latest research literature and classic cases
- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

- Manual skills (e.g., acupuncture techniques, massage force)
- Interpersonal communication skills to build trust and empathy with patients
- Holistic differentiation and individualized adjustment for complex cases.
- Ethical Judgment and Treatment Responsibility in Cross-Cultural Contexts

**Skills to build:**

- use of digital diagnostic devices such as tongue and pulse diagnosis
- Application of telemedicine platforms and patient management
- Continuously learn the latest developments in AI-assisted diagnosis and treatment.
- Multimedia production for patient communication and health education

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Lithuania?**
A: In Lithuania, salaries for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals vary widely. Entry-level practitioners earn around €1,200–€1,500 gross per month, while experienced professionals can earn €1,800–€2,500. Many work in private practice, so income may fluctuate. Salaries are generally lower than in Western Europe but reflect the local cost of living.

**Q: Can non-EU Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals work in Lithuania via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU professionals can apply for the EU Blue Card in Lithuania. Requirements include a valid job offer, recognized higher education (or equivalent experience), and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary (approx. €2,100/month in 2023). Qualifications must be recognized by Lithuanian authorities. The Blue Card allows family reunification and a pathway to permanent residence.

**Q: What licensing or registration is required to practice as a Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional in Lithuania?**
A: To practice legally, professionals must register with the Lithuanian Health Ministry or an authorized body. Specific requirements depend on the modality (e.g., acupuncture, herbal medicine). You may need a diploma from an accredited institution and to pass a certification exam. Some professions (e.g., homeopathy) are not fully regulated, so always check with the State Health Care Accreditation Agency.
