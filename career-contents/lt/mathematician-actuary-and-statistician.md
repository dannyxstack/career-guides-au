**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard statistical model running and parameter tuning.
- Routine data cleaning and basic report generation
- Simple risk probability calculation and reserve estimation
- Routine forecasting and updating of economic indicators
- Basic regression analysis and hypothesis testing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Identifies complex risk factors and non-linear relationships using machine learning models.
- Analyze economic policy texts and market sentiment via natural language processing
- Uses AI to simulate large-scale economic scenarios and stress tests.
- Automated data exploration and visualization to quickly uncover insights
- Using AI-assisted validation of model assumptions and result robustness

**Human moat:**

- Deep understanding and critical thinking of model assumptions and limitations
- Ability to communicate complex risk models to non-technical management
- Making Professional Judgments Under Uncertainty and Sparse Data
- Ultimate responsibility for ethical duties and regulatory compliance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level statisticians earn around €1,800–€2,500 gross per month, while experienced actuaries in insurance can earn €3,500–€5,000. Senior roles or those in fintech may exceed €6,000. The average gross monthly salary in the sector is approximately €3,200.

**Q: What work visa or EU Blue Card options exist for mathematicians and statisticians moving to Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU professionals can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a higher education degree and a job offer with salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (€26,400 in 2024). Alternatively, a national work permit is available for qualified roles. Both require employer sponsorship and meet labor market needs.

**Q: What is the job market like for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Lithuania?**
A: Demand is strong, especially in fintech, insurance, banking, and data analytics. Vilnius and Kaunas are major hubs. Many international companies have R&D centers. Proficiency in data science, statistical software (R, Python), and English is key. The field is growing with the digital economy, offering good career progression.
