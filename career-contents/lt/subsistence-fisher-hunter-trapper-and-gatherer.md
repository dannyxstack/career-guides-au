**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers and gatherers in Lithuania?**
A: Subsistence activities in Lithuania are primarily for personal consumption and not a primary income source. Surplus sales may yield small, irregular earnings, often below the minimum wage. Most practitioners engage in these traditional activities as a supplement to other employment or for personal use.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as subsistence fishers or hunters in Lithuania via the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card targets highly skilled workers. Subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping and gathering are not considered high-skilled occupations in Lithuania. Non-EU nationals would need a standard work permit under general migration rules, but such roles rarely meet the conditions for a Blue Card.

**Q: What do subsistence fishers and gatherers in Lithuania typically do?**
A: Subsistence fishers fish in inland waters using small boats or nets, while gatherers collect wild berries, mushrooms, and herbs. Hunters and trappers take small game. All activities require licenses and must comply with seasonal and conservation regulations. The produce is mainly for personal use, though small-scale sales occur.
