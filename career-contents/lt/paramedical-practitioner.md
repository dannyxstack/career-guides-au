## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate treatment records and reports
- Preliminary diagnostic assistance based on images and data
- Patient appointment management and follow-up reminders
- Basic parameter calculation and adjustment of exercise prescriptions

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI motion capture and biomechanical analysis to optimize rehabilitation plans
- Generate clinical documentation quickly using speech recognition and NLP
- Identify complication risks early through AI predictive models
- Remotely monitor patient compliance with home-based training and intervene promptly
- Using digital twins to simulate treatment outcomes

**Human moat:**

- Fine perception in manual therapy and palpation
- Patient emotional support and therapeutic communication
- Complex clinical decision-making and individualised care planning
- Clinical judgment in interdisciplinary collaboration
- Ethics and accountability (e.g., tracing medical errors)

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic tool operation (e.g., Therabot, Kinvent)
- Ability to analyze and interpret health data.
- Use of telemedicine platforms for digital patient communication
- Advanced application of electronic health record systems.
- Human-machine collaborative therapy plan design
- Digital therapeutics product evaluation and integration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for paramedical practitioners in Lithuania?**
A: The average gross monthly salary for paramedical practitioners in Lithuania ranges from €1,200 to €1,500, depending on experience, employer, and location. Salaries are higher in major cities like Vilnius and for those working in private emergency services. Overtime and night shifts may increase earnings.

**Q: Can non-EU paramedical practitioners work in Lithuania via the EU Blue Card or a work visa?**
A: Yes, non-EU paramedics can work in Lithuania through a work permit or the EU Blue Card. Both require a job offer and recognition of qualifications by the Lithuanian Centre for Quality Assessment in Higher Education or the Ministry of Health. The EU Blue Card additionally requires a higher education degree and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross annual salary.

**Q: What are the career prospects for paramedical practitioners in Lithuania?**
A: Paramedical practitioners in Lithuania can work for the national emergency medical service (Greitoji medicinos pagalba), hospitals, or private clinics. Career advancement includes becoming a team leader, training instructor, or specializing in areas like intensive care. The profession faces a growing demand due to an aging population and increasing focus on pre-hospital care.
