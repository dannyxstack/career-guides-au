## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard Meal Formula Generation and Nutrient Calculation
- Automated dietary record analysis (generate reports from diaries)
- Preliminary dietary advice for common chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes)
- Online nutrition education and general dietary advice
- Database query and literature review assistance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted clinical diagnosis (e.g., identifying malnutrition risk)
- Generate personalized nutrition plans (combined with genetic and microbiome data)
- Real-time nutrition monitoring and feedback via wearable devices
- Patient data management (automatic sorting of medical records and dietary history)
- Automation of online booking and follow-ups

**Human moat:**

- Complex nutritional interventions for multiple comorbidities (e.g., kidney disease with diabetes)
- Enteral/parenteral nutrition formulation and critical care management
- Behaviour change counselling (psychological support, overcoming eating habits)
- Medical team coordination (communicating with doctors, nurses, and pharmacists)
- Ethical judgment and patient privacy protection

**Skills to build:**

- Clinical nutrition assessment and disease management (e.g., ICU nutritional support)
- Hands-on AI tools (e.g., IBM Watson Nutrition Advisor)
- Health data analysis (basic Python/R, electronic medical record systems)
- Telemedicine communication and patient education
- Culturally sensitive nutrition counselling (diverse clients)
- Research and evidence-based practice updates

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for dieticians and nutritionists in Lithuania?**
A: In Lithuania, dieticians and nutritionists typically earn between €1,200 and €2,000 gross per month, depending on experience, employer (public vs. private sector), and location. Entry-level positions start around €1,000, while senior roles in private clinics or consulting can exceed €2,500. Salaries are generally lower than in Western Europe but align with local cost of living.

**Q: Can non-EU dieticians work in Lithuania via an EU Blue Card or other routes?**
A: Yes, non-EU dieticians can work in Lithuania through the EU Blue Card if they have a higher education diploma and a job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the average Lithuanian wage (approx. €2,200 gross/month in 2024). Alternatively, a national work permit (D visa) is possible for qualified specialists. Professional qualifications must be recognized by the Lithuanian health authorities.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to practice as a dietitian in Lithuania?**
A: To practice as a dietitian in Lithuania, you need a university degree in dietetics or nutrition (at least bachelor's), followed by internship and professional registration with the Lithuanian Ministry of Health. For nutritionists without clinical focus, requirements may vary. EU qualifications are recognized automatically; non-EU credentials must undergo a recognition process (nostrification) and possibly language exams.
