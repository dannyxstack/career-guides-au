## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard conference interpreting (e.g., business negotiations)
- Simple telephone interpreting or community interpreting
- Basic subtitling/transcription proofreading
- Standardized oral exam scoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time captioning assisting interpretation (AI providing background information)
- Term base management (AI automatically extracts terms)
- AI speech-to-text note-taking assistance during simultaneous interpretation
- Remote interpreting platforms (AI optimizes audio/video).
- Quality self-check (AI playback comparison against standard translation)

**Human moat:**

- Cultural context understanding and emotional conveyance
- Rapid decision-making under high pressure
- Confidentiality and ethical judgment
- Interpretation of Non-Verbal Signals (Tone, Body Language)
- Transcreation and rhetorical refinement

**Skills to build:**

- Specialized domain knowledge (legal, medical, financial)
- AI-assisted translation tools (e.g., Otter.ai, SDL Trados)
- Remote interpreting platform operations
- Terminology management and data annotation
- On-site equipment commissioning and troubleshooting
- Advanced cross-cultural communication skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for translators and interpreters in Lithuania?**
A: In Lithuania, the average gross monthly salary for translators and interpreters ranges from €1,200 to €2,000, depending on experience, language pair, and specialization. Freelancers may earn more but with variable income. Senior specialists or those with rare language combinations can command higher rates, especially in business or legal contexts.

**Q: What is the work visa or EU Blue Card route for linguists to work in Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU linguists can work in Lithuania via an EU Blue Card (requires a job offer with salary at least 1.5x the average gross salary, currently ~€2,340/month) or a National Work Visa for specific job offers. The employer must prove the position cannot be filled locally. A temporary residence permit is then obtained.

**Q: What career opportunities exist for linguists in Lithuania?**
A: Linguists in Lithuania work in translation agencies, localization companies, government institutions, international organizations, and as freelancers. Demand is high for English-Lithuanian pairs, but also for Scandinavian, German, and French. Specializations in legal, medical, or technical translation offer better prospects. Remote work is common.
