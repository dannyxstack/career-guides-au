## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Lithuania?**
A: Income for subsistence farmers in Lithuania is highly variable and generally low, as production mainly covers family needs. Surplus sales may generate some revenue, but average monthly earnings from such small-scale farming often range from €300 to €600, supplemented by EU Common Agricultural Policy subsidies. Official statistics do not specifically track subsistence farmer salaries.

**Q: What work visa or EU Blue Card options exist for subsistence farmers to work in Lithuania?**
A: Subsistence farming does not qualify for the EU Blue Card, which requires a highly skilled job with a university degree. Non-EU nationals may apply for a temporary residence permit for self-employment if they invest in agricultural activities and meet conditions set by the Migration Department. Seasonal work permits are another option for temporary farm labor, but not for independent subsistence farming.

**Q: What are the main duties of subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Lithuania?**
A: Subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Lithuania manage small plots to grow crops such as potatoes, grains, and vegetables, while raising animals like cattle, pigs, or poultry primarily for family consumption. Tasks include planting, harvesting, feeding, and basic animal husbandry. They also maintain farm infrastructure and may sell minor surpluses at local markets.
