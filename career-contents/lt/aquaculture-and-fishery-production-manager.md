## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager in Lithuania?**
A: Salaries for Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers in Lithuania vary by experience, employer, and region. Entry-level managers may earn around €1,500–€2,000 gross monthly, while experienced managers can earn €3,000–€4,500. Larger companies or those with international operations often offer higher compensation. Bonuses and benefits may also apply.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Managers to work in Lithuania?**
A: Non-EU professionals can apply for a National Visa (D) for employment, requiring a work permit from the Lithuanian Labour Exchange. Alternatively, the EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified workers with a university degree and a job offer meeting the salary threshold (currently at least 1.5 times the average Lithuanian salary). Both routes require employer sponsorship.

**Q: What qualifications and skills are needed for an Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager in Lithuania?**
A: Typically, a bachelor's degree in aquaculture, fisheries, marine biology, or a related field is required. Practical experience in fish farming or hatchery management is essential. Managers need strong knowledge of production cycles, water quality management, and fish health. Leadership, budgeting, and compliance with EU and Lithuanian aquaculture regulations are also important.
