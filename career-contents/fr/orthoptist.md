# Orthoptist — career analysis · France

**Occupation code: J1407 (ROME)**

Axis correction specialists are responsible for diagnosing and treating visual function abnormalities such as strabismus, amblyopia, and diplopia, using non-surgical methods to train and correct vision.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Junior optic axis correctionists can enter hospital ophthalmology departments or private clinics, gain experience, and be promoted to senior technician or department head. They can also shift to teaching, research, or open independent practice clinics.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Axis correction specialists use AI to automatically analyze eye movement data, reducing manual measurement time
- AI-assisted generation of preliminary visual training plans, replacing basic plan design
- Automated report generation, replacing manual case summaries
- Some standard visual screenings have been replaced by AI remote diagnostic tools
- AI manages patient appointments and follow-up reminders, reducing administrative workload

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI provides real-time visual tracking feedback, enhancing training effectiveness evaluation
- AI analyzes large amounts of case data to assist in diagnosing difficult strabismus/amblyopia
- AI-generated personalized training plans to improve patient adherence
- AI-assisted remote monitoring of patient progress, expanding service coverage
- AI tools help explain conditions to patients and improve communication efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face interaction with patients, building trust and encouragement
- Handling complex, atypical cases (such as perioperative management)
- Manually operate special inspection equipment (such as prisms, eye shields)
- Clinical judgment and ethical decision-making (when to refer to surgery)
- Interdisciplinary collaboration (with ophthalmologists, physical therapists, etc.)

**Skills to build:**

- Master AI-assisted diagnostic tools (such as automatic eye-tracking software)
- Data Analysis and Visualization (for patient progress reports)
- Digital Health Communication Skills (Remote Patient Management)
- Basic programming or scripting (for customizing AI workflows)
- Continuous Learning New Visual Training Device (VR/AR)
- Interdisciplinary teamwork and project management

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- ophthalmology demand
- visual rehabilitation
- pediatric care

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for visual axis orthodontics?**
A: Entry-level orthoptists earn around €25,000-30,000 per year, while senior practitioners can earn €42,000-55,000. Private clinics or independent practice offer higher income.

**Q: How can foreign visual axis correctionists immigrate to France?**
A: You must hold a French-recognized Orthoptiste certificate (Certificat de capacité d'orthoptiste), pass the qualification certification (reconnaissance des qualifications), and then apply for a work visa or talent passport.

**Q: Are visual axis correctors regulated in France?**
A: Yes, orthoptists are regulated health professionals, requiring a national competency certificate and registration with ARS.
