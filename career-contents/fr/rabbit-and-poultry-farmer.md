# Rabbit And Poultry Farmer — career analysis · France

**Occupation code: A1409 (ROME)**

Responsible for the daily feeding, breeding, health management, and farm operations of rabbits and poultry (chickens, ducks, geese, etc.), ensuring animal welfare and product quality (meat, eggs).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Breeders can be promoted from junior breeders to farm supervisors or farm managers; After accumulating experience, you can start your own family farm. Attending professional training (such as BP REA) can enhance management skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically records animal weight, feed consumption, and egg production
- Ventilation, lighting, and temperature are automatically adjusted based on sensor data
- AI cameras are used to identify vulnerable individuals and trigger alerts
- Automated feeding system and egg collection equipment operation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze farming data optimizes breeding plans and feed formulas
- Predict disease outbreaks and intervene early through AI
- Drones are used to monitor large outdoor farming areas
- AI-assisted market price forecasting and sales decisions
- Automated generation of farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment to respond to sudden illnesses and abnormal behaviors in animals
- Ethical decisions regarding animal welfare issues
- Hands-on ability to repair and maintain complex automated equipment
- Build trust and affinity in human interactions with animals

**Skills to build:**

- AI-driven farming management software operation
- IoT sensor system maintenance
- Fundamentals of Data Analysis (Excel/Python)
- Application of precision agriculture technology
- Automation and robot system commissioning
- Sustainable farming practices and certification knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- animal welfare
- biosecurity
- short food supply chain

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Loves animals and outdoor work, and doesn't mind physical labor
- They are willing to live in rural areas and can adapt to early risings and seasonal busyness
- Farm or breeding experience is preferred

**Who is not suited**
- Slaughter and animal handling work are unacceptable
- Seeking high salary and city lifestyle

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average income of rabbit and poultry farmers?**
A: In France, employed farmers earn about 20,000–40,000 euros annually, while income for self-employed farmers fluctuates greatly depending on size and market.

**Q: Can foreigners immigrate to France through this profession?**
A: EU citizens are free to work; Third-country nationals need employers to prove labor shortages, which are difficult and are generally not considered skilled migration occupations.

**Q: What qualifications are required?**
A: Typically, you need a CAPA or BTSA agricultural diploma, an animal health certificate (Certiphyto), and farm entrepreneurship training is required to apply for subsidies.
