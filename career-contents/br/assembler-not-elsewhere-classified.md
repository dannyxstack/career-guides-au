# Assemblers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8219 (ISCO08)**

Assemblers not elsewhere classified perform manual or machine-assisted assembly of products across various manufacturing sectors, such as automotive, electronics, furniture, and machinery. They read blueprints, fit parts, use hand tools, and ensure quality control. This occupation is essential for production lines but often offers limited career advancement without additional training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically moves from line assembler to lead assembler, then supervisor or quality inspector. Technical courses in mechatronics or industrial automation can enhance advancement. Some workers transition to maintenance or machine operation. Without upskilling, opportunities are limited. The occupation often serves as an entry point into formal manufacturing.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive parts assembly (e.g., electronic component insertion, screw fastening)
- Vision-Based Quality Inspection (AI Vision System Replacement)
- Simple material handling and sorting (replaced by AMR)
- Record production data (replaced by automated MES system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for real-time assembly guidance, reducing training time
- Use collaborative robots for heavy or precision assembly, with worker supervision and fine-tuning
- Use AI predictive maintenance tools to monitor equipment and replace worn parts in advance
- Optimize processes based on digital twin simulation of assembly workflows

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard parts or unexpected anomalies (e.g., flexible adjustments when part tolerance is out of spec)
- Hand-finishing small-batch, multi-variety custom assemblies
- Make experience-based comprehensive judgments on assembly quality
- Cross-workstation collaboration and on-site problem-solving skills

**Skills to build:**

- Collaborative robot programming and debugging.
- Operation of digital twin and AR-assisted assembly systems
- Basic data analysis (reading from MES system to improve efficiency)
- Mechatronics maintenance skills
- Lean production and continuous improvement methods
- Cross-disciplinary communication (liaising with engineers, suppliers).

**Key growth areas:**

- Automotive assembly
- Electronics manufacturing
- Renewable energy equipment
- Agribusiness machinery

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an assembler in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are typically around the national minimum wage (R$ 1,500-2,000 per month) for entry-level positions, varying by region and industry. Experienced assemblers at large firms can earn up to R$ 3,500 per month, but the average remains low.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for assembly jobs in Brazil?**
A: It is very difficult because VITEM V requires specialized skills rarely met by assemblers. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is more feasible for citizens of neighboring countries, but the job must still be formal. Most assemblers are Brazilian nationals.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities for assemblers?**
A: With additional training, assemblers can become quality inspectors, team leaders, or machine operators. Technical courses in mechatronics or automation can open paths to higher-paying roles. Without upskilling, progression is limited.
