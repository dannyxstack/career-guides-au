# Secondary Education Teacher — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2330 (ISCO08)**

Secondary Education Teachers in Brazil deliver instruction to students aged 15-18 in public and private schools, specializing in subjects like mathematics, sciences, languages, or humanities. They plan lessons, assess student progress, and prepare learners for higher education or the workforce. This role requires a Licenciatura degree and proficiency in Portuguese. Employment is regulated by the Ministry of Education, with public sector positions typically accessed via competitive exams (concurso público).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 718,575. Brazil's secondary education sector faces a growing demand for qualified teachers, especially in STEM subjects, foreign languages (English, Spanish), and inclusive education. The government has implemented policies to attract and retain teachers, including salary bonuses for hard-to-staff areas and career progression programs. However, budget constraints and regional disparities persist, leading to higher opportunities in the Southeast and South regions. The aging teacher workforce also drives replacement demand.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for secondary education teachers often begins with a Licenciatura degree and entry into teaching positions. Experienced teachers can advance to coordinator or pedagogical supervisor roles, pursue postgraduate specializations, or transition to school administration. Public sector teachers may progress through salary tiers based on experience and continuing education. Private schools offer faster advancement but less job security. Some teachers also move into curriculum development, educational consulting, or university teaching.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What factors affect salary for secondary education teachers in Brazil?**
A: Salaries depend on the state, level of education (licenciatura vs. specialization), years of experience, and whether the school is public or private. Public sector salaries are set by law and may include additional benefits.

**Q: What visa is needed for a foreign teacher to work in Brazil?**
A: Foreign teachers require an employer-sponsored work visa (VITEM V) or, if from a Mercosur country, can apply for Mercosur Residence. Additionally, they must have their qualifications recognized by the Ministry of Education and prove Portuguese proficiency.

**Q: What is the job outlook for secondary education teachers?**
A: Demand is high in STEM subjects, English, and special education, but overall competition can be strong. Opportunities exist in both public and private institutions, with public sector positions often requiring a competitive exam (concurso público).
