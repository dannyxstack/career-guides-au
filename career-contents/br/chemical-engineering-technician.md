# Chemical Engineering Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3116 (ISCO08)**

Chemical engineering technicians assist chemical engineers in developing, testing, and improving chemical processes and products. They work in sectors such as petrochemicals, biofuels, pharmaceuticals, and food processing, operating and monitoring equipment, analyzing samples, and maintaining safety standards. In Brazil, these technicians are often employed in the oil and gas industry (especially pre-salt), ethanol production, mining, and environmental compliance. They typically hold a technical diploma or a technologist degree and may be registered with the Regional Chemistry Council (CRQ). The role requires practical skills, attention to detail, and the ability to work in industrial settings with hazardous materials.

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 62,513. The employment outlook for chemical engineering technicians in Brazil is moderately positive, driven by growth in biofuels, pre-salt oil extraction, and environmental regulations. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior technician, advancing to senior technician, supervisor, or team leader. Specializations in process control, instrumentation, or environmental safety can lead to higher pay and responsibilities. Some technicians pursue further education to become chemical engineers (bachelor's degree), which opens roles in R&D, project management, or regulatory affairs. Others move into sales of chemical equipment or consultancy. The role is hands-on and can lead to operations management in industrial plants. Continuous learning in new technologies (e.g., digital twins, IoT) is valued.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a chemical engineering technician in Brazil?**
A: The pre-tax annual salary for a chemical engineering technician in Brazil typically ranges from 30,000 to 60,000 BRL (US$ 6,000–12,000), depending on experience, sector, and region. Technicians in the oil and gas or pharmaceutical industries often earn higher salaries, while those in smaller firms or interior areas tend to earn less.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a chemical engineering technician in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but work authorization requires employer sponsorship. The employer must demonstrate that no qualified Brazilian worker is available for the role. The process involves applying for a VITEM V visa or a CNIg authorization. Additionally, the foreigner must meet the CRQ registration requirements if the role involves regulated chemistry activities. Mercosur nationals can apply for the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which is more straightforward.

**Q: What are the main industries hiring chemical engineering technicians in Brazil?**
A: The top industries are petrochemicals (e.g., Braskem, Petrobras), biofuels (ethanol plants), pharmaceuticals (e.g., EMS, Hypera), food and beverage processing, mining, and environmental services. The most opportunities are in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Bahia, and Minas Gerais.
