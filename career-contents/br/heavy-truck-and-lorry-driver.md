# Heavy Truck and Lorry Driver — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8332 (ISCO08)**

Heavy truck and lorry drivers operate large vehicles to transport goods over long distances. In Brazil, this occupation is critical to agribusiness, industrial supply chains, and e-commerce logistics. Drivers must hold a national driver's license (CNH) category D or E and often complete specialized courses for dangerous goods. The work demands physical endurance, alertness, and flexibility due to irregular hours and extended trips.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,069,301. Brazil faces a chronic shortage of qualified heavy truck drivers, driven by an aging workforce and poor working conditions. Demand is strong from agribusiness, retail, and infrastructure projects. The government has considered measures to attract younger workers and improve licensing pipelines, but the gap persists. Employment is expected to remain robust with steady, albeit competitive, hiring.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for truck drivers typically moves from regional routes to interstate or specialized transport (e.g., hazardous materials or oversized loads). Experienced drivers may become owner-operators or move into logistics coordination, fleet management, or driving instructor roles. Some drivers transition to bus driving or heavy machinery operation. Continuing education in logistics or safety can open supervisory roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving (autonomous truck pilot programs)
- Basic route planning and GPS navigation adjustments
- Basic vehicle status monitoring and routine inspection recording
- Standardized cargo handling (integration with automated warehousing)
- Fatigue driving risk detection (AI takeover warning)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Dynamic route optimization (real-time traffic, weather, queue times)
- Vehicle maintenance prediction (AI analysis engine data for early warning)
- Delivery confirmation and customer communication (auto-photo, e-signature)
- Multimodal transport coordination (rail, port, warehouse system integration)
- Driving safety assistance (collision warning, blind spot monitoring)

**Human moat:**

- Complex loading/unloading and on-site flexibility (e.g., construction sites, refrigerated trucks)
- Driving on unstructured roads in remote areas
- Customer relationships and non-standard delivery requirements
- Rapid decision-making in emergencies (accidents, failures)
- Compliance and safety responsibilities (cargo inspection, dangerous goods transport)

**Skills to build:**

- Autonomous driving system operation and monitoring
- Use of logistics scheduling software and multimodal transport platforms
- Basic Maintenance of New Energy Trucks (Electric/Hydrogen)
- Safety compliance and digital document management
- Customer communication and on-site problem solving
- Basic data analysis (fuel efficiency, route optimization)

**Key growth areas:**

- Agribusiness
- E-commerce Logistics
- Infrastructure Construction
- Fuel & Chemical Transport

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for heavy truck drivers in Brazil?**
A: Average monthly salary ranges from BRL 4,000 to BRL 7,000, varying by region, cargo type, and experience. Danger pay or overtime can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a heavy truck driver in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. Employer sponsorship under VITEM V is possible, but drivers typically need specialized skills or experience. Mercosur nationals can work more easily via the residence agreement.

**Q: What are the typical working hours for a truck driver in Brazil?**
A: Drivers often work 12-14 hour days with regulated rest periods. Long-haul routes may require weeks on the road.
