# Mechanical Engineer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2144 (ISCO08)**

Mechanical engineers design, develop, and oversee the manufacturing of mechanical systems, including machinery, engines, thermal devices, and industrial equipment. In Brazil, they work across automotive, energy, aerospace, and general manufacturing sectors, requiring registration with the Regional Council of Engineering and Agronomy (CREA) for legal practice.

Specialization in renewable energy, automation, or project management offers faster progression. CREA registration is mandatory for senior and lead roles. Lateral moves into industrial engineering, R&D, or technical sales are common.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is typical salary range for a mechanical engineer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. Entry-level engineers (0-3 years) earn between BRL 50,000 and 80,000 annually; mid-level (4-9 years) BRL 80,000 to 130,000; senior (10+ years) BRL 130,000 to 200,000. Public sector and oil & gas tend to pay higher.

**Q: How can a foreign mechanical engineer obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: The main route is an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa. The Brazilian company must file a request with the Ministry of Labour (via CNIg). Requirements include a recognized engineering degree, proof of professional experience, and registered employment contract. For Mercosur nationals, the Mercosur Residence Agreement simplifies the process.

**Q: Do mechanical engineers need CREA registration to work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, CREA (Regional Council of Engineering) registration is mandatory for all engineering activities that affect public safety, such as signing off on projects. Without it, you cannot legally practice as an engineer. Registration requires an accredited degree and approval of a professional ethics exam.
