# Glass Maker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7315 (ISCO08)**

Glass makers, cutters, grinders and finishers shape, cut, grind and finish glass and glass products. In Brazil, these skilled workers are employed in industries such as construction, automotive, furniture, and solar energy. Tasks include cutting glass sheets to size, beveling edges, drilling holes, and applying finishes. The occupation requires manual dexterity, precision, and knowledge of glass properties. Most workers gain skills through vocational training at SENAI or on-the-job experience. While not a high-demand field, it offers steady opportunities in specialized manufacturing and installation sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level workers start as assistants, learning on the job. With experience, they can become specialist cutters, grinders, or glass installers. Some advance to supervisor roles or open their own workshops. Certification from SENAI or other institutions can accelerate career progression. Emerging trends include laminated and tempered glass for safety, curved glass for architecture, and photovoltaic glass, creating niche specializations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated cutting and grinding: CNC machines and robots perform precise glass cutting and polishing, reducing manual work
- Quality inspection: AI vision systems can quickly detect defects such as bubbles and scratches, replacing manual inspection
- Dimensional measurement and calibration: laser scanners automatically complete workpiece measurement and parameter adjustment
- Batch polishing: robotic arms with grinding heads can perform standard polishing tasks continuously
- Material handling and sorting: Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and robotic arms handle and classify glass panels

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Customized design and processing: AI-aided design software (CAD/CAM) helps workers quickly create complex geometric patterns
- Equipment maintenance and programming: workers can acquire robot programming and preventive maintenance skills to improve production efficiency
- Process optimization: AI analyzes historical data to optimize parameters such as furnace temperature and cutting path, reducing scrap
- Virtual training: AR/VR simulation training systems allow novices to quickly master high-risk operations
- Collaborative work: Workers and cobots cooperate to handle and position irregular glass

**Human moat:**

- Art carving and manual finishing: unique patterns, engraving, etc. rely on creativity and manual dexterity, difficult to automate
- On-site installation and repair: architectural glass must adapt to site conditions, requiring flexible adjustments and judgment
- Non-standard parts handling: glass with irregular shapes or special sizes requires manual intervention and decision-making
- Quality anomaly handling: identification and manual correction of complex defects (e.g. internal stress).
- Customer communication and custom solutions: understand client needs and translate them into feasible process plans

**Skills to build:**

- CNC programming and operation (G-code)
- Robot maintenance and collaborative robot application basics
- AI vision system debugging and maintenance
- CAD/CAM software applications (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD)
- Data analysis and process optimization (Python/R)
- Safety regulations and equipment operation certifications (e.g., ISO standards)

**Key growth areas:**

- Construction
- Automotive
- Solar Energy
- Architectural Glass

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a glass maker in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level glass makers earn around R$1,800–R$2,500 per month, while experienced specialists can earn R$3,000–R$4,500. Supervisors or those with CNC skills may earn up to R$6,000. Annual pre-tax income typically ranges from R$22,000 to R$72,000, depending on location and experience.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa in Brazil as a glass maker?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The employer must sponsor a VITEM V visa, proving that no Brazilian worker is available. This is rare for basic glass making; however, specialized roles (e.g., automotive glass or stained glass artisans) may qualify. Mercosur nationals have an easier route via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for glass makers?**
A: With experience, glass makers can become supervisors, quality inspectors, or start their own glass workshops. Specializing in architectural glass, solar panels, or automotive glass can lead to higher pay. Certification in CNC operation or glass tempering improves prospects.
