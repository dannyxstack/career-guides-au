# Metal Moulder and Coremaker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7211 (ISCO08)**

Metal moulders and coremakers create sand moulds and cores for casting metal parts, primarily in foundries. They interpret blueprints, prepare moulding boxes, pack sand, and remove patterns to form cavities. After pouring and cooling, they shake out castings and clean them. This occupation requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and knowledge of metallurgy. In Brazil, it is concentrated in industrial regions like São Paulo, Minas Gerais, and Rio Grande do Sul, serving sectors such as automotive, machinery, and construction.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 19,450. Brazil's foundry industry faces moderate demand due to industrial recovery and infrastructure projects. However, automation is gradually reducing manual roles. Skilled moulders remain needed for complex, low-volume castings. The occupation is not on a formal shortage list but experiences localised shortages in industrial hubs. Growth is tied to manufacturing output and export of iron and steel products. Long-term outlook is stable but declining for pure manual roles; upskilling to CNC or metallurgical technician roles is advisable.

**Competition: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or helper, advancing to moulder, then senior moulder or supervisor. With additional training, one can move into patternmaking, metallurgical inspection, or foundry management. Many also transition to quality control or process engineering. The trade offers stability but limited upward mobility without formal education. Certifications from SENAI or technical schools boost prospects. Self-employment via small jobbing foundries is possible but rare.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate 3D printing paths for sand moulds/cores using CAD models
- AI vision system detects casting defects in real-time and automatically sorts them
- Robots automatically complete sand mold assembly and pouring operations
- AI optimizes pouring parameters and adjusts cooling system

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI simulation software assists in predicting casting shrinkage and porosity locations, guiding process improvements.
- AR headset displays sand core assembly sequence and position calibration
- AI-driven quality analysis system generates defect root cause reports
- The digital twin system monitors the entire casting process in real time and warns of anomalies

**Human moat:**

- Process innovation capability for handling non-standard, small-batch castings
- Skills in manual repair and fine-tuning of complex sand moulds/cores
- On-site decision-making ability for multi-variety quick mold changes.
- Team coordination and safety risk management responsibilities

**Skills to build:**

- Process parameter adjustment for 3D printed sand molds/cores
- Casting simulation software (Magma, ProCAST) operation and result interpretation
- Basic maintenance of automated production lines (robots, PLC)
- Metallurgy and Defect Analysis (metallography, X-ray)
- Data-driven process optimization (statistical process control)
- Basic operations and maintenance of digital twin systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Foundry
- Automotive
- Infrastructure
- Industrial Machinery

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a metal moulder in Brazil?**
A: Average monthly salary ranges from BRL 2,200 to BRL 3,500 for experienced workers, depending on region and industry. Entry-level positions may start at minimum wage (BRL 1,320). Overtime and hazard pay can increase earnings.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a metal moulder in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but the process is restrictive. Employers must prove no Brazilian worker is available (labor market test). The VITEM V visa requires a job offer and approval from the Ministry of Labor. Mercosur nationals have easier access via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: What are the opportunities for advancement?**
A: With experience and technical courses, moulders can become supervisors, patternmakers, or quality inspectors. Further education in metallurgical engineering opens management roles. The career is stable but competition for top positions requires continuous upskilling.
