# Mobile Farm and Forestry Plant Operator — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8341 (ISCO08)**

Mobile farm and forestry plant operators drive, operate, and maintain self-propelled agricultural and forestry machinery such as tractors, harvesters, and forwarders. They perform tasks like plowing, planting, harvesting, and timber extraction, ensuring efficient land use and crop or wood production. The role requires physical stamina, mechanical aptitude, and adaptability to seasonal demands and varying terrain. Operators often work in remote rural areas and must comply with safety and environmental regulations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Progression typically begins as an assistant or general farm worker, advancing to operate larger machinery. Experienced operators may become team leaders, farm managers, or independent contractors. Specialization in precision agriculture or forestry harvesting can lead to higher pay. Some operators transition to machinery dealerships as technicians or sales representatives. Certification and multi-machine proficiency improve prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Path planning and driving control for autonomous tractors or harvesters
- Automatic seeding and fertilizing operations based on GPS and sensor data
- Use AI visual systems to automatically identify crop maturity and perform harvesting
- Use drones to automatically inspect farmland and generate work instructions

**Human moat:**

- Unstructured communication and coordination skills with base and team

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a mobile farm and forestry plant operator in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and crop. For a formal employee (CLT), annual pre-tax income ranges from BRL 20,000 to BRL 35,000. Self-employed operators may earn more during harvest seasons. Larger agribusinesses in states like Mato Grosso and São Paulo pay above average.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa to be a mobile farm operator in Brazil?**
A: It is possible but restricted. The VITEM V employer-sponsored visa is the main route, requiring proof that no local worker can fill the position. This is hard to prove for unskilled roles. Mercosur nationals have easier access via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: Do mobile farm operators need formal education?**
A: Formal education is not mandatory, but a technical course or NR-31 certification is highly valued. On-the-job training is common. A CNH category C or higher is required for any road travel.
