# Toolmaker and Related Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7222 (ISCO08)**

Toolmakers and related workers fabricate, maintain, and repair precision tools, dies, jigs, fixtures, and molds used in manufacturing processes. They operate machine tools like lathes, milling machines, and grinders, and interpret technical drawings to produce components to tight tolerances. In Brazil, this occupation is essential in the automotive, aerospace, and machinery sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts with an apprenticeship or technical course, advancing to senior toolmaker, then to tooling engineer or supervisor. Some specialize in CNC programming or mold design. The path can also lead to quality control or process engineering. Continuous upskilling in CAD/CAM and automation is crucial for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual CNC programming and tool path generation
- Simple repetitive machine operations (e.g., loading/unloading workpieces, starting machining cycles)
- Routine dimensional measurement and quality recording (manual measurement with calipers and micrometers)
- Paper-based drawing management and document archiving
- Basic troubleshooting (based on fixed diagnostic procedures)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted tool path optimization (automatic collision avoidance, improved machining efficiency)
- Machine learning-based online quality inspection (real-time prediction of dimensional deviations)
- Digital twin simulation of machining processes (pre-validate fixture designs)
- Smart production scheduling system integration (automatic assignment of machine tasks)
- Augmented reality-assisted assembly and debugging (overlaying virtual guidance)

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment in complex fixture design and debugging
- On-site diagnosis and repair of multi-process integration issues.
- Ability to communicate non-standard requirements with clients/engineers
- Safety responsibility and process innovation decisions
- Precision manual finishing and assembly touch

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX machining modules)
- Robot-assisted loading/unloading programming and integration
- Python or MATLAB for machining data analysis
- Basics of additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and smart tool management
- Quality control data visualization (e.g., Power BI)

**Key growth areas:**

- Automotive
- Aerospace
- Industrial Machinery
- Mold and Die Making

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for toolmakers in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region; entry-level toolmakers earn around 30,000 BRL/year, while senior ones can earn over 72,000 BRL/year. The median is about 48,000 BRL/year. (Note: These figures are illustrative; official statistics should be consulted.)

**Q: Can a foreign toolmaker get a work visa for Brazil easily?**
A: It is possible but requires an employer sponsor and approval by the Ministry of Labor (CNIg). The process is bureaucratic and can take months. Priority is given to roles with proven skill shortages. Mercosur nationals have easier access via the Residence Agreement.

**Q: How long does it take to become a certified toolmaker in Brazil?**
A: Typically 2-3 years for a technical course or apprenticeship. Full proficiency and journeyman status take about 4-6 years of on-the-job experience.
