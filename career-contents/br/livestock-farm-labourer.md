# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

Livestock farm labourers perform manual tasks on farms raising cattle, pigs, sheep, poultry, and other animals. Duties include feeding, watering, cleaning enclosures, milking, herding, assisting with births, and basic health monitoring. The work is physically demanding and often involves long hours outdoors in varied weather conditions. Labourers typically work under the supervision of farm managers or owners, and while no formal education is required, on-the-job training is provided. The role is essential to Brazil's large agricultural sector, particularly in states like Mato Grosso, Minas Gerais, and Rio Grande do Sul.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 540,599. Brazil is a global leader in livestock production, ensuring steady demand for farm labourers. However, increasing mechanization and adoption of precision farming may reduce the need for unskilled manual labour over the next decade. Employment is expected to remain stable in small and medium-sized farms, while large commercial operations may shift toward skilled workers. Government programs supporting family farming provide some buffer. The occupation is sensitive to economic cycles and commodity prices, particularly beef and poultry exports.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression is limited without further education or training. Labourers may advance to senior herder, milking supervisor, or farm foreman roles after years of experience. Some transition into self-employment as small-scale livestock farmers, often with government support. Upskilling through technical courses from SENAR (e.g., animal nutrition, artificial insemination) can open paths to specialized technician roles. However, most remain in the occupation, as upward mobility is constrained by low formal education requirements and a flat hierarchy.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a livestock farm labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries for livestock farm labourers in Brazil are typically at or slightly above the national minimum wage, reflecting the unskilled nature of the work. Income varies by region, employer, and experience, but is generally low compared to other sectors.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a livestock farm labourer in Brazil?**
A: It is possible but uncommon. Employers must sponsor a VITEM V visa and demonstrate that no local worker is available. Mercosur nationals (e.g., from Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay) can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for an easier process.

**Q: What are the main challenges of this occupation in Brazil?**
A: The work is physically demanding with long hours outdoors. Career progression is limited, and income is low. Automation and mechanization are slowly reducing demand for manual labour, but the role remains essential for smaller farms.
