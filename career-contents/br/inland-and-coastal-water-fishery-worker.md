# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and coastal waters fishery workers catch fish, crustaceans, and molluscs in rivers, lakes, and coastal areas using nets, traps, and lines, often operating small boats in artisanal or small-scale commercial settings. The role is physically demanding, weather-dependent, and concentrated in Brazil's Amazon basin and coastal communities, with many workers in informal arrangements. Sustainability concerns and overfishing are pressing issues, while aquaculture offers alternative opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is typically flat; most workers remain independent fishers or crew members. Some advance to boat owners or shift to aquaculture, fish processing, or tourism-related activities. Formal training is rare, and experience is key. The occupation offers little upward mobility, and many workers exit due to low income or physical strain.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- aquaculture
- sustainable fishing
- coastal tourism
- artisanal fisheries

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for this occupation in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are low and often seasonal; many workers are informal. Monthly income typically ranges near the minimum wage (around BRL 1,300 in 2024) but can be lower due to seasonality. Earnings depend on catch volume and market prices.

**Q: Can foreign nationals work as fishery workers in Brazil?**
A: No. This occupation does not meet the criteria for employer-sponsored work visas (e.g., VITEM V) or the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which require higher-skilled roles. There is no specific visa pathway for foreign fishers.

**Q: What are the main challenges faced by fishery workers?**
A: Key challenges include declining fish stocks due to overfishing and environmental degradation, intense competition, hazardous working conditions, and lack of social protection. Climate change also threatens traditional fishing grounds.
