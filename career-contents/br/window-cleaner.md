# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners manually clean windows, glass surfaces, and frames in residential, commercial, and industrial buildings. They use ladders, scaffolding, or hydraulic platforms and must follow safety protocols. In Brazil, most window cleaners work for specialized cleaning companies or are self-employed. The job requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and a tolerance for heights. It is an entry-level occupation with low formal education barriers, though safety training is mandatory.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 5,558. Employment for window cleaners in Brazil is expected to remain stable, driven by ongoing construction and maintenance of commercial buildings. Urban centers like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro offer more opportunities. However, informal and undocumented work is common, which limits wage growth and social protections. New safety regulations (NR-35) may push more workers into formal employment, slightly improving conditions. Automation (e.g., drone cleaning) poses a minor long-term threat but is unlikely to replace manual cleaning in complex settings.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for window cleaners in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience. Entry-level workers earn around the minimum wage (R$1,320/month in 2024). Experienced or specialized cleaners (high-rise) can earn up to R$2,500/month. Self-employed workers may earn more depending on client volume.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa to work as a window cleaner in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult because the occupation is considered low-skilled. An employer must sponsor a VITEM V visa and prove no qualified Brazilian is available. Mercosur citizens have an easier path via the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which allows free movement for work.

**Q: What training is required to become a window cleaner in Brazil?**
A: No formal education is required, but a mandatory NR-35 safety-at-height course is needed if working above 2 meters. The course typically lasts 40 hours and costs R$200–R$800. On-the-job training is common.
