# Personal Services Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5169 (ISCO08)**

Personal Services Workers Not Elsewhere Classified perform a variety of personal care and support tasks in private households, hotels, or other settings, including roles such as dog walker, butler, personal assistant, or concierge. In Brazil, the occupation is largely informal, with limited formal employment opportunities. Demand is growing in luxury hospitality and affluent urban households, but most workers operate without formal contracts or labor protections.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a personal services worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by role and location. Informal workers earn around BRL 1,500–3,000 per month, while formal positions in luxury hotels or wealthy households can reach BRL 4,000–6,000. Hourly rates for dog walkers or personal assistants are typically BRL 20–50.

**Q: Can I get a work visa for Brazil as a personal services worker?**
A: It is very difficult. The VITEM V visa requires employer sponsorship and proof that the role requires specialized skills, which is rare for this occupation. Mercosur Residence is an option for nationals of member countries if they can secure formal employment, but informal work is not recognized.

**Q: Are there any mandatory certifications to work as a personal services worker in Brazil?**
A: No, there are no mandatory certifications. However, certain roles may require a driver's license (e.g., for driving clients) or first aid training (for caregiving). Formal qualifications are not needed for entry-level positions.
