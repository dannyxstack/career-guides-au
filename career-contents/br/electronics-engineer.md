# Electronics Engineer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2152 (ISCO08)**

Electronics engineers design, develop, test, and supervise the manufacturing of electronic equipment and systems, including communications, control systems, and instrumentation. In Brazil, they work across telecommunications, automation, consumer electronics, and energy sectors, often in industrial hubs like São Paulo, Campinas, and Manaus. The role requires a bachelor's degree in electronic engineering and registration with the regional engineering council (CREA).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior engineer or analyst, advancing to senior engineer, project manager, or technical specialist. With experience, one can move into R&D, systems architecture, or consultancy. Post-graduate specializations (e.g., telecommunications, embedded systems) enhance prospects. Senior roles often require a master's degree or MBA. Many engineers eventually transition into management or entrepreneurship.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use EDA software for schematic and PCB layout design and verification.
- Perform standardized electronic component testing and parameter verification
- Generate engineering documents and reports that meet standards
- Simple troubleshooting and repetitive debugging

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI simulation tools to quickly optimize circuit design parameters

- Collaborates with digital twin systems for virtual prototype validation.
- Assisted technical literature search and patent analysis via NLP tools

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for an electronics engineer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. For a mid-level engineer (5-10 years), expect BRL 8,000–15,000 per month. Senior roles can reach BRL 20,000+ in industries like telecom and automation. Public sector pays less but offers stability.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as an electronics engineer in Brazil?**
A: The most common is the VITEM V (temporary work visa), sponsored by a Brazilian employer. The process involves a labor contract, approval from the Ministry of Labor (CNIg), and registration with the Federal Police. Mercosur nationals can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for easier entry.

**Q: Is electronics engineering on Brazil's shortage list?**
A: Not officially, but demand is steady, especially in telecommunications and automation. Certain regions like Manaus (electronics hub) may have localized shortages. For immigration, it is not a priority occupation but remains viable for employer sponsorship.
