# Undertaker and Embalmer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5163 (ISCO08)**

Undertakers and embalmers prepare deceased bodies for burial or cremation, performing tasks such as embalming, restoration, and cosmetic application. They also coordinate funeral arrangements, manage logistics, and provide support to grieving families. In Brazil, this occupation is regulated and requires specific technical training and certification. Professionals work in funeral homes, cemeteries, and crematoria, often collaborating with funeral directors and clergy. The role demands empathy, attention to detail, and emotional resilience due to its sensitive nature.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a funeral assistant or apprentice, advancing to embalmer/thanatopraxist after certification. Experienced professionals may become funeral directors, managing entire funeral homes or opening their own businesses. Specialization in advanced restorative arts or forensic embalming can lead to higher income and prestige. Continuous education in new techniques and regulations is essential. Some professionals transition to teaching or consulting roles within the mortuary science field.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for undertakers and embalmers in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and employer. Entry-level professionals earn around minimum wage to 2,500 BRL monthly, while experienced embalmers and funeral directors can earn 4,000 to 8,000 BRL or more. Large cities like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro offer higher pay. Income can be supplemented by commission or on-call work.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as an undertaker or embalmer in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The candidate must have a job offer from a Brazilian funeral home that sponsors a VITEM V work visa. The employer must demonstrate that the foreigner has specialized skills not available locally. Recognition of foreign qualifications may be required, and the process can be lengthy. Mercosur nationals have an easier pathway under the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: Is certification always required to work as an embalmer in Brazil?**
A: Yes, in most states a technical diploma in thanatopraxy from an accredited institution is mandatory. Some states also require registration with a professional council. Without certification, one may work as an assistant but cannot perform embalming independently. Certification ensures compliance with health and safety regulations.
