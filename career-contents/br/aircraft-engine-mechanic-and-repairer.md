# Aircraft Engine Mechanic and Repairer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7232 (ISCO08)**

Aircraft engine mechanics and repairers inspect, maintain, and repair aircraft engines, ensuring safety and performance. They work in hangars, repair stations, and on airfields, using specialized tools and diagnostic equipment. In Brazil, these professionals are essential for the country's growing aviation sector, including commercial airlines, general aviation, and Embraer's aerospace industry. Certification by the National Civil Aviation Agency (ANAC) is mandatory. Employment is concentrated in major cities with airports and in the São José dos Campos region (Embraer hub).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or junior mechanic, advancing to lead mechanic, inspector, or supervisor. Specialization in specific engine types (e.g., Pratt & Whitney, CFM International) or roles (e.g., line maintenance, overhaul) can enhance earnings. Experienced workers may become instructors or move into management. The field values ANAC certifications and type ratings, which can open doors to higher-paying positions at major airlines or Embraer.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an aircraft engine mechanic in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, location, and employer. Entry-level mechanics typically earn between BRL 2,000 and BRL 4,000 per month, while experienced senior mechanics can earn BRL 6,000 to BRL 10,000 or more. Major airlines and Embraer tend to pay higher wages.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to get a work visa as an aircraft mechanic?**
A: Yes, generally you need a job offer from a Brazilian employer to apply for a VITEM V work visa. The employer must obtain authorization from the Ministry of Labor. However, if you are a national of a Mercosur country, you can apply for the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which does not require a prior job offer.

**Q: Is ANAC certification recognized internationally?**
A: ANAC certification is valid only in Brazil. To work abroad, mechanics often need to obtain certification from the local aviation authority (e.g., FAA in the US, EASA in Europe). However, experience and training in Brazil can help in the conversion process.
