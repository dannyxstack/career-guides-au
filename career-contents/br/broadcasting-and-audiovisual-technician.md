# Broadcasting and Audiovisual Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3521 (ISCO08)**

Broadcasting and audiovisual technicians operate and maintain equipment used for television, radio, film, and live events, including cameras, sound systems, lighting, and editing software. They work in studios, at remote locations, or in post-production facilities, ensuring high-quality audio and video signals. In Brazil, these professionals are essential for the country's large media industry, which includes major broadcasters like Globo and Record, as well as a growing streaming and independent production sector. The role often requires technical expertise, creativity, and the ability to work under pressure in live settings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression often starts as an assistant or trainee, moving to operator and then senior technician or specialist in areas like sound, lighting, or camera. Experienced technicians can become technical directors, supervisors, or freelance consultants. Many professionals diversify into adjacent fields like multimedia production or IT support for broadcasting. Certification and continuous learning in new technologies (e.g., 4K/8K, IP-based systems) are key to advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated audio/video editing and mixing
- Automatic signal quality monitoring and alarm
- Standard format conversion and file transcoding
- Automatic subtitle generation and synchronization
- Rule-based content distribution scheduling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted real-time audio noise reduction and restoration
- Smart teleprompter and auto lens tracking
- Automated live streaming director switching recommendation
- AI-driven post-production color grading and effects generation
- Real-time analysis of audience interaction data to adjust content

**Human moat:**

- Complex wiring, debugging, and troubleshooting at large events
- Real-time decision-making in response to emergencies (e.g., equipment failure, severe weather)
- Communicate in real-time with directors, hosts, and musicians to understand artistic intent.
- Maintenance and operation of non-standard equipment (old analog consoles, special cameras)
- ultimate responsibility for ensuring safe broadcast and compliance of programs

**Skills to build:**

- AI tools in DaVinci Resolve or Adobe Premiere Pro (auto-tagging, scene editing)
- Python or node-based automation workflows (e.g., Nuke, Houdini)
- Cloud broadcasting and remote production (e.g., AWS Elemental, LiveU)
- Immersive audio production (Dolby Atmos, Spatial Audio)
- Basic network engineering (IP video streaming, SRT, NDI)
- Project management and cross-departmental communication

**Key growth areas:**

- digital TV
- streaming services
- live events
- film production

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a broadcasting technician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. Entry-level technicians earn around BRL 24,000–36,000 annually; senior technicians can earn BRL 48,000–72,000. Freelancers may have higher but irregular income.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a broadcasting technician in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but they need an employer-sponsored work visa (VITEM V) or, if from a Mercosur country, can apply for residence under the Mercosur Agreement. The employer must demonstrate that the position cannot be filled locally.

**Q: Is registration with CREA mandatory for broadcasting technicians?**
A: It depends on the state and specific duties. For technicians handling electrical or engineering aspects, CREA may be required. For production roles, it is generally not mandatory.
