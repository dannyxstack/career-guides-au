# Social Work and Counselling Professionals — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2635 (ISCO08)**

Social work and counselling professionals in Brazil address social and emotional issues, providing support to individuals, families, and communities. They work in diverse settings such as healthcare, education, social services, and NGOs, focusing on mental health, child welfare, poverty alleviation, and human rights. The role requires empathy, strong communication, and problem-solving skills, along with a bachelor's degree in social work (Serviço Social) and registration with the regional council (CRESS).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 148,801. Brazil's social work demand is driven by persistent inequality, an aging population, and growing mental health awareness. The 2022-2025 job outlook is positive, with expansion in public healthcare (SUS), social assistance (CRAS/CREAS), and school social services. However, competition in major cities is high, and salaries in the public sector are modest. The profession is expected to grow 5-7% annually, with increased need in disaster response and community development.

With experience and postgraduate specialization (e.g., mental health, family therapy), professionals can move into supervisory roles, program coordination, or teaching. Some pursue clinical social work or private practice, though regulation limits independent counselling. Senior roles include policy development and management in government or NGOs.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for social workers in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level social workers in the public sector earn around 30,000-45,000 BRL annually. With experience and specialization, salaries can reach 60,000-80,000 BRL in large cities or federal positions. Private practice or clinical roles may vary widely.

**Q: Can a foreign social worker obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The main route is an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa, requiring a formal job offer from a Brazilian organization that demonstrates the need for a foreign professional. Alternatively, Mercosur nationals can use the Residence Agreement. Note that CRESS registration requires a Brazilian degree or revalidation, which is complex for foreigners.

**Q: Is social work a regulated profession in Brazil?**
A: Yes, social work (Serviço Social) is regulated. All practitioners must hold a bachelor's degree from a recognized institution and register with the Regional Council of Social Work (CRESS) in their state. Without registration, practice is illegal.
