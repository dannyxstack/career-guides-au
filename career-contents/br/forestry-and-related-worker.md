# Forestry and Related Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry and related workers in Brazil perform manual tasks related to planting, cultivating, maintaining, and harvesting forests, as well as conserving biodiversity and managing natural resources. They work in the Amazon rainforest, reforestation projects, timber companies, and environmental conservation areas. Tasks include tree felling, log extraction, weed control, fire prevention, and ecosystem monitoring. The role often requires physical endurance, familiarity with forest machinery, and knowledge of safety regulations.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 82,112. Brazil's forestry sector is evolving with increased focus on sustainable practices, reforestation, and bioeconomy. Employment demand is driven by environmental restoration programs, Amazon conservation policies, and growing demand for certified timber. However, mechanization and automation (e.g., drones for mapping) may reduce demand for low-skilled manual workers. Skilled workers with training in sustainable forestry and environmental monitoring will be more sought after. Regional variations persist, with the Northern and Central-West regions offering the most opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts as a field laborer, moving to specialized roles such as forest firefighter, tree climbing specialist, or machinery operator. With experience and further education (e.g., technical course), workers can become supervisors, forest technicians, or environmental inspectors. Some advance to management positions in timber or conservation companies. Certifications in safety, pesticide application, or first aid can enhance prospects. There is also a pathway to self-employment as a contractor for reforestation or logging services.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry worker in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level forestry workers earn around R$1,500 to R$2,500 per month, though experienced workers in specialized roles (e.g., forest firefighter) can earn up to R$4,000. Salaries vary by region, with the Amazon often offering higher pay due to remoteness.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a forestry worker in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. Most visas require an employer sponsor and proof that no Brazilian worker is available. The VITEM V visa is the main route, often requiring approval from the Ministry of Labour. Mercosur citizens have an easier path through the Mercosur Residence Agreement, but low-skilled roles are rarely sponsored.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for a forestry worker?**
A: With experience and training, you can move into supervisory roles, become a forest technician, or specialize in areas like fire management or machinery operation. A technical degree can lead to positions in environmental compliance or reforestation project management.
