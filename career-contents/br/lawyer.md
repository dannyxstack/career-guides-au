# Lawyer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2611 (ISCO08)**

Lawyers in Brazil provide legal advice and representation to individuals, businesses, and government entities. They interpret laws, draft legal documents, and argue cases in court. The profession requires a bachelor's degree in law (Bacharelado em Direito), registration with the Brazilian Bar Association (OAB), and a licensing exam. Brazil's legal system is civil law-based, and lawyers often specialize in areas like corporate, criminal, labor, or tax law. The job involves high responsibility, long hours, and continuous learning due to evolving legislation.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,099,045. Brazil's legal market is stable but highly competitive, with a large number of law graduates each year. Demand is consistent in large cities like São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Brasília, especially in corporate law, litigation, and compliance. Economic fluctuations and regulatory changes influence hiring. The profession is not on official shortage lists, but niche specializations (e.g., digital law, mergers & acquisitions) can offer better opportunities. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior associate at a law firm or public defender's office, then to senior associate, partner, or opening an independent practice. Some lawyers transition to in-house counsel for companies, enter the judiciary or public prosecution, or pursue academia. Specialization and networking are crucial. Many lawyers obtain postgraduate degrees (LL.M., MBA) to differentiate. Pro Bono work and social impact roles are growing. The path requires resilience due to initial low salaries and intense competition.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a lawyer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely. Entry-level lawyers earn around BRL 36,000–60,000 per year. Mid-career lawyers in firms can earn BRL 80,000–200,000, while partners or top earners may exceed BRL 500,000. Public sector lawyers earn according to fixed salary tables (e.g., public defender's salary starts around BRL 100,000).

**Q: Can a foreign lawyer work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. You need an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa and must obtain revalidation of your law degree (through a Brazilian university) and pass the OAB exam to practice. This is a lengthy and difficult process, often requiring a full law degree equivalency. Many foreign lawyers work as foreign legal consultants without OAB registration, but cannot practice Brazilian law.

**Q: Is there high demand for lawyers in Brazil?**
A: Demand is moderate but highly competitive due to a large number of law graduates annually. Opportunities are better in large urban centers and specialized fields like corporate law, compliance, and digital law. The profession is not on official shortage lists.
