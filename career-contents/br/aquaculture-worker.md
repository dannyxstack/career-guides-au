# Aquaculture Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6221 (ISCO08)**

Aquaculture workers in Brazil are involved in the cultivation and harvesting of aquatic organisms such as fish, shrimp, mollusks, and algae. Their tasks include preparing ponds and tanks, feeding stock, monitoring water quality, controlling disease, and managing breeding and harvesting operations. They work in coastal and inland farms, often in rural or remote areas, and may operate boats, nets, and aerators. The role requires physical stamina and basic knowledge of biology and water management.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 17,763. Brazil is one of the world's largest aquaculture producers, with tilapia and shrimp farming leading production. The sector is expanding due to growing domestic consumption and export opportunities, particularly in the Northeast and South regions. Government programs like 'Plano Safra' support small-scale producers. Employment is expected to grow steadily, though seasonal and informal work is common. Investment in technology and sustainability practices may create demand for more skilled workers.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a general worker, advancing to senior or specialist roles (e.g., hatchery technician, quality controller). With experience and technical training, workers can become supervisors or farm managers. Entrepreneurship is common, as many workers eventually start their own small farms. Additional certifications in aquaculture management or fish health can lead to higher positions. The field offers opportunities for those willing to relocate to rural areas and learn practical skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding system replaces manual timed feeding
- Water quality monitoring sensors and AI analysis replace manual sampling and testing.
- Underwater drone inspections replace manual observation of fish school status.
- Automated harvesting equipment replaces manual picking and sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted disease early warning system enhances early identification and prevention efficiency.
- Data-driven precision feeding decisions to optimize feed utilization
- Digital twin aquaculture model assists in environmental parameter regulation
- AI analysis of growth curves to optimize harvest time windows

**Human moat:**

- On-site judgement and emergency response to sudden illness or abnormal environmental conditions
- Intuition and experience for the overall ecological balance of farming systems
- Manual observation and early intervention for abnormal animal behavior (AI struggles to capture subtle signs)
- Cross-disciplinary coordination and decision-making with experts such as veterinarians and nutritionists

**Skills to build:**

- Sensor and automation equipment operation and maintenance
- Data processing and basic statistics (interpreting breeding data)
- Use of AI Tools (e.g., Disease Diagnosis Applications)
- IoT system management and troubleshooting
- Biosecurity and sustainable aquaculture practices
- Basic programming and scripting (automated workflow customization)

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable aquaculture
- tilapia farming
- shrimp farming
- inland fisheries

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an aquaculture worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and scale of operation. Entry-level workers often earn around the minimum wage (approx. R$1,300/month), while experienced supervisors can earn R$2,500–3,500/month. Benefits may include housing or food in remote farms.

**Q: Can foreign aquaculture workers get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: It is difficult due to the manual nature of the work. Work visas (VITEM V) require employer sponsorship and proof that the role cannot be filled locally. Mercosur nationals may use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for temporary stays, but skilled qualifications are still preferred.

**Q: What are the main career advancement opportunities?**
A: Workers can advance to senior technician, farm supervisor, or manager. Some start their own small aquaculture businesses. Technical certifications in fish health or hatchery management enhance prospects.
