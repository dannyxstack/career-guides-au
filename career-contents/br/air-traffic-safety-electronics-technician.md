# Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3155 (ISCO08)**

Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technicians install, maintain, and repair electronic systems used in air traffic control, including radar, communication, and navigation equipment. They work under the Brazilian Air Force's Department of Airspace Control (DECEA) or for contracted service providers, ensuring compliance with strict safety standards. This role requires expertise in electronics, radio frequency theory, and computer systems, as well as the ability to work under pressure during peak traffic hours. Technicians also perform routine inspections, diagnose faults, and update software to support Brazil's growing aviation sector.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level technicians typically start after a technical course or military training, then progress through on-the-job experience and additional DECEA certifications. Senior technicians can become shift supervisors, system specialists, or move into management roles such as Head of Maintenance or Regional Coordinator. Further education in electrical engineering or avionics can lead to careers in system design or regulatory oversight. Lateral moves within the air traffic control ecosystem (e.g., to equipment supply companies) are also common, but the core career path remains within the military-aligned public service structure.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are competitive and typically align with public sector pay scales, including benefits such as stability, retirement contributions, and hazard pay. For entry-level roles, compensation is above the national average, while senior technicians earn significantly more, especially in remote or high-traffic airports.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as an Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is highly restricted due to security requirements. Foreigners must obtain a VITEM V visa sponsored by a Brazilian employer, prove DECEA certification (or equivalent recognized by the Brazilian Air Force), and pass a security clearance. Mercosur nationals may use the Mercosur Residence Agreement to simplify the process.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for this role?**
A: Technicians can advance to senior technical positions, shift supervisors, or system managers within DECEA. With additional education, they may move into engineering roles, regulatory compliance, or training positions. Lateral moves to private sector companies that provide equipment or services to air traffic control are also possible.
