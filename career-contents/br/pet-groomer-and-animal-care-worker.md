# Pet Groomer and Animal Care Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5164 (ISCO08)**

Pet groomers and animal care workers provide grooming, hygiene, and basic care services for domestic animals such as dogs and cats. They perform tasks such as bathing, brushing, clipping fur, trimming nails, and cleaning ears, often in pet shops, veterinary clinics, or mobile grooming units. In Brazil, this occupation has grown with increased pet ownership and the humanization of pets, but work is frequently informal or self-employed. Training typically involves short vocational courses rather than formal degrees.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 172,364. The Brazilian pet care market has expanded steadily, driven by rising disposable incomes and a cultural shift toward treating pets as family members. Employment for pet groomers and animal care workers is expected to grow at an above-average rate in urban centers, particularly in the Southeast and South regions. However, competition is high due to low entry barriers, and many workers remain in the informal sector. Formalization and specialization (e.g., breed-specific grooming, mobile services) offer better income prospects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from assistant to senior groomer or shop manager. Some workers open their own pet grooming salons or mobile businesses. Specializing in advanced techniques (e.g., creative grooming, therapeutic baths) or expanding into related services (pet sitting, walking, boarding) can increase earnings. Certification from professional associations (e.g., ABP – Associação Brasileira de Pet Shop) adds credibility. The occupation offers flexible hours but modest income, especially for employees.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a pet groomer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region and experience. Entry-level groomers in pet shops earn about 1,200–2,000 BRL per month (pre-tax), while experienced self-employed groomers can make 3,000–5,000 BRL. Owners of established salons may earn higher but have business costs. Annual pre-tax income typically ranges from 15,000 to 60,000 BRL.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a pet groomer in Brazil?**
A: It is possible but difficult for non-Mercosur citizens. The main route is the VITEM V employer-sponsored work visa, but pet grooming shops rarely sponsor foreign workers due to abundant local labor. Mercosur citizens can apply for residence to work as self-employed. In practice, most foreign pet groomers are already legal residents through family or Mercosur.

**Q: What are the best areas for career growth in pet grooming?**
A: Specializing in creative grooming (e.g., intricate dyeing, haircuts), working with high-demand breeds, offering mobile grooming services, or opening a full-service pet shop. Adding services like pet sitting, training, or selling pet products can also boost income.
