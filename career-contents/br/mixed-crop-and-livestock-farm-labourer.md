# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9213 (ISCO08)**

Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourers perform a variety of manual tasks on farms that combine crop cultivation and animal husbandry. Their duties include planting, tending, and harvesting crops; feeding, watering, and herding livestock; maintaining fences, equipment, and buildings; and assisting with basic farm operations. This role is essential to Brazil's diverse agricultural sector, which spans grains, coffee, cattle, and more. Workers often operate in seasonal cycles, requiring flexibility and physical stamina. The occupation is prevalent in rural areas, especially in the Midwest, Southeast, and South regions, and is a primary entry point for unskilled agricultural workers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 10,578. Employment for mixed crop and livestock farm labourers in Brazil is expected to remain stable, driven by the country's strong agribusiness sector. Demand is supported by large-scale commodity production (soy, corn, beef) as well as growing niches like organic farming and agroecology. However, mechanization and precision agriculture may reduce the need for manual labour in some segments. Seasonal work cycles and informal employment remain common. The post-pandemic recovery of domestic and export markets, plus government support for family farming, may sustain job numbers. Yet, conditions are often precarious, with low wages and limited formal contracts.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for farm labourers is limited without further training. With experience, workers may become specialized equipment operators, herdsmen, or team leaders. Some advance to farm managers after years of on-the-job learning and technical courses. Others transition to related roles in agricultural input sales, logistics, or rural extension. Formal education (e.g., technical course in agriculture) can significantly improve prospects. However, many labourers remain in informal or temporary positions. The sector sees high turnover, and advancement often requires relocating to more commercial farms or agribusiness hubs.

- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots

**Where AI augments the role:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are low, usually around the national minimum wage. In 2024, the full-time equivalent pre-tax annual salary is approximately R$ 18,000, though actual wages vary by region, farm size, and formal/informal status. Workers may receive in-kind benefits like housing or food.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a farm labourer in Brazil under a work visa?**
A: Yes, but it is rare. The VITEM V visa requires employer sponsorship and a formal contract, which is uncommon for low-skilled agricultural work. Mercosur citizens have an easier path via the Residence Agreement. Overall, Brazil strongly prioritizes local workers for manual farm jobs, making migration difficult.

**Q: What training is needed to become a Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer?**
A: No formal education is required; most learning is on-the-job. However, courses from SENAR (Serviço Nacional de Aprendizagem Rural) in tractor operation, pesticide handling, or animal husbandry can improve skills and employability. A technical course in agriculture may open doors to higher-level roles.
