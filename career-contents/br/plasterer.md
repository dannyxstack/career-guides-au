# Plasterer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers apply plaster, stucco, and similar materials to walls, ceilings, and partitions, creating smooth or decorative surfaces. They work on new construction, renovations, and restoration projects, using hand tools and power equipment to mix and apply materials. In Brazil, plasterers are essential in the booming construction sector, particularly in residential and commercial real estate development, as well as infrastructure projects. The role requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and knowledge of different plastering techniques. With Brazil's ongoing urbanization and housing demands, plasterers remain in steady demand across the country.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Plasterers typically start as apprentices or helpers, learning on the job or through vocational courses. With experience, they can become master plasterers, supervisors, or self-employed contractors. Some specialize in decorative plasterwork (e.g., cornices, molding) or restoration of historic buildings. Career progression may involve moving into project management or starting a construction business. Formal certification from institutions like SENAI can enhance credentials and lead to higher pay.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Construction
- Renovation
- Infrastructure
- Finishing

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a plasterer in Brazil?**
A: Annual salaries for plasterers in Brazil typically range from BRL 24,000 to BRL 48,000, depending on experience, region, and employer. Senior or specialized plasterers can earn more, especially in large cities like São Paulo or Rio de Janeiro.

**Q: Can a foreign plasterer get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it requires an employer sponsor and authorization from the Ministry of Labor (CNIg). The company must prove that no local worker is available for the job. While possible, it may be easier for non-Mercosur citizens with specialized skills. Mercosur nationals have easier access via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for plasterers in Brazil?**
A: Experienced plasterers can become supervisors, start their own contracting business, or specialize in decorative plastering or historical restoration. With additional training, they may move into project management or related construction trades.
