# Customs and Border Inspector — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3351 (ISCO08)**

Customs and border inspectors in Brazil, known as Analistas Tributários da Receita Federal do Brasil, work under the Ministry of Economy to enforce customs laws, inspect cargo and passengers at ports, airports, and border crossings, combat smuggling, and assess duties and taxes. They ensure compliance with trade regulations and contribute to national security and revenue collection.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with approval in a competitive public exam, followed by on-the-job training. Inspectors can advance to supervisory roles, regional coordination, or specialized units like risk analysis or fraud investigation. Promotions are based on seniority, performance, and additional certifications, with potential to reach senior analyst or management positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically scan passports and verify visa authenticity
- Automated verification and risk assessment of standard cargo declarations
- Automated matching of basic biometric information (e.g., fingerprints, facial features)
- Routine passenger information entry and system updates
- Automated processing for template-based document filling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted risk analysis systems help identify high-suspicion passengers or cargo

- Database-linked early warning system enhances smuggling pattern recognition capabilities
- Automated report generation tools save time on paperwork
- AI-assisted decision support system providing compliance advice

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a customs inspector in Brazil?**
A: Starting salaries for Analistas Tributários range from R$10,000 to R$15,000 per month, with senior inspectors earning up to R$25,000, plus benefits. These figures are subject to official pay scales and are conservative estimates.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a customs inspector in Brazil?**
A: Generally, no. Customs inspector positions require Brazilian citizenship or at least permanent residency, and are typically filled through public exams open only to citizens. Work visas are not a viable route for this career.

**Q: What are the main career advancement opportunities?**
A: Advancement includes promotion to senior analyst, team leader, or specialized roles in risk management, anti-smuggling, or international trade. Further education and additional certifications can accelerate progression.
