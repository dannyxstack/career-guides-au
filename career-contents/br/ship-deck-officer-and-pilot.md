# Ship’s Deck Officer and Pilot — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3152 (ISCO08)**

Ships’ deck officers and pilots are responsible for navigating and safely operating vessels in Brazil’s extensive maritime, river, and port environments. They command watch, manage cargo operations, ensure compliance with maritime regulations, and pilot ships in and out of harbors. These professionals hold advanced certifications and often specialize in offshore oil and gas support, container shipping, or coastal trade.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a deck cadet on merchant vessels, advancing to third officer, second officer, chief officer, and captain. River and harbor pilots follow a separate certification track after years of sea service. Officers may move into shipping management, maritime safety inspection, or pilotage. Continuing professional development through STCW courses and simulator training is essential for career advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data integration and collision avoidance computation
- Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS) automatic route planning and updates
- Repetitive data collection and analysis in weather route optimization
- Automated recording and report generation of routine navigation logs
- Automatic interpretation of sensor data in ship performance monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Combine AI weather models and real-time ocean data to make more accurate route adjustment decisions
- Use digital twins and predictive maintenance systems to identify equipment issues early
- Optimizes workforce and fatigue management through AI-assisted crew scheduling systems
- Computer vision-based berthing and anchoring operation assistance
- AI-generated emergency scenario simulations for training and crisis decision-making rehearsals

**Human moat:**

- Take ultimate decision-making responsibility in emergencies (e.g., engine failure, man overboard)
- Statutory qualifications and experience required by international maritime regulations (SOLAS, STCW)
- Cross-cultural crew management and interpersonal communication (multi-national team collaboration on board)
- Manual control and situational judgment in complex port environments (e.g., tugboat coordination)
- Long-term experience (intuitive judgment) with hulls, machinery, and marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced configuration and troubleshooting of electronic charts and autopilot systems
- Basic data analysis (Python/R) for interpreting ship sensor data
- Digital twin and predictive maintenance software operation
- Maritime cybersecurity knowledge (to counter onboard system attacks)
- Advanced English Communication and Cross-Cultural Leadership
- Use of fatigue management and compliance software (e.g., rest time monitoring)

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore Oil & Gas
- Port Operations
- River Navigation
- Maritime Security

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for a deck officer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary with rank and type of vessel. Entry-level third officers earn around 5,000-8,000 BRL per month, while captains on offshore support vessels can exceed 20,000 BRL monthly. Bonuses for long voyages and offshore work are common.

**Q: Can a foreign deck officer work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Foreigners need an employer-sponsored work visa (VITEM V) and must have their foreign CoC recognized by the Brazilian Navy. Mercosur nationals have an easier path via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.
