# Garden and Horticultural Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9214 (ISCO08)**

Garden and horticultural labourers perform manual tasks in gardening, landscaping, and horticulture, including planting, weeding, pruning, watering, and maintaining lawns, gardens, parks, nurseries, and greenhouses. In Brazil, this occupation is common in urban landscaping, agriculture, and public green spaces. Work is often seasonal and informal, with many labourers employed without formal contracts. The job requires physical stamina and basic knowledge of plants and tools. While barriers to entry are low, wages are typically at or slightly above the minimum wage. The occupation is not a primary migration route due to its low skill classification, but opportunities exist for Mercosur nationals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is possible through experience and specialization. Labourers can advance to crew leader, supervisor, or specialized roles such as pesticide applicator or irrigation technician. Some pursue short courses in landscape design or sustainable agriculture to start their own small landscaping businesses. In large agribusinesses, workers may move into machine operation or administrative roles. However, without additional training or certifications, upward mobility is limited, and many workers remain in entry-level positions. The occupation is often a stepping stone for those with limited formal education.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garden and horticultural labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are generally low, often around the national minimum wage (approximately R$1,500 per month). Formal employment may include benefits, but many workers earn daily wages or work informally. Income can vary by region and employer.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a garden labourer in Brazil?**
A: It is possible but difficult. Employers must prove that no qualified Brazilian worker is available, which is challenging for low-skilled positions. The VITEM V visa is the main option, but approval is not guaranteed. Mercosur nationals have a simpler path via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities for garden labourers in Brazil?**
A: With experience, workers can become supervisors, landscape designers, or start their own small business. Certifications in horticulture, pest management, or irrigation can help. However, formal career progression is limited without additional education.
