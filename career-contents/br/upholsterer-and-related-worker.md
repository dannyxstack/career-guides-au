# Upholsterer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7534 (ISCO08)**

Upholsterers and related workers in Brazil specialize in covering furniture, vehicle seats, and other items with fabric, leather, or synthetic materials. They work in workshops, factories, or on-site, using hand tools and sewing machines. The occupation requires precision and attention to detail, often involving restoration of antiques or custom designs. In Brazil, upholsterers are part of the broader trades sector, with employment opportunities concentrated in furniture manufacturing, automotive assembly, and interior design. Many professionals learn through vocational courses offered by institutions like SENAI, and most work in small to medium-sized enterprises.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression for upholsterers typically starts as an apprentice or helper, advancing to master upholsterer or shop manager. Specialization in areas like automotive, marine, or antique restoration can enhance earnings. Some experienced professionals start their own workshops. Continuous learning, such as mastering digital design tools or new materials, is increasingly valued. The profession offers stable employment for those willing to adapt, though many remain self-employed.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD software for standardized layout and cutting
- Sewing machine operation for simple straight stitching
- Mechanical cutting and forming of foam and filling materials
- Automated recording of inventory management and material ordering
- Repetitive filling and fastening tasks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates and modifies soft package patterns
- AR/VR tools visualize final results, reducing rework
- Machine vision detects fabric defects, improving quality
- Digital cutting beds improve material utilization rates
- Smart supply chain systems optimize material procurement

**Human moat:**

- Customized complex patterns and hand-sewing techniques
- The ability to communicate with clients and understand personalized needs
- Experience handling special fabrics (e.g., antique textiles, leather)
- Flexibility for on-site installation, adjustment, and repair
- Comprehensive assessment of furniture structure and comfort

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/3D modeling software (e.g., Rhino, SketchUp)
- Proficient in operating digital cutting and sewing equipment
- Familiar with AR/VR presentation tools
- Develop project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and eco-design trends
- Learn basic programming to customize automated workflows

**Key growth areas:**

- Furniture
- Automotive
- Restoration
- Custom Design

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an upholsterer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region and experience. Entry-level workers usually earn around the minimum wage (R$1,320 per month in 2024), while experienced upholsterers can earn between R$2,500 to R$4,000 monthly. Specialists in automotive or luxury furniture may earn more. Self-employed workers have variable income.

**Q: Can a foreign upholsterer get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it requires a formal job offer from a Brazilian employer who must apply for authorization with the Ministry of Labor (CNIg). The VITEM V visa is the main route. For citizens of Mercosur countries, the Mercosur Residence Agreement offers a simpler process. Proof of qualifications (certificates or experience) is mandatory.

**Q: Is there demand for upholsterers in Brazil?**
A: Demand is moderate, with more opportunities in large cities and in sectors like furniture manufacturing, automotive interiors, and restoration. Competition is high for well-paying positions, but skilled craftsmen are often sought after for custom work.
