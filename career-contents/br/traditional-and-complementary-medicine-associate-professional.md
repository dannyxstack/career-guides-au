# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Associate Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and complementary medicine associate professionals in Brazil practice therapies such as acupuncture, herbal medicine, and homeopathy, often in private clinics or integrated healthcare settings. They work under the supervision of higher-level practitioners or autonomously where regulation permits. The field is growing due to rising interest in holistic health and preventive care, but formal recognition varies by modality. In Brazil, these professionals may need certification from recognized associations or registration with local health councils, depending on the state.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a traditional and complementary medicine associate professional in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by modality and region. Acupuncturists in private practice may earn between BRL 2,500 and 6,000 per month, while those in wellness centers or hospitals might earn BRL 3,000-8,000. Annual income can be BRL 30,000-96,000, depending on client volume and specialization.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a traditional medicine practitioner in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You typically need employer sponsorship (VITEM V) and recognition of your qualifications by Brazilian councils. For some modalities, you may need to pass local exams. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is easier for citizens of neighboring countries.

**Q: Are traditional medicine professions regulated in Brazil?**
A: Regulation varies. Acupuncture and homeopathy are recognized by the Federal Council of Medicine (CFM) but only for medical doctors. Other modalities like herbalism are less regulated. Practitioners often join voluntary associations for credibility.
