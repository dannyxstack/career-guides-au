# Government Regulatory Associate Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3359 (ISCO08)**

Government regulatory associate professionals enforce compliance with laws and regulations in areas such as taxation, labor, environment, and public health. They conduct inspections, audits, and investigations, issue fines or corrective orders, and advise on regulatory matters. These roles are typically filled through competitive public service exams (concursos) and are central to Brazil's governance and rule of law.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 29,600. Employment outlook in Brazil is stable but constrained by fiscal austerity and hiring freezes. Demand exists for specialists in environmental, tax, and digital regulation as the government modernizes oversight. Opportunities are greatest in large agencies (Receita Federal, Ministério do Trabalho, IBAMA) and at state/municipal levels. However, public sector modernization may reduce manual inspection roles in favor of data-driven compliance.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a government regulatory associate professional in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by agency, level, and region. Entry-level through public exam typically starts at 4,000–8,000 BRL/month, rising to 15,000+ BRL/month for senior roles. Additional benefits include stability, pension, and bonuses. Public sector salaries are published and subject to fiscal limits.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a government regulatory associate professional in Brazil?**
A: Extremely difficult. Most career positions require Brazilian citizenship and passing a public exam. Foreigners may only work in temporary technical cooperation roles via international agreements or as specialists contracted by international organizations. Permanent migration for this occupation is not feasible.

**Q: What are the key steps to become a regulatory associate professional in Brazil?**
A: Complete a bachelor's degree (Law, Accounting, etc.), prepare for and pass the public exam (concurso) for the desired agency. After approval, undergo training. Some areas require additional certifications (e.g., environmental licensing). Continuing education helps in promotion.
