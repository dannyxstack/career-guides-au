# Other Cleaning Workers — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other cleaning workers perform a variety of cleaning tasks in settings such as offices, hotels, hospitals, and residential buildings, using manual or light machinery. They ensure cleanliness, hygiene, and orderliness of interiors, surfaces, fixtures, and fittings. This occupation covers roles like janitors, maids, and cleaners not elsewhere classified. In Brazil, these workers are often employed directly or via outsourcing firms, with a high turnover rate. The work is physically demanding and typically requires no formal education, making it accessible to low-skilled workers, including recent migrants.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cleaning workers is limited but possible. Many start in entry-level cleaning roles and may advance to supervisory positions (e.g., cleaning supervisor) or specialize in industrial or hospital cleaning. Some gain experience to become self-employed contractors. However, without formal training, vertical mobility is low. Horizontal moves to housekeeping or janitorial work in higher-end venues can improve earnings. Long-term, many workers transition to other low-skilled service jobs.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to operate cleaning robots or smart cleaning equipment
- Basic computer operation and simple programming (e.g., setting cleaning routes)
- Understanding the Internet of Things (IoT) technology to monitor the status of cleaning equipment
- Knowledge of Disinfection and Chemical Safety in Learning Environments
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn data analysis fundamentals to optimize cleaning efficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- Hospitality
- Healthcare
- Residential Services
- Janitorial

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaner in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and sector, but generally range from the minimum wage (around R$1,412 per month in 2024) to R$1,800, with slight increases in formal sectors.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a cleaner in Brazil?**
A: Yes, especially if from a Mercosur country under the Residence Agreement. For others, employer sponsorship via VITEM V is possible but rare due to low skill requirements and preference for local workers.
