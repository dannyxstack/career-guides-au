# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists assess, plan, and implement rehabilitative programs for patients with physical impairments or disabilities due to injury, illness, or aging. Using exercises, manual therapy, and equipment, they help improve mobility, function, and quality of life. In Brazil, they work in hospitals, clinics, sports facilities, home care, and public health, often collaborating with other healthcare professionals.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 304,285. Brazil's aging population and rising chronic diseases like diabetes and obesity are increasing demand for physiotherapy. The public health system (SUS) and private sector are expanding rehabilitation services, especially in large cities. Government initiatives for primary care and home care also boost employment. However, regional disparities exist, with more opportunities in the Southeast and South.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression includes specialization in orthopedics, neurology, geriatrics, or sports physiotherapy; moving into management, teaching, or private practice. Many pursue graduate degrees or specialization courses. The field offers diverse settings from hospitals to sports clubs. Entrepreneurial physiotherapists often build successful clinics. Continuing education is essential for career advancement.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a physiotherapist in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region, experience, and sector. Entry-level positions typically start around the median for healthcare workers, while experienced physiotherapists in private practice or specialized fields can earn significantly higher. Public sector salaries follow civil service scales, often with benefits.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as a physiotherapist in Brazil?**
A: Foreign physiotherapists generally need an employer-sponsored VITEM V work visa. The employer must obtain approval from the Ministry of Labour and provide evidence of your diploma and CREFITO registration. Mercosur nationals can benefit from the simplified Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: Is my foreign physiotherapy degree recognized in Brazil?**
A: Foreign degrees must be revalidated by a Brazilian public university with an equivalent course. This process can take months and requires translations. Once revalidated, you must register with CREFITO to practice legally.
