# Mining and Metallurgical Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3117 (ISCO08)**

Mining and metallurgical technicians support engineers in the extraction and processing of minerals and metals. They conduct field surveys, monitor production processes, perform quality control tests, and ensure compliance with safety and environmental regulations. Common in Brazil's mining hubs like Minas Gerais and Pará, these technicians work in open-pit or underground mines, as well as processing plants. The role requires a mix of outdoor fieldwork and lab or office tasks, often in remote locations. Technicians may specialise in mineral exploration, ore processing, or metallurgical analysis.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 78,412. Brazil's mining sector, a key economic driver, faces fluctuating global commodity prices but long-term demand for iron ore, gold, and bauxite remains strong. New projects in the Amazon and revitalisation of older mines in Minas Gerais are expected to sustain technician demand. However, automation and stricter environmental licensing may slow hiring. The occupation is not on official shortage lists, but skilled technicians with experience in sustainable practices and remote operations are valued. The sector's cyclical nature means job openings rise during commodity booms.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from junior technician to senior or supervisory roles, often after 5–10 years of experience. Technicians can specialise in areas such as mineral processing, mine planning, or environmental compliance. Further education (e.g., engineering degree) opens pathways to management or consulting. Continuous learning of new technologies like automated drills and real-time data analytics is crucial. Many technicians eventually become operations managers or mine superintendents, especially in large companies like Vale or Anglo American.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual drafting of mine design and construction drawings
- Resource reserve estimation based on experience
- Manually write routine mining plans and progress reports
- Manual monitoring of equipment status and energy consumption
- On-site Inspections for Hazard Identification

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI for 3D modeling of ore deposits and resource assessment
- Optimizing autonomous truck routes and blasting plans using AI
- Using machine learning to predict rock mass stability and support requirements
- Integrating digital twin technology for real-time monitoring and adjustment of mining operations
- Quickly parse geological reports and compliance documents using natural language processing

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making under complex geological conditions
- Communication and coordination with government/community/Indigenous peoples
- Safety regulation enforcement and emergency incident command
- Multidisciplinary team collaboration integrating AI recommendations
- Comprehensive assessment of ethics and environmental impact

**Skills to build:**

- Geostatistics and machine learning applied to resource modeling
- Operation and configuration of automated scheduling systems (e.g., Minestar)
- Python/R data analysis and digital twin platform usage
- Mine simulation software (e.g., Vulcan, Surpac) with AI plugins
- Project Management and Cross-Departmental AI Solution Integration
- Data visualization and report automation tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainability
- Automation
- Remote Operations
- Deep Sea Mining

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for mining and metallurgical technicians in Brazil?**
A: Salaries range from R$ 35,000 to R$ 75,000 annually, depending on experience, location, and company size. Senior technicians at major mining firms can earn up to R$ 100,000.

**Q: Can a foreign technician easily get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Brazil does not have a points-based system. The employer must sponsor a VITEM V visa and obtain CNIg authorization, proving no qualified Brazilian worker is available. Mercosur nationals have easier access via the Mercosur Residence Agreement.
