# Valuer and Loss Assessor — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3315 (ISCO08)**

Valuers and loss assessors evaluate the value of real estate, machinery, antiques, and other assets for purchase, sale, taxation, or insurance purposes. Loss assessors specifically investigate insurance claims, determining policy coverage, quantifying losses, and negotiating settlements. In Brazil, these professionals often work in real estate (avaliadores de imóveis), insurance (peritos de seguros), or auctions. They combine market analysis, inspection, and legal knowledge to produce reliable reports. The role requires strong analytical skills, integrity, and familiarity with Brazilian property and insurance regulations.

Career progression typically starts as a junior appraiser or claims trainee within a real estate agency, bank, or insurance company. Advancing to senior valuer or loss assessor usually requires 3–5 years of experience, professional certifications, and a track record of accurate reports. Specialization in niche areas (e.g., luxury properties, industrial assets, or complex insurance claims) can lead to higher earnings and independent consultancy. Some may become partners in appraisal firms or instructors. Licensure (e.g., CRECI for real estate) is mandatory for certain roles, and continuing education is highly valued.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a valuer or loss assessor in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and sector. A junior real estate valuer earns around BRL 3,000–5,000 per month, while experienced professionals can earn BRL 8,000–15,000. Insurance loss assessors generally earn BRL 4,000–10,000. Independent valuers may have higher income but irregular work. All figures are pre-tax.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a valuer in Brazil without a job offer?**
A: Generally, no. The VITEM V visa requires a specific employer sponsor. Mercosur Residence is possible for citizens of member countries without a prior job offer. For others, the employer must demonstrate the need for a foreign professional.

**Q: What are the main career advancement opportunities for valuers in Brazil?**
A: Valuers can specialize in niche areas (luxury, commercial, industrial), become senior appraisers, start their own appraisal firms, or move into teaching. Some transition to investment analysis or property development. Certifications like the IBA valuations certificate can boost career growth.
