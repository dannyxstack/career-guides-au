# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technicians in Brazil operate and maintain diagnostic imaging devices such as X-ray, MRI, CT, and ultrasound machines, as well as equipment for radiation therapy. They work in hospitals, clinics, and diagnostic centers, ensuring accurate image capture and patient safety. This role requires technical proficiency, adherence to radiation protection protocols, and collaboration with radiologists and physicians. With Brazil's expanding healthcare network and aging population, demand for these technicians is steadily rising, particularly in urban and underserved regions.

Experienced technicians can specialize in modalities like MRI, CT, or oncology dosimetry, or move into supervisory roles (e.g., chief technician). Further education, such as a technology degree (Tecnólogo) or specialization, can lead to positions in equipment sales, training, or hospital administration. Some technicians pursue university degrees in radiology technology to qualify for higher-level roles.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a Medical Imaging Technician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience, but typically range from BRL 2,500 to BRL 5,000 per month in private clinics and hospitals. Public sector positions may offer lower base pay but better benefits. Specializing in advanced modalities like MRI or CT can increase earnings.

**Q: Can foreign-trained technicians easily obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Work visas (VITEM V) require an employer sponsor and proof that the applicant's qualifications meet Brazilian standards, including CRTR registration. The process can be bureaucratic, and the occupation is not on any shortage list that facilitates fast-track visas. Mercosur citizens may have an easier process under the residence agreement.

**Q: Is CRTR registration mandatory for all technicians in Brazil?**
A: Yes, any person performing radiology or imaging procedures must be registered with the CRTR in their state. Registration requires completion of a recognized technical course and passing a professional exam (when applicable). Working without registration is illegal.
