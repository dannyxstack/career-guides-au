# Tree And Shrub Crop Grower — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6112 (ISCO08)**

Tree and shrub crop growers in Brazil plan, organize, and perform farming operations to cultivate and harvest trees and shrubs such as coffee, cocoa, rubber, fruit trees (e.g., oranges, apples), and nut trees. They manage soil preparation, planting, irrigation, pest control, and harvesting. This occupation is vital to Brazil's agribusiness sector, particularly in coffee (the world's largest producer) and citrus (leading orange juice exporter). Work is predominantly in rural areas, often on family farms or large commercial plantations, and demands practical knowledge of crop cycles, machinery use, and sustainable farming practices.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm laborer or assistant, advancing to a grower or farm manager after gaining experience and possibly technical training. Many growers own or lease land, operating as self-employed farmers. In larger agribusinesses, opportunities exist to become supervisors, agronomists, or operational managers. Specialization in high-value crops (e.g., organic coffee, specialty cacao) can increase earnings. Horizontal moves to related areas like agricultural consultancy, input sales, or supply chain management are possible. Lifelong learning through courses offered by SENAR or SEBRAE enhances mobility.

**Human moat:**

- On-site coordination and negotiation with seasonal workers, cooperatives, and buyers.

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a tree and shrub crop grower in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region and crop. Small-scale growers may earn around the minimum wage (R$1,320/month in 2023), while experienced managers in large agribusinesses can earn up to R$5,000/month. Annual income for self-employed growers depends on harvest yields and market prices.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a tree and shrub crop grower in Brazil?**
A: It is challenging. The VITEM V visa requires an employer to prove that the role cannot be filled by a Brazilian. For low-skilled agricultural work, this is rarely accepted. The Mercosur Residence Agreement offers a simpler path for nationals of Mercosur countries, allowing them to work as independent growers.

**Q: How can I advance from a field worker to a farm manager?**
A: Gain experience, take technical courses from SENAR (e.g., in crop management or agribusiness), and learn about business planning. Many managers start as family farm successors. Networking with cooperatives and large employers also helps.
