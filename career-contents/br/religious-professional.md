# Religious Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2636 (ISCO08)**

Religious professionals include priests, ministers, rabbis, imams, and other spiritual leaders who conduct worship services, provide pastoral care, and perform religious rites. They work in churches, temples, mosques, and other faith-based institutions, offering guidance and community support. In Brazil, they are recognized under the Brazilian Classification of Occupations (CBO) and typically require theological education and denominational ordination. The role is deeply rooted in faith traditions, with responsibilities ranging from preaching and teaching to counseling and administering sacraments.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with lay ministry or assistant roles, followed by ordination and leadership of a congregation. Advancement may involve pursuing higher theological degrees (master's or doctorate) or specialized training in pastoral counseling, youth ministry, or conflict mediation. Senior positions such as bishop, archbishop, or denominational head require extensive experience and denominational approval. Some religious professionals transition into teaching, counseling, or nonprofit leadership.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a religious professional in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely; many are volunteers or receive modest stipends. Full-time clergy in large institutions may earn 30,000-60,000 BRL annually, while senior roles can reach 90,000 BRL or more in wealthy denominations.

**Q: What visa options exist for foreign religious professionals in Brazil?**
A: Foreign religious workers typically apply for the VITEM V temporary work visa, requiring employer sponsorship from a registered religious institution. Mercosur nationals may use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for easier residency.

**Q: Is theological education mandatory to become a religious professional in Brazil?**
A: While not legally mandatory, most denominations require at least a bachelor's degree in theology and ordination for leadership roles. Some smaller congregations may accept candidates without formal education.
