# Manufacturing Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9329 (ISCO08)**

Manufacturing labourers perform a variety of simple manual tasks in factories, such as loading and unloading materials, assembling products, packaging, and cleaning work areas. They typically work under direct supervision and are essential to production lines in industries like automotive, electronics, textiles, and food processing. This role requires physical stamina and the ability to follow instructions, but does not demand formal qualifications. Opportunities are concentrated in industrial regions of Brazil, including São Paulo, Minas Gerais, and the South. Employment is often formal with registered work contracts, though turnover can be high due to the repetitive nature of the work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is limited for manufacturing labourers. With experience and additional training (e.g., SENAI courses), workers can advance to machine operator, quality inspector, or team leader roles. Some transition to logistics or maintenance. The path to supervisory positions requires leadership skills and often further education. Without upskilling, long-term prospects are stagnant. The role is often a starting point for younger workers entering the labour market.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive material handling and palletizing
- Basic cleaning and tidying work
- Simple packaging and box sealing operation
- Manual quality visual inspection and counting
- fixed route material delivery

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use handheld terminals or AR glasses to receive digital instructions, improving accuracy
- Operate semi-automated equipment, such as collaborative robot-assisted assembly
- Monitor production line anomalies through data dashboards and report promptly
- Use AI-driven predictive maintenance tools to provide equipment status feedback
- Learn basic programming, adjust automation workstation parameters

**Human moat:**

- Flexible physical ability for handling unstructured scenarios (e.g., irregular workpiece handling)
- Ability to quickly adapt to task switching in high-mix, low-volume production
- Communication and adaptability to collaborate with colleagues on complex assembly
- Rapid diagnosis and manual intervention for on-site anomalies (e.g., equipment jams, quality deviations)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy: scanning, touchscreen operation, digital records
- Basic operation and safety protocols for collaborative robots.
- Simple sensor identification and alarm processing
- Lean production and 5S management
- Automated logistics system (AGV/conveyor) anomaly handling
- Basic English or technical document reading

**Key growth areas:**

- automation adaptation
- logistics integration
- quality control
- lean manufacturing

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a manufacturing labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region, but typically range from minimum wage (around R$1,320 per month in 2024) to slightly higher, depending on experience and industry. Annual pre-tax income approximately R$15,840 to R$20,000.

**Q: Is it possible for a foreign manufacturing labourer to obtain a work visa in Brazil?**
A: Generally difficult as the occupation is low-skilled. Employer-sponsored VITEM V visas require proof of specialized knowledge; for labourers, it's rarely approved. However, Mercosur citizens can apply for residency under the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which allows work without specific skill requirements.

**Q: What are the main industries employing manufacturing labourers?**
A: Automotive, electronics, textiles, food processing, and general manufacturing.
