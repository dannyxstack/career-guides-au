# Handicraft Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7319 (ISCO08)**

Handicraft workers create, repair, and restore handmade items using traditional techniques and materials such as wood, clay, textiles, metal, and glass. They produce decorative and functional objects, often reflecting local culture and heritage. This occupation includes artisans, potters, weavers, woodcarvers, and jewelers who work independently or in small workshops. Many operate informally, selling at fairs, markets, or online platforms. The work requires creativity, manual dexterity, and knowledge of materials and techniques.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Experienced artisans may gain recognition through exhibitions, teach workshops, or collaborate with designers. Formal employment is rare; success depends on marketing and networking. ## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a handicraft worker in Brazil?**
A: Income varies widely; many earn between R$ 15,000 and R$ 40,000 per year, depending on sales and reputation. High-end artisans may earn more, but most supplement income with other activities.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a handicraft worker in Brazil?**
A: It is challenging; most visas require employer sponsorship, which is uncommon for artisans. Self-employed options exist under Mercosur Residence for regional citizens, but it requires proof of sufficient income and a business plan.

**Q: What are the career prospects for handicraft workers?**
A: Prospects are limited; many remain self-employed. Networking and online sales can improve income. Some progress to teaching or opening studios, but formal employment in the sector is rare.
