# Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2132 (ISCO08)**

Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers provide expert guidance to farmers, foresters, and fishers on sustainable practices, crop management, livestock care, soil conservation, and resource efficiency. They analyze production methods, recommend improvements, and support adoption of new technologies to boost productivity while minimizing environmental impact. In Brazil, they work with smallholders, large agribusinesses, cooperatives, and government agencies to enhance rural development and food security. Their role includes training, data interpretation, and policy advice, bridging scientific research and practical application. This occupation is vital for Brazil's agribusiness sector, the country's economic powerhouse, and aligns with global sustainability goals.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 64,633. Brazil's agribusiness sector continues to expand, driven by global demand for commodities and the need for sustainable intensification. Employment for advisers is projected to grow steadily, particularly in regions like the Matopiba agricultural frontier and the Amazon's sustainable forestry initiatives. The aging rural workforce and increasing climate variability create demand for technical expertise. Government programs such as ABC Plan (Low Carbon Agriculture) and rural extension services support job creation. However, competition is moderate, with opportunities concentrated in major producing states (MT, GO, MG, PR, SP) and for those with specialization in precision agriculture or bioeconomy.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts as a field technician or extension agent, then advances to senior adviser, team leader, or regional coordinator. Experienced advisers may become consultants, open private firms, or move into agribusiness management, research, or policy roles. Certification in specialized areas (e.g., organic farming, irrigation) enhances prospects. The shift toward digital agriculture (e.g., drone monitoring, software analytics) is reshaping the role, requiring continuous upskilling. Many professionals transition to corporate roles in input suppliers, cooperatives, or financial institutions. Public-sector careers with EMBRAPA or state extension agencies offer stability but slower advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate standardized soil test reports and crop growth cycle analyses
- Automated fertiliser recommendation based on historical data and satellite imagery
- Draft basic sustainable forest management plans
- Automatically aggregate fishing catch data and generate compliance reports
- Answer common pest and disease control questions (knowledge base chatbot)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize precision agriculture decision-making recommendations using AI remote sensing analysis and big data
- Simulate long-term environmental impacts of different planting/breeding strategies using AI models
- Real-time monitoring of weather and market data, dynamically adjusting advisor recommendations
- Automates data collection and initial analysis, allowing consultants to focus on farmer communication and customized solutions
- AI-assisted generation of visual reports to improve communication efficiency with farmers

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and long-term relationships with farmers
- On-site problem diagnosis based on local experience and intuition
- Handling complex, unique sustainability conflicts involving multiple stakeholders
- Adaptability to unstructured, low-information-density environments (e.g., remote farms)
- Cross-disciplinary integration (agriculture, ecology, economics) providing customized solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI tools (e.g., remote sensing analysis platforms, agricultural decision support systems)
- Data science fundamentals (Python/R, statistical modeling, data visualization).
- Precision agriculture technology (drones, sensors, IoT data analysis)
- Environmental and social impact assessment methodology
- Communication and change management skills (to promote adoption of new technologies among farmers)
- Continuous learning and industry knowledge updates (policy, market, biotechnology).

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable agriculture
- Precision farming

- Climate adaptation

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience. Entry-level advisers earn around BRL 40,000 to 60,000 annually, while senior roles can reach BRL 120,000 or more. Public sector positions (e.g., extension agents) often start lower but include benefits.

**Q: Can I work as a foreign adviser in Brazil without a permanent residence visa?**
A: Yes, you can obtain a VITEM V work visa with an employer sponsor, or use the Mercosur Residence Agreement if you are a citizen of a Mercosur country. The process requires a formal job offer and approval from the Ministry of Labor.

**Q: What are the main challenges for an adviser in Brazil?**
A: Challenges include navigating diverse climates and soil types, dealing with infrastructure gaps in remote areas, and keeping up with rapidly evolving agricultural technologies. Familiarity with Portuguese and Brazilian regulations is essential.
