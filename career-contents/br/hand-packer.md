# Hand Packer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9321 (ISCO08)**

A hand packer manually packs products into containers, boxes, or wrapping materials, ensuring items are correctly sorted, counted, and labeled for shipment or storage. This role is common in warehouses, distribution centers, and manufacturing plants across Brazil, particularly in sectors like food processing, retail logistics, and e-commerce. The work is repetitive and often requires physical stamina, with tasks varying from simple packaging to quality checks. Hand packers typically work under supervision and may rotate among packing stations. Employment is concentrated in the Southeast and South regions, where industrial and logistics hubs are largest.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 220,116. The outlook for hand packers in Brazil is stable but with limited growth, as automation and mechanization continue to replace manual packing in large-scale operations. However, demand remains steady in small and medium enterprises, seasonal agriculture, and last-mile distribution for e-commerce. The occupation offers low barriers to entry but high turnover, with wages near the minimum wage. Geographic mobility within Brazil is possible, especially in booming logistics corridors like São Paulo, Minas Gerais, and Paraná. The occupation is not on any official shortage list.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is limited for hand packers, but experienced workers may advance to team leader, quality inspector, or warehouse supervisor roles with additional training. Upskilling in logistics software or forklift operation can open higher-paying positions. Some hand packers transition into other low-skilled roles in transport or production. Formal education is rarely required, but completing short courses in logistics or workplace safety can improve prospects. The occupation is often a first job or temporary bridge for workers entering the labor market.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a hand packer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries for hand packers in Brazil are around the national minimum wage, currently about R$ 1,320 per month (pre-tax annual about R$ 15,840). In logistics hubs like São Paulo, wages may be slightly higher, up to R$ 1,800 per month, but rarely exceed R$ 2,000. Overtime and productivity bonuses can add 10-20%.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a hand packer in Brazil?**
A: It is extremely difficult. Hand packers are considered low-skilled, and Brazil's work visa system (VITEM V) requires employer sponsorship and proof that no Brazilian worker is available. Such visas are almost never approved for this occupation. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is a theoretical option for nationals of Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, and other signatories, but it is rarely used for unskilled work.

**Q: What career progression is possible for a hand packer?**
A: With experience, hand packers can move to team leader, quality control inspector, or warehouse supervisor. Obtaining a forklift license or completing logistics courses can lead to higher-paying roles. However, without additional education, upward mobility is limited.
