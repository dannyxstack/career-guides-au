# Ships' Engineers — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3151 (ISCO08)**

Ships' Engineers, also known as marine engineers, operate, maintain, and repair the mechanical, electrical, and propulsion systems on vessels. In Brazil, they work on cargo ships, oil tankers, offshore platforms, and passenger vessels. They ensure engine room safety, compliance with maritime regulations, and efficient fuel consumption. The role requires a mix of theoretical knowledge and hands-on skills, with responsibilities ranging from routine maintenance to emergency repairs. Ships' Engineers are essential to Brazil's maritime industry, supporting the country's extensive coastline, port activities, and offshore oil and gas sectors. The occupation is regulated by the Brazilian Navy (Marinha do Brasil), requiring specific certifications and sea service.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for Ships' Engineers typically starts as a Junior Engineer or 4th Engineer, advancing to 3rd Engineer, 2nd Engineer, and eventually Chief Engineer. In Brazil, promotion depends on sea service hours, additional certifications, and passing exams by the Brazilian Navy. Engineers often move from smaller vessels to larger, more complex ships, or transition to shoreside roles in shipyards, port operations, or maritime management. Specialization in areas like dynamic positioning or liquefied gas handling can boost career prospects. Some pursue degrees in marine engineering to qualify for senior positions or teaching roles.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for Ships' Engineers in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and sector. A Junior Engineer (4th Engineer) earns around 40,000 to 60,000 BRL per year, while a Chief Engineer on an oil tanker can earn 150,000 to 250,000 BRL annually. Offshore oil and gas contracts often pay higher, but figures are pre-tax and typically include food and accommodation.

**Q: Can a foreign Ships' Engineer work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Foreigners must have their qualifications validated by the Brazilian Navy (DPC) and obtain a CRP (Certificado de Registro Profissional). An employer must sponsor a VITEM V work visa or, if from a Mercosur country, use the Mercosur Residence Agreement. The process is lengthy and requires proof of sea service and training.
