# Subsistence Mixed Crop and Livestock Farmer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6330 (ISCO08)**

Subsistence Mixed Crop and Livestock Farmers in Brazil operate small family-owned farms, growing diverse crops and raising livestock primarily for their own consumption and local barter. They employ traditional and agroecological methods, with minimal reliance on external inputs. These farmers play a key role in food security and preserving agrobiodiversity, particularly in the Northeast and Amazon regions. They often work on less than 10 hectares and may engage in informal markets. The occupation is characterized by low income and high self-sufficiency skills.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence farmer in Brazil?**
A: Subsistence farmers primarily produce for their own consumption; income from sales is minimal and irregular. Annual income often ranges from zero to a few thousand reais, but varies greatly depending on location, market access, and crop success.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa to work as a subsistence farmer in Brazil?**
A: No, subsistence farming is not considered a recognized professional occupation for visa purposes. Foreigners cannot obtain a work visa specifically for subsistence farming unless they have permanent residency or Brazilian citizenship. Employer sponsorship is not applicable for self-employed subsistence farming.

**Q: What support is available for subsistence farmers in Brazil?**
A: Government programs such as PRONAF (National Program for Strengthening Family Agriculture) provide credit, technical assistance, and insurance. Additionally, extension services via EMATER and NGOs offer training in agroecology and sustainable practices.
