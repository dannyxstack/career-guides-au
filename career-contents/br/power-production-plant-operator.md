# Power Production Plant Operator — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3131 (ISCO08)**

Power production plant operators control, monitor, and maintain machinery that generates electric power from various sources, including hydroelectric, thermal, nuclear, wind, and solar plants. They ensure safe, efficient, and continuous operation by adjusting controls, performing routine inspections, and responding to alarms or emergencies. This role requires technical knowledge of thermodynamics, electrical systems, and safety protocols, particularly in Brazil's diverse energy matrix dominated by hydroelectricity but increasingly integrating renewables. Operators often work in shifts and must adhere to strict regulatory standards from ANEEL and labor authorities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a trainee or assistant operator, advancing to senior operator, shift supervisor, and plant manager. Many operators obtain technical certifications (e.g., NR-10, NR-13) and may pursue engineering degrees to move into maintenance, planning, or safety roles. Specialization in renewable energy (solar, wind) or automation can enhance prospects. Brazil's large energy companies (Eletrobras, Petrobras, private utilities) offer structured career paths, but geographic mobility is often required as plants are in remote areas. Some operators transition to consultants or trainers.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a power production plant operator in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and plant type. Entry-level operators earn around R$2,500–R$3,500 per month, while experienced senior operators can earn up to R$7,000–R$10,000. For precise figures, consult official salary surveys from CAGED or industry unions.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a power plant operator in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The most common route is the VITEM V work visa, requiring a job offer and employer sponsorship. The employer must prove that the role cannot be filled locally. Mercosur citizens have an easier path via the residence agreement. The occupation is not on a priority shortage list, so visa approval may take several months.

**Q: What certifications are mandatory for power plant operators in Brazil?**
A: NR-10 (safety with electricity) and NR-13 (pressure vessels) are mandatory for most roles. Additional plant-specific certifications (e.g., for nuclear, wind) may be required. All certifications must be renewed periodically and are issued by accredited institutions.
