# Handicraft Worker in Wood, Basketry and Related Materials — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7317 (ISCO08)**

Handicraft workers in wood, basketry, and related materials create functional or decorative items using traditional techniques, often preserving cultural heritage. They work with materials like timber, rattan, straw, and fibers, producing furniture, baskets, carvings, and ornamental objects. This occupation is common in Brazil's artisanal and tourism sectors, particularly in regions like the Northeast and Amazon, where local crafts are integral to community livelihoods.

Career progression typically moves from apprentice to master artisan, often through family or community learning. Many start as helpers and later specialize in techniques like wood carving or weaving. Experienced workers may lead workshops, sell through cooperatives, or open small businesses. Formal certification is not common, but recognition by artisan associations can enhance reputation. The path is heavily skill-dependent and informal, with limited upward mobility in terms of salary.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a handicraft worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely due to informal self-employment. Many earn between R$ 1,500 and R$ 3,000 per month, but income depends on skill, region, and seasonal tourism. Formal employment is rare and pays closer to minimum wage (R$ 1,320 as of 2024).

**Q: Can a foreign handicraft worker obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: It is difficult. The standard VITEM V visa requires an employer sponsor and proof that no local worker can fill the role. For artisan work, this is rarely granted unless the skill is highly unique. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is an alternative for citizens of Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, and others, but does not require a specific occupation.

**Q: Is there formal recognition for handicraft skills in Brazil?**
A: Yes, some states and municipalities have artisan registries and certification programs, but they are voluntary. The federal government supports the sector through the Brazilian Handicraft Program (PAB), which provides training and marketing support but not formal qualifications.
