# Journalist — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

Journalists research, write, and report news and current events for print, broadcast, and digital media. They gather information through interviews, investigations, and observation, then produce stories that inform the public. In Brazil, journalists work for newspapers, TV stations, radio, online portals, and news agencies. The role demands strong writing skills, ethical judgment, and the ability to work under tight deadlines.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 219,775. Employment in journalism is stable but competitive in Brazil. Traditional media is contracting, while digital and independent journalism are growing. Demand is rising for multimedia skills, data journalism, and investigative reporting. The industry is concentrated in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Brasília. Journalists face challenges from fake news and declining ad revenue, but quality journalism remains valued.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression often starts as a reporter or writer, advancing to senior reporter, editor, or specialized correspondent. Many journalists move into content management, communications, or entrepreneurship (e.g., freelance or own media outlets). Digital skills and a strong portfolio are key. Work can be project-based or permanent, with opportunities in both private and public sectors.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standard news briefs (e.g., financial reports, sports scores)
- Speech-to-text and automatic subtitle generation
- Fact-checking and data reconciliation (e.g., verifying statistical data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted data analysis and visualization (quickly identify trends)
- AI-recommended topics and audience preference prediction
- AI compiles multilingual information summaries

**Human moat:**

- Exclusive interviews and in-depth investigations (interpersonal insight and trust)
- Ethical judgment and sensitive issue gatekeeping
- Creative storytelling (features, commentary)

**Skills to build:**

- Data journalism (Python/R/data visualization)
- Multimedia production (video/podcast editing)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT-assisted writing)
- Fact-checking and information verification
- Audience Analysis and Digital Marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- digital media
- investigative journalism
- data journalism
- multimedia

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for journalists in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level journalists earn between 2,000 and 4,000 BRL per month. Experienced reporters can earn 6,000–12,000 BRL, while top journalists at major outlets may exceed 20,000 BRL. Freelancers' income varies widely.

**Q: Can foreign journalists work in Brazil without a job offer?**
A: Generally no. The VITEM V visa requires an employment contract. However, Mercosur nationals can work under the residence agreement without a specific job offer.
