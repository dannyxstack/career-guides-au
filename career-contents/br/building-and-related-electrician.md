# Building and Related Electrician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7411 (ISCO08)**

Building and related electricians install, maintain, and repair electrical systems in residential, commercial, and industrial buildings. They work with wiring, lighting, distribution boards, and safety devices, ensuring compliance with Brazilian electrical standards (NBR 5410). This occupation is critical for construction projects and building maintenance, with strong demand driven by infrastructure development and energy efficiency upgrades.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with an apprenticeship or technical course (e.g., SENAI). Junior electricians advance to senior roles, team leads, or site supervisors. Specialization in solar photovoltaic systems, building automation, or industrial maintenance can boost income. Many electricians eventually become self-employed contractors or open small businesses. Formal education (e.g., technologist degree) and CREA registration open doors to project management or inspection roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual preparation of wiring plans and material lists (AI can automatically optimize generation)
- Basic electrical safety calculations (e.g., load calculations, wire sizing)
- Standardized test recording and report writing
- Remote diagnosis of simple faults (via AI vision and sensor data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

- Communication and compliance sign-offs with clients, contractors, and regulators
- Practical hands-on ability in harsh environments (heights, confined spaces)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for a building electrician in Brazil?**
A: The average pre-tax salary ranges from R$30,000 to R$55,000 per year, depending on experience, certification, and region. Senior roles or those specializing in industrial automation can earn over R$70,000 annually.

**Q: Can a foreign electrician migrate to Brazil for work?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The main route is an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa, requiring proof of qualifications and company need. Mercosur citizens have an easier path via Residence Agreement. Electricians must have their foreign credentials validated and register with CREA to work legally.

**Q: Is it easy to find work as an electrician in Brazil?**
A: Yes, especially for qualified electricians with formal certification. Demand is steady due to construction and maintenance needs. However, competition can be higher in major cities, and many electricians work informally. Specialization in solar energy or automation improves prospects.
