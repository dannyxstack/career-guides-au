# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6224 (ISCO08)**

Hunters and trappers capture or kill wild animals for food, fur, trade, or population control, often in remote or rural areas of Brazil. This occupation involves tracking, setting traps, using firearms, and skinning or preparing carcasses. It is a traditional livelihood in the Amazon and other biomes, but strictly regulated due to conservation concerns and environmental laws. Most work is informal or seasonal, with few formal employment opportunities. The role requires knowledge of local wildlife, survival skills, and compliance with IBAMA (Brazilian Institute of Environment and Renewable Natural Resources) licensing. It is not a typical career path for migrants.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: very low (★☆☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. —. Employment for hunters and trappers in Brazil is declining due to stricter environmental regulations, deforestation limits, and reduced demand for wild game and fur. The rise of ecotourism and conservation initiatives has shifted focus away from extractive activities. Indigenous and traditional communities still practice subsistence hunting, but commercial opportunities are scarce. The formal market is very small, primarily in wildlife management (culling invasive species) or scientific collection. Overall outlook is negative, with few new job openings.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income range for a hunter and trapper in Brazil?**
A: Income is highly variable and often informal. Most hunters earn from sale of meat, skins, or byproducts. A full-time hunter might earn between BRL 15,000 and BRL 30,000 annually, but many supplement with other activities.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa to hunt in Brazil?**
A: It is extremely difficult. The occupation is not eligible for standard skilled migration. Employer-sponsored VITEM V is possible only if the hunter has unique expertise (e.g., scientific collection) and a Brazilian employer provides a contract with CNIg approval. In practice, it is almost never granted for commercial hunting.

**Q: Is a license required to hunt in Brazil?**
A: Yes, a hunting license from IBAMA is mandatory for legal hunting. It requires completion of an approved course, proof of firearm registration if applicable, and compliance with species and area quotas. The license is not easily obtained and is subject to strict review.
