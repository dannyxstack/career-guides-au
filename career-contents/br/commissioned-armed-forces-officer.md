# Commissioned Armed Forces Officer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 0110 (ISCO08)**

Commissioned Armed Forces Officers lead and manage military personnel and operations within the Brazilian Army, Navy, and Air Force. They are responsible for strategic planning, command execution, and ensuring national defense readiness. Entry requires completion of a rigorous military academy program, with careers progressing through ranks from Aspirante (cadet) to high-level command positions. Officers may specialize in areas such as aviation, intelligence, engineering, or logistics. The role demands strong leadership, discipline, and physical fitness, often involving deployments and relocations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression follows a hierarchical rank structure: Second Lieutenant to General/Admiral. Advancement depends on time-in-service, performance evaluations, and completion of mandatory courses (e.g., Command and Staff School). Specialization in areas like cyber defense or aviation enhances prospects. Top ranks require political appointments. Lateral entry for civilians is extremely rare; nearly all officers start as cadets. The path offers strong job security, pension, and benefits, but demands mobility and long hours.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Commissioned Armed Forces Officer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and branch. A Second Lieutenant (beginning rank) earns around BRL 6,000–8,000 per month, while a Colonel may earn BRL 20,000–30,000 per month. Additional benefits include housing, food, and health care. Figures are pre-tax annual, so multiply by 12 for annual estimate.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a Commissioned Armed Forces Officer in Brazil?**
A: Direct entry is extremely rare. Brazil typically requires citizenship for commissioned officers. Foreign military personnel may participate in exchange programs or serve as attachés under diplomatic visas. Formal work authorization (VITEM V) is possible only if sponsored by the Ministry of Defense for specific technical roles.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist beyond military service?**
A: Many officers transition to civilian careers in defense contracting, logistics, security management, or government positions. The leadership and technical skills acquired are valued by private sector employers, especially in aerospace and security industries.
