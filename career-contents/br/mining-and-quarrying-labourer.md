# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers perform manual tasks in extracting minerals, ores, and stone from open-pit or underground mines, as well as in quarries and gravel pits. They assist with drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials, and maintain equipment and worksites. This occupation requires physical stamina and safety awareness, often working in remote or hazardous environments. Labourers typically start with minimal formal education, gaining skills on the job. In Brazil, the sector is significant for commodities like iron ore, gold, and construction aggregates, with major operations in states such as Minas Gerais, Pará, and Bahia.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 33,641. Brazil's mining sector is a key economic driver, with sustained demand for iron ore globally. However, employment for labourers is cyclical, tied to commodity prices and investment cycles. Automation is increasing in large-scale mining, potentially reducing demand for manual labour over the long term. Short-term outlooks are moderate, with growth in small-scale quarrying for local construction. Job security is limited, and labourers often face informal work arrangements. The government's mining regulatory reforms and environmental licensing can affect project timelines and hiring.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression from labourer is possible through experience and additional training. Labourers may advance to become machine operators (e.g., haul truck drivers, drill operators) or blasters. Supervisory roles like shift supervisor or mine foreman require longer experience and often completion of technical courses. Some transition into safety roles (e.g., mining safety inspector) after obtaining certifications. Formal technical education (e.g., técnico em mineração) can accelerate advancement. Overall, the path is more limited compared to skilled trades, but lateral moves within the industry are common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- Open-pit mining
- Small-scale quarrying
- Rehabilitation work
- Construction aggregates

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining and quarrying labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary significantly by region, employer, and experience. On average, a labourer earns between R$1,200 and R$2,000 per month, though larger mining companies may offer higher wages plus benefits. Income is often close to the federal minimum wage.

**Q: Can foreign labourers easily get a work visa for mining jobs in Brazil?**
A: Visa sponsorship for low-skilled positions is rare because the applicant must demonstrate specialized skills not available locally. Mercosur nationals have an easier pathway via the residence agreement, but non-Mercosur applicants typically require an employer to prove a hiring need and obtain CNIg approval.

**Q: What certifications are required to work as a mining labourer in Brazil?**
A: Mandatory NR-22 mining safety training is required by law. Many companies also require a basic health certificate and first aid training. On-the-job training for specific equipment is provided.
