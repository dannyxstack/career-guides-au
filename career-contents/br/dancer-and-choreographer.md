# Dancer or Choreographer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

Dancers and choreographers in Brazil work in a range of settings from theatre and television to nightclubs and festivals. They create, perform, and teach dance routines, often blending traditional Brazilian styles like samba and forró with contemporary forms. Employment is project-based and competitive, concentrated in major cities such as Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo. Many professionals combine performance with teaching or studio management to sustain income.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,102. The outlook for dancers and choreographers in Brazil is shaped by the recovery of the cultural sector after the pandemic. Government investment in arts and tourism, along with growth in streaming content, supports steady demand for live performances and TV productions. ## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a dancer or choreographer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on project, employer, and region. Freelance dancers often earn between R$1,500 and R$4,000 per month, while choreographers with regular contracts may earn R$5,000 to R$10,000. Annual pre-tax income for a mid-career professional is roughly R$40,000–R$80,000.

**Q: Can a foreign dancer obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, through the VITEM V visa, which requires an employment contract with a Brazilian company or event producer. The process involves approval from the National Immigration Council (CNIg). Alternatively, nationals of Mercosur countries can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for easier access.

**Q: What are the best cities for dance careers in Brazil?**
A: Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo offer the most opportunities in theatre, TV, and festivals. Salvador and Recife are strong for traditional and Afro-Brazilian dance. Brasília and Belo Horizonte also have growing cultural scenes.
