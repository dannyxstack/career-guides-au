# Deep-sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6223 (ISCO08)**

Deep-sea fishery workers operate fishing vessels to catch fish and other marine life in offshore waters, primarily in Brazil's Exclusive Economic Zone. They work on industrial trawlers, longliners, or purse seiners, handling nets, lines, and onboard processing equipment. The role demands physical endurance, seamanship skills, and familiarity with navigation and safety protocols. Employment is often seasonal or contract-based, with crews spending weeks at sea. The occupation is concentrated in coastal states like Santa Catarina, Rio Grande do Sul, and Pernambuco, serving both domestic consumption and export markets for tuna, shrimp, and sardines.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a deckhand or fisher, advancing to boatswain, mate, and eventually captain after gaining sea hours and certifications. Many workers alternate between fishing seasons and other maritime jobs like offshore oil or cargo shipping. Specialization in a type of fishery (e.g., longline tuna) can lead to higher wages. Formal education is rarely required; experience and nautical licenses are key. The path is linear but limited by physical demands; few transition to shore-based roles in seafood processing or marine inspection.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a deep-sea fishery worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by vessel size, catch value, and profit-sharing arrangements. Deckhands can earn approximately 2 to 4 times the minimum wage per month during season, while captains may earn 5 to 10 times. Income is unstable due to seasonal closures and catch fluctuations. There are no official aggregate statistics.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for deep-sea fishing in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is not a common migration route. Employers must prove no qualified Brazilian is available. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is the most accessible pathway for citizens of member states. Non-Mercosur nationals face stricter requirements and longer processing through VITEM V and CNIg authorization.

**Q: What is the career progression for a deep-sea fishery worker?**
A: Starting as a deckhand, you can advance to fisherman, master fisherman, boatswain, and eventually captain. Each step requires additional sea hours and maritime certifications. Specializing in a specific fishery (e.g., tuna longlining) can boost income. Many workers leave the sea after 10-15 years due to physical toll.
