# Electrical and Electronic Equipment Assembler — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8212 (ISCO08)**

Electrical and electronic equipment assemblers put together components and subassemblies of electrical and electronic products, such as circuit boards, wiring harnesses, and consumer electronics, using hand tools and automated machinery. They follow detailed schematics and quality standards to ensure products function correctly. Employment is concentrated in manufacturing hubs like Manaus and São Paulo, often in factories producing appliances, automotive parts, and industrial equipment.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Entry-level assemblers typically start after short on-the-job training or vocational courses. With experience, they can advance to team leader, quality inspector, or production supervisor. Specialization in SMD (surface-mount device) assembly or programmable logic controllers (PLCs) can lead to technician roles. Further education in electronics engineering opens pathways to design or management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive electronic component insertion and soldering
- Visual inspection and testing based on preset criteria
- Simple wire harness organization and connection
- Material handling and sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted complex fault diagnosis and troubleshooting
- Assembly guidance and real-time verification using AR headsets
- Predictive maintenance data analysis
- Programming and debugging of automated equipment

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard, flexible assembly requirements
- Understanding of new circuit design and hands-on prototype verification
- Cross-system integration and process optimization
- On-site emergency handling and customer communication

**Skills to build:**

- Industrial Robot Programming and Debugging
- PLC and automation system maintenance
- Python/C++ data analysis and scripting
- SMT patch and advanced packaging technology
- Lean manufacturing and Six Sigma methodology.
- Digital Twin and Industrial IoT Fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an electrical assembler in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are modest compared to other technical professions, generally competitive within the manufacturing sector. Employers often provide additional benefits such as meal vouchers, transportation allowances, and overtime pay.

**Q: Can I work in Brazil as an electrical assembler from abroad?**
A: Yes, but it requires an employer-sponsored work visa (VITEM V). The employer must demonstrate that no qualified Brazilian worker is available, which makes sponsorship challenging for this occupation.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for assemblers?**
A: With experience, assemblers can advance to team leader, quality inspector, or production supervisor. Further technical training can lead to electronics technician or engineering roles.
