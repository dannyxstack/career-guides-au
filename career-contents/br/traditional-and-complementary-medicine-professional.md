# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and complementary medicine professionals in Brazil practice holistic health approaches such as acupuncture, herbal medicine, and naturopathy. They are regulated by the Ministry of Health and must hold specific qualifications. Employment is primarily in private clinics, hospitals, and integrative health centers, with growing demand in urban areas for non-pharmacological treatments.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 941. The Brazilian government has expanded public coverage of complementary therapies through the SUS (Sistema Único de Saúde), driving steady job growth. However, professional regulation remains fragmented, and competition is moderate. Opportunities are strongest in large cities with a wellness-oriented population, but rural areas may face shortages. The COVID-19 pandemic increased interest in integrative approaches, sustaining demand.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a degree in a specific modality (e.g., acupuncture) and gaining clinical experience. Many professionals diversify into multiple modalities or open their own clinics. Further specialization in fields like pediatric or geriatric complementary medicine can lead to higher income. Leadership roles in integrative health centers or teaching positions are possible with advanced degrees.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a traditional and complementary medicine professional in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by modality and experience. In private practice, monthly income can range from R$ 3,000 to R$ 10,000. Public sector positions start around R$ 4,000. Professionals in large cities or with established practices earn more, but income is often unstable due to variable patient flow.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa to practice traditional medicine in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The VITEM V visa requires an employer sponsor and recognition of your qualifications by the Ministry of Health. For professions like acupuncture, you may need additional Brazilian certification. The Mercosur Residence is easier for nationals of neighboring countries but still requires professional registration.

**Q: What are the educational pathways to become a traditional medicine professional in Brazil?**
A: Options include a bachelor's degree in naturopathy (4 years) or a postgraduate specialization in acupuncture or herbal medicine (1-2 years). Public universities offer free programs, but competition is high. Private education costs range from R$ 30,000 to R$ 250,000 total.
