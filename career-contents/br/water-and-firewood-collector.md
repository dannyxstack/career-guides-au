# Water and Firewood Collector — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9624 (ISCO08)**

Water and firewood collectors are workers in rural or remote areas of Brazil who gather water from natural sources and collect firewood for fuel. This occupation is predominantly informal, with no formal educational requirements, and is often associated with subsistence living or small-scale family agriculture. Workers typically use manual tools and physical labor, facing harsh environmental conditions and low income. The role is declining in urban areas but persists in isolated regions with limited access to utilities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for water and firewood collectors is virtually nonexistent. Workers typically remain in the same role for life, transitioning to other informal labor (e.g., farming, construction) only if opportunities arise. There are no formal advancement paths, certifications, or training programs. The occupation is seen as a last-resort livelihood for those with limited education or mobility. Many collectors are older adults or indigenous people in remote communities.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a water and firewood collector in Brazil?**
A: Income is extremely low and often below the official minimum wage (R$ 1,320/month in 2023). Most collectors work informally and earn per piece or daily rates, ranging from R$ 50 to R$ 150 per day, depending on region and demand.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as a water and firewood collector in Brazil?**
A: No. This occupation is almost exclusively informal and does not meet the requirements for any Brazilian work visa (e.g., VITEM V). The Mercosur Residence Agreement also requires formal employment, which is not available for this role.

**Q: Are there any training programs for water and firewood collectors in Brazil?**
A: No formal training programs exist. The skill is passed on through informal family or community learning. Some NGOs offer basic environmental awareness courses, but these do not lead to certification or improved income.
