# Environmental and Occupational Health and Hygiene Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2263 (ISCO08)**

Environmental and occupational health and hygiene professionals in Brazil assess, monitor, and mitigate risks to human health in workplaces and the environment. They design and implement preventive programs, conduct inspections, and ensure compliance with labor and environmental regulations. This role is critical in industries such as mining, agriculture, manufacturing, and construction, where exposure to hazardous substances is common. Professionals often work for government agencies, private companies, or consulting firms, and must stay updated on evolving safety standards and best practices.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

Career progression typically starts with a bachelor's degree in environmental engineering, occupational therapy, nursing, or related fields. Many professionals gain experience as technicians or assistants before advancing to senior analyst or manager roles. Specialization in areas like industrial hygiene, toxicology, or ergonomics opens doors to consulting or regulatory positions. Government careers offer stability, while private sector roles often provide higher salaries. Advanced degrees (specialization, master's) can lead to leadership positions in multinational corporations or research institutions.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an environmental and occupational health professional in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level professionals earn around BRL 60,000-80,000 annually, while senior roles in mining or oil & gas can reach BRL 150,000-200,000. Government positions are lower but offer benefits.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as an environmental and occupational health professional in Brazil?**
A: Yes, the VITEM V visa is employer-sponsored and requires a job offer. Professionals with a bachelor's degree and relevant experience are eligible. Mercosur citizens can use the Residence Agreement for easier access.

**Q: Is a Brazilian degree mandatory to work in this field?**
A: Not mandatory, but foreign degrees must be validated by a Brazilian university for regulated professions (e.g., engineering, nursing). Validation can take 6-12 months and cost BRL 1,000-2,000.
