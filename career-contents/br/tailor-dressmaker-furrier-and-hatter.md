# Tailor, Dressmaker, Furrier Or Hatter — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors, dressmakers, furriers, and hatters create, alter, and repair custom garments, including suits, dresses, fur items, and hats, often working in small ateliers, boutiques, or as self-employed professionals. In Brazil, they serve a niche market that values bespoke clothing for events, weddings, and professional attire, with a strong presence in fashion centers like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro. The profession requires precision, creativity, and manual dexterity, and relies heavily on traditional craftsmanship techniques.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 441,012. The Brazilian tailoring industry faces challenges from fast fashion and mass production, leading to a gradual decline in demand for basic alteration services. However, growth persists in high-end bespoke tailoring, sustainable fashion, and costume production for cultural events. The occupation is expected to remain stable but with limited expansion, as the market shifts toward customization and quality. Opportunities may arise in tourism-related sectors and among aging populations seeking garment repairs. Overall, the outlook is moderate with competitive pressures.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to positions as junior tailor or dressmaker. Experienced professionals can become master tailors, open their own ateliers, or specialize in haute couture, costume design, or fur and leather work. Some transition into teaching at vocational schools or move into fashion design roles. Advancement often requires building a client base and reputation, with self-employment being a common path. Digital marketing and online custom clothing platforms are emerging avenues for growth.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a tailor in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, location, and specialization. Entry-level tailors may earn around the minimum wage (about BRL 1,320 per month), while experienced master tailors in high-end ateliers can earn significantly more, though precise figures are not provided here.

**Q: Can a foreign tailor obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: It is possible but uncommon. A foreign tailor would need a job offer from a Brazilian employer and demonstrate specialized skills not readily available locally. The VITEM V work visa is the primary option, but approval is not guaranteed and typically requires proof of unique expertise.
