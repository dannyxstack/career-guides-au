# Field Crop and Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Brazil cultivate a wide range of crops, from soybeans and corn to leafy greens and tomatoes, using both traditional and modern agricultural techniques. This occupation spans small family farms to large agribusiness operations in regions like the Midwest, Southeast, and Northeast. Growers must manage planting, irrigation, pest control, and harvesting while adapting to climate variability and market demands. The role is physically demanding and requires practical knowledge of soil management, crop rotation, and increasingly, sustainable practices.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or apprentice, advancing to grower or farm manager after acquiring technical knowledge. Formal education, such as a technical course in agriculture or a bachelor's in agronomy, accelerates advancement. Growers can specialize in organic farming, agronomy consulting, or agribusiness management. Diversification into niche crops or agro-processing offers higher income potential. Many growers eventually own or co-own farm operations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Agriculture
- Organic Farming
- Sustainable Agriculture
- Export-Oriented Crops

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income range for field crop and vegetable growers in Brazil?**
A: Income varies widely by region, crop type, and farm scale. Small-scale growers often earn near minimum wage, while skilled technicians or managers on large commercial farms can earn above-average wages. The occupation generally offers modest financial returns compared to urban professions.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign field crop and vegetable growers?**
A: The most straightforward path is the Mercosur Residence Agreement for nationals of member countries. For others, the VITEM V work visa requires employer sponsorship and proof of specialized skills, which may be challenging for this occupation unless the grower has advanced agronomy expertise.

**Q: How can I advance my career as a field crop grower in Brazil?**
A: Pursuing formal education such as a technical course or agronomy degree opens doors to farm management, consulting, or agribusiness roles. Specializing in high-demand areas like organic farming or precision agriculture can also boost career prospects and income.
