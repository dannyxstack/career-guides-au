# Cleaner — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9112 (ISCO08)**

Cleaners and helpers in offices, hotels, and other establishments maintain cleanliness and hygiene by sweeping, mopping, dusting, and disposing of waste. This occupation requires minimal formal education, typically learned on the job, and is a major entry point into the labor market. Workers are employed across industries, including hospitality, corporate offices, and public facilities. The role is physically demanding and often involves part-time or shift work. Despite low barriers to entry, job stability can be low due to high turnover and informal arrangements.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for cleaners is limited. Starting as a cleaner, one may advance to a senior cleaner, team leader, or supervisor with experience. Some transition to self-employment, offering services directly to clients. Further mobility often requires additional training in areas like industrial cleaning or facility management. The occupation is often a stepping stone for new entrants to the labor market, but long-term prospects are modest without upskilling.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Clean floors using smart vacuum and mop robots
- Operation monitoring of automatic window cleaning robots
- Dust removal in standard areas such as corridors and halls following preset routes.
- Use automatic disinfection robots to disinfect bathrooms and kitchens.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling system to optimize multi-site cleaning routes and schedules
- Use smart sensors to monitor dirt levels and precisely schedule cleaning frequency
- Receive health and safety alerts via wearable devices to enhance personal protection
- Adjusts service details using a customer review AI system to improve satisfaction
- Use digital tools to manage cleaning supplies, reducing waste

**Human moat:**

- Identification and targeted treatment of complex stubborn stains (e.g., carpet stains, oil stains).
- Communicate with clients to understand specific needs and flexibly adjust cleaning methods
- Ability to handle unexpected situations (e.g., breaking items, pet accidents)
- Fine cleaning and care of valuable items (e.g., antiques, silk)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic AI device operation and simple troubleshooting
- Use cleaning management software for scheduling and customer relationship management
- Learn specialized cleaning techniques (e.g., stone care, disinfection)
- Improve communication and customer service skills
- Basic English proficiency to interact with smart devices or serve foreign clients
- Learn about personal protective equipment and eco-friendly cleaning agents

**Key growth areas:**

- Hospitality
- Facility Management
- Urban Services
- Event Cleaning

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a cleaner in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, but generally range from minimum wage to about 1.5 times the minimum wage. In large cities and premium hotels, pay may be slightly higher, but overall it remains one of the lowest-paid occupations.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as a cleaner in Brazil?**
A: It is challenging. The VITEM V visa requires specialized skills or professional qualifications that cleaners typically lack. The Mercosur Residence Agreement may be an option for nationals of Mercosur countries who have a formal job offer, but approval is not guaranteed.

**Q: What are the typical working hours for a cleaner?**
A: Cleaners often work full-time (40-44 hours per week) but part-time and shift work, including early mornings, evenings, and weekends, are common, especially in hotels and offices.
