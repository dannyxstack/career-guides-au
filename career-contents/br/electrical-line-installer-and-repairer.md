# Electrical Line Installers and Repairers — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7413 (ISCO08)**

Electrical line installers and repairers are responsible for installing, maintaining, and repairing high-voltage electrical power lines and cables, including those that carry electricity from power plants to substations and ultimately to homes and businesses. In Brazil, these professionals work for utility companies, construction firms, and telecommunications providers, often at great heights and in challenging weather conditions. The role requires strong technical knowledge of electrical systems, safety procedures, and the use of specialized equipment such as bucket trucks and tensioning devices. With Brazil's ongoing investments in energy infrastructure and renewable energy sources, line installers play a critical role in ensuring reliable power distribution across the country.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for electrical line installers typically begins with an apprenticeship or vocational training, followed by entry-level positions as a lineman or cable splicer. With experience, they can advance to senior line installer, crew leader, or supervisor roles. Further specialization in high-voltage transmission, renewable energy systems, or fiber-optic cable installation can lead to higher pay and job security. Some line installers also transition to related fields such as electrical engineering technology or project management after obtaining additional certifications or degrees.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an electrical line installer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region, experience, and employer. Entry-level positions typically earn around R$2,500–R$3,500 per month, while experienced line installers in high-demand areas can earn R$5,000–R$7,000 or more. Additional certifications and specialized skills can lead to higher pay.

**Q: Can foreign electrical line installers get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is employer-sponsored. The company must apply for a VITEM V visa through the Ministry of Labor (CNIg) and prove that no qualified Brazilian worker is available. Skills in high demand, such as high-voltage or renewable energy work, may improve chances. Mercosur citizens can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for easier entry.

**Q: What are the main career advancement opportunities?**
A: With experience, line installers can become crew leaders, supervisors, or specialists in areas like fiber optics or smart grid technology. Some pursue further education to become electrical engineers or project managers. Continuing education is key to advancing in this field.
