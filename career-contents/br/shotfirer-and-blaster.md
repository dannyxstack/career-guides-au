# Shotfirers And Blasters — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters are skilled professionals responsible for the safe placement, detonation, and monitoring of explosives in mining, quarrying, construction, and demolition operations. They assess geological conditions, calculate charge quantities, and follow strict safety protocols to control blasts and minimize environmental impact.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for shotfirers and blasters starts with a technical course and apprenticeship under a licensed blaster. After gaining experience and advanced certifications, one can become a blasting supervisor, safety inspector, or move into explosives logistics and consulting. The path is linear, with senior roles requiring additional training in geotechnical engineering and project management. Some transition into mining engineering or environmental blasting specialties.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Infrastructure
- Mining
- Quarrying
- Demolition

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a shotfirer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience, but are generally above the national average for skilled trades. Entry-level positions start around BRL 2,500 per month, while experienced blasters can earn BRL 8,000 or more, especially in remote mining sites.

**Q: Can a foreign blaster get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. You need a sponsoring employer and must hold the mandatory Brazilian Army blaster certification. The VITEM V visa is the primary route, but Mercosur nationals have an easier path via the residency agreement.

**Q: What training is required to become a shotfirer?**
A: Completion of a technical course in blasting (18 months) followed by 1-2 years of supervised on-the-job training is standard. You must also pass the Brazilian Army's certification exam to handle explosives.
