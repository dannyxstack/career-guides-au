# Ships’ Deck Crews and Related Workers — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ships’ Deck Crews and Related Workers perform a variety of tasks on board vessels, including navigation, mooring, cargo handling, maintenance, and ensuring safety compliance. They work on cargo ships, passenger liners, offshore platforms, tugboats, and fishing vessels. The role requires physical stamina, teamwork, and adherence to maritime regulations. In Brazil, this occupation is essential for the nation's extensive coastal shipping, port operations, and offshore oil and gas industries.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 48,622. Brazil's employment outlook for ships’ deck crews is stable, driven by growth in coastal shipping (cabotage), offshore oil and gas exploration, and port infrastructure projects. The government's 'BR do Mar' program aims to expand cabotage, increasing demand for qualified deck personnel. However, automation and larger vessels may reduce crew sizes. Job opportunities are concentrated in coastal states like Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, and Espírito Santo.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a deckhand or ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman, boatswain, and then officer roles (e.g., deck officer, master) after obtaining required certifications and sea time. Specialization in offshore support, tanker operations, or cruise ships can accelerate advancement. Continuous training in STCW (Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping) is mandatory for career growth.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for ships’ deck crew in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by rank, vessel type, and experience. Entry-level deckhands earn around R$30,000–50,000 annually pre-tax, while able seamen and boatswains earn R$50,000–80,000. Officers and masters can earn significantly more. Many positions include additional benefits such as food, accommodation, and hazard pay.

**Q: How can a foreigner get a work visa for this job in Brazil?**
A: Foreign deck crew must obtain a VITEM V temporary work visa sponsored by a Brazilian employer. The employer must prove that no qualified Brazilian worker is available, obtain CNIg approval, and provide a labor contract. Certifications from the Brazilian Navy (e.g., CPP, STCW) may be required. Mercosur citizens can apply for residence under the Mercosur Agreement with fewer requirements.

**Q: Are Brazilian maritime certificates recognized internationally?**
A: Brazilian certificates (CPP, STCW) are issued in compliance with international STCW standards, so they are generally recognized by most maritime authorities. However, endorsement by the flag state of the vessel may be needed for foreign-flagged ships.
