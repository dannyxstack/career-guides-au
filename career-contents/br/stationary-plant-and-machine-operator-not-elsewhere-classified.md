# Stationary Plant and Machine Operator — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8189 (ISCO08)**

Operators of stationary industrial machinery, including pumps, compressors, conveyors, and other equipment used in manufacturing, energy, water treatment, and similar sectors. They monitor controls, adjust settings, perform routine maintenance, and ensure safe and efficient operation. This occupation covers a wide range of industries, from food processing to petrochemicals, and requires attention to detail and basic technical knowledge.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 22,929. Employment prospects for stationary plant and machine operators in Brazil are moderate, with demand tied to the performance of the industrial sector. Regions with strong manufacturing hubs, such as São Paulo, Minas Gerais, and Rio Grande do Sul, offer more opportunities. Automation may reduce demand for low-skilled operators, but skilled operators with technical certifications remain needed for complex machinery. The oil and gas, energy, and water treatment sectors provide stable employment.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a machine assistant or trainee, advancing to operator, then senior operator, and eventually supervisor or plant foreman. Additional technical courses in automation or mechanics can lead to roles in maintenance or process control. Some operators transition to quality control or safety inspection. Lateral moves across industries are common due to transferable skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated production quality inspection using sensors and AI vision
- Predictive maintenance scheduling for equipment based on historical data
- Standardized material handling and packaging processes.
- Adaptive adjustment and optimization of production parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted predictive maintenance improves equipment reliability
- Real-time production data dashboards assist operational decisions
- Smart scheduling system optimizes multi-machine coordination efficiency
- Remote expert collaboration system enables rapid fault diagnosis

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of non-standard mechanical faults.
- Ability to flexibly switch between multiple varieties and small batch production
- Perception of subtle anomalies like unusual equipment noises and vibrations.
- Safety management and emergency shutdown procedures

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and PLC debugging.
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) data analysis
- Collaborative robot (KUKA/Universal Robots) operation
- Lean production and Six Sigma methods
- Understanding of AI tools in industrial applications

**Key growth areas:**

- Manufacturing
- Energy
- Oil & Gas
- Water Treatment

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for stationary plant and machine operators in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by industry and region. Entry-level operators earn around BRL 1,500-2,000 per month, while experienced operators in sectors like oil and gas can earn BRL 3,000-5,000. Exact figures depend on employer, location, and certifications.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a stationary plant machine operator in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult. The main route is a VITEM V work visa, which requires an employer sponsor and proof that no Brazilian worker is available. The process is time-consuming, and this occupation is not considered high-skilled, so visas are rarely granted. Mercosur nationals can use the residence agreement for easier access.
