# Sales Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5249 (ISCO08)**

Sales workers not elsewhere classified encompass a diverse range of roles including door-to-door sales, telemarketers, retail sales assistants, and street vendors. In Brazil, these workers are often employed in informal or formal sectors, with many earning a commission-based income. The occupation is characterized by high turnover and variable earnings, typically requiring only basic education and on-the-job training. With the growth of e-commerce and digital sales channels, some roles are evolving, but the core function remains direct customer engagement to promote and sell products or services.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression is limited without additional education or specialization. Entry-level sales workers can advance to senior sales representative, team leader, or move into management roles. Some transition to more specialized sales in fields like real estate, financial products, or B2B services. Upskilling in digital marketing, CRM tools, and negotiation can improve prospects. Entrepreneurship is also a common path, with some starting their own small retail businesses. However, the majority remain in similar roles due to low mobility.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a sales worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and performance, but average monthly earnings range from R$ 1,200 to R$ 3,000 before commissions. Many workers earn minimum wage plus incentives. Full-time annual pre-tax income typically falls between R$ 14,400 and R$ 36,000. Commission-based roles can exceed this.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for a general sales job in Brazil?**
A: Generally, it is difficult. The VITEM V visa requires employer sponsorship and proof that the foreigner has specialized skills not available locally, which sales roles often lack. The Mercosur Residence Agreement is an option for nationals of Mercosur countries (e.g., Argentina, Uruguay) and allows work in any occupation, including sales.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for sales workers in Brazil?**
A: Progression often involves moving into team leadership, account management, or specialized sales (e.g., financial products, real estate). Further education in marketing or business administration can open doors to management positions. Some workers transition to entrepreneurship.
