# Kitchen Helper — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9412 (ISCO08)**

Kitchen helpers perform basic preparatory and cleaning tasks in kitchens of restaurants, hotels, cafeterias, and other food service establishments. They assist cooks and chefs by washing, peeling, and cutting vegetables, cleaning kitchen equipment and utensils, stocking supplies, and maintaining cleanliness. The role requires physical stamina and the ability to work in a fast-paced environment. In Brazil, kitchen helpers are often employed in the informal sector, with many working without formal contracts, particularly in small establishments and regions with high tourism activity.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 777,041. The demand for kitchen helpers in Brazil is closely tied to the hospitality and food service sectors, which are recovering from the pandemic and benefiting from increased domestic tourism and dining-out trends. However, the role faces moderate pressure from automation (e.g., automatic dishwashers and food processors) and a shift towards casual dining chains that standardize processes. Growth is expected to remain steady but not explosive, with opportunities concentrated in urban centers and tourist destinations. The formalization of employment remains a challenge, as many positions are filled informally.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Kitchen helpers typically enter the role with no formal training and learn on the job. Career progression often leads to line cook, prep cook, or short-order cook after gaining experience and possibly completing short vocational courses. Some advance to chef de partie or kitchen supervisor in structured establishments. Others move into related roles such as catering assistant, fast-food service, or even entrepreneurial small-food operations. The path is largely based on experience and employer reputation, with formal education (e.g., culinary degrees) opening up faster advancement to higher-skilled kitchen roles.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a kitchen helper in Brazil?**
A: Salaries for kitchen helpers vary widely by region and formality. In formal positions, the average monthly salary ranges from R$ 1,200 to R$ 1,800 for a 44-hour week. Informal workers may earn less, often between R$ 800 and R$ 1,200 per month, with no benefits. Tipping is common in some establishments and can supplement income.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a kitchen helper in Brazil?**
A: It is very difficult. Kitchen helper is classified as low-skilled, and the VITEM V work visa requires proving that the position cannot be filled by a Brazilian. Applications are rarely approved for this role. The main alternative is the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which allows citizens of member countries (Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, etc.) to work in Brazil in any occupation with fewer restrictions.
