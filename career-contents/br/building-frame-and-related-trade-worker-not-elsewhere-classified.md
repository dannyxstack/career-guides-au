# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building frame and related trades workers not elsewhere classified include a variety of skilled construction occupations such as steel fixers, formwork carpenters, scaffolders, and reinforced concrete workers. They are essential in erecting structural frameworks for buildings, bridges, and other infrastructure projects. These professionals interpret technical drawings, prepare materials, assemble and install frameworks, and ensure structural integrity. In Brazil, these trades are predominantly performed by workers with vocational training from institutions like SENAI, and many gain expertise through on-the-job experience. The role demands physical stamina, attention to safety standards, and teamwork on construction sites.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 223,392. Brazil's construction sector is expected to maintain steady demand for building frame trades due to ongoing urbanization, housing deficits, and infrastructure investments, particularly in the North and Northeast regions. Government programs like Minha Casa Minha Vida and new sanitation projects drive employment. However, economic cycles and political uncertainty can cause fluctuations. Skilled workers with safety certifications (NR-18) and experience in reinforced concrete or steel structures will remain in demand. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or assistant, advancing to qualified tradesperson after 2-4 years of experience and certification. With further training, workers can become supervisors, site managers, or self-employed contractors. Specialization in areas like pre-stressed concrete, steel erection, or formwork design can lead to higher pay. Some workers transition to related fields such as civil engineering technology or safety inspection. Continuous upskilling and staying updated with new materials and techniques are important for long-term career growth.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- Urbanization
- Infrastructure
- Housing
- Renewable Energy

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do building frame trades workers earn in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and employer, but are generally above the national minimum wage. Workers with specialized skills or in high-demand areas may earn significantly more.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a building frame trades worker in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from a Brazilian employer who must obtain authorization from the Ministry of Labor. The process is easier for citizens of Mercosur countries under the Mercosur Residence Agreement.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: With experience and additional training, workers can become supervisors, site managers, or start their own construction businesses. Specializing in areas like steel structure or concrete formwork can also increase earning potential.
