# Animal Producers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 6129 (ISCO08)**

Animal producers not elsewhere classified encompass a diverse group of professionals engaged in raising and managing animals outside mainstream livestock or poultry farming. This includes beekeepers (apiculture), rabbit breeders (cuniculture), worm farmers (vermicomposting), insect breeders, and other niche animal production activities. In Brazil, these roles are often small-scale or family-run, with increasing interest in sustainable and organic practices. The work involves feeding, health monitoring, breeding, and harvesting animal products such as honey, meat, eggs, or compost. Producers may also process and sell products directly to consumers or local markets. The occupation requires practical animal husbandry skills, basic business knowledge, and adaptability to environmental and market conditions. While not a regulated profession, adherence to health and environmental standards is essential.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with hands-on experience, often through family farms or internships. Producers may expand scale, diversify products, or vertically integrate into processing and direct sales (e.g., farmers' markets, online). Some move into consultancy, teaching workshops, or supplying equipment/breeding stock to new entrants. Formal education, such as technical courses in beekeeping or agroecology, can enhance credibility. With experience, producers may join cooperatives to gain market power. However, formal career ladders are rare; success depends on entrepreneurial skill and niche specialization. Many remain self-employed, and transition to large-scale commercial farming is uncommon.

- Real-time monitoring and automatic adjustment of environmental parameters (temperature, humidity, ammonia)

- Automatic generation and preliminary analysis of farm production reports

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an animal producer (not elsewhere classified) in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by animal type and scale. Small-scale beekeepers or rabbit breeders often earn between R$ 18,000 and R$ 36,000 per year, though profits can be higher with direct sales. Formal employees in larger operations may receive a monthly salary near the minimum wage (around R$ 1,320/month in 2024), plus benefits. Income is heavily dependent on market niche, productivity, and seasonality.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa in Brazil as an animal producer?**
A: Generally difficult, as the occupation is not considered high-skill. The VITEM V visa requires a job offer from a Brazilian employer and proof of specialized knowledge that cannot be filled locally, which is rare for niche animal production. Mercosur nationals can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement if they have sufficient means and intend to work as self-employed producers. Other pathways, like a startup visa or investment visa, may be possible for larger-scale commercial operations.

**Q: How can I succeed as an animal producer in Brazil?**
A: Focus on niche markets with growing demand, such as organic honey, rabbits for gourmet meat, or earthworms for organic fertilizers. Obtain certification (organic, good practices) to access premium prices. Join cooperatives for better market access. Leverage online sales and local farmers' markets. Continuous learning through SENAR courses and agroecology workshops is key.
