# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers perform manual tasks in forestry operations such as planting, tending, and felling trees, as well as clearing undergrowth and maintaining forest trails. They work in reforestation, timber extraction, and conservation projects across Brazil, particularly in the Amazon and Atlantic Forest regions. The occupation requires physical stamina and the ability to work outdoors in challenging conditions.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 195,923. Employment of forestry labourers in Brazil is expected to remain stable in the short term, driven by demand for sustainable forestry and carbon credit projects. However, mechanization and automation of tasks like felling and hauling are reducing the need for manual labour. Growth opportunities lie in reforestation initiatives and environmental conservation programs, but overall demand is projected to decline gradually over the next decade.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for forestry labourers is limited; advancement typically involves moving into supervisory roles or specializing in areas like forest management or environmental restoration. Some workers transition to related fields such as landscaping or agriculture. Training programs offered by institutions like SENAR (National Rural Learning Service) can improve skills and lead to technician-level positions, but upward mobility is modest.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable forestry
- reforestation
- carbon credits
- native species planting

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a forestry labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are low, typically around the minimum wage (approximately R$1,320 per month in 2024), though experienced workers in remote areas may earn slightly more. Annual pre-tax income ranges from R$15,000 to R$20,000.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a forestry labourer in Brazil?**
A: It is rare, as the occupation is low-skilled and not eligible for employer-sponsored work visas (VITEM V) which require higher qualifications. Mercosur citizens may obtain residence, but demand is minimal.
