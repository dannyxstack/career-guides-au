# Fishery And Aquaculture Labourer — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 9216 (ISCO08)**

Fishery and aquaculture labourers perform manual tasks in the capture and cultivation of fish, crustaceans, molluscs, and aquatic plants. Their duties include preparing and mending nets, feeding and handling fish, cleaning tanks and ponds, harvesting products, and maintaining equipment. They work in marine and freshwater environments, often in physically demanding conditions. The occupation is a key entry-level role in Brazil's fishing and aquaculture sectors, which span coastal communities and inland water bodies.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 19,873. Brazil's fishing and aquaculture sector is stable, with aquaculture showing moderate growth due to rising domestic demand for fish protein. However, capture fisheries face challenges from overfishing and stricter environmental regulations. Employment opportunities are concentrated in coastal states like Santa Catarina, Bahia, and Pará, as well as in inland fish-farming regions. The labour market is largely informal, with many workers lacking formal contracts. Government initiatives to promote sustainable fishing and aquaculture may create incremental jobs, but overall employment growth is slow.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for fishery and aquaculture labourers is limited. With experience and additional training, they may advance to roles such as fishing boat skipper, aquaculture farm supervisor, or equipment operator. Some workers transition into related fields like fish processing or vessel maintenance. Formal education is not required, but technical courses in aquaculture or fishery management can improve advancement prospects. The informal nature of much of the sector means that career paths are often unstructured.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated feeding systems take over scheduled and quantitative feeding tasks
- Water quality sensors automatically monitor and record parameters like pH and dissolved oxygen.
- Drones or underwater robots automatically inspect fish cages, replacing manual visual inspection
- AI video analysis automatically counts fish schools and detects diseases or abnormal behavior
- Environmental control systems (e.g., aerators) automatically start/stop based on sensor data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted water quality data analysis to predict algal blooms or disease outbreaks, guiding preventive measures
- Automated feeding systems optimize feeding strategies based on fish activity data to improve feed conversion rates
- Digital twin models simulate different breeding scenarios to assist production planning
- Wearable devices monitor worker health to prevent heatstroke or fatigue
- AI document assistant automatically generates farming logs and compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- On-site response capability for sudden equipment failures, extreme weather, and other abnormal conditions
- Manual experience-based judgement of fish and shrimp behaviour and health (e.g., abnormal swimming, colour changes)
- Interpersonal and negotiation skills in coordinating with suppliers, veterinarians, and regulatory bodies
- On-site safety judgment and emergency rescue (e.g., drowning, electric shock)

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use mainstream smart aquaculture platforms (e.g., AquaManager, eFishery)
- Basic data processing and analysis skills (Excel or simple BI tools)
- Basic skills in operating and maintaining automatic feeding systems, drones, and ROVs.
- Understand working principles of aquaculture Internet of Things (IoT) sensors
- Sustainable farming principles (e.g., antibiotic reduction, carbon footprint accounting)
- English communication skills to suit international companies or export requirements

**Key growth areas:**

- Aquaculture
- Fish farming
- Sustainable fishing
- Coastal communities

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a fishery and aquaculture labourer in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are generally around the minimum wage, approximately R$1,500–2,000 per month before taxation, varying by region and formality of work.

**Q: Can I obtain a work visa to be a fishery labourer in Brazil?**
A: It is difficult. This occupation is not considered skilled, so employer-sponsored VITEM V visas are rarely approved. Mercosur nationals may use the residence agreement.

**Q: What are the prospects for advancement in this career?**
A: Advancement is limited. With experience, you may become a fishing boat skipper or aquaculture farm supervisor, but formal training is often needed.
