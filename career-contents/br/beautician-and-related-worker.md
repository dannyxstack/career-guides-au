# Beautician and Related Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5142 (ISCO08)**

Beauticians and related workers in Brazil provide personal care services including hair styling, skincare, makeup, manicure, and pedicure. They work in salons, spas, or as self-employed professionals, serving a diverse clientele across urban and rural areas. The occupation is dominated by women and often involves entrepreneurial activities. While formal employment exists, informality is high, especially among manicurists and hairdressers. Growth is driven by increasing demand for aesthetic services, but competition is intense due to low barriers to entry. Many workers pursue continuous training to specialize and improve earnings.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,395,170. Employment outlook for beauticians in Brazil is stable but competitive. The sector has grown with rising consumer spending on personal appearance, especially in larger cities. However, economic downturns can reduce discretionary spending. The COVID-19 pandemic caused temporary closures, but recovery has been strong. The trend toward formalization and regulation may increase, but currently, many work informally. Employment opportunities are available in salons, clinics, and as freelancers. Growth areas include organic products, men's grooming, and specialized treatments. The occupation is not considered a skilled migration pathway.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts with apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to specialization in areas like haircutting, colorimetry, or aesthetic procedures. Experienced beauticians may open their own salons, become trainers, or move into product sales. Some pursue higher-level courses in areas like dermatology or cosmetics. The career path is flexible but competitive, with income varying widely based on clientele, location, and reputation. Many workers remain self-employed, building a loyal customer base. Professional certifications can enhance credibility and earning potential.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a beautician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely from minimum wage (around R$1,320/month) to over R$5,000/month for specialized professionals in upscale salons or self-employed workers with a loyal clientele. Earnings depend on location, specialization, and experience.

**Q: Can a foreign beautician get a work visa in Brazil?**
A: It is difficult for beauticians to obtain a work visa (VITEM V) because the occupation is not classified as skilled in high demand. However, nationals of Mercosur countries can apply for temporary residence under the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which allows them to work in any occupation without employer sponsorship.
