# Economist — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2631 (ISCO08)**

Economists in Brazil analyze data, trends, and policies to provide insights into economic issues such as inflation, employment, and fiscal policy. They work in government agencies, financial institutions, consulting firms, and academia, applying economic theory to real-world problems. The role requires strong analytical skills and proficiency in statistical software.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior analyst, moving to senior economist, and then to chief economist or policy advisor. Many advance into specialized fields like environmental or behavioral economics. Public sector careers offer stability, while private sector roles provide faster progression and higher salaries but less job security.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an economist in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience. Entry-level economists earn around BRL 60,000 annually, while senior economists can make up to BRL 200,000. Public sector roles tend to pay less but offer strong benefits.

**Q: What visa options are available for foreign economists to work in Brazil?**
A: Foreign economists typically need an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa. Those from Mercosur countries can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement. The CNIg can also authorize work for highly skilled professionals in priority areas.

**Q: Is CORECON registration mandatory for all economists in Brazil?**
A: Yes, CORECON registration is mandatory to legally practice as an economist in Brazil, whether in the public or private sector. It requires a recognized degree and passing a professional exam.
