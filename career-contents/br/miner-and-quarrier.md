# Miner or Quarry Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers extract minerals and other geological materials from the earth, working in underground mines, open-pit mines, or quarries. Their tasks include drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting ore, as well as operating heavy machinery. The occupation demands physical stamina, attention to safety protocols, and adaptability to harsh environments. In Brazil, the sector is concentrated in states like Minas Gerais, Pará, and Bahia, with a significant presence of large mining companies and cooperatives.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with entry-level roles as general laborers or equipment operators. With experience and training, workers can advance to supervisory positions, such as shift supervisor or mine manager. Specialization in areas like drilling, blasting, or haulage can lead to higher pay. Technical courses and safety certifications (NRM) are common pathways for advancement. Some workers transition to related fields like civil construction or logistics.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Operating mining equipment such as drills and excavators (automated remote control replaces manual driving)
- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)
- Daily equipment inspection and simple maintenance (predictive maintenance system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)
- Production scheduling and resource allocation (AI optimization algorithm)

**Human moat:**

- On-site assessment and handling of emergencies (e.g., collapses, gas leaks)
- Adaptive operation for abnormal geological conditions (unstructured environment)
- Team safety management and communication coordination
- Mining regulation compliance and government reporting responsibilities
- Complex repairs and equipment modifications (requiring physical hands-on skills)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated mining systems and remote-control equipment
- Data analysis and use of mining software (e.g., Surpac, Datamine)
- Skills in drone and sensor maintenance
- Basic programming and automation scripting (Python)
- Safety regulations and emergency response training
- Hazardous environment work skills (e.g., confined space entry)

**Key growth areas:**

- Safety technology
- Automation
- Sustainable mining
- Remote operations

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a miner in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by region, experience, and company size. Entry-level miners may earn around the national minimum wage (about R$1,320 per month in 2024), while experienced workers in large mining firms can earn between R$2,500 and R$5,000 monthly. Hazard pay and overtime can increase earnings. For specific data, refer to PNAD Contínua or industry salary surveys.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a miner in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but the process is restricted. Foreign workers need a VITEM V work visa sponsored by a Brazilian employer, who must demonstrate that the role cannot be filled locally. Nationals of Mercosur countries can use the Mercosur Residence Agreement for easier entry. Due to the hazardous nature, employers often prefer local hires.

**Q: What are common career advancement opportunities for miners?**
A: Miners can progress to supervisory roles (e.g., shift supervisor, mine foreman) or specialize in technical areas like drilling, blasting, or equipment maintenance. Obtaining additional certifications (e.g., in safety management or operation of automated systems) can lead to higher-paying positions. Some may transition to roles in mining engineering or logistics within the same company.
