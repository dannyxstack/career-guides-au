# Mining Supervisor — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3121 (ISCO08)**

Mining Supervisors coordinate and oversee daily operations in surface and underground mines, ensuring safety, productivity, and compliance with regulations. They manage crews, monitor equipment, and implement extraction plans. In Brazil, they are critical to the mineral-rich states like Minas Gerais and Pará, where iron ore, gold, and bauxite mining dominate. The role requires strong leadership, technical knowledge of mining processes, and adherence to strict safety standards (NR-22).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a mining technician or equipment operator, advancing to shift supervisor, then mine coordinator, and ultimately mine manager. Specialization in safety (NR-22) or automation (autonomous haulage) can accelerate growth. Many supervisors hold a technical degree (Tecnólogo) or engineering diploma. Continuous learning in risk management and digital tools is increasingly valued for senior roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual inspection and equipment status checks (replaced by sensors and drones)
- Daily production report generation (completed by automated data systems)
- Simple fault diagnosis (taken over by AI predictive maintenance system)
- On-site safety monitoring (replaced by video analysis and early warning systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use digital twins to optimise mining plans and schedules.
- Reducing equipment downtime through AI-powered predictive maintenance
- Intelligent risk analysis enhances safety management efficiency
- Big data analytics optimizes ore grade control

**Human moat:**

- Emergency decision-making and on-site judgment for sudden incidents
- Team morale management and conflict mediation
- Experience in flexible adjustments under complex geological conditions
- Communication and coordination with regulators and the community

**Skills to build:**

- Industrial IoT platform operation (e.g., SCADA systems)
- Geographic Information System (GIS) and remote sensing data analysis
- Automated machinery and equipment management
- Basic data analysis in Python or SQL
- Lean management and digital project management
- Human-machine collaboration and cross-department communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Iron ore expansion
- Critical minerals

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much does a Mining Supervisor earn in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region, company size, and experience level. Official statistics (such as RAIS or CAGED) provide detailed ranges. Generally, supervisors earn well above the national average, with senior roles in large mining corporations offering premium pay. For precise figures, consult the most recent Ministry of Labor reports.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as a Mining Supervisor in Brazil?**
A: Yes, a foreigner can obtain a VITEM V visa if sponsored by a Brazilian mining company. The process requires a labor contract, proof of qualifications, and approval from the Ministry of Labor. Alternatively, Mercosur nationals can use the residence agreement for easier entry. Shortage of qualified supervisors may expedite approvals.

**Q: What is the typical work schedule for a Mining Supervisor?**
A: Mining supervisors often work rotational shifts (e.g., 14 days on, 14 days off) in remote mining camps. Hours can be long, including nights and weekends. The job involves both office planning and field supervision, requiring physical presence in noisy, dusty environments.
