# Electronics Engineering Technician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 3114 (ISCO08)**

Electronics engineering technicians in Brazil assist engineers in designing, testing, and maintaining electronic systems, equipment, and components. They work across industries such as telecommunications, industrial automation, consumer electronics, and renewable energy. Technicians typically hold a technical diploma or higher education degree and are registered with the regional engineering council (CREA). Their responsibilities include assembling circuits, troubleshooting malfunctions, installing equipment, and ensuring compliance with safety standards. The role requires practical skills, attention to detail, and the ability to read schematics and technical manuals.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for electronics engineering technicians typically begins with a technical diploma and entry-level roles in maintenance or assembly. With experience, technicians can advance to senior technician, team leader, or specialist roles in areas like automation, telecom, or embedded systems. Further education (e.g., a bachelor's degree in engineering) can open paths to design or management positions. Many technicians also pursue certifications in specific technologies (e.g., PLC programming, fiber optics) to enhance mobility. Public sector jobs and large corporations offer stable careers, while self-employment or freelance work is possible for experienced technicians.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an electronics technician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience, typically ranging from R$30,000 to R$60,000 annually, with senior technicians or those in specialized fields earning up to R$80,000. Public sector roles may have lower entry but better benefits.

**Q: Can foreign electronics technicians work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, through an employer-sponsored VITEM V work visa or the Mercosur Residence Agreement for nationals of member countries. The employer must demonstrate the need for a foreign worker and comply with CNIg regulations. Alternatively, a CNIg professional authorisation may be obtained for certain roles.

**Q: What are the main industries hiring electronics technicians in Brazil?**
A: Major employers include telecommunications companies, industrial automation firms, renewable energy companies (solar, wind), consumer electronics manufacturers, and maintenance services. The automotive and aerospace sectors also offer opportunities.
