# Structural Metal Preparer and Erector — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7214 (ISCO08)**

Structural metal preparers and erectors assemble, install, and repair metal frameworks for buildings, bridges, industrial plants, and other structures. In Brazil, these skilled tradespeople are essential to the construction and manufacturing sectors, working with steel beams, columns, and trusses. They interpret blueprints, cut and weld metal components, and use rigging equipment to position and secure structures. The occupation requires technical training, physical stamina, and adherence to safety standards, particularly for work at heights. Demand is tied to infrastructure projects, industrial expansion, and oil and gas developments, with opportunities in both large-scale construction and specialized fabrication shops.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or technical course, leading to a journeyman role. With experience, workers can become lead erectors, supervisors, or project coordinators. Specialization in areas like high-rise construction, bridge erection, or offshore platforms can increase earning potential. Additional certifications in welding, rigging, or safety (e.g., NR-35) enhance prospects. Some move into related fields such as structural inspection or training. Union membership (e.g., Sindicato dos Trabalhadores nas Indústrias da Construção) provides support. Advancement may require relocation to regions with major projects. Overall, the path is linear but rewarding for those with technical skill and dedication.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a structural metal preparer and erector in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and employer. Entry-level workers earn around R$2,500 per month, while experienced professionals in major industrial projects can earn up to R$4,500 or more, with additional overtime and benefits.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for this occupation in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The primary route is the VITEM V visa, which requires a job offer and employer sponsorship, along with proof that no qualified Brazilian worker is available. Mercosur nationals benefit from the Mercosur Residence Agreement, which simplifies the process.

**Q: What is the career progression like for this role?**
A: With experience and certifications, workers can advance to lead erector, supervisor, or project manager. Specializing in niche areas like offshore platforms or high-rise construction can boost earnings. Many also move into related fields such as structural inspection or training.
