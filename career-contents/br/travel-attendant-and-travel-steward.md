# Travel Attendants and Travel Stewards — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 5111 (ISCO08)**

Travel Attendants and Travel Stewards, including flight attendants and train stewards, ensure passenger safety, comfort, and service during commercial air or rail journeys. They perform pre-flight/trip safety checks, demonstrate emergency procedures, serve meals and beverages, assist passengers with special needs, and manage in-flight/train sales. In Brazil, this occupation is predominantly in the aviation sector (comissários de voo) and requires certification from the National Civil Aviation Agency (ANAC) for air roles, while train stewards follow similar service standards on long-distance railway routes. The role demands strong customer service skills, composure during emergencies, and flexibility with irregular schedules and time zones.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 16,615. Brazil's domestic air travel is recovering and growing, driven by tourism and economic activity, which sustains demand for flight attendants. However, automation of some in-flight services and cost-cutting by airlines may slow hiring. Rail travel is expanding in select corridors (e.g., Rio-São Paulo), but remains a smaller employer. The post-pandemic recovery in international tourism also supports jobs at major carriers like LATAM and Gol. Overall, employment is expected to grow steadily at 2-4% annually, with competition for positions at legacy airlines remaining high due to the attractive lifestyle.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior attendant on domestic routes, advancing to international flights after seniority. Senior attendants can become pursers or lead stewards overseeing cabin crews. Some transition to ground roles such as in-flight trainer, cabin crew supervisor, or airport operations. Lateral moves exist to hospitality management or tourism. Brazilian airlines require periodic recurrent training and ANAC recertification. Career longevity is limited by physical demands, with many exiting after 10–15 years, though a few stay to retirement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI automatically generates flight briefings and safety demonstration videos
- AI chatbots handle routine passenger inquiries and ticket changes
- AI analyzes passenger emotions in real time and pushes personalized service suggestions
- AI-driven automated scheduling and fatigue management optimization
- AI conducts crew training assessments and compliance checks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI provides real-time multilingual translation assistance to enhance communication efficiency with foreign travelers
- AI enhances emergency drill quality by simulating unexpected situations through cockpit sensors
- AI analysis of passenger feedback data to assist in optimising service processes
- AI automatically records service operations, reducing paperwork burden

**Human moat:**

- Human judgment and handling of in-flight emergencies such as illness, fire, hijacking
- Emotional reassurance and conflict resolution for anxious, intoxicated, or non-compliant passengers
- Command capability to organize orderly passenger evacuation during emergencies
- Discerning subtle needs and providing value-added services with a personal touch in multicultural contexts.
- Mandated safety duties (e.g., closing doors, checking equipment)

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a flight attendant in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level flight attendants at major Brazilian airlines earn around BRL 3,000 to BRL 5,000 per month (pre-tax) including flight allowances, with senior attendants on international routes reaching BRL 8,000 to BRL 12,000. Benefits often include health insurance, meal allowances, and discounted travel.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a flight attendant in Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Foreign nationals must have an employer (Brazilian airline) sponsor a VITEM V work visa, and hold ANAC certification (usually obtained in Brazil). Mercosur citizens can use the Residence Agreement for a simpler process. Local competition is intense, so visas are not common except for specialized roles (e.g., language skills for international routes).

**Q: Is there a demand for train stewards in Brazil?**
A: Train steward roles are rare, limited to long-distance passenger services like the Serra Verde Express and a few other scenic routes. The demand is low compared to aviation, and positions are often filled internally from railway staff. Most travel attendant jobs in Brazil are in aviation.
