# Veterinarians — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2250 (ISCO08)**

Veterinarians diagnose and treat animal diseases, perform surgeries, and promote public health and animal welfare. They work in clinics, farms, research institutions, and government agencies, addressing livestock productivity, companion animal care, and zoonotic disease control. The profession requires a bachelor's degree in veterinary medicine and registration with the Regional Council of Veterinary Medicine (CRMV). Brazil's diverse agricultural sector and growing pet industry drive demand, with opportunities in agribusiness, food safety, and public health.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 131,283. Employment outlook for veterinarians in Brazil is positive due to expanding agribusiness and increasing pet ownership. The livestock and poultry sectors require veterinarians for health management and food safety compliance. Urbanization fuels demand for companion animal services, while public health programs need veterinarians for disease surveillance. However, competition is high in metropolitan areas, with many graduates. Remote agricultural regions offer better opportunities. The profession is regulated, ensuring stable demand but also limiting supply without proper credentials.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with general clinical practice or work on farms. Veterinarians can specialize in areas such as surgery, equine medicine, epidemiology, or food inspection. Advancement includes owning a private clinic, becoming a technical director in agribusiness, or pursuing roles in government agencies (e.g., Ministry of Agriculture). Academic careers require master's or PhD degrees. Leadership positions in research or regulatory bodies are possible with experience. The career path offers diversity but requires continuous learning.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening of imaging (X-ray/ultrasound) assisted by AI
- Standardized medical record entry and prescription generation
- Automated appointment management for routine vaccinations and deworming
- Automated interpretation of lab sample analysis (blood count/chemistry)
- Simple symptom consultation in telemedicine

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems provide reference for rare case identification
- Robot-assisted surgery improves precision in minimally invasive procedures
- Continuous monitoring of patient rehabilitation data via smart wearable devices
- Big data analytics to predict disease outbreaks and medication trends
- Personalized medical plan generation (based on genetics and medical history)

**Human moat:**

- Manual dexterity and adaptability in complex surgeries
- Empathetic communication and trust-building with pet owners.
- Cross-species clinical judgment (especially for large animals)
- Ethical decisions (e.g., timing of euthanasia)
- On-site handling ability in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Operating AI diagnostic imaging tools and verifying results
- Telemedicine platform usage and data analysis
- Genomics and precision medicine knowledge
- Basics of animal behavior and psychology
- Advanced management of digital health records (EHR)
- Surgical Robot Collaboration Skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Agribusiness
- Companion Animals
- Food Safety
- Zoonosis Control

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Those passionate about animal health and welfare, including both companion animals and livestock
- People who enjoy clinical work, surgery, and hands-on procedures in diverse settings
- Individuals interested in agribusiness, food safety, and public health roles

**Who is not suited**
- Those who dislike irregular working hours, on-call duties, and physical demands of farm work
- Individuals who prefer minimal interaction with animals or blood and bodily fluids

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for veterinarians in Brazil?**
A: Starting salaries range from BRL 48,000 to 72,000 per year (BRL 4,000-6,000/month). Experienced veterinarians can earn between BRL 96,000 and 180,000 annually (BRL 8,000-15,000/month). Salaries vary by region, with higher pay in agricultural areas and lower in saturated urban markets.

**Q: Do I need a job offer to immigrate as a veterinarian to Brazil?**
A: Yes, typically. The main work visa (VITEM V) requires employer sponsorship and labor ministry approval (CNIg). You must also have CRMV registration. Citizens of Mercosur countries can apply for residence under the Mercosur Agreement, which is easier but still requires a job or proof of profession.

**Q: Is there a shortage of veterinarians in Brazil?**
A: In remote agricultural regions, yes—there is a shortage of veterinarians willing to work in rural areas. However, in major cities like São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, the market is competitive with many graduates. Overall, the profession is regulated and demand is stable but not categorized as a critical shortage occupation.
