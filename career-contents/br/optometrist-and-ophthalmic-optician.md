# Ophthalmic Optician — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2267 (ISCO08)**

Ophthalmic opticians in Brazil, also known as optometrists or ópticos, are healthcare professionals specializing in vision care, including prescribing and dispensing corrective lenses, detecting eye abnormalities, and providing pre- and post-operative care for refractive surgery. They work primarily in optical stores, clinics, and hospitals. The profession is regulated by the Conselho Federal de Óptica e Optometria, requiring a technical or higher education degree in optometry. With an aging population and increased screen time, demand for vision correction services is growing, though competition from ophthalmologists remains. Career progression includes specialization in contact lenses, low vision, or clinic management.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 4,093. The Brazilian employment outlook for ophthalmic opticians is stable, with growth driven by rising demand for corrective eyewear, contact lenses, and refractive surgery. The aging population increases the need for presbyopia and cataract management, while digital eye strain pushes younger demographics to seek care. However, the profession faces competition from ophthalmologists, who can perform medical procedures. Public health initiatives and private sector expansion, particularly in optical retail chains, offer job opportunities. Emerging tele-optometry may create new roles. Overall, moderate growth is expected, with urban areas offering more prospects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for ophthalmic opticians typically begins with a technical degree and initial roles as assistant opticians in retail stores. With experience and additional certifications (e.g., contact lens fitting, low vision), opticians can advance to senior optician, store manager, or clinical specialist. Some pursue further education to become optometrists (if recognized) or transition to ophthalmology assistance. Entrepreneurial opticians may open their own optical shops. Specialization in pediatric optometry, sports vision, or geriatric care enhances career prospects. Continuing education and membership in professional councils are essential for advancement.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an ophthalmic optician in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, with higher earnings in private practice and large cities. Entry-level opticians earn around R$ 1,500 per month, while experienced opticians in managerial roles can earn over R$ 5,000 per month.

**Q: Can foreign opticians get a work visa for Brazil?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Foreign opticians need an employer sponsorship via a VITEM V visa, and their qualifications must be validated and recognized by the Conselho Regional de Óptica e Optometria. Mercosur nationals face fewer barriers.

**Q: What are the main challenges in this profession?**
A: Competition from ophthalmologists and limited public recognition of optometry as a standalone profession are key challenges. Also, staying updated with technology and regulations requires continuous education.
