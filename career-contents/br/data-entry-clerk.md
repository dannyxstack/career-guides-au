# Data Entry Clerk — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 4132 (ISCO08)**

Data Entry Clerks are responsible for accurately inputting, updating, and maintaining data into computer systems and databases from various sources, such as paper documents or electronic files. They ensure data integrity and perform routine data verification and correction tasks. In Brazil, this occupation is common across many sectors, including finance, healthcare, government, and retail. While the role requires basic computer literacy and attention to detail, it is often entry-level and does not demand advanced education. Automation and digitalization are increasingly reducing demand for manual data entry, making it a declining occupation with limited career advancement prospects.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 20,979. The employment outlook for Data Entry Clerks in Brazil is negative due to automation, artificial intelligence, and process digitalization. Many routine data entry tasks are being replaced by software, optical character recognition, and integrated systems. Demand is expected to continue shrinking, especially in large corporations and government agencies. However, some demand remains in small businesses and sectors with legacy paper-based processes. Opportunities will likely shift toward roles that combine data entry with analysis or customer service. Job seekers should focus on upskilling in data analysis or administrative management to remain competitive.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression for Data Entry Clerks is limited. Typically, after gaining experience, individuals may move into supervisory roles such as data entry team lead, or transition into related fields like administrative assistant, office manager, or data analyst (with additional training). Some clerks specialize in specific industries (e.g., medical coding, legal document processing) to increase earning potential. However, without further education or certification, upward mobility is constrained. The occupation is often a stepping stone rather than a long-term career, and many workers use it as an entry point to office work before branching out.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually enter data from paper or electronic documents into databases or spreadsheets
- Check format and basic accuracy of input data
- Repetitive batch data entry, such as invoices and forms
- Basic data cleaning and deduplication

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses AI tools to automatically extract document data, with humans handling exceptions.
- Use automated scripts to verify data consistency and improve efficiency
- Analyze data trends and generate reports through AI-assisted analysis

**Human moat:**

- Complex data judgment and anomaly handling capability
- Client communication and business understanding (e.g., explaining data deviations)
- Cross-system data integration and process optimization
- Maintain data privacy and security compliance

**Skills to build:**

- Learn Python or VBA for automated data processing
- Proficiency in advanced Excel functions (e.g., Power Query, macros)
- Familiar with OCR and RPA tools (e.g., UiPath)
- Data quality management and database basics (SQL)
- Basic data analysis and visualization (e.g., Tableau)

**Key growth areas:**

- automation
- digital transformation
- data analysis
- administrative support

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Data Entry Clerk in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and sector, but typically range from one to three times the national minimum wage (around R$1,500 to R$3,000 per month). Large companies and specialized sectors may pay slightly more, while entry-level positions often start at minimum wage.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa for a Data Entry Clerk job in Brazil?**
A: It is difficult because data entry is not considered a skilled occupation for employer-sponsored work visas (VITEM V). However, Mercosur citizens can obtain residency under the Mercosur Residence Agreement and work in any job, including data entry. For non-Mercosur nationals, a work visa is unlikely.

**Q: What are the best ways to advance from a Data Entry Clerk role in Brazil?**
A: Pursuing certifications in data analysis, office management, or specialized software (e.g., SAP, Salesforce) can open doors. Gaining experience and moving into supervisory or administrative assistant roles is common. Some workers also transition to fields like customer service or accounting with additional training.
