# Mining Manager — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 1322 (ISCO08)**

Mining managers plan, organize, direct and coordinate operations activities within mines and quarries, including resource extraction, safety, and logistics. They oversee production targets, manage teams of engineers and technicians, ensure compliance with environmental and safety regulations, and optimize operational efficiency. In Brazil, mining managers are critical in mineral-rich regions such as Minas Gerais and Pará, working with multinational and state-owned companies. The role demands strong leadership, technical expertise in mining engineering, and familiarity with Brazilian regulatory frameworks.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 5,575. Brazil's mining sector is projected to grow driven by global demand for iron ore, gold, and base metals, as well as infrastructure projects. However, regulatory changes and environmental licensing can impact operations. The adoption of digital technologies (automation, remote monitoring) and sustainability practices creates demand for managers with tech and ESG skills. The sector faces a shortage of experienced managers, particularly in remote Amazonian regions. Medium-term outlook is stable, with opportunities in large mining companies and consultancies.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins as a mining engineer or geologist, moving into supervisory roles (shift supervisor, mine operations coordinator) before reaching management. Further advancement leads to operations director or regional manager. Specialization in safety, environmental management, or new technologies can accelerate growth. Lateral moves into mining consulting or corporate roles (e.g., VP of Operations) are possible. Brazilian mining managers often hold postgraduate degrees (MBA, mining management) and must maintain CREA registration.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated data collection and report generation (e.g., output, equipment status)
- Basic scheduling and task assignment (AI-optimized scheduling)
- Routine safety monitoring and alarm filtering (AI anomaly detection)
- Standard document generation and initial compliance checks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological modeling and ore body prediction (AI-accelerated analysis)
- Real-time equipment health monitoring and predictive maintenance
- Optimize mining plans and resource allocation (operations research AI)
- Automated analysis and early warning of environmental compliance data
- Virtual training and AR-assisted on-site guidance

**Human moat:**

- Complex decision-making (e.g., responding to sudden geological disasters)
- Stakeholder communication and team leadership
- Final judgment on safety and responsibility (legal, ethical)
- Long-term strategic planning and capital investment decisions

**Skills to build:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a mining manager in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on location, company size, and experience. Senior managers at large mining companies (Vale, Anglo American) earn well above the national average, often with extensive benefits. Entry-level managers earn less but still above median. Specific figures depend on the region and contract terms.

**Q: How can a foreign mining manager obtain a work visa for Brazil?**
A: The process involves a Brazilian employer sponsoring the worker through a CNIg application, demonstrating that the role requires specialized skills not available locally. The worker must also obtain CREA equivalency if holding a foreign engineering degree. The visa (VITEM V) is then issued for up to 2 years, renewable.
