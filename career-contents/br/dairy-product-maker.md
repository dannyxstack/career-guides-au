# Dairy Products Maker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7513 (ISCO08)**

Dairy Products Makers operate machinery and perform manual tasks to process milk into products such as cheese, butter, yogurt, and powdered milk. They work in dairy processing plants, ensuring hygiene standards, monitoring fermentation, pasteurization, and packaging. This role requires attention to detail, physical stamina, and adherence to strict food safety regulations (e.g., ANVISA). In Brazil, the occupation is essential to the agribusiness sector, particularly in states like Minas Gerais, Goiás, and Rio Grande do Sul, where dairy farming is prominent.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a production assistant or machine operator, advancing to shift supervisor or quality control technician. Further specialization can occur in artisan cheese making, dairy technology, or food safety. With experience and additional training (e.g., technical courses from SENAI), workers may move into plant management or consultancy. The trend toward value-added products (e.g., organic, lactose-free) offers opportunities for skilled makers to command higher wages.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a Dairy Products Maker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary by region and experience level. Entry-level workers typically earn between R$1,500 and R$2,500 per month, while experienced artisans can earn up to R$4,000. Large industrial plants may offer slightly higher wages, but overall the occupation offers modest income.

**Q: Can a foreigner obtain a work visa as a Dairy Products Maker in Brazil?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not considered high-skilled. The VITEM V visa requires employer sponsorship and proof that the foreigner has specialized knowledge unavailable locally. Mercosur nationals may find it easier under the Residence Agreement, but sponsorship is still required.

**Q: What are the opportunities for career advancement?**
A: Progression includes moving to supervisor, quality control, or plant management roles. Specializing in artisan cheese or organic products can open niche markets. Additional technical qualifications from SENAI or dairy institutes improve prospects.
