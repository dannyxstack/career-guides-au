# Building Structure Cleaner — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7133 (ISCO08)**

Building structure cleaners in Brazil maintain the cleanliness and appearance of building exteriors and interiors, including windows, facades, roofs, and chimneys. They use specialized equipment such as scaffolding, ladders, and pressure washers, and often work at heights. The role requires physical stamina, attention to safety, and basic knowledge of cleaning agents. In Brazil, this occupation is common in urban areas with high-rise buildings and commercial properties. Most cleaners work for cleaning companies or are self-employed, with demand fluctuating with construction activity and property maintenance needs.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for building structure cleaners is limited but possible. Entry-level workers can advance to supervisor roles or specialize in high-rise cleaning, industrial cleaning, or hazardous material removal. Some may start their own cleaning businesses. Training is typically on the job, but certifications in safety (e.g., working at heights) can improve prospects. Experienced cleaners may transition into related fields like facility management or inspection. Overall, the path is horizontal rather than vertical, with income growth tied to specialization or entrepreneurship.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building structure cleaner in Brazil?**
A: Salaries are generally low, ranging from about R$18,000 to R$30,000 per year pre-tax, depending on experience, region, and employer. Specialized cleaners working at heights or with hazardous materials may earn more.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa as a building structure cleaner in Brazil?**
A: It is difficult as the occupation is not considered skilled. The VITEM V visa requires employer sponsorship and proof that no Brazilian worker is available, which is unlikely for cleaning roles. Mercosur nationals may work under the Mercosur Residence Agreement without a specific skill requirement.

**Q: What are the main risks of this job?**
A: The main risks include falls from heights, exposure to cleaning chemicals, and physical strain. Proper safety training and equipment are essential to mitigate these risks.
