# Craft and Related Worker — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

Craft and Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified encompass a diverse group of skilled artisans and manual workers who create, repair, or restore products by hand, including traditional crafts, artistic metalwork, wood carving, textile arts, and other specialized trades. In Brazil, these workers are often found in informal or small-scale enterprises, cultural industries, and tourism-related sectors, preserving regional heritage while adapting to modern market demands. Their work combines manual dexterity, creativity, and technical knowledge, typically acquired through apprenticeships or vocational training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for craft workers often moves from apprentice to journeyman to master artisan or independent studio owner. Many diversify skills or specialize in high-demand niches like eco-friendly materials or luxury bespoke items. Formalization through certifications (e.g., SENAI courses) can open doors to higher-value projects and export markets. Some transition into teaching or cultural entrepreneurship, while others remain in traditional family businesses.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a craft worker in Brazil?**
A: Salaries vary widely by skill and region, but most entry-level craft workers earn between R$ 1,200 and R$ 2,500 per month. Experienced artisans or those in high-demand niches may earn R$ 3,000–R$ 5,000, while master craftspeople with established clientele can exceed R$ 7,000.

**Q: Can a foreign craft worker get a Brazilian work visa?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. The most common route is an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa, requiring a job offer from a Brazilian company that proves no local worker is available. Alternatively, citizens of Mercosur countries can obtain residency more easily. Independent craft work (e.g., selling at markets) generally requires a business visa or local registration.

**Q: Are craft workers considered skilled labor in Brazil?**
A: Craft occupations are generally classified as semi-skilled or skilled manual work. They are not on official shortage lists, but specialized traditional crafts (e.g., restoration, indigenous art) may have niche demand. Formal certifications from SENAI can improve recognition.
