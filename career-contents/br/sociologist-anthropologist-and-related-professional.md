# Sociologist, Anthropologist or Related Professional — career analysis · Brazil

**Occupation code: 2632 (ISCO08)**

Social scientists who study human society, culture, and social behavior using qualitative and quantitative methods. They work in research institutes, universities, government agencies, and NGOs, analyzing social trends, cultural patterns, and public policy impacts. In Brazil, they often collaborate with public bodies to inform social programs and with private sector for market research and organizational development.

**Job demand: low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 4,743. Employment in social sciences in Brazil is moderate and tied to government funding cycles and academic research budgets. Demand is stable in universities and public research agencies like IBGE and IPEA. Growth is seen in corporate social responsibility, environmental impact studies, and data-driven social analysis. Many professionals pursue advanced degrees (master's or doctorate) to access higher-level positions in academia or specialized consultancy. ## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sociologist in Brazil?**
A: Entry-level salary ranges from BRL 30,000 to 50,000 per year. Experienced professionals in academia or government can earn BRL 80,000 to 120,000. Private sector roles in market research may pay between BRL 60,000 and 100,000 annually.

**Q: Can a foreign sociologist work in Brazil?**
A: Yes, via an employer-sponsored VITEM V visa. The employer must submit a work authorization to the Ministry of Labour. Additionally, citizens of Mercosur countries can apply for simplified residence under the Mercosur Agreement.

**Q: Is registration with the Conselho Regional de Sociologia mandatory?**
A: Yes, to legally practice as a sociologist in Brazil, you must register with the Regional Council of Sociology (CRS). This requires a recognized degree and proof of good standing.
