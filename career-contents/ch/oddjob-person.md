## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for odd-job persons in Switzerland?**
A: Pay for odd-job persons in Switzerland varies widely by task and region. Hourly rates often range from CHF 20 to 30 for common tasks like cleaning or gardening. Many workers are self-employed and negotiate per assignment. Income is generally irregular and below the national average.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as an odd-job person in Switzerland via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA citizens generally cannot obtain a work visa for odd-job roles, as such positions do not meet the specialist criteria. The EU Blue Card requires a university degree and a minimum salary of CHF 85,560 (2024), which odd-job work rarely satisfies. Only EU/EFTA nationals are normally permitted for this type of casual employment.

**Q: What are the career prospects for odd-job persons in Switzerland?**
A: Odd-job roles are typically temporary or supplementary, with limited career progression. Some workers may gain experience to transition into specialized trades (e.g., cleaning or maintenance). The sector offers flexibility but no formal advancement structure. Demand fluctuates with seasons and local economic conditions.
