**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Image recognition-based automatic detection of cable wear
- Use drones for overhead cable inspection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AR headset displays complex rigging connection steps and safety annotations
- AI real-time analysis of tension data and early warning of potential breakage risks

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for riggers and cable splicers in Switzerland?**
A: According to industry data, the typical gross monthly salary for riggers and cable splicers in Switzerland ranges from CHF 4,500 to CHF 6,500 for entry-level positions, with experienced professionals earning up to CHF 8,000 or more. Variations depend on region, employer, and specialization.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU riggers and cable splicers to work in Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work permit subject to quotas and a labor market test. The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified workers with a recognized university degree and a minimum salary threshold (approx. CHF 85,000 in 2023). For riggers and cable splicers without a degree, a standard work permit is possible if no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate is found.

**Q: What training is required to become a rigger or cable splicer in Switzerland?**
A: The typical path is a 3-4 year apprenticeship (EFZ) in electrical installation, telecommunications, or construction. Specialized on-the-job training and courses in cable splicing or rigging are also common. Continuing education options include advanced federal diplomas in related fields.
