**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use a potter's wheel or molds for batch forming of standardized vessels
- Mechanical reproduction of basic carving and patterns
- Automated monitoring and adjustment of kiln temperature and time
- Automatic calculation and mixing of glaze formulas

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Quickly design complex ceramic shapes using 3D modeling software
- Use AI to generate glaze formulas and pattern ideas
- Use AR technology to show customers virtual finished ceramic products
- Analyze market data to optimize product design and pricing

**Human moat:**

- Irreproducible personal style and artistic expression in handmade pottery
- Ability for manual adjustments to handle customized, non-standard requirements.
- Deep perception and control over clay material properties
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional craft culture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for potters in Switzerland?**
A: Potters in Switzerland earn an average monthly salary between CHF 4,000 and CHF 5,500, depending on experience, region, and employer. Specialized or self-employed potters may earn more. According to the Swiss Federal Statistical Office, median income for craft workers is around CHF 4,800.

**Q: What are the official visa routes for foreign potters to work in Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA nationals need a work permit (B or L) requiring employer sponsorship and proof that no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate is available. The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified workers with a university degree, which potters typically do not hold. National visa quotas apply.

**Q: What training is required to become a potter in Switzerland?**
A: Becoming a potter in Switzerland typically requires a 3-4 year apprenticeship (EFZ) in ceramic design or pottery. This combines on-the-job training with vocational school. Some attend specialized art schools. After apprenticeship, potters can work professionally or pursue further specialization in areas like ceramic art.
