**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine sample preparation and processing (e.g., pipetting, centrifugation)
- Standard biochemical tests (e.g., ELISA, PCR result interpretation)
- Record and organize experimental data
- Reagent inventory management and ordering
- Repetitive animal husbandry and observation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- High-throughput sequencing data analysis (AI-assisted variant identification)
- Experimental protocol optimization (machine learning predicts optimal conditions)
- Literature mining and hypothesis generation (Natural Language Processing)
- Automatic analysis of microscopic images (cell counting, phenotype recognition)
- Multi-omics data integration (transcriptome and proteome correlation analysis)

**Human moat:**

- Unstructured experimental troubleshooting (e.g., investigating unexpected contamination)
- Ethical Decision-Making and Animal Welfare Assessment
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication (with clinicians, engineers)
- Experimental innovation design and hypothesis generation.
- Regulatory compliance and quality control responsibilities

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a life science technician in Switzerland?**
A: Life science technicians in Switzerland earn between CHF 60,000 and CHF 85,000 annually, depending on experience, location, and employer. Entry-level roles start around CHF 55,000, while senior technicians can exceed CHF 90,000. Salaries are higher in cantons with strong pharmaceutical and biotech sectors, such as Basel and Zurich.

**Q: What are the work visa and EU Blue Card routes for life science technicians to work in Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA citizens need a Swiss work visa, with employers required to prove no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate exists (labor market test). The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified non-EU nationals with a recognized degree and a job offer meeting the salary threshold (CHF 85,560 in 2024). Quotas apply for both routes.

**Q: What are the main employers and sectors for life science technicians in Switzerland?**
A: Life science technicians are primarily employed in pharmaceutical companies (e.g., Novartis, Roche), biotech firms, research institutes, and contract research organizations. Major hubs include Basel, Zurich, and the Lake Geneva region. Positions are found in laboratories, production, quality control, and R&D. Universities and institutes like ETH Zurich also offer research technician roles.
