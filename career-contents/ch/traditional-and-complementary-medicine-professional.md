**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions

- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.

- Knowledge retrieval: quickly obtain the latest research literature and classic cases
- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Switzerland?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, location, and specialization. As of 2023, naturopaths or traditional Chinese medicine practitioners typically earn between CHF 60,000 and CHF 90,000 annually. Income often depends on private practice, insurance recognition, and patient volume.

**Q: Can non-EU/EFTA Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA professionals need a work permit, subject to federal quotas and a labor market test. The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified workers, typically requiring a university degree and a job offer with a salary above CHF 85,560 (2024). Traditional medicine roles may qualify if recognized as a shortage occupation.

**Q: What are the professional recognition requirements for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Switzerland?**
A: Since 2017, certain complementary medicine methods (e.g., acupuncture) are federally recognized. Practitioners must hold a diploma from a recognized school, register with the Swiss Foundation for Complementary Medicine (ASCA) or a health insurer, and follow cantonal regulations. Unrecognized fields have fewer formal requirements.
