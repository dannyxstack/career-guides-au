## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for mining and quarrying labourers in Switzerland?**
A: Monthly gross salaries for mining and quarrying labourers in Switzerland typically range from CHF 4,000 to CHF 5,500, depending on experience, region, and employer. These figures are based on industry surveys and collective agreements. Overtime and shift work may increase earnings.

**Q: Can non-EU/EFTA nationals obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for this occupation in Switzerland?**
A: Switzerland generally does not grant work permits for low-skilled occupations like mining and quarrying labourers to non-EU/EFTA nationals. The EU Blue Card is reserved for highly qualified professionals and does not apply here. Only EU/EFTA citizens have free-movement access to this role. Non-EU nationals would need a specific shortage occupation permit, which this occupation does not qualify for.

**Q: What are the main duties of a mining and quarrying labourer in Switzerland?**
A: Mining and quarrying labourers in Switzerland assist with extracting, loading, and transporting minerals and rocks. They operate machinery such as drills and conveyor belts, perform cleaning and basic maintenance, and follow strict safety protocols. Work often takes place in alpine quarries or gravel pits, with seasonal variations due to weather conditions.
