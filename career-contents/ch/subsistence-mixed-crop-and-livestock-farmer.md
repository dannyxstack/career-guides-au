## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Switzerland?**
A: Income is minimal as production is mainly for self-consumption. Small-scale farmers may earn less than CHF 20,000 per year, often supplemented by off-farm work or direct payments from the Swiss government. Most subsistence farmers rely on other employment to cover living costs.

**Q: Is it possible for a non-EU/EFTA national to obtain a work visa to work as a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA nationals generally cannot obtain a work permit for subsistence farming, as it does not meet the criteria for skilled work or shortage occupation. Only EU/EFTA nationals benefit from free movement under the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons. Exceptional permits are not granted for this occupation.

**Q: What is the typical career path for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Switzerland?**
A: Many start as hobby farmers or inherit land. Formal training in agriculture is available but not mandatory. Due to high land costs and strict regulations, full-time subsistence farming is rare; most combine it with other jobs or transition to organic or direct-market farming to remain viable.
