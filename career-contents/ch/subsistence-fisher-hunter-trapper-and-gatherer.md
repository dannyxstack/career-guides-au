## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Switzerland?**
A: Subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, and gathering are not significant economic activities in Switzerland. Income is typically negligible, as these activities are primarily for personal or family consumption. Practitioners often have other primary sources of income. No official salary data exists; it is considered a traditional or recreational pursuit rather than a profession.

**Q: Can a non-EU national obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a subsistence fisher, hunter, trapper, or gatherer in Switzerland?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified workers and does not apply to subsistence activities. Non-EU nationals can only obtain a work permit if they possess exceptional professional qualifications and the activity is deemed economically necessary. Subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, and gathering are not recognized as standard professions for immigration. No specific visa route exists for this occupation.

**Q: Are there any professional organizations or training requirements for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Switzerland?**
A: There are no formal training requirements or professional organizations for subsistence activities in Switzerland. Hunting and fishing are regulated by cantonal authorities; licenses are required for recreational hunting and fishing, but subsistence as a full-time activity is virtually non-existent. Practitioners typically learn through family traditions or local community knowledge.
