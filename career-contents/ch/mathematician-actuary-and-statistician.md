- Standard statistical model running and parameter tuning.
- Routine data cleaning and basic report generation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Identifies complex risk factors and non-linear relationships using machine learning models.
- Analyze economic policy texts and market sentiment via natural language processing

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Switzerland?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level positions start around CHF 80,000–90,000 per year. Experienced professionals, especially in banking or insurance, can earn CHF 130,000–180,000. Actuaries and senior statisticians in high-demand fields may exceed CHF 200,000. Location also matters, with Zurich and Geneva offering higher pay.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians to work in Switzerland?**
A: Non-EU/EFTA nationals typically need a work permit, which requires a job offer and proof that no suitable Swiss or EU/EFTA candidate is available. The EU Blue Card is available for highly qualified workers with a job paying at least CHF 86,040 (2024 threshold). For EU/EFTA citizens, free movement applies, but registration is required.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence affecting job opportunities for mathematicians, actuaries, and statisticians in Switzerland?**
A: AI is transforming roles by automating routine data analysis and modeling tasks. Swiss professionals increasingly need skills in machine learning, deep learning, and big data. However, demand for experts who can develop, validate, and interpret AI models remains strong, particularly in finance, insurance, and pharma sectors in Switzerland.
