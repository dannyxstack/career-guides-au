# Fishing Laborer — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 9541 (CNO)**

Fisheries workers engage in fishing operations in marine or inland waters, including operating nets and gear, sorting and preliminary handling of catch, and maintaining fishing boat equipment. This profession is physically demanding, usually sailing with fishing boats, with harsh working conditions and significant weather conditions.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 25,000. Employment prospects in Spain's fisheries sector are stable but limited, and due to the EU Common Fisheries Policy (PES) and fishing quota restrictions, the industry size is gradually shrinking. Coastal areas (such as Galicia and Andalusia) still have demand, but automation and resource sustainability requirements may reduce low-skilled jobs.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Usually, they start as deck assistants and can be promoted to fisherman or tackle supervisor after gaining experience; a few can become boat captains. However, the path to advancement is limited, and many people spend their entire lives doing basic manual labor. Some have shifted to related fields such as aquaculture or fisheries regulation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI visual recognition is used to automatically sort fish
- Unmanned boats or remote-controlled robots are used to perform net casting and reeling
- Automatically records the types and quantities of catch through an electronic monitoring system
- Using an AI-powered positioning system to automatically maintain the position of fishing vessels
- Automatically alarms equipment faults through sensors and analysis platforms

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI predicts fish migration routes to improve fishing efficiency
- Optimize operation timing and routes by combining weather forecasts and marine data
- AI-assisted compliance checks automatically compare fisheries regulations with real-time data
- Digital twin technology is used for fishing vessel equipment maintenance simulation
- AI-driven health monitoring systems enhance crew safety

**Human moat:**

- Emergency decision-making and judgment under complex sea conditions
- Fine adjustment and on-site repair capabilities for fishing gear
- Efficient communication and coordination with crew, ports, and regulatory authorities
- Adaptive experience in ecological changes and unconventional situations
- Leadership and Team Management (especially for small fishing vessels)

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and basic maintenance of onboard AI systems
- Application of Remote Sensing and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) in fisheries
- Data recording and analysis skills (such as electronic fishing logs)
- Basic programming (Python) is used for fish catch data analysis
- Cross-cultural communication and compliance document processing
- Application of renewable energy and energy-saving technologies on fishing vessels

**Key growth areas:**

- Declining
- Seasonal
- Physical labor
- Coastal regions

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People with strong physical strength who can adapt to the harsh conditions at sea
- Those willing to accept seasonal work and who don't mind being away from home for long periods
- People with a strong interest in marine fisheries

**Who is not suited**
- People with seasickness or health issues
- Those seeking stable promotions and high income

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the approximate annual salary for fisheries workers?**
A: Junior about €18,000-22,000, mid-level €22,000-28,000, senior can reach €35,000. Income greatly affected by catch volume, voyage frequency, and vessel type.

**Q: Can non-EU immigrants work in Spanish fisheries?**
A: It's very difficult. This occupation does not fall under the high-skilled quota; employers must prove that there are no suitable local candidates and that salaries are relatively low, which usually do not meet the requirements for the EU Blue Card or the High-Skilled Talent visa.

**Q: What educational background is required?**
A: No formal degree is required, but you must complete Maritime Safety Training (STCW) and obtain a Fisheries Seafarer Registration Certificate. Training is usually as short as a few weeks and inexpensive.
