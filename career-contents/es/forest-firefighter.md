# Forest Firefighter — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 5932 (CNO)**

Forest firefighters are responsible for preventing, detecting, and extinguishing forest fires, protecting the natural environment and resident safety. They carry out firefighting tasks, patrol and monitoring, maintain firefighting equipment, and participate in post-fire assessments.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 9,000. Forest fires in Spain are frequent, especially during the dry summer season, resulting in a stable demand for forest firefighters. As climate change intensifies and fire risks rise, governments continue to invest in fire prevention and firefighting teams.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Gradually rise from basic firefighters to fire captains and regional commanders. By attending advanced training (such as aerial firefighting and fire management), you can move into technical roles (such as fire analyst) or management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for automated fire patrol and early detection
- Based on meteorological data and historical fire conditions, AI generates fire risk prediction reports
- An automated system for fire equipment inventory management and maintenance reminders
- Data collection and preliminary analysis during post-fire assessment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fire scene situational awareness: real-time analysis of thermal imaging and wind direction data to recommend the optimal extinguishing route
- Decision Support System: Recommends fire force deployment plans based on fire intensity, resources, and terrain
- Virtual reality simulation training: Enhance emergency response and teamwork skills at fire scenes
- Real-time communication and collaboration platform: AI translation and noise filtering enhance multi-team collaboration efficiency
- Intelligent personal protective equipment: Biosensors monitor firefighters' vital signs and provide early warnings

**Human moat:**

- Physical strength and mental resilience in extremely high temperatures and thick smoke
- Rapid judgment based on experience: making decisions in unpredictable situations such as sudden wind changes and fire jumps
- Team collaboration and on-site command: Human leadership and trust that cannot be replaced by AI
- Face-to-face communication and reassurance with residents and other emergency departments

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- Geographic Information System (GIS) and remote sensing data analysis
- Use and interpretation of AI-assisted decision-making tools
- VR fire scene simulation training
- Basic programming and automation scripts (e.g., Python)
- Advanced first aid and wilderness survival skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Wildfire Prevention
- Climate Resilience
- Emergency Response
- Aerial Firefighting

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Excellent physical fitness and adaptable to harsh outdoor environments
- Possess team spirit and emergency response skills
- Willing to contribute to public safety and environmental protection

**Who is not suited**
- Fear of heights or weak psychological resilience to fire scenes
- Unable to adapt to seasonal/temporary contracts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are the salary levels for forest firefighters?**
A: Junior: approximately €20,000-€25,000 per year, mid-level €26,000-€32,000, senior €33,000-€40,000, including allowances.

**Q: Can foreigners become forest firefighters in Spain?**
A: It's very difficult. This position typically requires Spanish nationality or EU citizenship, and requires passing rigorous physical fitness tests and background checks. Non-EU personnel are almost impossible to access through the skilled migration pathway.

**Q: What educational background is required to become a forest firefighter?**
A: At least complete secondary education and pass specialized forest fire training courses organized by the autonomous region, which usually include firefighting techniques, first aid, and physical training.
