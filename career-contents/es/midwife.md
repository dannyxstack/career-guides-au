# Midwife — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 2123 (CNO)**

Midwives are professional medical personnel responsible for providing care to women during pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum, while also caring for the newborn's health. They provide prenatal checkups, childbirth support, and postpartum follow-ups independently or in collaboration with obstetricians in hospitals, clinics, or communities.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 12,000. Spain faces a shortage of midwives, especially in remote areas where employment prospects are promising. An aging society and birth rates maintain stable demand, and the public healthcare system continues to recruit. Free movement in the EU brings competition, but local qualifications are highly recognized.

**Competition: medium (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Junior midwives can gain experience at public hospitals or health centers, and can then be promoted to senior midwives or supervisors. They can also specialize in high-risk pregnancies, perinatal mental health, or breastfeeding counseling. Some enter teaching or research directions through master's degrees.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine prenatal checkup data entry and report generation
- Automatic interpretation and early warning of physiological signals such as fetal heart monitoring
- Standardized distribution of patient education materials and automated FAQ responses
- Electronic archiving of delivery records and preliminary quality review screening
- Some automatic phone or message reminders for postpartum follow-up

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted prenatal risk assessment to identify high-risk pregnancies
- The intelligent fetal monitoring system provides real-time warnings to support clinical decision-making
- Virtual reality simulations train complex childbirth scenarios
- Mobile applications enable remote postpartum care guidance and monitoring
- Big data analysis optimizes the allocation of community prenatal checkup resources

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face emotional support and psychological counseling
- Rapid clinical judgment and manual operation in emergency labor
- Establish long-term trust relationships with mothers and families
- Interdisciplinary coordination and decision-making for complex complications
- Physical palpation and observation in unstructured settings

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis and remote monitoring tool operation
- Ability to integrate data analysis and clinical decision-making
- Digital patient communication and health coaching technology
- Multidisciplinary collaboration and complex case management
- Virtual simulation teaching and ongoing professional development
- Privacy and Ethical Awareness (AI Clinical Use)

**Key growth areas:**

- Midwifery Shortage
- Aging Workforce
- Birth Rate
- Rural Healthcare

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Spain admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- A caring, stress-resistant, and communicative woman or man
- Those who wish to work directly with mothers and newborns in the medical field
- People willing to serve in public systems or remote areas

**Who is not suited**
- People sensitive to blood and medical emergencies
- People who are not good at staying up late or standing for long periods

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for midwives in Spain?**
A: Junior midwives earn approx. €24,000–30,000/year, mid-level €32,000–40,000, and senior up to €55,000. Public hospitals offer stable salaries; private or self-employed income fluctuates but has higher potential.

**Q: How can non-EU midwives immigrate to Spain?**
A: You must first pass academic qualification (homologación), which is quite challenging. Afterwards, you can apply for a work contract and apply for employment-based residence or a high-skilled visa. The certification process may take 1-2 years, so it is recommended to prepare in advance.

**Q: Is midwifery in Spain a sought-after profession?**
A: Yes, midwives are listed as a scarce occupation (ocupación de difícil cobertura) in several autonomous regions in Spain, especially in rural and island regions, where public hospitals have high recruitment demand.
