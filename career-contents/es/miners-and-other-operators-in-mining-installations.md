# Miners And Other Operators In Mining Installations — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 8111 (CNO)**

Responsible for underground or open-pit mining operations, including drilling, blasting, loading and transporting minerals, operating mining machinery and equipment, and ensuring a safe working environment. This profession has stable demand in Spain's major mining areas.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Common promotion paths: from ordinary miner (Minero) to drilling operator (Operador de perforación) or demolition specialist (Artillero), then to mining supervisor (Jefe de turno) or safety coordinator (Coordinador de seguridad). Some have shifted to mining machinery maintenance or geological technicians through training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Autonomous haulage systems (AHS) take over transport cycle operations.
- GPS navigation and sensors automatically handle path planning and obstacle avoidance
- AI scheduling system optimises fleet allocation, replacing manual scheduling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of equipment faults to improve maintenance efficiency
- Virtual reality (VR) simulators for safety training and operation drills
- Data analysis to optimise fuel consumption and tyre wear.

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment and decision-making in complex geological conditions
- On-site rapid repair skills for equipment breakdowns
- Cross-device coordination and multitasking
- Safety procedure execution and risk assessment

**Skills to build:**

- Automated system operation and monitoring (e.g., AHS console)
- Basic data analysis (Python/R)
- Remote device diagnostics and maintenance (IoT sensors)
- Safety regulation updates and digital management.
- Cross-departmental collaboration and communication skills
- Drone inspection operations.

**Key growth areas:**

- Mining industry
- Energy transition
- Automation in mining
- Safety compliance

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Healthy individuals who can adapt to working underground or in harsh environments
- Those willing to learn to operate heavy machinery and blasting techniques
- People who enjoy stable employment and don't mind manual labor

**Who is not suited**
- Those who feel uncomfortable in confined spaces or dark environments
- People who pursue high salaries and are unwilling to take on high-risk jobs

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a Spanish miner?**
A: In Spain, junior miners earn about 22,000-28,000 euros per year before tax, intermediate workers about 28,000-36,000 euros, senior supervisors up to 45,000 euros, and underground mining usually comes with additional subsidies.

**Q: Can third-country citizens immigrate to Spain through miner work?**
A: Yes, but you need a Spanish employer to apply for work residence (Cuenta ajena) and prove that the position is difficult to recruit locals. Miners are in short supply, but language (Spanish) and certification are key factors.

**Q: What educational qualifications are needed to become a miner?**
A: Typically, it is necessary to complete mining-related courses from FP Grado Medio or Superior and obtain a blasting safety permit. Some positions only require a high school diploma plus on-the-job training.
