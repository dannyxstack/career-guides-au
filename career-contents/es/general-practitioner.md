# General Practitioner — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 2111 (CNO)**

General practitioners are at the heart of primary healthcare in Spain, responsible for diagnosing and treating common diseases, managing chronic conditions, and referring specialists to specialists. They work in public healthcare systems (SNS) or private clinics and are patients' first point of contact.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 78,000. Spain faces a shortage of general practitioners, especially in rural and aging areas. The government is increasing training quotas and improving working conditions to attract more doctors. Digital transformation and telemedicine will create new opportunities.

**Competition: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

General practitioners can be promoted from primary care center doctors to medical coordinators, health management positions, or specialist training (such as family and community medicine). You can also open a private clinic or engage in academic research.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate medical record summaries and referral letters
- Preliminary diagnostic recommendations are provided based on symptoms and guidelines
- Manage medication reminders and appointment scheduling for chronic diseases
- Read and conduct preliminary analysis of laboratory reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI image analysis assists in diagnosing skin diseases or X-rays
- Optimize treatment plans through AI-driven clinical decision support systems
- Quickly retrieve the latest medical literature using natural language processing
- With remote monitoring, AI analyzes patients' chronic disease data for early intervention

**Human moat:**

- Complex and atypical case judgment based on clinical experience
- Build trust and empathetic communication with patients
- Multidisciplinary coordination and personalized treatment decision-making
- Comprehensive assessment and accountability for ambiguous symptoms

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and interpretation of AI-assisted diagnostic tools
- Data-driven clinical decision-making capabilities
- Telemedicine and patient digital platform management
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and leadership
- Continuous learning and the ability to update evidence-based medicine

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- primary care demand
- digital health
- chronic disease management

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a general practitioner in Spain?**
A: General practitioners in Spain earn about €40,000–60,000 before tax, about €30,000–40,000 at entry-level, and up to €80,000 at senior levels. Private sector revenues may be higher.

**Q: How to immigrate to Spain as a foreign general practitioner?**
A: Non-EU doctors must obtain academic qualification (homologación) and obtain their practice qualification through the MIR exam or special pathways. You can apply for the Highly Skilled Professional Visa (Ley 14/2013) or the EU Blue Card.

**Q: What career development paths are available for general practitioners?**
A: You can be promoted to medical coordinator, community health manager, or become a specialist through specialized training (such as geriatrics and sports medicine). They can also open private clinics or engage in medical education.
