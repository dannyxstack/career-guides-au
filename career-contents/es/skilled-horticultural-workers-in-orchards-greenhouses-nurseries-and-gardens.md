# Skilled Horticultural Workers in Orchards, Greenhouses, Nurseries and Gardens — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 6120 (CNO)**

Responsible for planting, maintaining, and harvesting fruit trees, vegetables, flowers, and ornamental plants; operate greenhouse equipment; manage irrigation and fertilization; implement pest and disease control. Propagating plants in nurseries and preparing them for sale.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 185,000. Spain's horticulture industry continues to grow driven by export demand and organic farming, but the workforce is aging significantly and there is a shortage of young workers. Southern production areas such as Andalusia and Murcia offer more job opportunities, but work is highly seasonal and income fluctuates greatly.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

You can be promoted from an ordinary worker to a team leader or technician, responsible for production planning and quality control. After gaining experience, you may become a farm manager or independently contract horticultural services. Some workers turned to agricultural technology promotion or green certification consulting.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record plant growth data and environmental parameters
- Scheduled switching for routine irrigation and fertilization
- Visual identification and judgment of pests and diseases
- Repeated regulation of greenhouse environments (temperature, humidity).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI crop models optimize irrigation and fertilization plans
- Accurately identify pests and diseases through computer vision and early warning systems
- Automated harvesting robots improve efficiency and reduce losses
- Adjust planting strategies using AI to analyze soil and meteorological data
- Use recommendation systems to plan crop rotation and variety selection

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive perception and complex judgment of plant health, soil quality, and pests and diseases
- On-site decision-making to respond to sudden environmental changes (such as extreme weather or equipment failures).
- Hands-on maintenance and calibration of automated equipment
- Communicate and coordinate with clients and teams, as well as train newcomers

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart greenhouse systems (such as Priva and Argus).
- Basic Data Analysis and Visualization (Excel, Simple BI Tools)
- Fundamentals of drone/robot collaborative operations
- IoT sensor deployment and data interpretation
- Knowledge of Biological Pest and Disease Control (Non-Chemical)
- Continuous learning ability and adaptability to new technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Greenhouse automation
- Precision agriculture
- Export horticulture

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are the wage levels for horticultural workers?**
A: Entry-level workers earn about EUR 16,000-20,000 annually, intermediate EUR 20,000-26,000, and senior supervisors up to EUR 34,000. Wages vary by season, region, and skill level.

**Q: How to immigrate to Spain through gardening work?**
A: Can apply through an employed work visa (Cuenta ajena) or a high-skilled visa (Ley 14/2013). Horticulture is a shortage occupation but requires relevant training or experience. Also possible to enter with a job search visa (Búsqueda de empleo).

**Q: What educational qualifications are required to work in horticulture in Spain?**
A: Typically, a horticulture-related certificate from FP Secondary Vocational Education (Grado Medio) or Advanced Vocational Education (Grado Superior) is required. A bachelor's degree is not mandatory, but it can help advance to management positions.
