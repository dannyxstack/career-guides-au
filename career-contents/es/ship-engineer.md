# Ship Engineers — career analysis · Spain

**Occupation code: 3151 (CNO)**

Responsible for the operation, maintenance, and repair of ship power systems, mechanical, and electrical equipment, ensuring navigation safety and energy efficiency. Hold senior technical positions on merchant ships, fishing vessels, and special vessels.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Gradually promoted from junior engineer to chief engineer or chief engineer, and can transition to positions in ship management companies, classification societies, or shore-based technical supervision positions. Some senior chief engineers can serve as instructors in maritime education and training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine voyage data recording and report generation
- Basic meteorological route planning and optimization
- Marine machinery condition monitoring and early warning
- Cargo loading calculation and balance adjustment
- Communication relay and standard message forwarding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted navigation decisions to improve flight safety and fuel efficiency
- Real-time weather and sea condition forecasting, supporting dynamic adjustment of speed and route
- Automated fault diagnosis and predictive maintenance to reduce downtime
- Intelligent Cargo Monitoring Systems to Improve Stowage Accuracy and Safety
- Augmented reality (AR) assisted navigation at night and in adverse weather

**Human moat:**

- Ship handling and emergency response capabilities in crisis situations
- Team management and crew leadership (especially captain duties)
- Special cargo handling and complex port operations experience
- Regulatory compliance and maritime safety inspection responsibilities
- Flexible judgment in unpredictable marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI navigation and decision support systems
- Data analysis and interpretation of predictive models
- Remote operation and automated system monitoring
- Cybersecurity basics (ship system protection)
- STCW certification renewal (including E-navigation module)
- Cross-cultural communication and team management

**Key growth areas:**

- Maritime

- Energy Efficiency

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for Spanish marine engineers? Does overtime and allowance be included?**
A: Junior engineers earn about €35,000-50,000 annually, while chief engineers can earn €80,000-120,000. Salary usually includes base salary and marine allowance (additional daily allowance), with overtime pay calculated separately. LNG ships and large container ships pay higher wages.

**Q: How can third-country marine engineers obtain work and residency through Spanish skilled migration?**
A: Common paths: 1) Obtain a work contract with a Spanish shipowner, apply for Tarjeta azul UE or high-skilled professional visa; 2) Use STCW certificate and sea service proof for homologación (qualification recognition), then apply directly; 3) Come to Spain via a vocational training program and then obtain qualification recognition. Shortage occupations exempt from labour market test.

**Q: What education and training are required to become a marine engineer in Spain?**
A: Typically, you need to complete a Bachelor's degree in Navigation and Marine Engineering (Grado, 4 years) or Advanced Vocational Training (FP Grado Superior, 2 years), and obtain a certificate of competency through STCW certification training and examination. All courses are taught in Spanish, with a B2 Spanish proficiency recommended.
