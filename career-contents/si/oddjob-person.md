## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for odd-job persons in Slovenia?**
A: Odd-job persons in Slovenia are often paid hourly, with rates varying by task and employer. For occasional work through student services or informal agreements, the minimum hourly wage is around €7.50 (2024), but many jobs pay slightly more. Full-time equivalents may earn minimum wage (€1,203.36 gross/month), but odd jobs are rarely full-time.

**Q: Can a foreign national work as an odd-job person in Slovenia under the EU Blue Card or other work visa?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers and does not apply to odd-job persons. Foreign nationals must obtain a work permit (e.g., single permit for employment) and a temporary residence permit for employment. Employers must first check with the Employment Service for available local workers. Self-employment is possible via a business visa, but odd jobs are typically not sponsored.

**Q: What types of tasks do odd-job persons typically perform in Slovenia?**
A: Odd-job persons in Slovenia handle a variety of short-term, non-specialized tasks such as cleaning, gardening, minor repairs, moving assistance, and event setup. Many are students working through student services (e.g., e-Študent or Študentski servis) or retirees supplementing income. Tasks are often temporary and paid by the hour or by the job.
