- Surveillance systems automatically analyze abnormal behavior, replacing manual monitoring

- AI risk scoring system assists in identifying prisoners with high violence potential, enhancing early warning capability
- Smart sensor monitoring devices track inmate health status in real time, enhancing emergency response speed

- Multilingual AI translation helps prison officers communicate with non-local inmates, reducing cultural conflicts

- Discretion and judgment under the legal framework (e.g., granting privileges or penalties)

- Quick response to unstructured emergencies in a prison environment.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a prison guard in Slovenia?**
A: Prison guards in Slovenia are civil servants with pay determined by the state pay scale. Starting monthly gross salary is around €1,200–€1,400, increasing with seniority and rank. Additional allowances for shift work and hazard pay can add 10–20%. Experienced guards may earn up to €1,800 monthly gross. Actual figures vary by institution and collective agreements.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as prison guards in Slovenia via the EU Blue Card or work visa?**
A: Prison guard roles in Slovenia typically require Slovenian citizenship or EU/EEA nationality due to civil service regulations. Non-EU citizens may apply for a single work permit if a labor market test is passed and the employer sponsors. The EU Blue Card is not applicable as prison guard is not considered a highly qualified professional occupation. Specific restrictions apply, and citizenship is often mandatory.

**Q: What training is required to become a prison guard in Slovenia?**
A: Candidates must complete a secondary education and pass a physical and psychological exam. Selected recruits undergo a 6–12 month training program at the Ministry of Justice’s Prison Administration Training Centre, covering law, self-defense, and crisis management. Upon completion, a probationary period follows. Continuing education is required for promotion to higher ranks.
