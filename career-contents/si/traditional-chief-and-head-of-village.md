## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Traditional Chief or Head of Village in Slovenia?**
A: In Slovenia, Traditional Chiefs and Heads of Villages are usually unpaid volunteers or receive a symbolic honorarium from the local community. Their role is honorary and based on tradition rather than a paid profession. Any compensation is minimal and varies by village, often covering only expenses. No official salary scale exists.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a Traditional Chief or Head of Village in Slovenia via the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card is for highly skilled non-EU workers with a university degree and a high salary. Traditional Chiefs and Heads of Villages are not considered highly skilled professions and are typically unpaid. Non-EU nationals would need a standard work permit, but such roles are rarely granted as they are voluntary and community-specific.

**Q: How does one become a Traditional Chief or Head of Village in Slovenia?**
A: This role is not a formal occupation but a traditional community position. It is typically inherited or elected by village members based on local customs, age, and respect. No formal education is required. The position is recognized in some rural areas, especially in parts of Prekmurje and Styria, but has no legal employment status.
