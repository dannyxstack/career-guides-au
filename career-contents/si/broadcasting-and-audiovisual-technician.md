## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated audio/video editing and mixing
- Automatic signal quality monitoring and alarm
- Standard format conversion and file transcoding
- Automatic subtitle generation and synchronization
- Rule-based content distribution scheduling

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted real-time audio noise reduction and restoration
- Smart teleprompter and auto lens tracking
- Automated live streaming director switching recommendation
- AI-driven post-production color grading and effects generation
- Real-time analysis of audience interaction data to adjust content

**Human moat:**

- Complex wiring, debugging, and troubleshooting at large events
- Real-time decision-making in response to emergencies (e.g., equipment failure, severe weather)
- Communicate in real-time with directors, hosts, and musicians to understand artistic intent.
- Maintenance and operation of non-standard equipment (old analog consoles, special cameras)
- ultimate responsibility for ensuring safe broadcast and compliance of programs

**Skills to build:**

- AI tools in DaVinci Resolve or Adobe Premiere Pro (auto-tagging, scene editing)
- Python or node-based automation workflows (e.g., Nuke, Houdini)
- Cloud broadcasting and remote production (e.g., AWS Elemental, LiveU)
- Immersive audio production (Dolby Atmos, Spatial Audio)
- Basic network engineering (IP video streaming, SRT, NDI)
- Project management and cross-departmental communication

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for broadcasting and audiovisual technicians in Slovenia?**
A: Salaries for broadcasting and audiovisual technicians in Slovenia typically range from €1,200 to €1,800 gross per month, depending on experience, employer, and location. Senior roles or those with specialized skills may earn higher. Pay is often supplemented by overtime and shift allowances, especially for live event or 24/7 broadcast operations.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for foreign broadcasting and audiovisual technicians to work in Slovenia?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work in Slovenia via the EU Blue Card (for highly qualified workers with a recognized diploma and salary threshold) or a single work permit (for standard employment). The employer must first apply for a work permit through the Employment Service of Slovenia. Both routes require a valid employment contract and meet specific criteria.

**Q: What is the typical career progression for broadcasting and audiovisual technicians in Slovenia?**
A: Technicians often start as assistants or operators, advancing to senior technician or specialist roles (e.g., sound, lighting, or camera). With experience, they can become technical supervisors, broadcast engineers, or studio managers. Some move into related fields like media production or IT support. Continuing education and certifications (e.g., from RTV Slovenija or private institutions) enhance progression.
