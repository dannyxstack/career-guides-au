## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Slovenia?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Slovenia generally have low and variable incomes, often supplemented by EU Common Agricultural Policy subsidies. Average gross monthly earnings for agricultural workers are around €1,200–€1,500, but subsistence farmers typically earn less, with many relying on own consumption of produce to offset costs.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a subsistence livestock farmer in Slovenia via the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card is intended for highly skilled workers requiring a university degree or equivalent and a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (€23,000+). Subsistence farming generally does not meet these criteria. Instead, non-EU nationals may apply for a work permit through seasonal employment or general employment schemes.

**Q: What are the main responsibilities of a subsistence livestock farmer in Slovenia?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Slovenia manage small herds of cattle, sheep, or goats primarily for family consumption and local markets. Duties include feeding, health monitoring, milking, pasture management, and maintaining farm infrastructure. They also handle haymaking and comply with EU animal welfare and environmental standards, often working alone or with family labor.
