## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for street and related services workers in Slovenia?**
A: In Slovenia, street and related services workers typically earn between €800 and €1,100 gross per month. Pay varies by employer, location, and hours worked. The minimum wage is €1,203.36 gross (2024), but many workers in this sector may earn less due to part-time or irregular hours. Collective agreements may apply in some municipalities.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for street and related services workers to work in Slovenia?**
A: Non-EU workers can apply for a Slovenian work permit via the single permit procedure or the EU Blue Card if they have a higher education qualification. For low-skilled occupations like street services, a single work permit is the main route, requiring a job offer and employer sponsorship. Labour market tests may apply. Seasonal work permits are also available for short-term employment.

**Q: What are the typical working conditions for street and related services workers in Slovenia?**
A: Workers in this sector often have irregular hours, including nights, weekends, and holidays. Employment is frequently part-time or seasonal, with limited benefits. Contracts may be temporary. Workers must register with the Employment Service of Slovenia and comply with health and safety regulations. The job involves physical activity and exposure to weather conditions.
