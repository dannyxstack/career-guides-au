## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary or income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Slovenia?**
A: Due to the nature of subsistence activities, individuals in this occupation often do not receive a regular salary. In Slovenia, commercial fishing and hunting are regulated; subsistence practices are limited. Any income is typically informal and varies greatly. The national average minimum monthly salary is around €1,200, but this occupation does not have a standard wage structure.

**Q: Can subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers obtain an EU Blue Card to work in Slovenia?**
A: The EU Blue Card is reserved for highly skilled workers with a higher education degree and a job offer meeting salary thresholds (at least €39,000 annually in Slovenia). This occupation typically does not meet these criteria. Third-country nationals may seek a single work permit for employment, but opportunities for subsistence fishers/hunters are negligible due to strict regulations and limited demand.

**Q: What are the main career prospects for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Slovenia?**
A: Career prospects are very limited as this occupation is not a mainstream employment category in Slovenia. Strict regulations on hunting and fishing, combined with small-scale opportunities, mean most individuals engage in these activities as a supplement to other income. Formal employment is rare; most operate as self-employed or in family businesses. The trend is declining due to conservation laws.
