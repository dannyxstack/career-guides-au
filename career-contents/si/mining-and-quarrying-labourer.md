## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for mining and quarrying labourers in Slovenia?**
A: The pay for mining and quarrying labourers in Slovenia typically starts from the national minimum wage, which is approximately €1,200 gross per month in 2024. Experienced workers or those in specialized tasks may earn up to €1,600 gross per month, depending on the employer and region.

**Q: How can a foreigner obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a mining labourer in Slovenia?**
A: Foreign workers can apply for a Slovenian work visa through the Employment Service of Slovenia. The EU Blue Card is not available for low-skilled occupations like mining labourers; instead, a standard work permit is required, which involves employer sponsorship and a labor market test to demonstrate that no EU citizen can fill the position.

**Q: What are the main duties and working conditions for mining and quarrying labourers in Slovenia?**
A: Mining and quarrying labourers perform manual tasks such as drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials. Work is physically demanding, often in outdoor or underground environments. Basic safety training is mandatory. No formal education is required, but vocational training or experience in heavy machinery operation is advantageous.
