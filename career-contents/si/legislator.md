**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Comparison and discrepancy analysis of legal provisions
- Automatic classification and draft reply to voter letters
- Transcription and summary generation of parliamentary speeches
- Preliminary data statistics and visualization for policy impact reports
- Automatic archiving and retrieval of hearing materials

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use NLP to analyze large-scale public opinion to grasp sentiment trends
- AI-assisted simulation of economic and social impacts of different legislation
- Real-time public opinion monitoring and early warning system enhances decision-making timeliness
- Optimize legislators' schedules and resource allocation through smart recommendation systems.
- AI-assisted drafting of policy briefs and speech drafts

**Human moat:**

- Negotiation and compromise skills in complex conflicts of interest
- Intuitive judgment representing constituency emotions and values
- Ethical law-making in the face of ethical dilemmas
- Cross-party consensus building and crisis leadership

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary of a member of the Slovenian National Assembly?**
A: As of 2024, a member of the Slovenian National Assembly earns a gross monthly salary of approximately €5,500 to €6,000, along with additional allowances for travel, accommodation, and office expenses. Salaries are adjusted periodically based on national average wages.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as legislators in Slovenia through the EU Blue Card?**
A: No, the EU Blue Card is designed for highly skilled employment and does not apply to political offices such as legislators. Only Slovenian citizens can stand for election to the National Assembly. EU citizens may be eligible for local council positions under specific conditions, but non-EU citizens generally cannot become legislators.

**Q: What is the typical career path to become a legislator in Slovenia?**
A: Most legislators in Slovenia begin by joining a political party and gaining experience through local government roles, such as municipal councillor or mayor. Others may come from civil service, advocacy groups, or academia. Successful candidates often build a public profile, network within their party, and secure a place on the electoral list for parliamentary elections.
