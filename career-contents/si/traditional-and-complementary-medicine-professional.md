## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.
- Personalized treatment plans: recommend optimized herbal combinations or acupuncture plans based on big data.
- Knowledge retrieval: quickly obtain the latest research literature and classic cases
- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

- Manual skills (e.g., acupuncture techniques, massage force)
- Interpersonal communication skills to build trust and empathy with patients
- Holistic differentiation and individualized adjustment for complex cases.
- Ethical Judgment and Treatment Responsibility in Cross-Cultural Contexts

**Skills to build:**

- Big data analysis of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and AI tool operation
- use of digital diagnostic devices such as tongue and pulse diagnosis
- Application of telemedicine platforms and patient management
- Continuously learn the latest developments in AI-assisted diagnosis and treatment.
- Multimedia production for patient communication and health education

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Slovenia?**
A: Salaries for traditional and complementary medicine professionals in Slovenia vary widely based on experience, specialization, and employment type. Self-employed practitioners may earn between €1,200 and €2,500 monthly. Those employed in private clinics or wellness centers might earn a gross monthly salary of €1,500 to €3,000, with higher incomes possible for established specialists.

**Q: Can I work in Slovenia as a Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, if you have a recognized higher education degree and a job offer meeting the EU Blue Card salary threshold (at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary). Your qualifications must be officially recognized in Slovenia, and the profession may require registration with the relevant authority. Ensure your prospective employer supports the application.

**Q: Is formal registration required to practice as a Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional in Slovenia?**
A: Yes, practitioners must register with the Slovenian Ministry of Health or an authorized professional body, depending on the therapy. For example, acupuncturists need certification from the Medical Chamber. Compliance with national standards and ongoing education is mandatory. Verification of foreign credentials is required before registration.
