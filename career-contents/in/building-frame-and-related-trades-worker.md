# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7119 (NCO2015)**

Building frame and related trades workers not elsewhere classified include occupations such as steel fixers, formworkers, and scaffolders who construct and repair building frames using materials like steel, concrete, and timber. They read blueprints, erect scaffolding, place reinforcement, and pour concrete. This role is essential in India's booming construction sector, especially in urban infrastructure and real estate projects, but it is largely informal with limited formal training pathways.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a helper, moving to a skilled worker (6-12 months on-job), then to a supervisor or subcontractor after years of experience. Formal certifications (e.g., from Construction Skill Development Council of India) can accelerate advancement. Some workers specialize in high-rise construction or pre-fabricated structures. Limited upward mobility without formal education; many remain in informal employment.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- High-rise construction
- Infrastructure projects
- Prefabricated structures
- Steel construction

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a building frame worker in India?**
A: Entry-level workers earn ₹120,000-240,000 annually (₹400-800/day). Skilled workers with 3-6 years earn ₹240,000-420,000. Supervisors can earn up to ₹720,000. Salaries vary by region, with metro cities paying higher.

**Q: Can I get an Indian work visa as a building frame trades worker?**
A: Unlikely. India's Employment Visa requires high-skilled roles (min ₹20 lakh salary). Construction trades are considered low-skilled and rarely qualify. OCI holders (persons of Indian origin) can work freely, but others typically require employer sponsorship for specialized technical roles.

**Q: What training is needed to become a building frame worker?**
A: Most workers learn on the job. Optional training includes a 1-year ITI course in construction trades or short certificate programs from the Construction Skill Development Council (CSDC). Safety certifications are increasingly valued.
