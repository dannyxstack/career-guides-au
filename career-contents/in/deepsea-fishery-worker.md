# Deep-sea Fishery Worker — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 6223 (NCO2015)**

Deep-sea fishery workers operate and maintain fishing vessels and equipment to catch fish and other marine life in offshore waters, often spending extended periods at sea. They handle nets, lines, traps, and other gear, and process the catch on board. The work is physically demanding and subject to weather conditions and safety risks.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 108,521. India's deep-sea fishing sector is growing slowly due to modernization efforts and government schemes like the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, which aims to increase fish production. However, the workforce remains informal and seasonal, with limited formal employment growth. Demand is stable but not booming, and mechanization may reduce manual jobs over time.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for deep-sea fishery workers is limited. Workers typically start as deckhands and may advance to boat operators or crew supervisors with experience. Some transition into fishing vessel ownership or work as independent contractors. Formal training and certifications can improve safety and opportunities, but most workers learn on the job.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Fishing vessel navigation and route planning (AIS + autopilot)
- Fish catch classification and initial weighing (computer vision)
- Fish school detection and net deployment position optimization (AI sonar analysis)
- Predictive maintenance for fishing vessel equipment (IoT + machine learning)
- Catch tracking and compliance report generation (automated data entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted fish school prediction (combining meteorological and oceanographic data)
- Real-time deck safety monitoring (AI alert wearable)
- Automation of catch processing (robot-assisted)
- Resource management and sustainable fishing strategies (AI models)
- Market sales data analysis (providing optimal auction timing)

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making in complex maritime environments (storms, currents)
- Manual net repair and deck emergency handling
- Judging fish behavior based on experience (limited AI data)
- Communication and coordination with fleet and shore teams
- Flexible operations to adapt to seasonal and regulatory changes

**Skills to build:**

- AI/Computer Vision Applications in Fisheries
- Data analysis and prediction tools (Python, R)
- Drone operation and maintenance
- Marine sensor and IoT device management
- Sustainable fisheries regulations and certifications
- Deck automation system operation

**Key growth areas:**

- Mechanization
- Sustainable Fishing
- Export Demand
- Aquaculture Competition

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical monthly income for a deep-sea fishery worker in India?**
A: Entry-level workers earn about ₹10,000-15,000 per month, but income is often irregular and depends on catch success. Senior workers or boat operators can earn ₹30,000-40,000 monthly.

**Q: Can foreigners work as deep-sea fishery workers in India?**
A: This is not a viable migration route. Employment Visas require specialized skills, and deep-sea fishing is considered manual labor with low salary thresholds. Indians working abroad in this sector face similar restrictions.

**Q: Do I need a certificate to work on a deep-sea fishing vessel in India?**
A: For larger vessels, a Seaman's Service Book and Basic Safety Training are mandatory. Smaller boats may not require certificates, but having them improves employment chances.
