# Craft And Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7549 (NCO2015)**

This ISCO-08 residual category covers craft and related workers whose specific occupations are not classified elsewhere, including roles such as kite makers, bead stringers, and hand-loom operators. These workers typically perform manual tasks to produce or repair goods using traditional or semi-skilled methods, often in informal settings. Employment is concentrated in India's unorganized sector, with many self-employed or working in small workshops.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is often flat; many remain in the same craft for life. Advancement may involve starting a small business or diversifying skills. Traditional apprenticeship (father-to-son) is common. Without formal certification, mobility is low. Some workers shift to construction or manufacturing for steadier income.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised jewellery grinding and polishing
- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments
- Repeating pattern reproduction in hand carving
- Traditional mold making and casting processes
- Simple wood/stone cutting and shaping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Conceive personalized jewelry/musical instrument designs using AI design software
- Accelerated prototyping through 3D scanning and modeling
- Using AI quality inspection system to improve product precision
- Using AR/VR for complex assembly guidance
- Customized client communication and quoting via digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Highly customized artistic creation and aesthetic judgment
- Experience and tactile feel of handling rare materials
- On-site repair and adaptation of complex non-standard parts
- Cultural story interpretation through in-depth communication with clients
- Overall process control through multi-process integration

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM and 3D modeling (e.g., Rhino, Fusion 360)
- Digital fabrication equipment operator (3D printing, CNC engraving)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g. Midjourney, DALL-E for concept design)
- Materials science and sustainable process knowledge
- Digital customer management and custom platform operations
- Cross-sector collaboration and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Niche crafts
- Repair services
- Handicrafts
- Informal sector

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a craft worker in India?**
A: Salaries vary widely. Entry-level workers earn around INR 60,000-120,000 per year, while experienced artisans may earn up to INR 480,000. Most workers are self-employed or in the informal sector, so income is irregular.

**Q: Can a craft worker migrate to India under an Employment Visa?**
A: Rarely. Employment Visas require a high-skill role with a salary threshold of USD 25,000/year, which most craft workers cannot meet. Short-term Business Visas for craft fairs are possible but do not allow local employment.
