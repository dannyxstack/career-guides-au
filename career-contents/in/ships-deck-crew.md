# Ship's Deck Crew — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 8350 (NCO2015)**

Ship's deck crews and related workers perform a variety of tasks on board ships, including navigation watch duties, mooring, cargo handling, and maintenance of deck equipment. This occupation is vital for maritime transport and fishing industries, particularly in coastal states like Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Kerala. Work often involves long periods at sea and adherence to strict safety protocols.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 75,870. India's maritime sector is growing with increasing port traffic and a push for coastal shipping. However, automation and larger vessels are reducing crew demand. Job growth is steady but slow, with opportunities concentrated in merchant navy, oil tankers, and fishing fleets. Shortage of certified crew may persist due to long training requirements and tough working conditions.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level positions include deck cadet or ordinary seaman. With experience and certification, one can advance to able seaman, bosun, and ultimately officer ranks (chief officer, master). Career progression requires passing exams conducted by the Directorate General of Shipping. Many workers pursue marine engineering or offshore roles for higher pay.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and AI tool usage (e.g., route optimization software)
- Data analysis and dashboard interpretation skills
- Skills in remotely operating drones or robots.
- Advanced maritime regulations and certification updates
- Soft skills: cross-cultural communication and crisis management
- Digital forensics and cybersecurity fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

- Merchant Navy
- Offshore Support
- Fishing Fleet
- Coastal Shipping

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do ship deck crew earn in India?**
A: Entry-level ordinary seamen earn around INR 3-6 lakh per year, while experienced chief officers can earn INR 20 lakh or more. earnings vary significantly by vessel type (merchant navy, oil tankers) and foreign flag employment.

**Q: Can foreign ship crew work in India?**
A: Yes, but they need an Employment Visa sponsored by an Indian company, which typically requires a minimum salary of INR 2 million per year. Crew visiting temporarily may use a Seaman Visa.
