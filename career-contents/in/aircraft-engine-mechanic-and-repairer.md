# Aircraft Engine Mechanic and Repairer — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7232 (NCO2015)**

Aircraft engine mechanics and repairers inspect, maintain, and repair aircraft engines and their components to ensure safe and efficient operation. They work in hangars, repair stations, or on airfields, using specialized tools and diagnostic equipment. This role requires a strong understanding of mechanical and electrical systems, adherence to strict safety regulations, and the ability to work under time pressure. In India, most mechanics are employed by airlines, MROs (maintenance, repair, and overhaul organizations), and defense forces. The occupation is specialized and often requires formal training and certification from the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a trainee or apprentice, advancing to junior mechanic, then senior mechanic, and eventually lead technician or supervisor roles. Specialization in specific engine types (e.g., Pratt & Whitney, CFM) or avionics can lead to higher pay. Experienced mechanics may move into inspection, quality control, or management positions within MROs or airlines. Some pursue DGCA licenses (B1.1) to enhance credentials. Lateral movement to aircraft manufacturing or defense sectors is also possible.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for an aircraft engine mechanic in India?**
A: Entry-level mechanics earn around ₹3-5 lakhs per annum, mid-level (licensed) earn ₹6-10 lakhs, and senior roles can exceed ₹12 lakhs. Salaries vary by employer, location, and experience.

**Q: Can foreign aircraft mechanics work in India?**
A: Yes, through an Employment Visa (minimum salary ~$25k/year) or Intra-Company Transfer. Visa sponsorship is required, and the DGCA may require license validation. India does not have a points-based system; employer sponsorship is essential.
