# Shoemakers and Related Workers — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7536 (NCO2015)**

Shoemakers and related workers craft and repair footwear using leather, synthetic materials, and textiles. In India, this occupation is largely informal, with many artisans working in small workshops or self-employed. Skills are learned through apprenticeships, and the trade faces competition from mass-produced footwear. The sector is concentrated in traditional clusters like Agra and Chennai.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,855,182. The Indian footwear industry is growing, but demand for handmade shoes is declining. Automation and cheap imports are squeezing traditional shoemakers. However, niche markets for custom and orthopedic shoes offer some resilience. Employment is expected to remain stable but with limited formal job growth. Most new entrants will be in repair services or low-end manufacturing.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most shoemakers remain in the same role. Experienced workers may become supervisors or open their own workshops. There is no formal career ladder. Upskilling into design or management is rare. The trade is often passed down through families. Young people are less interested, leading to an aging workforce.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- repetitive manual operations such as shoe upper sewing and sole bonding
- Routine machine operations such as leather cutting and component sorting
- Visual inspection in quality checks (replaced by computer vision).
- Inventory Management and Order Processing (ERP System Automation)
- Last replication and scaling (3D scanning and digitization alternatives).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design software to generate creative shoe sketches and color schemes
- Using 3D modeling and virtual try-on to reduce physical sample shoe production
- Optimizing leather cutting layouts through data analysis to reduce waste
- Customized customer communication and foot measurement via digital platforms.
- using augmented reality (AR) to assist complex repairs and renovations

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment of leather texture and defects in hand-finishing and restoration
- Deep communication and aesthetic co-creation with clients in high-end custom products
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional shoemaking techniques (e.g., Goodyear welt structure)
- Comprehensive craftsmanship flexibly addressing unconventional issues in small workshops

**Skills to build:**

- Proficient in CAD/CAM footwear design software (e.g., Shoemaster)
- Learn 3D modeling and rapid prototyping (RP technology)
- Familiar with automated cutting and sewing robot operation
- Knowledge of leather material science and sustainable processes
- Develop skills in client needs analysis and customised design
- Use e-commerce and social media for brand marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Niche custom footwear
- Repair services
- Orthopedic shoes
- Leather craftsmanship tourism

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a shoemaker in India?**
A: A mid-level shoemaker (4-10 years) earns between INR 200,000 and INR 360,000 per year, depending on location and skill.

**Q: Can a foreign shoemaker get a work visa for India?**
A: No, shoemaking does not qualify for India's Employment Visa (minimum salary ~USD 25,000 per year) and is not considered a specialized skill. Only Business Visa for short visits is possible.
