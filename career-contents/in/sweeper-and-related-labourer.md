# Sweepers and Related Labourers — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9613 (NCO2015)**

Sweepers and related labourers perform manual cleaning tasks such as sweeping streets, parks, and public spaces, as well as cleaning in commercial and residential buildings. This occupation is foundational to public hygiene and sanitation, with workers typically employed by municipal corporations, facility management companies, or as daily-wage labourers. The work involves using brooms, dustpans, and other manual tools, with limited mechanisation in many areas. In India, this role is often characterised by low wages, informal employment, and minimal social security coverage, though government initiatives and outsourcing to private firms are gradually improving conditions.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 1,443,603. The demand for sweepers is expected to remain stable in India, driven by urbanisation, Swachh Bharat Mission (Clean India Mission) targets, and growing awareness of sanitation. Employment growth will be modest but consistent, especially in Tier-2/3 cities and rural areas undergoing infrastructure upgrades. However, mechanisation (e.g., street-sweeping machines) may gradually reduce manual roles in large metros. The informal nature of employment means many positions are contractual, with limited benefits. Overall, the occupation offers low-skilled, often daily-wage opportunities with high turnover.

**Competition: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression from sweeper to supervisor or sanitation inspector is possible with experience, but requires literacy, basic supervision skills, and often government contractual upgrades. Many workers remain in the same role for decades due to limited formal training pathways. Some transition to larger facility management companies that offer better wages and benefits. Government schemes like the National Urban Livelihoods Mission (NULM) provide skill training for self-employment (e.g., small cleaning businesses), but upward mobility is generally slow and constrained.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Daily floor cleaning and garbage collection
- Surface wiping with fixed cleaning agents
- Repeatedly patrol and clean along predetermined routes
- Simple waste sorting and disposal

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Report facility issues using a mobile app

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a sweeper in India?**
A: The average monthly salary for a sweeper in India ranges from ₹6,000 to ₹12,000 (approx. $70-$140 USD) for entry-level workers. Municipal employees may earn slightly more, with regularisation and benefits like provident fund. Daily-wage workers earn between ₹200 to ₹400 per day. Income varies widely by city and employer type.

**Q: Can Indian sweepers migrate to another country for work?**
A: Sweepers rarely qualify for skilled migration visas (e.g., Canada's Express Entry, UK Skilled Worker) due to low skill level and salary. However, some migrate to Gulf countries (Saudi Arabia, UAE) on domestic worker or labour visas for cleaning jobs. These roles are typically low-paid and tied to a sponsor (kafala system). The salary is often below ₹400,000 per year.
