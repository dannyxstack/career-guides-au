# Field Crop and Vegetable Growers — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 6111 (NCO2015)**

Field crop and vegetable growers plan, organize, and perform farming operations to cultivate field crops such as cereals, grains, and vegetables, including seeds, nursery, and market gardening. They work on commercial farms, often using manual labor or basic machinery, and are responsible for soil preparation, planting, irrigation, pest control, and harvesting. This occupation is predominantly rural and informal in India, with most workers being self-employed or family laborers.

Government schemes like PM-KISAN and crop insurance may provide support, but overall, the occupation offers modest prospects for wage growth. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most growers remain in small-scale or subsistence farming. Some may expand landholdings or diversify into high-value crops (e.g., organic, exotic vegetables). Formal career paths are rare; workers typically start as family helpers or laborers and may become independent farmers. Government extension services and agricultural universities offer training, but certification is uncommon. Technology adoption (e.g., drip irrigation, mechanized harvesters) can improve productivity but requires capital.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- drip irrigation
- high-value crops
- farm mechanization

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a field crop grower in India?**
A: Most field crop growers earn between INR 100,000 and 250,000 per year, depending on land size and crop type. Large, mechanized farms may yield up to INR 500,000, but earnings are volatile due to monsoon dependence and market prices.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a field crop grower in India?**
A: Generally no, because it is a low-skilled occupation and India's Employment Visa requires specialist skills with a minimum salary of ~USD 25,000. Manual farm work does not qualify. Business or investor visas may allow ownership, but not direct employment.
