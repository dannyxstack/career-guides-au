# Assembler Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 8219 (NCO2015)**

Assemblers not elsewhere classified (ISCO-08 8219) include a wide range of manual assembly workers who put together components or parts to create finished products across manufacturing sectors such as electronics, automotive, machinery, and consumer goods. This occupation involves repetitive tasks, often on production lines, requiring basic technical skills and attention to detail. In India, these roles are prevalent in industrial zones, special economic zones (SEZs), and manufacturing clusters, employing a large semi-skilled workforce.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a helper or trainee, moving to assembler, then senior assembler or team leader. With additional training, workers can become technicians or quality inspectors. Some transition into production supervision or maintenance roles. Upskilling in automation, CNC, or robotics can lead to higher-paying positions. Workers often move within the same company or to other manufacturing firms.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive parts assembly (e.g., electronic component insertion, screw fastening)
- Vision-Based Quality Inspection (AI Vision System Replacement)
- Simple material handling and sorting (replaced by AMR)
- Record production data (replaced by automated MES system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for real-time assembly guidance, reducing training time
- Use collaborative robots for heavy or precision assembly, with worker supervision and fine-tuning
- Use AI predictive maintenance tools to monitor equipment and replace worn parts in advance
- Optimize processes based on digital twin simulation of assembly workflows

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard parts or unexpected anomalies (e.g., flexible adjustments when part tolerance is out of spec)
- Hand-finishing small-batch, multi-variety custom assemblies
- Make experience-based comprehensive judgments on assembly quality
- Cross-workstation collaboration and on-site problem-solving skills

**Skills to build:**

- Collaborative robot programming and debugging.
- Operation of digital twin and AR-assisted assembly systems
- Basic data analysis (reading from MES system to improve efficiency)
- Mechatronics maintenance skills
- Lean production and continuous improvement methods
- Cross-disciplinary communication (liaising with engineers, suppliers).

**Key growth areas:**

- Electronics assembly
- Automotive component assembly
- Machinery assembly
- Solar panel assembly

## 6. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals comfortable with repetitive manual tasks and working in a factory environment.
- Those seeking entry-level employment with minimal educational requirements.
- Candidates willing to upskill for career progression to technician or supervisory roles.

**Who is not suited**
- People seeking desk jobs or varied daily routines.
- Those averse to physical work and long standing hours.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an assembler in India?**
A: Average salary ranges from INR 120,000 to INR 500,000 per year depending on experience, skills, and location. Entry-level assemblers earn around INR 120,000-200,000; senior assemblers can earn up to INR 500,000.

**Q: Can an assembler migrate to India for work?**
A: Migration for assembler roles is difficult because India's employment visa requires high skill and a salary above INR 1.625M/year, which is far above typical assembler pay. Intra-company transfer or business visas may be possible but rare.
