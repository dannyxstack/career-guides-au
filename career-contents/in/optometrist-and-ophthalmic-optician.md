# Optometrists And Ophthalmic Opticians — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 2267 (NCO2015)**

Optometrists and ophthalmic opticians examine eyes, prescribe and fit corrective lenses, and detect eye diseases. They work in clinics, hospitals, and retail optical chains. In India, the profession is regulated by the Optometry Council of India and requires a Bachelor of Optometry or equivalent degree. Entry-level practice is limited under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, but growing demand for vision care drives employment.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 16,922. India's optometry workforce is expanding due to rising screen time, aging population, and increasing awareness of eye health. Government initiatives like the National Programme for Control of Blindness & Visual Impairment and school screening programs generate public-sector demand. Private optical chains and corporate hospitals also hire. However, rapid automation of refraction and AI diagnostics may reduce demand for low-skilled tasks, favoring advanced clinical roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Entry-level optometrists start as refractionists or sales opticians, moving to clinical or managerial roles after 3-5 years. Specializations in contact lenses, low vision, paediatrics, or geriatric optometry offer higher pay. Experienced professionals can open private clinics, become franchise partners, or transition to ophthalmic technology sales. Higher degrees (M.Optom) enable teaching or research positions in academic institutions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement
- AI analyzes corneal topography data to generate preliminary lens fitting plans.
- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Learning to operate digital optometric equipment and AI-assisted diagnostic systems
- Master the use of remote eye testing platforms
- Improve communication and customer management skills
- Learn basic data analysis to interpret AI reports
- Track new eye health technologies (e.g., OCT interpretation).
- Strengthen personalized eyewear fitting and problem-solving skills.

**Key growth areas:**

- Refraction and Lens Fitting
- Low Vision Care
- Digital Eye Strain Management
- Rural Outreach

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for an optometrist in India?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 per month in rural or small clinics, and ₹25,000 to ₹30,000 per month in urban retail chains or hospitals.

**Q: Can an Indian optometrist work abroad on an employment visa?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult due to strict licensing and registration requirements in most countries. Under India's employer-sponsored visa system, an optometrist needs a local job offer and must meet salary thresholds (~₹20-25 lakh/year) for an employment visa. Many optometrists instead migrate to the Middle East via contracts.

**Q: Do I need a Master's degree to practice optometry in India?**
A: No, a Bachelor of Optometry (B.Optom) is sufficient for clinical practice. A Master's degree is optional for specialization or academic positions.
