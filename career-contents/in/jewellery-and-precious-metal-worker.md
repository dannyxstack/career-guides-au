# Jewellery and Precious Metal Worker — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7313 (NCO2015)**

Jewellery and precious metal workers design, fabricate, repair, and appraise jewellery, gems, and precious metal items. In India, this occupation is rooted in traditional craftsmanship and is concentrated in artisan clusters like Jaipur, Mumbai, Delhi, and Surat. Workers use tools and techniques such as soldering, stone setting, casting, and polishing. The sector includes both high-end bespoke pieces and mass-market costume jewellery. Employment is largely informal, with many self-employed artisans or small workshop employees. Growth is driven by domestic consumption, export demand, and rising incomes.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 491,356. The Indian gems and jewellery sector is expected to grow at 6-8% annually, supported by rising disposable incomes, wedding spending, and export demand. However, competition from machine-made and imported jewellery may suppress wage growth for manual workers. The government's 'Make in India' initiative and efforts to formalise the sector through hallmarking and GST could benefit skilled artisans. Automation in casting and polishing may reduce demand for low-skill work, but bespoke and repair work remains resilient.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or helper, moving to skilled artisan, then master craftsman or supervisor. Many specialise in stone setting, engraving, or diamond grading. Experienced workers may open their own workshops or become jewellery designers. Certification from bodies like the Gemological Institute of India (GII) or IIJT can enhance prospects. The industry values hands-on experience over formal education, but diplomas in gemology or jewellery design can accelerate advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated polishing and grinding: AI-controlled robotic arms can efficiently perform basic grinding and polishing, replacing repetitive manual labor.
- CAD-assisted design modeling: AI generates multiple design variants, reducing the need for manual drafting and wax pattern carving.
- Quality inspection: computer vision systems automatically detect gem flaws and metal defects, replacing visual inspection.
- Inventory and order management: AI automatically optimises material procurement and order scheduling, reducing administrative positions.
- Basic repairs: standardized ring resizing, chain repair, etc., can be done by automated equipment.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex custom design: AI generates design sketches, which craftsmen refine, boosting creative efficiency.
- Customer preference analysis: AI analyzes sales data to assist designers in creating styles that better align with target markets.
- Material simulation: AI simulates the appearance of different metal and gemstone combinations, reducing the cost of producing physical samples.
- Education training: AR/VR combined with AI teaching to accelerate beginners' learning of carving and inlay techniques.
- Repair solution recommendations: AI scans damaged jewelry and suggests optimal repair paths to assist craftsmen in decision-making.

**Human moat:**

- Handcrafted carving and artistic creation: unique aesthetic sense and manual dexterity that AI cannot replicate.
- Customer trust and personalized service: Jewelry purchases involve high emotional value, making human communication irreplaceable.
- Complex setting and restoration: high technical difficulty gem setting and antique restoration rely on artisan experience.
- Security and compliance responsibility: authenticity and origin of precious metals and gems (e.g., conflict-free diamonds) require human endorsement.

**Skills to build:**

- AI design tools: Master AI plugins for CAD software such as Rhino 3D and MatrixGold.
- 3D printing and modeling: learn digital manufacturing processes, such as 3D printing wax patterns and lost-wax casting.
- Gemology certification: Obtain professional qualifications such as GIA to enhance appraisal authority.
- Customer experience design: learn personalized marketing and AR virtual try-on technology.
- Sustainable supply chain knowledge: understanding blockchain traceability and fair trade certification.
- Data analysis: using AI tools to analyze market trends and guide design strategies.

**Key growth areas:**

- Bespoke Jewellery
- Exports
- Hallmarking
- E-commerce

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a jewellery worker in India?**
A: Entry-level workers earn around INR 1.2-3 lakh per year, while experienced master craftsmen can earn INR 6-12 lakh annually. Salaries vary significantly by region and employer.

**Q: Can foreign jewellery workers easily migrate to India for work?**
A: No. India does not have a points-based system for skilled migration. Employment requires employer sponsorship and a min salary of ~USD 25k for an Employment Visa, which is high for this trade. Most workers are Indian nationals.
