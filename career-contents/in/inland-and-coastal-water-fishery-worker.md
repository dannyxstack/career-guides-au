# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 6222 (NCO2015)**

Inland and coastal waters fishery workers engage in fishing operations in rivers, lakes, canals, and coastal waters using nets, traps, lines, and other gear. They operate small fishing vessels, sort and preserve catches, and maintain equipment. The role is physically demanding and requires knowledge of fish behavior, navigation, and safety procedures. In India, this occupation is largely informal, with many workers in small-scale or artisanal fisheries, though some are employed by larger enterprises. The sector is vital for food security and livelihoods in coastal and riverine communities.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 480,464. Employment in India's inland and coastal fisheries is expected to remain stable due to domestic demand for fish and government schemes like the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, which aims to boost production. However, mechanization and aquaculture growth may reduce demand for traditional workers. Climate change and overfishing pose risks, but inland fisheries, especially in states like West Bengal and Andhra Pradesh, offer opportunities. Overall outlook is moderate, with slow growth and some consolidation in organized sectors.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; entry-level workers gain experience and may advance to lead small crews or own vessels. Some diversify into aquaculture, fish processing, or trading. Government training programs under the Fisheries Skill Development scheme provide certification. However, most remain in informal employment with low social security. The path offers minimal formal career progression, with income variability based on catch.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- Inland fisheries
- Aquaculture expansion
- Coastal regulation
- Sustainable practices

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a fishery worker in India?**
A: Income varies widely; entry-level workers earn about ₹10,000-20,000 per month, often on a daily wage basis. Experienced workers or vessel owners can earn ₹25,000-50,000 monthly, but earnings are highly seasonal and dependent on catch.

**Q: Can inland and coastal waters fishery workers migrate abroad for work?**
A: Generally no; this occupation does not qualify for skilled migration visas (e.g., India's employment visa requires salary ≥$25,000 and specialist skills). Most work is informal and domestic.

**Q: What career progression options exist?**
A: Progression is limited. Workers may become crew leads, own small vessels, or transition to aquaculture, fishing gear repair, or fish processing. Government training programs can help, but formal advancement is rare.
