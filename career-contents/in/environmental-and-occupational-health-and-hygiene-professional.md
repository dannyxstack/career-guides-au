# Environmental and Occupational Health and Hygiene Professional — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 2263 (NCO2015)**

Environmental and Occupational Health and Hygiene Professionals assess, plan, and implement programs to recognize, monitor, and control environmental hazards and occupational health risks. They work to prevent disease, injury, and disability by ensuring compliance with health and safety standards in workplaces and communities. Their responsibilities include conducting inspections, sampling contaminants, advising on protective measures, and training workers. In India, these professionals are increasingly needed due to growing industrialization and stricter regulatory frameworks under the Factories Act and Environment Protection Act.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a safety officer or environmental analyst, advancing to senior EHS manager or consultant. Specialization in industrial hygiene, environmental auditing, or occupational medicine opens further opportunities. Many professionals transition into corporate roles as sustainability heads or regulatory affairs managers. Certification from bodies like the Board of Certified Safety Professionals (BCSP) or Indian Association of Occupational Health (IAOH) enhances prospects. The field offers stable employment with increasing responsibilities in policy formulation and training.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary progression for this role in India?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from INR 400,000 to 600,000 per year. With 4-7 years of experience and certifications, it rises to INR 700,000-1,200,000. Senior roles with 8+ years can earn INR 1,400,000-2,500,000, especially in multinational firms.

**Q: Are Indian EHS professionals eligible for work visas in other countries?**
A: Yes, but limited. Countries like Canada and Australia have demand for occupational hygienists under skilled migration. However, Indian qualifications may need assessment. In the EU, separate work permits are required. The Occupation Shortage List in the UK sometimes includes EHS roles.

**Q: What education is mandatory to practice as an environmental health professional in India?**
A: A bachelor's degree in environmental science, occupational health, or engineering is typically required. Master's degrees or diplomas in industrial safety are preferred. For medical roles, an MBBS or nursing degree is mandatory.
