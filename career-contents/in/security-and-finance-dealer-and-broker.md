# Securities and Finance Dealers and Brokers — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 3311 (NCO2015)**

Securities and Finance Dealers and Brokers (ISCO 3311) execute buy and sell orders for securities, commodities, and financial instruments on behalf of clients or their own firm. They work in stock exchanges, banks, and brokerage houses, requiring strong analytical skills and regulatory knowledge. In India, this role is concentrated in financial hubs like Mumbai, Delhi, and Bangalore, and employs over 60,000 professionals. The role demands NISM certification and a graduate degree in finance or commerce, with salaries varying widely based on experience and firm type.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 75,756. India's securities market is expanding due to rising retail participation, digitization, and regulatory reforms. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has introduced new investment products, driving demand for dealers and brokers. Employment growth is expected at 5-7% annually over the next five years, with opportunities in algorithmic trading and wealth management. However, automation of routine trades may limit demand for low-skilled brokers. The sector remains cyclical, with growth tied to equity market performance.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Entry typically as a dealer or broker assistant, progressing to senior dealer, then team lead. Many move to portfolio management, investment banking, or start their own brokerage. Career advancement requires passing higher-level NISM certifications and building a client network. The rise of fintech and AI-driven trading is shifting focus towards analytical and advisory roles. Professionals with CFA or MBA qualifications have better prospects for moving into fund management or research. Lateral moves to asset management firms are common after 5-7 years.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically execute stock, bond, and commodity orders through algorithms, no manual order placement
- AI automatically generates market analysis reports and investment briefs
- AI-driven chatbots handle common customer inquiries (e.g., account inquiries, transaction confirmations)
- Rule-based system automatically identifies potential suspicious transactions and triggers alerts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI analyzes customer transaction history and risk preferences to assist in generating personalized investment portfolio recommendations
- Real-time market sentiment monitoring and event impact simulation to improve decision quality for advisors
- Automatically generate compliance checklists and regulatory reports to reduce paperwork time
- Predict upselling and cross-selling opportunities through customer behavior models

**Human moat:**

- Build deep trust relationships, understand clients' non-financial needs (e.g., family wealth inheritance, emotional needs)
- Provide qualitative judgment and real-time adjustments to investment strategies in complex market environments
- Take on personalized responsibility for one-on-one consulting and crisis communication with high-net-worth clients
- Ethical judgment and interpretation concerning regulatory grey areas and new financial products

**Skills to build:**

- Financial data analysis tools (e.g., Python, R, SQL).
- Understanding of AI/machine learning applications in portfolio optimization
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and AI-enhanced customer interaction platforms
- Regulatory technology (RegTech) and compliance automation tools
- Understanding of complex financial derivative pricing models
- Soft skills: negotiation, empathy, crisis communication

**Key growth areas:**

- Algorithmic trading
- Wealth management
- Derivatives trading
- Fintech integration

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a securities dealer in India?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from INR 3-6 lakh per annum, mid-level (4-7 years) INR 8-20 lakh, and senior roles INR 20-80 lakh including bonuses. Top performers in proprietary trading can earn significantly more.

**Q: Can a foreigner work as a securities dealer in India?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. Most foreign workers come on an Employment Visa (sponsored by a recognized stockbroking firm) with minimum salary of around INR 16 lakh. The employer must prove no local candidate is available. Intra-company transfer is an option for multinational bank employees.
