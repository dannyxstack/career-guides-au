# Mixed Crop and Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9213 (NCO2015)**

Mixed crop and livestock farm labourers perform a variety of manual tasks on farms that combine crop cultivation and animal husbandry. They plant, cultivate, and harvest crops; feed, water, and tend livestock; clean barns and stables; operate and maintain farm machinery; and perform general farm maintenance. This occupation is prevalent across rural India, especially in states like Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Maharashtra. The work is seasonal and physically demanding, with low barriers to entry. Most workers are informally employed with minimal job security. The role is critical for India's agricultural sector, which employs nearly half the workforce, though productivity and wages remain low.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,139,019. India's agricultural sector is expected to see moderate growth due to government initiatives like the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi and focus on farm mechanization. However, the share of agriculture in GDP is declining as the economy shifts towards services and manufacturing. Demand for farm labourers may remain stable but could face downward pressure from mechanization and rural-urban migration. Policy support for minimum support prices and irrigation may sustain some demand. Overall, the outlook is flat with limited wage growth, and many workers seek alternative livelihoods.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; most workers remain in the same role without formal advancement. Some may become team leaders or supervisors on larger farms. A few transition into farm ownership if they acquire land, or move into allied activities like dairy or poultry. Skills development through government training schemes (e.g., Skill India) can help workers operate machinery or adopt better practices. However, with low formal education and lack of capital, upward mobility is rare. Many labourers eventually migrate to urban areas for better opportunities in construction or manufacturing.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using automated tractors for plowing, sowing, and harvesting
- Monitor crop growth and pests/diseases using sensors and AI systems
- Caring for livestock using automatic feeders and milking robots
- Use drones to inspect farms and produce yield maps
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans based on historical data and weather forecasts

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining smart agricultural equipment
- Using data analysis to improve crop yields and herd health
- Develop comprehensive agricultural strategies to balance ecology and economy
- Collaborate with AI for precision agriculture management
- Train new employees in using digital tools

**Human moat:**

- On-site diagnosis and repair of sudden mechanical faults
- Unstructured work in complex terrain or extreme weather conditions.
- Animal emergency medical care and behavioural intervention
- Compliance with organic certification and animal welfare standards.

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural drone operation and data analysis certification
- Use of precision agriculture software (e.g., FarmOS, Climate FieldView)
- Basic electrical and mechanical maintenance skills
- Digital literacy: Excel/data recording apps
- Basics of crop and livestock health management
- Training for safe operation of AGV/robot equipment

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Dairy and poultry integration

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for a mixed crop and livestock farm labourer in India?**
A: Annual salaries range from ₹60,000 for entry-level to ₹2,40,000 for experienced supervisors. Most workers are paid daily wages of ₹200-800 depending on region and season.

**Q: Can a foreign national work as a farm labourer in India?**
A: No, India does not offer work visas for low-skilled farm labour. The Employment Visa requires high skills and a minimum salary of ₹20 lakhs per annum, making it impossible for this occupation.
