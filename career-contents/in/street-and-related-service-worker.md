# Street and Related Services Worker — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9510 (NCO2015)**

Street and related services workers perform a variety of tasks on streets and in public places, such as selling goods, providing basic services like shoe shining or car washing, and collecting recyclable materials. This occupation is characterized by informal employment, low earnings, and minimal social protection. Workers are often self-employed or work for small, unregistered enterprises. The role requires little formal education and is common in urban and semi-urban areas across India.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 125,846. The outlook for street and related services workers in India is mixed. Urbanization and population growth sustain demand for low-cost services, but competition is intense. The informal nature of the work limits income growth and job security. Policy efforts to formalize the informal economy may reduce the number of street-based workers over the long term. Automation (e.g., digital payments) and e-commerce also pose challenges. Overall, employment is expected to remain stable but with low earnings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression is largely absent for street and related services workers. Most remain in the same role indefinitely, with occasional moves to slightly higher-earning activities (e.g., from rag picking to selling vegetables). Some transition to small-scale retail or services. There is little formal training or certification. Social mobility is limited due to lack of capital, education, and legal recognition. Government schemes for street vendors (e.g., PM SVANidhi) provide some support.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban growth
- Low-skilled demand
- Informal economy
- Self-employment

## 7. FAQ

**Q: How much do street workers earn in India?**
A: Earnings vary widely. A street vendor selling snacks or fruit can earn between INR 5,000 to 15,000 per month, while a shoe shiner might earn INR 3,000 to 8,000. Many live on less than USD 2 per day.

**Q: Can a street worker migrate to work abroad?**
A: No, this occupation does not qualify for skilled migration visas. Most destinations require formal qualifications and higher incomes. Only jobs in demand like IT or healthcare offer visa pathways. Some semi-skilled workers go to Gulf countries on low-skilled visas, but street services are not in demand.

**Q: Is there any career growth for street workers?**
A: Limited. Some street vendors accumulate capital to open a small shop or rent a permanent stall. Others diversify into multiple services. However, most remain in the same role without formal advancement.
