# Hunter and Trapper — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 6224 (NCO2015)**

Hunters and trappers pursue, capture, and kill wild animals for food, fur, or sport, often using traditional methods. In India, this occupation is largely confined to indigenous and rural communities, particularly in forested regions of Central, Eastern, and Northeastern states. Due to legal restrictions under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, commercial hunting is heavily regulated, with limited scope for gainful employment. Most engage in subsistence hunting or work as forest guards, trackers, or guides for wildlife tourism. The role is in decline due to conservation policies and modernization.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited. Most work informally, with no formal advancement. Some transition to wildlife conservation roles, such as forest guards or anti-poaching staff, which require additional qualifications. Others leverage local knowledge to become ecotourism guides or research assistants. Few hunters move into commercial trapping for pest control or scientific study, but such roles are rare. The profession is not recognized as a mainstream career path.

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for hunters in India?**
A: Most hunters earn below poverty line, typically INR 80,000–150,000 annually. Those employed by eco-tourism may earn INR 200,000–400,000, but these roles are rare.

**Q: Can foreign hunters get a visa to work in India?**
A: No. India does not issue work visas for hunting. The profession is restricted under wildlife laws, and foreign nationals cannot engage in hunting activities legally. Only tourists visiting on tourist e-visas may participate in non-consumptive wildlife tourism.

**Q: Is hunting a recognised career in India?**
A: No, it is not a mainstream career. It is a traditional subsistence activity. Some find work as forest guides or in anti-poaching squads, but formal opportunities are extremely limited.
