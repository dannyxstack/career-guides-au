# Bicycle and Related Repairers — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7234 (NCO2015)**

Bicycle and related repairers maintain, repair, and assemble bicycles, tricycles, and similar non-motorized vehicles. They diagnose mechanical issues, replace or repair parts such as brakes, gears, chains, and tires, and perform routine servicing. This occupation is essential for urban mobility and the growing cycling culture in India.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or helper, advancing to senior mechanic or shop supervisor. Experienced repairers may open their own workshops or specialize in high-end bicycles, e-bikes, or motorsports. Certification from training institutes like the Automotive Skill Development Council (ASDC) can enhance prospects. Lateral moves into sales, rental services, or parts supply are common.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use diagnostic software to automatically identify common faults (e.g., circuit issues)

- Inventory management and parts ordering automated by AI system

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face communication with clients to understand cycling preferences and customize solutions
- Handling replacement and adaptation of old or non-standard parts

- Sustainable repair concepts (e.g., battery recycling and upgrades)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: Can a bicycle repairer earn enough to support a family?**
A: In India, entry-level earnings are low (₹8,000-15,000/month). Experienced mechanics or shop owners can earn ₹30,000-50,000/month, sufficient for a modest family in smaller cities. Growth is limited without specialization.

**Q: Is it possible for a foreign bicycle repairer to get a work visa for India?**
A: Unlikely. India's Employment Visa requires a minimum salary of ₹20 lakh/year, which far exceeds bicycle repairer earnings. Business or tourist visas do not permit work. Migration is not a viable route.

**Q: What formal training is available in India for bicycle repair?**
A: Formal training is limited. The Automotive Skill Development Council (ASDC) offers short-term certification. Some Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) include bicycle repair in their motor mechanics course. Apprenticeship under experienced mechanics is the most common path.
