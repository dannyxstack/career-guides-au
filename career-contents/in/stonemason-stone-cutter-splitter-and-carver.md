# Stonemason, Stone Cutter, Splitter and Carver — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 7113 (NCO2015)**

Stonemasons, stone cutters, splitters, and carvers shape and install natural or artificial stone for construction, restoration, and decorative purposes. They work with granite, marble, sandstone, and other stone types, using hand and power tools to cut, shape, and finish stone for buildings, monuments, memorials, and landscaping. This occupation is deeply rooted in India's traditional architecture and modern construction, with demand driven by infrastructure projects, heritage conservation, and high-end residential and commercial developments.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 186,259. The Indian construction sector is expected to grow steadily, driven by government infrastructure initiatives like Smart Cities and housing schemes. Demand for skilled stonemasons remains moderate, as modern construction shifts toward concrete and prefabricated materials. However, niche demand persists for heritage restoration (e.g., temples, forts) and premium stone finishes in luxury projects. Skill development programs under the Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY) may boost formal training. Overall employment outlook is stable but with slow growth; many masons work informally, and wages vary by region and skill level.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an apprentice or helper, advancing to a skilled mason, then to a master craftsman or supervisor. Experienced stonemasons may specialize in carving, lettering, or intricate designs, often working on monuments or temples. Some move into independent contracting or start their own small businesses. Formal certification through the Construction Skill Development Council of India (CSDCI) can enhance mobility. However, automation (CNC stone cutting) and competition from cheaper materials limit advancement for traditional hand-carvers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for stone cutting and layout design
- Operating automatic stone cutting machines and CNC engraving machines
- Material quantity calculation and layout using BIM models
- Use robots for repetitive stone handling and laying

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using 3D scanning and digital twins for precise stone structure installation
- Use AR glasses to overlay design drawings on construction sites, reducing errors
- Using AI to optimize stone splicing plans, improving aesthetics and strength
- Conduct drone inspections of large stone structures and remotely monitor maintenance needs.

**Human moat:**

- Artistic judgment for complex stone carving and hand modeling
- Adaptive skills for integrated on-site stone and structure installation
- Empirical judgment of stone texture, color, and physical properties
- Ability to communicate customized design solutions with clients

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/BIM software operation (e.g., AutoCAD, Revit)
- CNC stone processing equipment programming and operation
- 3D scanning and digital modeling skills
- Engineering surveying and precision installation technology
- Drone operation and image analysis basics

**Key growth areas:**

- Heritage restoration

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a stonemason in India?**
A: A mid-level stonemason (4-10 years experience) earns between INR 2,50,000 and INR 4,50,000 per year. Senior master craftsmen can earn up to INR 8,00,000, especially in urban areas or specialty work.

**Q: Can foreign stonemasons work in India?**
A: It is difficult. India's Employment Visa requires a high salary threshold (over INR 1.6M/yr) and employer sponsorship for specialized roles. Most stonemasons would not meet the criteria; however, short-term Business Visas or OCI cards (for persons of Indian origin) may allow limited work.

**Q: How can I become a certified stonemason in India?**
A: You can pursue a 1-2 year ITI certificate in stonemasonry from a government institute, or complete an apprenticeship under a master mason. Voluntary certification through the Construction Skill Development Council (CSDCI) can improve job prospects.
