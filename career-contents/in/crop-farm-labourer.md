# Crop Farm Labourer — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9211 (NCO2015)**

Crop farm labourers perform manual tasks such as planting, weeding, harvesting, and sorting crops, primarily in rural India. They work under supervisors on farms or plantations, often seasonally, and are a critical part of India's agricultural workforce. The role requires physical stamina and minimal formal education, with wages varying by region and crop type.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 31,315,852. India's agricultural sector employs nearly 45% of the workforce, but the share is declining. Mechanisation and climate change are reshaping demand for manual labour. While subsistence farming persists, commercial farming may reduce unskilled roles. Government schemes like MGNREGA provide some wage support, but overall, the number of traditional crop labourers is slowly shrinking, especially in developed states.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Crop farm labourers typically have limited upward mobility. Some may become team leaders or supervisors with experience. Others transition to allied activities like livestock or agro-processing. Literacy and skill training programs (e.g., PM-KISAN, Krishi Vigyan Kendras) can help, but formal advancement is rare without education. Many labourers migrate seasonally within India.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual seeding and transplanting: replaced by automated seeders and transplanting robots
- Weed control and pest monitoring: AI visual recognition combined with precision spraying robots.
- Routine irrigation operations: sensors and smart irrigation systems automatically regulate
- Crop maturity detection and harvesting: computer vision and robotic arms automatically assess and pick
- Field data recording: automatic collection and upload by IoT sensors

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining complex agricultural machinery: AI-assisted diagnostics and remote guidance improve efficiency
- Precision agriculture data analysis: using AI to optimize planting density and fertilization timing
- Crop health monitoring: AI-assisted early disease detection and intervention suggestions
- Quality grading and packaging: AI vision systems assist manual sorting with high speed and precision

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making ability for sudden anomalies (e.g., extreme weather, equipment failure)
- Flexible operation in complex terrain and multi-crop mixed planting environments
- Interpersonal collaboration and communication with farmers and technology suppliers
- Quality assessment based on local experience (e.g., optimal harvest window)

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural machinery operation and basic maintenance.
- Use of precision agriculture tools (e.g., GPS navigation, drones)
- Data analysis and agricultural software application
- Basic programming and automation system configuration
- Read technical documentation in English or Italian
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Contract farming
- Supply chain labor
- Perishable horticulture

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for a crop farm labourer in India?**
A: Annual earnings range from ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000 for entry-level, up to ₹2,40,000 for experienced supervisors, but income is highly seasonal and varies by region and crop. Many labourers rely on MGNREGA (100 days of guaranteed work) for stability.

**Q: Can a crop farm labourer migrate to another country for work?**
A: Not as a skilled migrant. Crop farming is manual labour without visa pathways. Only highly skilled agricultural specialists (e.g., agronomists) with advanced degrees and high salaries (₹20 lakh+ in India) may qualify for employment visas to India or other countries.
