# Water And Firewood Collector — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9624 (NCO2015)**

Water and firewood collectors gather water from natural sources and collect firewood for household use, primarily in rural and remote areas of India where piped water and modern fuels are not available. This occupation is physically demanding and is predominantly performed by women and children, often as unpaid family labor or for small payments. It is characterized by low wages, precarious working conditions, and limited access to social protections.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: low (★☆☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 4,852. As India progresses towards universal access to piped water and cleaner cooking fuels under schemes like Jal Jeevan Mission and Ujjwala Yojana, the demand for water and firewood collectors is expected to decline gradually over the next decade. However, in remote regions with poor infrastructure, this occupation will persist. Automation is unlikely to replace this work due to its informal and dispersed nature. Employment is expected to shrink slowly, with no significant policy intervention expected to formalize the sector.

**Competition: very high (★★★★½).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★★).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is extremely limited; most collectors remain in the same informal work throughout their lives. Some may transition to other manual labor roles such as construction or domestic work if they migrate to urban areas. There are no formal training or certification pathways. Government programs like the National Rural Livelihoods Mission aim to create alternative livelihood opportunities, but actual uptake is low. The occupation is considered a last-resort survival activity.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Collect firewood using GPS and vehicle optimization systems
- Automated water pumps and pipe networks replace manual water collection
- Drones monitor forest resources, reducing manual patrols.
- Smart combustion equipment replaces demand for firewood
- Online platform matching reduces intermediaries between supply and demand

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assistant optimizes collection routes and scheduling
- portable water quality sensors for rapid detection of water source quality
- Mobile app connecting local users to share resources
- Solar-powered charging equipment extends operational battery life.
- Market information platform helps pricing and sales

**Human moat:**

- Flexibility in extremely remote or complex terrain areas
- Building and maintaining trust relationships with the local community
- Adaptability to handle emergency situations (e.g., droughts, fuel shortages)
- Multitasking coordination and manual repair skills
- Mastery of traditional knowledge (e.g., logging timing, water source distribution)

**Skills to build:**

- Using basic digital tools (maps, communication apps)
- Simple mechanical repairs (water pumps, saws)
- Basic financial management and bookkeeping
- Knowledge of sustainable resource management
- Customer communication and service skills
- first aid and safety operation certification

**Key growth areas:**

- Rural
- Informal
- Subsistence
- Manual

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical monthly income of a water and firewood collector?**
A: Most earn between INR 1,500 and 4,000 per month, depending on location and demand. This is far below the national minimum wage.

**Q: Can a water and firewood collector get a work visa abroad?**
A: No. This occupation is entirely informal and does not meet any skill threshold. No country offers work visas for such roles.
