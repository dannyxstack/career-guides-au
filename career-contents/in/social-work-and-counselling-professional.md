# Social Work and Counselling Professionals — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 2635 (NCO2015)**

Social work and counselling professionals in India provide psychosocial support, therapy, and guidance to individuals, families, and communities facing mental health issues, substance abuse, trauma, or social challenges. They work in NGOs, healthcare settings, schools, corporate wellness programs, and private practice, with growing demand due to increased mental health awareness and government initiatives.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 428,207. The employment outlook is positive, driven by rising mental health awareness, government schemes like the National Mental Health Programme, corporate employee wellness programs, and integration of counsellors in schools. The sector is expected to grow 15-20% over the next five years, with urban demand outpacing rural. However, formal job creation remains moderate, and many professionals work in unorganised or part-time roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with roles like counsellor or caseworker, advancing to senior counsellor, clinical supervisor, or programme manager. Specialisation in areas like child protection, addiction, or trauma therapy is common. Further education (Masters/PhD) or certifications (e.g., from RCI) enable independent practice or leadership. Some transition into training, policy advocacy, or academia.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate case reports and progress records
- Initial psychological assessment based on standardized questionnaires
- Crisis hotline initial screening and information triage
- Scheduling appointments and case management system operation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze social media data for early warning of community mental health risks
- Using virtual reality therapy to assist in PTSD treatment
- AI-supported multilingual automatic transcription and sentiment analysis improve consultation efficiency.
- Data-driven case management optimizes resource allocation.

**Human moat:**

- Establishes deep trust relationships and empathetic interaction
- Handling Ethical Dilemmas and Complex Legal Cases
- Flexible communication strategies in multicultural contexts

**Skills to build:**

- Digital literacy and use of AI tools (e.g., data analysis, VR therapy)
- Trauma-Informed Care and Crisis Intervention in Practice
- Cross-disciplinary collaboration skills (with healthcare and education sectors)
- Research evaluation and evidence-based practice methods
- Self-care and burnout management
- Multicultural competence and interpretation of Italian local policies

**Key growth areas:**

- Mental Health
- Corporate Wellness
- School Counselling
- Geriatric Care

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a social worker in India?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from ₹2-4 lakh per annum, typically in NGOs or government schemes. With a Master's degree, starting pay may be slightly higher, but overall the field is not highly remunerative.

**Q: Can foreign social work professionals get a work visa for India?**
A: It is difficult. The Employment Visa requires a minimum salary of ₹20 lakh, which most social workers do not earn. Business Visas do not permit employment. OCI cards are an option for those of Indian origin.

**Q: Is social work a skilled migration route to India?**
A: No. India does not have a points-based migration system, and social work is not listed as a shortage occupation for visa facilitation. Inbound work is employer-sponsored and rare for this field.
