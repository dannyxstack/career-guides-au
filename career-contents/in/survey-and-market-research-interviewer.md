# Survey and Market Research Interviewer — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 4227 (NCO2015)**

Survey and Market Research Interviewers conduct interviews to collect data for market research, opinion polls, and social studies. They work across various sectors including consumer goods, healthcare, and government, often on a project basis. In India, employment is concentrated in market research agencies, NGOs, and government statistical departments.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 15,220. Demand for survey interviewers in India is stable, driven by growing market research and government data collection needs. However, automation and online surveys are reducing telephone and face-to-face roles. Growth is expected in rural and semi-urban areas where digital literacy is lower, sustaining some field interviewer demand. The NSSO and PLFS provide periodic employment data, but overall workforce is modest and churn is high.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically moves from interviewer to team leader, then to research executive or analyst roles. Many interviewers transition into marketing, data analysis, or policy research. Certifications in market research can enhance prospects. However, the role is often entry-level with high turnover; experienced interviewers may specialize in difficult-to-reach populations or complex surveys.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised questionnaire surveys conducted by phone or online
- Data entry and basic cleaning
- Initial screening and recruitment of respondents
- Execution of interviews with preset scripts
- Summary statistics of simple opinion polls

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Designing more precise questionnaires and sampling plans
- Use AI analysis tools to mine deep insights from data
- Handling complex, semi-structured interviews and sensitive topics.
- Sentiment analysis using natural language processing techniques
- Provide training data and quality verification for AI systems

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and rapport
- Understand and manage complex, emotional respondent reactions
- Cross-cultural, multilingual communication skills
- Strategic thinking for project design and result interpretation.
- Data quality ethical judgment and compliance awareness

**Skills to build:**

- Data Analysis and Visualization (Python/R, Tableau)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT, SurveyMonkey AI)
- Questionnaire design and experimental methods
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
- Basic programming and automation scripts
- Domain knowledge (e.g., healthcare, finance)

**Key growth areas:**

- Government Surveys
- Healthcare Research
- Consumer Panels
- Rural Fieldwork

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a survey interviewer in India?**
A: Entry-level salaries range from INR 10,000-20,000 per month (INR 120,000-240,000 annually). Experienced interviewers may earn up to INR 50,000 per month, but roles are often project-based with variable income.

**Q: Can foreign nationals work as survey interviewers in India?**
A: Unlikely. The role does not meet the salary threshold (INR 2.1M) for an Employment Visa. Business Visas do not permit local employment. Only intra-company transfers or specialist roles may qualify.
