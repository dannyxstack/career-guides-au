# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · India

**Occupation code: 9215 (NCO2015)**

Forestry labourers perform manual tasks in silviculture, forest fire prevention, and timber harvesting, including planting, thinning, and clearing undergrowth. They work under supervisors in government forest departments or private plantations, often in remote areas with physical demands and seasonal employment patterns.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,145,540. India's forestry sector is stable but limited in growth, with employment concentrated in state forest departments and small-scale plantations. Mechanization is slow, keeping demand for manual labour steady in regions like Northeast and Central India. Climate change initiatives and afforestation programs may create temporary jobs, but overall workforce expansion is muted.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry is typically unskilled with on-the-job training. Progression to supervisor or forest guard requires passing departmental exams or gaining experience. Some labourers move to related roles in wildlife conservation or eco-tourism. Permanent positions are rare; most work is contractual or daily-wage.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- Afforestation
- Forest fire management
- Eco-tourism support
- Climate resilience

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry labourer in India?**
A: Annual salaries range from INR 120,000 to 240,000 (daily wage ~INR 400-800), often paid daily or weekly, with no benefits in casual contracts.

**Q: Can a forestry labourer get an Indian work visa?**
A: No. India's Employment Visa requires high-skilled roles with minimum salary of INR 1.6M (approx. USD 20,000), not applicable to manual labour. There is no low-skilled labour migration route.

**Q: What is the career progression?**
A: Limited. With experience, one may become a forest guard (requires passing state exams) or supervisor, but permanent government positions are scarce.
