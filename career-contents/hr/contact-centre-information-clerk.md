## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Answer standard FAQ questions (e.g. account balance, business hours)
- Handling simple order changes, returns, and exchange requests
- Transfer complex complaints to human agents
- Record and update customer information in the CRM system
- Send standard confirmation emails or SMS notifications

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted real-time translation, supporting multilingual customer service
- Emotion recognition alerts to help agents adjust communication strategies
- Smart knowledge base recommendations for quickly locating solutions to complex issues
- Automatic call summary generation reduces post-call documentation time
- Predict customer intent and prepare response scripts in advance

**Human moat:**

- Handle highly emotional, sensitive, or sudden complaints
- Coordinate across departments to solve complex non-standard issues.
- Build customer trust and maintain long-term relationships
- Respond to unforeseen emergencies (e.g., system failures)
- Deliver personalized, empathetic service experiences

**Skills to build:**

- Proficient in major AI customer service platforms (e.g., Zendesk AI, Salesforce Einstein)
- Learn basic data analysis (SQL/Excel) to analyze customer trends.
- Enhance conflict management and emotional communication skills
- Master the principles of Conversational Design
- Understand automation process building tools (e.g., UiPath, Automation Anywhere)
- Develop multilingual skills (especially English) to serve international clients

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a contact centre information clerk in Croatia?**
A: Salaries for contact centre information clerks in Croatia vary by experience and location. Entry-level positions often range from HRK 4,500 to HRK 5,500 net monthly. With experience, salaries can reach HRK 6,500 to HRK 8,000 net. Major cities like Zagreb may offer higher pay. Bonuses and shift allowances are common. Exact figures depend on the employer and specific role requirements.

**Q: How can a non-EU citizen work as a contact centre information clerk in Croatia via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU citizens can apply for an EU Blue Card in Croatia if they hold a university degree or have five years of relevant professional experience. The job must offer a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross salary in Croatia (currently around HRK 12,000 gross). Alternatively, a standard work permit is possible if the employer passes a labour market test. The employer sponsors the application.

**Q: What skills are most valued for contact centre information clerks in Croatia?**
A: Employers in Croatia seek fluency in Croatian and English; additional languages like German or Italian are a strong advantage. Key skills include excellent communication, problem-solving, computer literacy (CRM software), and patience. Training is usually provided for specific products or services. Career progression may lead to team leader or quality assurance roles.
