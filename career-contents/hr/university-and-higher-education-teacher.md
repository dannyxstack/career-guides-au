## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for university teachers in Croatia?**
A: Salaries in public universities are set by collective agreements and vary by academic rank. Junior assistant professors earn around €1,200–€1,500 net monthly, while full professors can earn over €2,500 net. Private institutions may offer different scales. Actual pay depends on experience, publications, and teaching load.

**Q: How can a foreign university teacher obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Croatia?**
A: Non-EU citizens need an EU Blue Card or work permit. The Blue Card requires a recognized degree, a valid employment contract of at least one year, and a salary meeting a legal threshold (currently set at 1.5 times the national average gross salary). The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled locally. Applications are lodged with the Croatian Ministry of Interior.

**Q: Do university teachers need to speak Croatian to work in Croatia?**
A: Yes, most teaching is conducted in Croatian, so fluency is required. However, some institutions offer programs in English, especially at postgraduate level, where English proficiency may suffice. For career advancement, knowledge of Croatian is generally expected, even for foreign staff. Many universities also require a PhD and a record of research publications.
