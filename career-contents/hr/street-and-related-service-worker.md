- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.

- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Croatia?**
A: Street and related services workers in Croatia typically earn the national minimum wage or slightly above. As of 2025, the gross minimum wage is about €840 per month. Actual pay may vary by employer and location, but it generally remains low-skilled, with limited opportunities for overtime or bonuses.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as street and related services workers in Croatia through a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work in this occupation via a standard work permit, but the EU Blue Card is not applicable as it requires a higher education degree and a higher salary threshold. Work permits are employer-sponsored and subject to labor market tests. The occupation is typically considered low-skilled, so quotas or restrictions may apply.

**Q: What are the main duties of street and related services workers in Croatia?**
A: Street and related services workers in Croatia perform tasks such as selling goods (e.g., newspapers, flowers) at street stands, cleaning public spaces, or providing services like shoe shining. They often work independently or for small businesses, with duties varying by city and season. The role requires physical stamina and basic customer service skills.
