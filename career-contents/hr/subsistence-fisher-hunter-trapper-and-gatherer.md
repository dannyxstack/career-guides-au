## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using smartphones, GPS, simple apps)
- Drone operation and image interpretation
- Basic data analysis (capture records, weather patterns)
- Knowledge of sustainable resource management
- Wilderness first aid and survival skills
- Small solar/portable power maintenance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers and gatherers in Croatia?**
A: In Croatia, subsistence fishers and gatherers operate informally; income varies widely by location, season, and harvest. Most rely on personal consumption and small-scale local sales. Average monthly earnings are difficult to quantify but typically low, often supplementing other livelihoods. Official statistics for this informal sector are not available.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a subsistence fisher in Croatia?**
A: Subsistence fishing and gathering are not eligible for the EU Blue Card, which targets highly skilled workers. Non-EU nationals may apply for a Croatian work permit only with a specific job offer from an employer, but such roles are rarely offered to foreigners. Practical migration routes for this occupation are virtually nonexistent.

**Q: What does a day in the life of a subsistence fisher in Croatia look like?**
A: Activities include fishing in coastal or inland waters using small boats or nets, collecting shellfish, and harvesting wild plants. Work follows seasonal patterns and local regulations. Many combine fishing with agriculture or tourism. The job is physically demanding, requires knowledge of local ecosystems, and is often passed down through generations.
