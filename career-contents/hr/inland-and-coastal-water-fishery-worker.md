## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Croatia?**
A: Salaries vary by employer, experience, and catch type. Most workers earn the national minimum wage, around 700-800 EUR net monthly. With experience and higher catches, earnings can reach 1,000 EUR net. Overtime and bonus pay may apply. Official statistics from the Croatian Bureau of Statistics show an average gross monthly salary of about 1,000 EUR for this sector.

**Q: What are the official routes for non-EU citizens to work as a fishery worker in Croatia?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work permit, which an employer must obtain. The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified positions (requiring tertiary education) and rarely applies here. Common routes include seasonal work permits (up to 6 months) or standard temporary residence and work permits based on an employment contract, subject to quota availability.

**Q: What qualifications or licenses are needed to work as a fishery worker in Croatia?**
A: No formal education is mandatory, but vocational training in fisheries is beneficial. For commercial fishing on the Adriatic, a valid fishing license is required, issued by the Ministry of Agriculture. Workers must also hold a seaman's book if working on vessels. Knowledge of EU fishing quotas and conservation rules is essential.
