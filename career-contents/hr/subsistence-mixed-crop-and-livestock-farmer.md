## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Croatia?**
A: Subsistence farmers in Croatia primarily produce for their own consumption, not for market sale. As such, they do not have a regular salary. Any income comes from occasional surplus sales at local markets. Average annual earnings vary widely but are generally low, often below the national minimum wage, and are not reported in standard employment statistics.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Croatia?**
A: Subsistence farming is typically a self-sufficient activity and does not qualify as paid employment under Croatian labour law. Therefore, it is not eligible for an EU Blue Card, which requires a highly skilled professional role. Non-EU citizens may enter Croatia for seasonal agricultural work via a temporary residence permit for seasonal workers, but not specifically for subsistence farming.

**Q: What are the prospects for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Croatia?**
A: Subsistence farming in Croatia is declining as rural populations age and younger generations migrate to cities. The occupation offers limited economic opportunities and is often combined with other income sources. Government subsidies under the Common Agricultural Policy support small farms, but many farmers face challenges from market competition and climate change.
