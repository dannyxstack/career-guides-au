**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for building frame and related trades workers in Croatia?**
A: Gross monthly salaries for these workers in Croatia typically range between €800 and €1,200, depending on experience, employer, and region. Overtime and bonuses may increase earnings. Social contributions and income tax are deducted at source.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card requirements for non-EU nationals in this occupation in Croatia?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a job offer from a Croatian employer. The employer must obtain a work permit from the Croatian Employment Service (HZZ). For the EU Blue Card, the gross annual salary must be at least 1.5 times the average Croatian salary (approx. €16,000 in 2023). Both routes lead to a temporary residence permit for work.

**Q: What are the main duties of a building frame and related trades worker in Croatia?**
A: These workers perform specialized tasks in constructing building frames, such as assembling and erecting steel or concrete structures, installing reinforcing bars, and performing formwork. They may also work on prefabricated elements. Tasks vary by project and require physical stamina, precision, and adherence to safety standards.
