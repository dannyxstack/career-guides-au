**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Preliminary screening and annotation of routine imaging (X-rays, CT scans).
- Automatic interpretation of laboratory test results
- Automated generation and coding of standard medical records
- Basic query on drug interactions
- Automated retrieval and summarization of evidence-based medical literature

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems improve recognition rates for rare diseases
- AI-driven personalized treatment plan design.
- Surgical robots assist in minimally invasive operations.
- Real-time monitoring data analysis to warn of patient deterioration
- Remote consultation and AI translation eliminate language barriers

**Human moat:**

- Complex clinical reasoning and differential diagnosis
- Immediate decision-making and surgical skills in emergencies
- Patient communication, empathy, and trust building
- Leadership and integration responsibilities in multidisciplinary collaboration
- Licensing requirements for autonomous prescribing and surgery under law and ethics

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for specialist medical practitioners in Croatia?**
A: In Croatia, specialist medical practitioners in the public health system earn a gross monthly salary between HRK 15,000 and HRK 25,000 (approx. €2,000–€3,300). Salaries in private practice or with additional on-call duties can be higher. According to the Croatian Medical Chamber, earnings vary by specialty and experience.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card routes for specialist doctors to work in Croatia?**
A: Non-EU specialist doctors can work in Croatia via the EU Blue Card (for highly skilled workers requiring a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary) or a standard work permit. Requirements include a recognized medical degree validated by the Croatian Medical Chamber, proof of Croatian language proficiency, and a job offer.

**Q: How are foreign medical qualifications recognized for specialist practice in Croatia?**
A: Foreign qualifications must be recognized by the Croatian Medical Chamber. For EU/EEA degrees, recognition is automatic. Non-EU degrees require equivalence assessment, language proficiency testing, and possible additional exams in Croatian. The process validates that the training meets Croatian specialty standards before full registration.
