## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for physicists and astronomers in Croatia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience. In academia, early-career researchers earn around €1,200-€1,800 gross monthly, while senior positions can reach €2,500-€3,500. In private R&D or industry, pay is higher, ranging from €2,000 to €4,000. The median gross annual salary is approximately €25,000-€35,000, but figures shift with inflation and funding.

**Q: What are the official routes for a foreign physicist or astronomer to work in Croatia?**
A: Non-EU nationals require a work and residence permit, typically obtained through an employer-sponsored application under the Law on Foreigners. An alternative is the EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers, requiring a valid employment contract, recognized qualifications, and salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary in Croatia. EU/EEA citizens have free movement and face no work visa requirements.

**Q: What are the main employment sectors for physicists and astronomers in Croatia?**
A: Primary employers include universities (e.g., University of Zagreb, University of Split), research institutes (e.g., Ruđer Bošković Institute, Institute of Physics), and the Croatian Astronomical Society. Opportunities also exist in meteorology, energy, medical physics, and tech companies. Astronomy roles are concentrated in observatories like Višnjan Observatory and the Hvar Observatory. Both fields often require PhDs for advanced positions.
