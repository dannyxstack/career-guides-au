# Gardener — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 6113 (ISCO08)**

Gardeners are responsible for planting, pruning, and maintaining plants, including flowers, shrubs, and lawns, commonly found in public green spaces, private gardens, and nurseries.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Can progress from junior gardener to senior horticulturist or supervisor, or specialize in landscape design, organic cultivation, or nursery management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automate lawn mowing, irrigation, and fertilization via robots or AI systems
- Uses image recognition to automatically monitor pests and diseases and generate reports
- Standardized planting layout and maintenance schedules automatically generated by AI planning tools
- Online customer service chatbot handling inquiries and appointments

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI vision tools assist in diagnosing plant diseases and nutrient deficiencies
- Drone and satellite image analysis for large-scale site health assessment
- AI optimizes environmental parameters such as irrigation, fertilization, and lighting.
- Preview landscape design effects via AR/VR to aid client decisions
- Automatically records plant growth data and generates personalized care recommendations

**Human moat:**

- Manual trimming and fine shaping of complex terrain on site
- Creative landscape design based on aesthetics and experience
- Rapid assessment and handling of unexpected conditions (e.g., extreme weather, soil changes)
- Communicate deeply with clients to understand their personalized needs and build trust

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted design tools (such as Landscape Design Pro)
- Drone operation and data analysis fundamentals
- Sensor and smart irrigation system control
- Integration of plant pathology and image recognition technology
- Digital customer communication and project management
- Basic programming and IoT device maintenance

**Key growth areas:**

- urban greening
- sustainable horticulture
- green roof
- vertical garden

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy outdoor work and physical activity
- Strong interest in plants, willing to learn gardening knowledge
- People who focus on practical skills rather than academic theory

**Who is not suited**
- People who are unsuited to harsh outdoor weather (rain, snow, high temperatures)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a horticultural worker in the Netherlands?**
A: Entry-level approx. €24,000–€30,000, mid-level €30,000–€38,000, senior €38,000–€48,000 (annual pre-tax salary).

**Q: How to become a horticulture worker in the Netherlands for non-EU citizens?**
A: Typically need to obtain a work visa first, but horticultural worker salaries are difficult to reach the high-skilled immigration threshold. It is recommended to obtain relevant educational qualifications first or through EU free movement (if EU residence is held).

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a horticulturist in the Netherlands?**
A: Typically requires an MBO secondary vocational education diploma in horticulture (3-4 years), or HBO higher vocational education (4 years).
