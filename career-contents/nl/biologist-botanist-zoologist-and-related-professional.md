# Biologist — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Conducts biological research covering botany, zoology, and related fields, potentially working at universities, research institutions, or pharmaceutical companies.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Starting as a junior researcher, you can progress to senior researcher, project leader, or professor. Transitioning to the biotechnology or pharmaceutical industry is also a common path.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine DNA/protein sequence alignment and annotation.
- Preliminary Classification and Counting of Microscope Images and Microdata
- Information extraction and summarization in literature reviews
- Daily monitoring and error reporting of laboratory automation equipment
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AlphaFold to predict protein structures and design experiments for verification
- Screen candidate drug targets using machine learning
- Integrating multi-omics data to uncover disease mechanisms.
- Using NLP tools to efficiently track and comprehensively analyse vast amounts of literature
- Simulating ecosystem dynamics to predict environmental change impacts

**Human moat:**

- Design experiments and explain causal mechanisms
- Cross-species, cross-disciplinary systemic biology thinking
- Ethical judgement and research integrity
- Observation and decision-making under uncontrollable factors such as field collection and animal behavior
- Translate AI predictions into actionable biological hypotheses

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and bioinformatics tools
- Machine learning fundamentals (scikit-learn, PyTorch)
- Statistical Modeling and Experimental Design
- Natural language processing is used for literature mining
- Data visualization and report automation
- Hands-on use of domain-specific AI tools (e.g., AlphaFold, Rosalind)

**Key growth areas:**

- Life Sciences
- Academic Research
- Biotechnology
- Pharmaceutical R&D

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Netherlands admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

**Official immigration source:** [EU Immigration Portal](https://immigration-portal.ec.europa.eu/index_en)

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Passionate about natural sciences, with research and analytical skills
- Willing to invest long-term in learning and research.
- Adapting to academic environment or unstable research funding

**Who is not suited**
- Pursuing high salary and quick returns
- Dislikes lab work and data collection

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary of a Dutch biologist?**
A: Junior researcher annual salary approx. €35,000-€45,000, experienced can reach €45,000-€60,000, senior positions up to €80,000.

**Q: How to immigrate to the Netherlands as a non-EU biologist?**
A: Possible via highly skilled migration (employer sponsorship and salary requirements met) or EU Blue Card. If graduated in the Netherlands, you can apply for the Orientation Year visa.
