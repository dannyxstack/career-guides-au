# Nursing Professional — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 2221 (ISCO08)**

Nursing professionals are responsible for providing comprehensive nursing services, including patient assessment, care planning, implementation and evaluation, playing a key role in healthcare institutions.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

AI's impact on nursing is mixed: structured tasks like data entry, scheduling, and basic triage are automated, but clinical judgment, patient communication, and nursing decisions have deep moats, and are enhanced by AI tools for efficiency and quality.

Registered nurses can advance to specialist nurses, nursing supervisors, or nursing managers, or become nursing researchers or educators through further study.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic recording and monitoring of patient vital signs (e.g., electronic medical record entry, vital signs collection)
- Basic consultation and triage (AI chatbot-based symptom collection and initial assessment)
- Nursing roster and resource scheduling (AI-optimised workforce planning)
- Medical record organization and compliance checks (natural language processing for report generation)
- Medication reminders and dose calculations (automated system checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted clinical decision support (providing evidence-based nursing recommendations and risk alerts).
- Personalized generation of patient education materials (AI creates easy-to-understand guides based on medical conditions)
- Remote patient monitoring and early warning (AI analysis of wearable device data)
- Nursing process optimization (AI analyzes efficiency bottlenecks, improves workflows)
- Training and simulation (AI-driven virtual patients for practicing complex scenarios)

**Human moat:**

- Complex clinical judgement and experience-based decision-making (e.g., multi-condition comprehensive assessment)
- Emotional support and building patient trust (empathy, non-verbal communication)
- Manual operation skills (e.g., fine motor skills for intubation, wound care)
- Professional responsibility under laws and regulations (requires practicing with a license, responsible for outcomes)
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and leadership (coordinating doctors, family members, social workers)

**Skills to build:**

- AI and data analysis basics (understanding algorithmic bias, validating AI outputs)
- Operate digital health tools (electronic medical records, remote monitoring platforms)
- Utilization of clinical decision support systems (integrating AI suggestions into care plans)
- AI-assisted communication and patient education (using AI-generated materials reworked)
- Data privacy and ethics (compliance with GDPR/medical regulations)
- Basic programming or scripting skills (automating repetitive tasks)

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- community care
- specialization
- digital health

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass Highly Skilled Migra (Highly Skilled Migrant) | Valid for those with a Bachelor of Nursing and a work contract, where the employer must be an approved sponsor; meet income requirements (2024 monthly salary approximately over 3,000 euros). |

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Patient, empathetic, and enjoy helping others
- Strong stress tolerance, able to adapt to shift work
- Those who wish to develop long-term in the medical field and are willing to keep learning.

**Who is not suited**
- Those not good at communication with people or emotionally sensitive
- Those who dislike heavy physical labor or irregular schedules

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for nursing professionals in the Netherlands?**
A: entry-level nurses earn about €28,000-34,000 before tax, mid-level €34,000-45,000, and advanced specialist nurses can reach €45,000-60,000.

**Q: How can non-EU nursing professionals migrate to the Netherlands?**
A: Common path is through the highly skilled migrant visa (kennismigrant) or EU Blue Card, requiring a job offer from a Dutch employer and BIG registration.

**Q: Is the nursing profession in the Netherlands in shortage?**
A: Yes, the Netherlands faces a severe shortage of care workers, especially in elderly care and community nursing, and the government is actively attracting international talent.
