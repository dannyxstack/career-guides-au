# Bricklayer — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 7112 (ISCO08)**

Bricklayers use bricks, concrete blocks, and other materials to construct walls, chimneys, arches, and other structures. It is one of the core trades in the Dutch construction industry. Tasks include preparing materials, mixing mortar, laying bricks precisely according to plans, and ensuring vertical and horizontal alignment.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 25,000. The Netherlands faces ongoing housing shortages, requiring the construction of approximately 100,000 homes annually, driving strong demand for bricklayers. Additionally, renovation projects of old buildings are increasing, providing stable employment in the industry. The position is expected to maintain moderate growth over the next five years, but must contend with rising raw material costs.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Entry-level bricklayers can start as apprentices, gaining 3-5 years of experience before advancing to skilled bricklayer or foreman. They can also transition to construction supervisor, project coordinator, or self-employment through further study. Obtaining a VCA safety certificate or BRL bricklaying certification can boost competitiveness.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Housing shortage
- Renovation projects
- Sustainable construction
- Infrastructure maintenance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Netherlands admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**

- Those wishing to work freely in the EU in blue-collar construction jobs

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of a bricklayer in the Netherlands?**
A: Starting annual salary for bricklayers around €28,000-€34,000, mid-level around €35,000-€42,000, senior up to €52,000+. Salary varies by experience, region, and project type.

**Q: How can non-EU citizens apply for a work visa as a bricklayer in the Netherlands?**
A: Usually requires employer-sponsored skilled migration visa, but bricklayer salaries are low, making it difficult. Consider first obtaining an EU Blue Card (though salary threshold is high) or the Netherlands' Orientation Year visa for graduates, then transferring to a regular work visa.

**Q: What is the future outlook for bricklayers in the Netherlands?**
A: Outlook is good. Housing shortages and renovation needs continue to drive employment, with the government planning to build 100,000 new homes annually. Sustainable construction (e.g., energy-efficient bricks) also brings new opportunities. However, economic fluctuations and raw material costs should be monitored.
