# Judge — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 2612 (ISCO08)**

Judges in the Dutch judicial system are responsible for hearing cases, making rulings, and ensuring the fair enforcement of the law. They typically work in district courts, appeal courts, or the Supreme Court, requiring deep legal knowledge and extensive experience.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career path typically starts as a lawyer or prosecutor, then applies for judge positions after gaining experience. Promotion can go from local courts to appellate courts to the Supreme Court. Requires passing judicial exams and strict selection.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial retrieval and classification of legal documents
- Generate factual evidence summaries for routine cases
- Format checking and grammar proofreading of judgment documents
- Automatic extraction of dispute focus in simple cases.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Rapid comparative analysis of massive case law using AI
- Automatically generate case summaries and timelines with AI tools.
- Recommending similar cases through legal AI systems.
- Use natural language processing tools to assist in drafting initial versions of judgments.

**Human moat:**

- Legal responsibility and ethical judgment with final decision-making authority
- Non-verbal Cues in Court Trials and Humanized Rulings
- Complex legal interpretation and value balancing
- Building authority and trust with the public and parties involved

**Skills to build:**

- Fundamentals of legal data science (SQL, Python)
- Operation of AI legal tools (e.g., ROSS, CaseText)
- Algorithm bias identification and legal ethics
- Electronic evidence review and discovery tools
- Data visualization to assist court presentations

**Key growth areas:**

- Legal tech
- Judicial reform
- Specialization
- International law

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level of judges in the Netherlands?**
A: Salaries for judges depend on experience and court level: junior judges earn around €50,000-€70,000, senior judges up to €150,000 or more. Supreme Court judges earn higher.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens become judges in the Netherlands?**
A: In theory, yes, but extremely difficult. Requires fluency in Dutch, a Dutch law degree, and passing the bar exam. Usually requires Dutch citizenship or permanent residency.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a judge in the Netherlands?**
A: Requires a Master of Laws degree from the Netherlands, completion of judicial training, and passing exams. Additionally, several years of legal practice experience.
