# Car, Taxi and Van Drivers — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 8322 (ISCO08)**

Driving a car, taxi or light van to transport passengers or goods. This plays an important role in city transport and logistics delivery in the Netherlands.

## 2. Qualifications & licensing

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dutch B-class driver's license (car). | CBR (Dutch Driving Test Authority) | Required | Essential, and requires passing both theoretical and practical exams |
| Taxi driver license (Taxi-vergunning) | Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO) | Required | Must hold to operate taxi services |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Can progress from taxi driver to fleet dispatcher or owner-operator, or transition into logistics distribution management roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Long-distance highway driving: AI autonomous driving systems can handle cruise control, lane keeping, and other operations.
- City delivery route planning: AI real-time path optimization to reduce manual decisions.
- Vehicle status monitoring and basic maintenance: AI sensors automatically detect and report faults.
- Passenger payments and ticketing: mobile payments and automated systems replace manual work.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI real-time traffic and navigation suggestions: improve driving efficiency and avoid traffic congestion.
- Vehicle autonomous driving assistance: reduces driving fatigue and improves safety.
- Customer service and communication: AI voice assistant helps handle passenger inquiries and complaints.
- Fleet management and scheduling: AI optimizes vehicle allocation, reducing empty mileage.

**Human moat:**

- The ability to make immediate judgments in response to unexpected road conditions and complex traffic environments.
- High emotional interaction and trust building with passengers and clients.
- Decisions involving legal liability (e.g., accident liability determination).
- Flexibility and adaptability for non-standard route adjustments.

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and troubleshooting of L2+ autonomous driving systems.
- Data analysis and fleet management software usage.
- Customer service and communication skills.
- Basic knowledge of vehicle electrical and sensor repair.
- Emergency driving and safety procedures training.

**Key growth areas:**

- Last-mile delivery
- Ride-hailing
- E-commerce logistics
- Urban mobility

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Enjoy flexible work and independent driving, adapt to variable scheduling
- Familiar with local road conditions, with strong service awareness

**Who is not suited**
- Dislikes long hours of sitting and traffic congestion
- Not suited for irregular schedules and client communication

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for taxi/truck drivers?**
A: Junior drivers earn approximately €25,000-30,000, while experienced drivers can earn €38,000-45,000, depending on the type of work and hours.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens immigrate to the Netherlands through the driver occupation?**
A: Regular drivers have difficulty obtaining work visas; can try self-employment visa or high-skilled migration (e.g., management). Note language and driver's license requirements.

**Q: What are the career advancement paths for drivers?**
A: Can advance from taxi driver to fleet dispatcher, transport manager, or start own business with multiple vehicles.
