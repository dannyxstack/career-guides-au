# Blacksmiths, Hammersmiths and Forging Press Workers — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 7221 (ISCO08)**

This occupation involves hand or machine forging of metal to produce tools, weapons, decorative items, etc. In the Netherlands, traditional blacksmiths often work on restoration and artwork, while industrial forging operators work with presses in manufacturing.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Blacksmiths and forgers face automation pressure, but traditional craftsmanship and custom work remain a human moat; AI can assist with design and process optimization, but physical operation and artistic creation are hard to fully replace.

Junior forging workers can advance to senior technician or supervisor; traditional blacksmiths can move into art restoration, custom forging; in the industrial sector, they can shift to CNC forging or mold design.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive metal forming operations (e.g., presses, hammering standardized parts)
- Simple Quality Inspection and Dimensional Measurement
- Basic material heating and cooling control
- Material handling and loading/unloading in industrial forging
- Simple adjustments of standard tools and molds

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Design complex forging molds and products using CAD/CAM software
- Optimizing forging temperature and pressure parameters through AI material simulation
- Predictive maintenance using smart sensors and data analysis
- AR-assisted fine manual forging and restoration guidance
- Digital rapid prototyping and rendering display of customized products

**Human moat:**

- Creative and aesthetic judgment in traditional handicrafts and artistic forging.
- Physical operation and adaptability in high-intensity, non-standard scenarios
- Fine work such as historical artifact restoration requires human experience and touch.
- Safety supervision and quality responsibility, especially for key structural components
- Building trust in customer relationships and customized communication

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM digital modeling and simulation
- Fundamentals of metal materials science and AI simulation tools
- Industrial robot operation and collaboration (e.g., forging robots)
- Use of data analysis and process monitoring systems
- Project management combining traditional craftsmanship with digitalization
- Customer communication and customized service skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Artisan revival
- Industrial automation
- Green energy components
- Heritage restoration

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Enjoys handcrafting and pays attention to detail
- Passionate about traditional crafts and metalworking
- Able to adapt to physical labor and high temperatures

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking high salaries or rapid career advancement
- Dislikes physical labour or repetitive work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a forge worker in the Netherlands?**
A: Entry-level approx. €25,000–€32,000, mid-level €32,000–€40,000, senior €40,000–€50,000 (annual salary before tax).

**Q: How can non-EU citizens immigrate to the Netherlands for forging work?**
A: The most common route is via the Highly Skilled Migrant visa, requiring employer sponsorship and meeting salary thresholds. Alternatively, you can first study (MBO) and then apply for the Orientation Year.
