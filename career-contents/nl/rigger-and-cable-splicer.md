# Rigger — career analysis · Netherlands

**Occupation code: 7215 (ISCO08)**

Responsible for installing, adjusting, and maintaining lifting equipment, cables, and rigging systems for construction, maritime, and industrial settings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Can progress from assistant to senior rigger, or move into management roles such as lifting supervisor or safety coordinator.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of load calculations and rigging configurations, optimizing lifting point positions using AI software.
- Automated generation and compliance check for lifting plans and risk assessment reports
- Equipment inspection and fault diagnosis based on sensors and drones, reducing manual inspections

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted lifting planning system improves design efficiency and safety for complex lifting plans
- Augmented reality (AR) headset overlays virtual rigging route for real-time installation guidance
- IoT sensors and digital twin technology for load monitoring and alerts, enhancing operator decision-making

**Human moat:**

- On-site physical connection and bundling operations, requiring hands-on ability and spatial awareness
- Quick judgment and manual emergency handling in critical situations, such as adjusting plans during sudden wind shifts
- Cross-trade coordination and communication, explaining technical details to other workers or supervisors
- Understand the structure and limits of non-standard, old equipment; AI models cannot cover all scenarios

**Skills to build:**

- Learn rigging design software or AI planning tools (e.g., 3D lift planning)
- Master basic IoT and sensor data analysis skills.
- Improve safety management knowledge, familiarise with interpreting AI-generated risk assessment reports
- Obtain certification for working at heights and operating heavy equipment.
- Participate in AR/VR simulation training to improve operational proficiency in complex environments.
- Learn the basics of digital twins and BIM to facilitate collaboration with engineers

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Port logistics
- Construction
- Industrial maintenance

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Netherlands admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy physical activity and outdoor work
- People who value safety standards and have strong hands-on abilities
- Open to individuals willing to work in special environments such as at heights or at sea.

**Who is not suited**
- People sensitive to heights or physically demanding tasks
- People who prefer offices or low-risk environments

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level of a rigger in the Netherlands?**
A: Annual junior income approximately €28,000-35,000, mid-senior up to €45,000-55,000, overtime and allowances extra.

**Q: Can foreign riggers work in the Netherlands through high-skilled migration pathways?**
A: Yes, if the annual salary meets the threshold, you can apply for a highly skilled migrant visa or EU Blue Card, with employer sponsorship.
