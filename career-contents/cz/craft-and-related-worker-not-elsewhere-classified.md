# Craft and Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

This residual ISCO-08 category covers craft workers in specialized trades not listed elsewhere, such as musical instrument makers, glass engravers, and restorers. In Czechia, these occupations often require vocational training and a trade license (živnostenský list). Employment is concentrated in small and medium enterprises, with a steady demand for high-quality craftsmanship in restoration, custom production, and niche manufacturing. The sector benefits from Czechia's strong tradition of skilled trades and tourism-related crafts.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 1,001. The Czech labour market shows stable demand for specialized craft workers, particularly in restoration of cultural heritage, custom furniture, and luxury goods. Demographic decline in younger generation entering trades poses a long-term challenge, but immigration from EU/EEA countries partly fills gaps. Automation has limited impact due to the hands-on nature of work. Growth is expected in eco-friendly crafts and artisanal food production. Overall, the outlook is moderate with opportunities for self-employment.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with apprenticeship (učiliště) or vocational school, leading to journeyman status. Many craft workers become self-employed or open their own workshops. Advanced training in master crafts (mistrovské zkoušky) allows for teaching or running a business. Some diversify into related fields like design or conservation. Entrepreneurship is common, as is seasonal work in tourism-related crafts (e.g., glassblowing). Networking within guilds and chambers of commerce is important.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised jewellery grinding and polishing
- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments
- Repeating pattern reproduction in hand carving
- Traditional mold making and casting processes
- Simple wood/stone cutting and shaping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Conceive personalized jewelry/musical instrument designs using AI design software
- Accelerated prototyping through 3D scanning and modeling
- Using AI quality inspection system to improve product precision
- Using AR/VR for complex assembly guidance
- Customized client communication and quoting via digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Highly customized artistic creation and aesthetic judgment
- Experience and tactile feel of handling rare materials
- On-site repair and adaptation of complex non-standard parts
- Cultural story interpretation through in-depth communication with clients
- Overall process control through multi-process integration

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM and 3D modeling (e.g., Rhino, Fusion 360)
- Digital fabrication equipment operator (3D printing, CNC engraving)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g. Midjourney, DALL-E for concept design)
- Materials science and sustainable process knowledge
- Digital customer management and custom platform operations
- Cross-sector collaboration and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- restoration
- custom production
- artisan food
- eco-crafts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a craft worker in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by specific craft and experience. Official data from the Czech Statistical Office shows average gross monthly wages for craft and related trades range from CZK 25,000 to 40,000, with higher earnings for master craftsmen and self-employed individuals. For precise figures, refer to the ISPV Regional Wage Survey.

**Q: Can a non-EU craft worker obtain an Employee Card for Czechia?**
A: Yes, if you have a firm job offer from a Czech employer and meet education/experience requirements. The Employee Card is a combined work and residence permit valid for up to 2 years (renewable). Some crafts may be exempt from labour market testing if listed on the shortage occupation list.

**Q: Is it required to speak Czech to work as a craft worker?**
A: Basic Czech is usually necessary for daily communication with customers and colleagues, especially in small businesses. However, in larger companies or tourist-oriented crafts, English may suffice initially. Learning Czech is strongly recommended for integration and trade licensing.
