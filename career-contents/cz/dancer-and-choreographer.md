# Dancer and Choreographer — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2653 (ISCO08)**

Dancers and choreographers in Czechia perform and create dance works across classical ballet, contemporary dance, and folk traditions. Employment is concentrated in state and municipal theatres, independent dance companies, and cultural festivals, primarily in Prague and Brno. Freelance and project-based work is common, with many artists supplementing income through teaching. The field is competitive and requires rigorous training, often from a young age at specialised conservatories or university dance departments.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with formal training at a conservatory or university, followed by entry into a professional company. Experienced dancers may advance to soloist or principal roles, then transition into choreography or teaching. Many combine performance with freelance teaching or administrative roles in cultural institutions. International mobility is high, especially within the EU, and networking is crucial for advancement.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for dancers in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by employer and region. Entry-level dancers in regional theatres earn around 25,000 CZK/month gross, while principal dancers in top Prague companies may earn up to 50,000 CZK/month. Freelancers have variable income.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to dance in Czechia?**
A: If you are an EU/EEA or Swiss citizen, you can work freely. Non-EU nationals need an Employee Card, which requires a job offer from a Czech employer and meeting general conditions (e.g., clean criminal record).

**Q: What training is required to become a professional dancer?**
A: Most professional dancers complete a 6-year conservatory program (equivalent to bachelor's) or a university bachelor's/master's in dance. Continuous training and auditions are essential.
