# Street and Related Services Worker — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 9510 (ISCO08)**

Street and Related Services Workers sell goods or provide services directly to the public on streets, markets, at special events, or door-to-door in Czechia. They include street vendors, market traders, and performers. Many are self-employed with a trade license, working in tourism-heavy areas, local markets, or festivals. The job requires physical stamina, basic math skills, and often language ability for tourist interactions. Employment is typically seasonal or project-based, with low barriers to entry but limited job security.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 372. Employment for street services in Czechia is expected to remain stable with slight growth tied to tourism and local events. However, e-commerce and supermarket expansion are reducing demand for traditional street vending. Seasonal and part-time opportunities persist, particularly in Prague, Brno, and spa towns. Self-employment remains common, but regulatory requirements (trade license, hygiene for food) and competition from digital platforms pose challenges. EU/EEA free movement ensures a steady supply of workers, but non-EU access is very restricted.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression is limited; most workers remain in the same role or move to retail or hospitality. With experience, some become supervisors of market stalls or start their own small businesses selling multiple products. Formal training is rare, but skills in customer service, inventory management, and basic finance can lead to supervisory roles in larger markets or event companies. The job is often a stepping stone for students or migrants.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

**Key growth areas:**

- tourism
- events
- local markets
- seasonal work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a street vendor in Czechia?**
A: Earnings vary widely: self-employed vendors may earn from CZK 10,000 to CZK 30,000 monthly depending on location, season, and product. Employees often receive minimum wage (CZK 18,900/month in 2025) plus commission. Official wage data is scarce due to informal work.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as street vendors in Czechia?**
A: It is very difficult. Self-employment requires a trade license and a long-term residence permit for business purposes, which has strict conditions (e.g., proof of sufficient funds and business plan). Employment as a street worker rarely meets the Employee Card salary threshold, so most non-EU workers are not eligible.

**Q: What career progression exists for street service workers?**
A: Limited. With experience, some become market managers or start their own vending business. A few move into retail store management after gaining sales skills. Formal training in business or hospitality can help, but many remain in the role long-term.
