# Physiotherapists — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Czechia specialize in assessing, planning, and implementing rehabilitation programs for patients suffering from physical injuries or disabilities. The profession is in high demand due to an aging population, increased emphasis on non-invasive treatments, and a growing sports culture. Both public healthcare and private clinics offer employment opportunities, with a strong presence in cities like Prague, Brno, and Ostrava. The field is regulated, requiring professional recognition for EU/EEA and third-country nationals alike.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression for physiotherapists in Czechia typically begins with a bachelor's degree and registration. Early-career professionals work in hospitals or rehabilitation centers, with opportunities to specialize in orthopedics, neurology, or pediatrics. Experienced physiotherapists may open private practices or advance to management roles in healthcare organizations. Some pursue master's degrees to teach or conduct research. Continuous professional development is required to maintain licensure and stay updated with new techniques.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient record organization and electronic health record entry
- Scoring and data analysis of standardized assessment scales.
- Generate routine reports on treatment progress
- Administrative tasks such as appointment scheduling and patient reminders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assists in analyzing patient movement data to optimize personalized treatment plans
- Utilize virtual reality technology for remote rehabilitation guidance and monitoring

**Human moat:**

- Professional judgment in interdisciplinary team collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a physiotherapist in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and sector. Entry-level public hospital positions may start around 30,000 CZK monthly, while private practice or specialized roles can exceed 60,000 CZK. For official data, consult the ISPV Regional Wage Survey.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work as a physiotherapist in Czechia?**
A: EU/EEA and Swiss citizens do not need a visa. Non-EU nationals require an Employee Card or EU Blue Card, plus professional recognition of qualifications. The employer must sponsor the visa.

**Q: What education is required to become a physiotherapist in Czechia?**
A: A bachelor's degree in physiotherapy from a recognized university is mandatory. EU degrees are automatically recognized; non-EU degrees require a recognition procedure by the Ministry of Health.
