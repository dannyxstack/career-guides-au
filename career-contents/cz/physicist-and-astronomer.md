# Physicist and Astronomer — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2111 (ISCO08)**

Physicists and astronomers in Czechia work in research institutions, universities, and R&D departments of private companies, focusing on fundamental and applied physics, astrophysics, and related fields. The country has a strong tradition in nuclear physics, astronomy, and materials science, with key employers including the Czech Academy of Sciences, Charles University, and industrial R&D centers. Opportunities also exist in data science and technology transfer, leveraging analytical skills.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a PhD and postdoctoral positions, leading to senior researcher or group leader roles in academia. In industry, physicists can advance to R&D management, project lead, or specialized consultant positions. Czechia offers a clear path from junior to senior researcher at institutions like the Czech Academy of Sciences, with opportunities to secure tenure or head research groups. Some professionals transition into entrepreneurship or tech startups.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physicist in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience: entry-level researchers earn around 35,000–45,000 CZK gross monthly, while senior physicists can earn 70,000+ CZK. Industry roles in R&D or data science often pay above average.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as a physicist in Czechia?**
A: EU/EEA citizens need no visa. Non-EU nationals typically require an Employee Card (with a job offer) or an EU Blue Card (if they meet the salary and education criteria). The EU Blue Card is recommended for highly qualified physicists.
