# Midwifery Professional — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2222 (ISCO08)**

Midwifery professionals in Czechia provide essential maternal and newborn care, working in hospitals, clinics, birth centers, and private practice. They support women throughout pregnancy, labor, and postpartum, as well as offering family planning advice. With a strong emphasis on patient-centered care and collaboration with obstetricians, midwives are integral to the healthcare system. Czechia's healthcare sector faces staffing challenges, particularly in rural areas, creating steady demand for qualified midwives. The profession requires a university degree, state registration, and continuous professional development. EU/EEA nationals can work freely, while non-EU professionals need to obtain recognition of qualifications and a work permit.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a midwifery professional in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and sector. Entry-level midwives in hospitals earn around 30,000–40,000 CZK monthly, while senior or private-practice midwives can earn 50,000–70,000 CZK. EU Blue Card thresholds are rarely met. For precise figures, consult the ISPV Regional Wage Survey.

**Q: What are the work visa requirements for non-EU midwives in Czechia?**
A: Non-EU midwives need an Employee Card, which requires a job offer from a Czech employer, recognition of their midwifery qualification by the Ministry of Health (nostrification), and Czech language proficiency at C1 level. The process can take 6–12 months. EU/EEA nationals do not need a visa.

**Q: How can I get my midwifery qualifications recognized in Czechia?**
A: Recognition (nostrification) involves submitting your diploma and transcripts to the Ministry of Health, paying a fee (approx. 1,500–3,000 CZK), and possibly taking a state exam or supplementary training. For EU/EEA diplomas, recognition is smoother under EU directives. Non-EU diplomas require full evaluation.
