# Insurance Representative — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 3321 (ISCO08)**

Insurance representatives in Czechia sell and manage life, non-life, and investment insurance products for individuals and businesses. They work for insurance companies, banks, or independent agencies, advising clients on coverage and handling claims. The role requires strong communication, sales skills, and knowledge of insurance regulations. The Czech insurance market is mature but evolving with digitalization, bancassurance, and new risk products. Employment is concentrated in Prague, Brno, and regional centers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior agent handling simple products, advancing to senior representative or team leader managing complex portfolios. Many move into underwriting, claims, or risk management. Professional certifications (e.g., from the Czech Insurance Association) enhance mobility. Some representatives become independent brokers or open their own agencies. Further education in finance or economics can lead to roles in bancassurance or corporate risk consulting. The path is linear but benefits from sales performance and specialization.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for insurance representatives in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and sales performance. Entry-level representatives earn around 30,000–40,000 CZK monthly gross, while experienced agents can earn 50,000–80,000 CZK plus commissions. Earnings may exceed 100,000 CZK for top performers in bancassurance or corporate insurance.

**Q: What visa do non-EU insurance representatives need to work in Czechia?**
A: Non-EU nationals require an Employee Card if they have a job offer. The employer must prove the position could not be filled locally (labour market test). Alternatively, an Intra-Company Transfer applies for multinational firms. EU/EEA nationals only need to register residence.

**Q: Is a university degree mandatory to become an insurance representative in Czechia?**
A: No, a secondary vocational education is sufficient, but a bachelor's degree in economics or finance can improve job prospects. Licensing from ČNB is mandatory for selling certain products, regardless of degree.
