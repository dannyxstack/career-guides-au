# University and Higher Education Teacher — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2310 (ISCO08)**

University and higher education teachers in Czechia deliver lectures, seminars, and tutorials at universities and other tertiary institutions. They also conduct research, publish academic findings, and supervise students' thesis work. The profession requires a doctoral degree (Ph.D.) in most cases, with strong command of both Czech and English. Czech universities increasingly recruit international faculty to support English-taught programmes and internationalisation efforts, especially in fields like STEM, economics, and social sciences. Employment is typically permanent after a probation period, but competition for tenure-track positions is high.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 31,395. The Czech government's higher education strategy (2021-2030) emphasises internationalisation, digitalisation, and applied research, which is expected to sustain demand for qualified academics. However, budget constraints may slow hiring in some public universities. The retirement of a significant cohort of professors (many over 55) opens opportunities for early-career academics. Fields with strong industry linkages (e.g., IT, engineering) offer better prospects, while humanities might face stagnant demand. EU funding schemes (e.g., Horizon Europe) also support research positions.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an assistant professor (odborný asistent), advancing to associate professor (docent) and full professor (profesor) via habilitation and professorial appointment processes. The habilitation (docentship) requires a research track record and a public lecture. Many academics also take on administrative roles or lead research groups. The system values publishing in high-impact journals and securing grants. Mobility between universities is common. Tenure is not automatic; performance evaluations matter. Some transition to private sector or leadership roles in R&D.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate course outlines and lecture drafts
- Basic Q&A and automated responses to common questions
- Preliminary grading and feedback on student assignments
- Literature review and data collation
- Administrative tasks (e.g., class scheduling, grade entry)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted personalised learning path design and adaptive assessment
- Use LLMs to quickly generate teaching cases and simulated discussions
- Assist with hypothesis testing, data analysis, and paper polishing in research
- Virtual classrooms and collaborative teaching with AI teaching assistants
- Knowledge graph construction and interdisciplinary curriculum planning

**Human moat:**

- Face-to-face mentorship and emotional support
- In-depth explanation of complex concepts and stimulation of critical thinking
- Formulation of original research questions and method design
- Ethical judgment and academic decision-making
- Creative integration in overall curriculum design

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI education tools (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning).
- Data analysis and statistical modeling (Python/R)
- Prompt engineering and large model fine-tuning
- Blended instructional design (MOOC/flipped classroom)
- Academic writing and AI-assisted polishing
- Data Privacy and AI Ethics

**Key growth areas:**

- Internationalisation
- Digital pedagogy
- STEM hiring
- Applied research

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a university teacher in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by rank (assistant, associate, full professor) and university. Entry-level assistant professors earn around 40,000–55,000 CZK monthly; full professors up to 80,000+ CZK. Additional income from research grants is common. Consult the latest ISPV wage surveys for precise data.

**Q: Can a non-EU national become a university teacher in Czechia?**
A: Yes. They need a valid job offer and a visa. The Employee Card is standard; for higher-ranked positions meeting the salary threshold, the EU Blue Card offers advantages. The employer must prove the position cannot be filled locally.

**Q: Do I need to speak Czech to teach at a Czech university?**
A: Not necessarily for English-taught programmes, but knowledge of Czech is an advantage for administration and integration. Some universities offer language support.
