# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners are responsible for cleaning windows, glass surfaces, and facades of residential, commercial, and industrial buildings. In Czechia, the occupation is primarily driven by the construction and real estate sectors, with steady demand from office buildings, hotels, and shopping centers. The work often involves using ladders, scaffolding, or hydraulic lifts, and requires physical fitness and comfort with heights. While many window cleaners are self-employed or work for small cleaning firms, larger companies and facility management firms also employ them. The job typically requires no formal education, but on-the-job training and safety certifications are common.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a window cleaner in Czechia?**
A: Salaries for window cleaners in Czechia vary by experience and employer, but are generally in line with the national average for cleaning occupations. Hourly rates are common, and wages can be higher for specialized high-rise work or during peak seasons. Exact figures depend on the region and company.

**Q: Can a non-EU national become a window cleaner in Czechia?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals can apply for an Employee Card if they have a job offer from a Czech employer. However, window cleaning is not considered a skilled occupation, so the application may face higher scrutiny and require showing that the position could not be filled by an EU citizen. It is advisable to consult with a lawyer.

**Q: Is window cleaning a regulated profession in Czechia?**
A: No, window cleaning is not a regulated profession in Czechia. No specific license is required, but safety training and certificates are highly recommended, especially for working at heights. Employers often provide on-the-job training.
