# Vocational Education Teacher — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2320 (ISCO08)**

Vocational education teachers in Czechia instruct students in secondary vocational schools and higher vocational institutions, combining theoretical knowledge with practical skills. They play a key role in dual education systems, liaising with employers to align curricula. Demand is steady due to an aging teacher workforce and emphasis on technical fields. Czech language fluency and recognized pedagogical qualifications are essential.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from teacher to senior teacher, department head, or school management (e.g., deputy headmaster). Experienced teachers may mentor new staff or train apprentices in companies. Continuing professional development is mandatory, often through state-ponsored courses. Some transition to adult education or inspectorate roles. Salaries increase with years of service, but caps exist.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generates standardized lesson plans and exam questions.
- Automatically grading multiple-choice questions and simple assignments

- Provide after-school tutoring through chatbots

**Human moat:**

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a vocational education teacher in Czechia?**
A: Salaries are based on the public sector tariff system. A starting teacher earns around 30,000–35,000 CZK monthly gross, rising with years of experience to 40,000–50,000 CZK. Additional pay for specializations or school management can increase this.

**Q: Can a non-EU national become a vocational education teacher in Czechia?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. They need a job offer, recognized Czech-equivalent qualifications, and Czech language proficiency (at least B2, often C1). The Employee Card route applies, with a labor market test. EU/EEA citizens face no such restrictions.

**Q: Do I need a teaching degree to teach vocational subjects?**
A: Not necessarily. A master's degree in the field (e.g., engineering) plus a pedagogical qualification (1-year program) is sufficient. The pedagogical qualification can be completed part-time while working.
