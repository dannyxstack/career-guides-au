# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Workers — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

Inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Czechia are primarily engaged in fish farming, pond management, and commercial fishing in reservoirs and ponds, especially in South Bohemia. The sector is traditional and small-scale, with a focus on carp and other freshwater species. Workers perform tasks such as feeding, harvesting, maintaining equipment, and monitoring water quality. The industry is regulated by EU quotas and environmental policies, and employs a limited but stable workforce.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from entry-level worker, advancing to experienced fisherman or pond master, and potentially to self-employed fish farmer or cooperative manager. Additional qualifications in aquaculture management or fish processing can lead to higher roles. The path is linear with limited vertical mobility, but experienced workers are valued for their practical skills.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- aquaculture
- sustainable fishing
- fish processing
- pond management

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for fishery workers in Czechia?**
A: Salaries in this sector are generally below the national average, varying by region and experience. Entry-level workers earn around the minimum wage, while experienced professionals may earn moderately more. Exact figures depend on employer and location.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as fishery workers in Czechia?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals can apply for an Employee Card with a job offer. However, because the occupation is not on the shortage list, a labor market test is required to prove no local candidate is available. The process can be lengthy.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for fishery workers?**
A: With experience and additional qualifications, workers can become pond managers, supervisors, or start their own fish farming business. Further education in aquaculture management can open up roles in research or administration.
