# Precision-instrument Maker and Repairer — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 7311 (ISCO08)**

Precision-instrument makers and repairers in Czechia manufacture, assemble, calibrate, and repair high-precision measuring, optical, and electromechanical instruments used in industries such as automotive, aerospace, medical devices, and scientific research. The role demands meticulous attention to detail and a strong understanding of mechanical and electronic systems.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational apprenticeship leading to a certified tradesperson. With experience, individuals can advance to specialist roles in calibration, quality control, or supervision. Further education can open doors to engineering positions, and experienced professionals may become independent contractors or open their own repair workshops.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repeated part dimension measurement and recording (replaced by AI vision and automated instruments)
- Calibration process for standard instruments (taken over by AI automatic calibration algorithms)
- Remote diagnosis of common faults (AI knowledge base provides solutions)
- Inventory management and material procurement (optimised by AI prediction systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis (AI-generated fault trees and historical cases)
- Fine-tuning decisions in precision adjustment (AI provides real-time compensation suggestions)
- Customized instrument design (AI-assisted 3D modeling and tolerance analysis)
- Repair plan optimization (AI compares efficiency and cost of multiple approaches)
- Multi-domain knowledge quick retrieval (AI integrating optics, mechanics, electronics materials)

**Human moat:**

- Manual finishing and tactile feedback (e.g., micron-level polishing, balance tuning)
- Repair of non-standard/antique instruments (no blueprints, no spare parts)
- Communicate with clients to understand custom requirements and translate them into technical solutions
- On-site emergency repairs (decision-making in uncontrollable environments)

**Skills to build:**

- Using AI-assisted fault diagnosis tools (e.g., image-based inspection systems)
- CNC machine and 3D printing operation (for manufacturing replacement parts)
- Digital measurement and reverse engineering software (e.g., CAD, laser scanning).
- IoT sensor and smart instrument debugging.
- Python scripts (automating repetitive data collection)
- Client communication and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- automotive
- medical devices
- optics
- precision engineering

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a precision-instrument maker in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, sector, and region, but are generally above the national average for manual trades. High-tech manufacturing hubs like Prague and Brno offer higher pay, and experienced professionals often receive bonuses. The occupation is also eligible for government incentives in shortage areas.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as precision-instrument makers in Czechia?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens can apply for an Employee Card if they have a job offer. The employer must demonstrate a labour market test. EU Blue Card is possible for those with high qualifications or salary. Intra-company transfers are also an option. The occupation is on the shortage list, which can expedite processing.
