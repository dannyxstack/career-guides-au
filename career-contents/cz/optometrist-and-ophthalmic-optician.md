# Optometrist and Ophthalmic Optician — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2267 (ISCO08)**

Optometrists and ophthalmic opticians in Czechia are healthcare professionals specializing in eye examinations, vision correction, and dispensing of optical aids. With an aging population and increased screen use, demand for their services is steady. The profession is regulated, requiring specific education and registration. Most practitioners work in optical stores, clinics, or hospitals. The field offers a balance of clinical and customer-facing work.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves gaining experience as a staff optometrist, then moving to managerial roles in optical chains or opening a private practice. Specialization in areas like pediatric optometry, contact lenses, or low vision can enhance career prospects. Continuing education is important to keep up with new technologies. Some optometrists transition into teaching or research at universities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use auto-refractor for objective refraction measurement
- AI analyzes corneal topography data to generate preliminary lens fitting plans.
- Inventory lens parameter matching and automated order processing

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis of eye abnormalities (e.g., diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Optimize patient examination and glasses dispensing workflows through digital processes
- Virtual try-on technology improves contact lens fitting efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Requires clinical license, responsible for diagnosis and prescription
- Clinical judgment when managing patients with complex strabismus, amblyopia, etc.
- Skills such as manually adjusting frames and edging lenses
- Building long-term patient trust and personalized service

**Skills to build:**

- Learning to operate digital optometric equipment and AI-assisted diagnostic systems
- Master the use of remote eye testing platforms
- Improve communication and customer management skills
- Learn basic data analysis to interpret AI reports
- Track new eye health technologies (e.g., OCT interpretation).
- Strengthen personalized eyewear fitting and problem-solving skills.

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Digital eye strain
- Contact lenses
- Pediatric optometry

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an optometrist in Czechia?**
A: The median gross annual salary for optometrists in Czechia is around 420,000 CZK, with experienced practitioners earning up to 600,000 CZK. Salaries vary by region and employer.

**Q: Can non-EU optometrists work in Czechia?**
A: Yes, they can apply for an Employee Card or EU Blue Card after securing a job offer and having their qualifications recognized by the Ministry of Health. The process may take several months.

**Q: What education is required to become an optometrist in Czechia?**
A: A Bachelor's degree in Optometry (3 years) from a recognized university, or a secondary vocational diploma in ophthalmic optics (4 years) for optician roles. Both require state registration.
