# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2230 (ISCO08)**

Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Czechia practice non-conventional healing methods such as acupuncture, herbal medicine, and traditional Chinese medicine. They work in private clinics, wellness centers, or integrate into mainstream healthcare. The profession is recognized but regulated, requiring certification or a medical degree for certain practices. Demand is rising due to growing interest in holistic health and wellness tourism.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves building a client base and specialization (e.g., acupuncture, herbalism). Some advance to teaching at private institutes or collaborating with conventional healthcare providers. Continuing education is key, as regulations evolve. Experience can lead to clinic ownership or roles in health tourism. However, formal recognition of qualifications from abroad may require additional exams or bridging courses.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.

- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professional in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by practice type and client base. According to official statistics, median monthly gross wage for healthcare professionals in similar roles is around 40,000–60,000 CZK, but self-employed practitioners may earn more or less. Exact figures depend on specialization and location.

**Q: Can a non-EU traditional medicine practitioner obtain a work visa for Czechia?**
A: Yes, through the Employee Card if they have a job offer and their qualifications are recognized by the Czech Ministry of Health. The EU Blue Card is an option for those with a higher education degree and a salary above the threshold. Recognition of credentials is a key step.

**Q: Is traditional medicine officially regulated in Czechia?**
A: Yes, certain practices like acupuncture are regulated and require certification from the Czech Medical Society. General practice of traditional healing without a medical degree may be restricted. It is advisable to check with the Ministry of Health.
