# Structural Metal Preparers and Erectors — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 7214 (ISCO08)**

Structural metal preparers and erectors in Czechia are skilled tradespeople who assemble, fit, and erect steel frameworks for buildings, bridges, and industrial structures. They interpret blueprints, cut and weld metal components, and use cranes to position beams. With a strong construction sector and growing infrastructure projects, demand remains steady. Workers often specialize in welding or crane operation and must adhere to strict safety standards. The role requires physical fitness, teamwork, and precision.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational apprenticeship in metalworking or welding. Experienced workers can advance to foreman, site supervisor, or specialized roles in precision welding or non-destructive testing. Additional certifications (e.g., international welding certificates) boost mobility and pay. Some move into project management or start their own contracting business. The trade offers stable employment with opportunities for skill upgrading.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Precise dimensional measurement and calibration using BIM and laser scanning
- Optimize cutting and welding parameters for steel structures using AI software
- Drones automatically check structural alignment and bolt tightening status

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Augmented Reality (AR)-assisted installation guidance and training

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a structural metal preparer and erector in Czechia?**
A: The gross monthly salary typically ranges from 30,000 to 45,000 CZK (approx. 1,200–1,800 EUR), depending on experience, location, and specialization (e.g., welding). Overtime and bonuses can increase earnings.

**Q: Do I need a visa to work in Czechia as a structural metal preparer?**
A: If you are a non-EU/EEA national, you need an Employee Card (work permit). This occupation is on the shortage list, which facilitates the process. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens can work freely without a visa.

**Q: Can I get certified in welding while working?**
A: Yes, many employers sponsor welding certification courses (e.g., EN 287-1). These typically last a few weeks and can be taken at vocational schools or private institutes. Company-sponsored training is common.
