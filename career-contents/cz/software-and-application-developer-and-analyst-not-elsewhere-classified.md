## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Typical career progression starts from junior developer, advancing to senior, then to roles like software architect, technical lead, or engineering manager. Specialization in areas such as machine learning, DevOps, or cybersecurity can lead to higher salaries and senior positions. Many professionals also transition into freelance or remote work.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Writing boilerplate code and repetitive CRUD operations
- Basic unit testing and debugging
- Data cleaning and simple report generation
- Standardised checks in code review
- Documentation writing and maintenance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI-assisted system architecture design and code optimization
- Use AI tools for rapid prototyping and idea validation
- Enhance data analysis and model iteration through AI
- AI-driven test generation and coverage analysis
- Leveraging AI to empower customer needs analysis and solution design

**Human moat:**

- Complex system architecture and domain modeling skills
- Cross-departmental collaboration and communication skills
- Creative problem solving and algorithmic innovation
- Deep understanding and translation of business logic
- Ethics, safety, and compliance decision-making

**Skills to build:**

- Mastering large language model (LLM) fine-tuning and prompt engineering
- Understanding MLOps and building AI model deployment pipelines
- Strengthening software architecture design and design patterns
- Enhance agile development and DevOps practices
- Learn data engineering and cloud-native technologies

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a software developer in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and location. A mid-level developer (3-5 years) typically earns between 600,000 and 900,000 CZK annually pre-tax. Senior developers and specialists can earn over 1,200,000 CZK. Prague salaries are generally higher.

**Q: Which visa should I apply for as a non-EU software developer?**
A: The most common is the Employee Card, which requires a job offer from a Czech employer. For those with a degree or high experience, the EU Blue Card offers faster processing and family reunification. Intra-company transfer is an option if moving within a multinational.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for software developers in Czechia?**
A: Developers can progress from junior to senior roles, then to software architect, team lead, or CTO. Specializations in AI, cloud, or cybersecurity open up higher-level positions. Many also transition into freelance or start their own software companies.
