# Journalist — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

Journalists in Czechia research, write, and present news and feature stories across print, broadcast, and digital media. The occupation spans reporters, editors, correspondents, and multimedia content creators employed by media houses, news agencies, or as freelancers. Prague is the primary hub, with regional outlets also hiring.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression often moves from junior reporter to specialist correspondent, editor, or news manager. Many journalists in Czechia build portfolios by freelancing or moving between outlets. Further specialization in business, politics, or culture, or transitions to PR and communications, are common paths.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standard news briefs (e.g., financial reports, sports scores)
- Speech-to-text and automatic subtitle generation
- Fact-checking and data reconciliation (e.g., verifying statistical data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted data analysis and visualization (quickly identify trends)
- AI-recommended topics and audience preference prediction
- AI compiles multilingual information summaries

**Human moat:**

- Exclusive interviews and in-depth investigations (interpersonal insight and trust)
- Ethical judgment and sensitive issue gatekeeping
- Creative storytelling (features, commentary)

**Skills to build:**

- Data journalism (Python/R/data visualization)
- Multimedia production (video/podcast editing)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT-assisted writing)
- Fact-checking and information verification
- Audience Analysis and Digital Marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Multimedia journalism
- Investigative reporting
- Data journalism
- Digital media

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for journalists in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience and media type. Entry-level reporters earn around 350,000–500,000 CZK/year, while senior editors at major outlets can exceed 800,000 CZK/year. Freelance income is less predictable.

**Q: Can a non-EU journalist get a work visa for Czechia?**
A: Yes, via the Employee Card with a job offer. The employer must prove the position cannot be filled locally. If the role is highly skilled (e.g., data journalist), an EU Blue Card may be possible.

**Q: Is Czech language proficiency required to work as a journalist in Czechia?**
A: Yes, for most local news roles; fluency in Czech is essential. However, English-language media (e.g., Prague Morning) may hire native English speakers without Czech proficiency.
