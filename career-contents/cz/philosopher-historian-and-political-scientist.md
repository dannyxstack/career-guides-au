# Philosophers, Historians and Political Scientists — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2633 (ISCO08)**

Philosophers, historians, and political scientists in Czechia are primarily employed in academic research, higher education, and public policy organizations, with a smaller presence in media, think tanks, and NGOs. The labour market is competitive, with most positions requiring advanced degrees (at least Master's, often PhD) and fluency in Czech for local roles. International researchers may find opportunities at universities or institutes, especially in English-language programs. Employment is concentrated in Prague, Brno, and Olomouc, with stable demand linked to state-funded research and EU projects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very high (★★★★½).**

Career progression in Czechia typically follows an academic path: from research assistant or PhD candidate to assistant professor, then associate professor (habilitation) and full professor. Public administration and policy roles offer advancement to senior analyst or director positions. Knowledge of Czech is crucial for most non-international roles. Postdoc positions abroad are common before returning to Czechia. Networking within Czech academic societies and EU research networks is important.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Basic literature search and organization.
- Repeat delivery of standard course content
- Simple data analysis and statistical report generation
- Academic citation and format checking
- Data collection work of junior research assistants

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a political scientist in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely: entry-level researchers may earn around 350,000–500,000 CZK annually, while senior professors or policy directors can earn up to 1,200,000 CZK. Most positions fall in the 500,000–900,000 CZK range.

**Q: Can a non-EU philosopher get a work visa for Czechia?**
A: Yes, via an Employee Card or EU Blue Card if you have a job offer. The EU Blue Card requires a salary of at least 1.5x the national average (approx. 1,350,000 CZK/year in 2025) and a recognized degree. The Employee Card has no salary minimum but takes longer.

**Q: Do I need to speak Czech to work as a historian in Czechia?**
A: For most positions, yes. While some international research projects or English-language programs at universities may hire without Czech, fluency is often required for teaching, archival work, and integration into the academic community.
