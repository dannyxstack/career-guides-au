# Domestic Housekeeper — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 5152 (ISCO08)**

Domestic housekeepers in Czechia provide cleaning, tidying, and basic household maintenance services in private homes. Often employed through agencies or directly by households, this role is essential for maintaining hygienic living conditions. While many positions are informal, formal employment is growing due to stricter immigration rules and demand from dual-income families. The job requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and reliability. Czechia's tight labour market pushes some households to hire non-EU workers, but regulations limit this to lower-skill permits.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 7,168. Employment outlook for domestic housekeepers is stable but modest. With an aging population and increasing female labour participation, demand for paid domestic help is rising. However, automation (e.g., robot vacuums) may reduce some tasks. The role is rarely a path to long-term residency, as Czechia's migration system prioritises skilled workers. Shortages are reported in Prague and larger cities, but wages remain low, discouraging Czech nationals. Growth areas include eco-friendly cleaning and specialized services for elderly or disabled clients.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited; domestic housekeepers typically move into supervisory roles in cleaning agencies, start their own small business, or transition to hotel housekeeping for better benefits. On-the-job training is common, and experience can lead to higher-paying private clients. Formal certifications (e.g., 'Profesionální úklid') exist but are not mandatory. Some workers shift to commercial cleaning, which offers more hours and stability. The sector has high turnover, creating opportunities for reliable workers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Vacuuming/mopping floors (replaced by robotic vacuums/mops)
- Dishwashing (replaced by dishwashers)
- Bed making, clothing folding (replaced by smart bed-making robots/folding machines)
- Regular surface dust removal (replaced by automated dusting robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling tools to plan cleaning routes and task priorities
- Monitoring appliance status and scheduling repairs using smart home systems
- Remote control of cleaning equipment via voice assistant or APP
- Use data analytics to optimize cleaning supplies inventory and procurement

**Human moat:**

- Handle complex cleaning issues such as accidental damage and special material stains
- Communicate with employers about customized service needs and build trust
- Manage multiple smart devices and coordinate troubleshooting.
- Engaging in fine manual work (e.g., antique furniture maintenance)

**Skills to build:**

- Smart home device operation and basic troubleshooting
- Basic data analysis (supply inventory management)
- Customer Service and Communication Skills
- Time management and multitasking coordination
- Knowledge of eco-friendly cleaning technologies

**Key growth areas:**

- eco-friendly cleaning
- elderly home services
- Prague high-end households
- short-term rental turnover

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a domestic housekeeper in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, but typical gross hourly wages are 130–180 CZK, resulting in a monthly full-time equivalent of 20,000–28,000 CZK. Prague rates are higher, around 150–200 CZK per hour. Tipped income from private clients is common but not always reported.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work permit for domestic housekeeping?**
A: It is difficult but possible via an Employee Card if the employer proves a shortage of Czech/EU workers. The job is not on the official shortage list, and low wages may not meet income thresholds. Those from non-EU countries usually prefer other routes.
