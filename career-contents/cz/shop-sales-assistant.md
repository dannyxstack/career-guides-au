# Shop Sales Assistants — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 5223 (ISCO08)**

Shop sales assistants in Czechia assist customers in retail stores, handling sales, inventory, and customer service. The occupation is widespread across the country, with many positions in supermarkets, department stores, and specialty shops. It is a common entry-level job, often part-time or temporary, with high turnover. The sector employs a significant number of women and students. While EU/EEA nationals can work freely, non-EU/EEA workers face restrictions due to the low-skilled nature of the role. Wages are typically near the minimum wage, and advancement opportunities are limited without further education or training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression for shop sales assistants in Czechia is limited without further qualifications. Some may advance to supervisory roles (e.g., department manager) or specialize in visual merchandising or procurement. Others move into retail management after completing vocational training or higher education. The role often serves as a stepping stone for students or those seeking flexible work. Long-term careers are possible in larger retail chains with internal promotion paths, but most positions are entry-level with high turnover.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shop sales assistant in Czechia?**
A: Salaries for shop sales assistants are generally low, often around the minimum wage (approximately 200,000–240,000 CZK per year pre-tax). Variations depend on region and employer.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA national work as a shop sales assistant in Czechia?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not considered skilled. An Employee Card may be issued only if the employer demonstrates no suitable candidate from Czechia or the EU/EEA, which is rarely successful for this role.

**Q: What career advancement options exist?**
A: With experience, you can become a shift supervisor, department manager, or move into retail buying. Further vocational training or a higher education degree in business can open doors to higher-level retail management.
