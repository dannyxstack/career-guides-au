# Dentist — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2261 (ISCO08)**

Dentists diagnose, treat, and prevent oral diseases and deformities. In Czechia, they work primarily in private practices or public healthcare facilities. The profession requires a six-year Master's degree in dental medicine, followed by a licensing exam. Demand remains high due to an aging workforce and increased focus on oral health, with many opportunities in rural areas.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 9,840. The employment outlook for dentists in Czechia is positive. An aging population and a significant share of dentists nearing retirement create steady demand. New graduates and foreign-trained professionals are needed, especially in underserved regions. However, competition in major cities like Prague is moderate. The profession benefits from stable public funding and growing private insurance coverage.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically moves from general practice to specialization (e.g., orthodontics, oral surgery) or to owning a private clinic. Many dentists engage in continuous education and may take on academic roles. Some join larger dental chains as clinical directors. The path is straightforward but requires dedication to ongoing training and business acumen for those opening their own practice.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening and anomaly marking of X-rays and CT scans (AI reading)
- Administrative paperwork such as patient appointment, billing, and medical record organization
- Standardized preventive procedures like teeth cleaning and fluoride application (automated dental chair)
- Basic intraoral scanning and digital model generation
- Automated review and filling of insurance claim documents

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnostic systems provide treatment suggestions, improving diagnosis accuracy
- Simulate surgical paths via digital twin to optimize implant/orthodontic planning
- Real-time voice recording and medical record generation, freeing doctor's attention
- Personalized prevention plan recommendations based on patient historical data
- Virtual reality training systems enhance complex surgical skills

**Human moat:**

- Complex Surgical Decisions and Manual Operations (e.g., Root Canals, Tooth Extractions)
- Patient communication, empathy, and behaviour management (especially for children/anxious patients)
- Interdisciplinary Comprehensive Diagnosis (e.g., Oral-Systemic Disease Links)
- Emergency management and clinical judgement for sudden conditions
- Professional licensing and legal liability

**Skills to build:**

- AI-assisted Diagnostic Tool Operation and Result Interpretation
- Digital oral scanning and 3D printing design
- Patient interaction via telehealth platforms
- Basic data analysis and clinical research
- Patient communication and digital health education
- Continuous learning of automated workflow integration

**Key growth areas:**

- geriatric dentistry
- cosmetic dentistry
- dental implants
- digital dentistry

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for dentists in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience, location, and specialization. According to official statistics, average gross monthly salary is among the highest in healthcare, with significant potential for income growth through private practice or specialization.

**Q: Can a non-EU dentist work in Czechia?**
A: Yes, non-EU dentists can work in Czechia if they have a job offer and obtain either an Employee Card or EU Blue Card. They must also have their qualifications recognized by the Ministry of Health and prove Czech language proficiency at B2 level.

**Q: Do I need to pass an exam to practice dentistry in Czechia?**
A: Yes, after completing an accredited dental medicine program, you must pass a licensing exam administered by the Czech Medical Chamber (ČLK). Foreign-trained dentists may need to take supplementary exams as part of the recognition process.
