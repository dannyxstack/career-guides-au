# Stonemason, Stone Cutter, Splitter and Carver — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 7113 (ISCO08)**

Stonemasons, stone cutters, splitters and carvers in Czechia work with natural stone to create, restore, and maintain buildings, monuments, and sculptures, blending traditional craftsmanship with modern techniques. This skilled trade is essential for preserving Czech cultural heritage, especially in historic cities like Prague, Český Krumlov, and Kutná Hora, where restoration projects are ongoing.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,552. Employment outlook for stonemasons in Czechia is stable, driven by a strong focus on heritage conservation and high-quality construction. Demand is steady but not rapidly growing, with opportunities mainly in restoration and bespoke stonework. However, an aging workforce and limited influx of young apprentices may create niche shortages in specialized areas like cathedral restoration or intricate carving. Overall, job prospects are moderate, with competition higher in urban centers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for stonemasons in Czechia typically starts with a 3-year apprenticeship at a vocational school (SOU), leading to a certificate (výuční list). Many then work as employees in construction or monument care, with paths to become a foreman, master craftsman, or self-employed specialist. Further specialization in stone carving, tombstone making, or heritage restoration can enhance earning potential and job security. Continuous learning through courses from the Chamber of Commerce or heritage institutes is valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for stone cutting and layout design
- Operating automatic stone cutting machines and CNC engraving machines
- Material quantity calculation and layout using BIM models
- Use robots for repetitive stone handling and laying

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using 3D scanning and digital twins for precise stone structure installation
- Use AR glasses to overlay design drawings on construction sites, reducing errors
- Using AI to optimize stone splicing plans, improving aesthetics and strength
- Conduct drone inspections of large stone structures and remotely monitor maintenance needs.

**Human moat:**

- Artistic judgment for complex stone carving and hand modeling
- Adaptive skills for integrated on-site stone and structure installation
- Empirical judgment of stone texture, color, and physical properties
- Ability to communicate customized design solutions with clients

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/BIM software operation (e.g., AutoCAD, Revit)
- CNC stone processing equipment programming and operation
- 3D scanning and digital modeling skills
- Engineering surveying and precision installation technology
- Drone operation and image analysis basics

**Key growth areas:**

- restoration
- heritage
- monument care
- natural stone

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a stonemason in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary significantly by experience, region, and employer. Entry-level apprentices earn minimum wage, while experienced stonemasons can earn above the national average. Refer to the official ISPV or ČSÚ wage surveys for current data.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as a stonemason in Czechia?**
A: EU/EEA citizens can work freely. Non-EU nationals need an Employee Card, which requires a job offer, employer sponsorship, and proof of qualifications. The process takes several months.
