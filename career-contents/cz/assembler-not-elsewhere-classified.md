# Assemblers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 8219 (ISCO08)**

Assemblers in this category perform manual assembly of products or components across various industries in Czechia, including automotive, electronics, machinery, and furniture. They follow blueprints or verbal instructions to fit, align, and fasten parts using hand tools, power tools, or automated equipment. In Czechia, demand is driven by the strong manufacturing base, especially in the automotive sector (e.g., Škoda Auto). The job typically requires good manual dexterity, attention to detail, and the ability to work in repetitive, fast-paced environments. Many positions are located in industrial zones of Moravia, Bohemia, and around Prague.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for assemblers in Czechia often starts with entry-level assembly positions, advancing to team leader or quality inspector roles. With additional vocational training, assemblers can move into specialized areas such as CNC machining, mechatronics, or industrial maintenance. Some may transition to production planning or supervisory roles. Continuing education through secondary technical schools or apprenticeship programs is common to enhance skills and earnings.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive parts assembly (e.g., electronic component insertion, screw fastening)
- Vision-Based Quality Inspection (AI Vision System Replacement)
- Simple material handling and sorting (replaced by AMR)
- Record production data (replaced by automated MES system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for real-time assembly guidance, reducing training time
- Use collaborative robots for heavy or precision assembly, with worker supervision and fine-tuning
- Use AI predictive maintenance tools to monitor equipment and replace worn parts in advance
- Optimize processes based on digital twin simulation of assembly workflows

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard parts or unexpected anomalies (e.g., flexible adjustments when part tolerance is out of spec)
- Hand-finishing small-batch, multi-variety custom assemblies
- Make experience-based comprehensive judgments on assembly quality
- Cross-workstation collaboration and on-site problem-solving skills

**Skills to build:**

- Collaborative robot programming and debugging.
- Operation of digital twin and AR-assisted assembly systems
- Basic data analysis (reading from MES system to improve efficiency)
- Mechatronics maintenance skills
- Lean production and continuous improvement methods
- Cross-disciplinary communication (liaising with engineers, suppliers).

**Key growth areas:**

- Automotive
- Electronics
- Precision Manufacturing
- Automation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an assembler in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by industry and region. Entry-level assemblers earn around 25,000–30,000 CZK/month. With experience or special skills, salaries can exceed 35,000 CZK/month. Overtime and shift bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EU national get a work visa for an assembler job?**
A: Yes, if the employer obtains a labor market test. However, assembler roles are not on the shortage list, so the Employee Card process is standard. The job must meet the minimum salary requirement (generally not below 50% of the average wage).

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for assemblers?**
A: Assemblers can progress to team leader, quality controller, or specialist in automated assembly. Further education can lead to technician or supervisor roles.
