# Other Cleaning Worker — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 9129 (ISCO08)**

Other Cleaning Workers (ISCO-08 9129) in Czechia encompass cleaners in offices, hotels, hospitals, and industrial facilities. This occupation is characterized by low entry barriers and a high share of part-time and temporary contracts. The sector employs a significant number of workers, including many from EU countries such as Slovakia, Romania, and Bulgaria, as well as third-country nationals. Cleaning work is essential across all industries, but it remains one of the lowest-paid occupations in the country.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for cleaners in Czechia is limited, but opportunities exist to move into supervisory roles (e.g., cleaning team leader) or to specialize in areas such as carpet cleaning, window cleaning, or disinfection services. Some workers may transition to facility management or start their own cleaning businesses. The occupation typically offers little formal training or advancement, but motivated individuals can improve their earnings by gaining certificates in specialized cleaning or safety.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic floor scrubbers or robot vacuums for floor cleaning
- Automatic vacuuming and mopping along preset routes
- Timely automatic spraying of disinfectant for environmental disinfection.
- Use AI trash bins to automatically sort and compact waste
- Monitor cleanliness via sensors and generate cleaning reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI scheduling systems to optimize cleaning routes and shifts.
- Using computer vision to detect stains and guide targeted cleaning
- Use voice assistants or apps to log cleaning tasks and feedback
- Use data analysis to predict peak hours and prepare in advance
- Operate semi-automatic cleaning equipment for high-altitude or corner cleaning

**Human moat:**

- Experience in cleaning complex stains and special material surfaces.
- Communicating with clients to confirm cleaning requirements and flexibly adjusting
- Expertise in non-toxic, environmentally friendly cleaning agents
- Identify safety hazards on site and take emergency measures on the spot
- Manual cleaning in narrow, irregular spaces is irreplaceable.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to operate cleaning robots or smart cleaning equipment
- Basic computer operation and simple programming (e.g., setting cleaning routes)
- Understanding the Internet of Things (IoT) technology to monitor the status of cleaning equipment
- Knowledge of Disinfection and Chemical Safety in Learning Environments
- Improve customer service and communication skills
- Learn data analysis fundamentals to optimize cleaning efficiency

**Key growth areas:**

- Commercial Cleaning
- Green Cleaning
- Facility Management
- Hospitality

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a cleaner in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and region, but generally correspond to the national minimum wage or slightly higher, especially in Prague. Overtime and night shifts may increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a cleaner in Czechia?**
A: Yes, with a valid Employee Card, provided they have a job offer from a Czech employer. However, the occupation is not on the shortage list, so the employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled by a Czech or EU citizen.

**Q: What advancement opportunities exist for cleaners?**
A: Progression is limited but possible to team leader, supervisor, or specialized roles such as hospital disinfection. Some open their own cleaning businesses.
