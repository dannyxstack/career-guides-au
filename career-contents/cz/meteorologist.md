# Meteorologist — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2112 (ISCO08)**

Meteorologists in Czechia study atmospheric phenomena to produce weather forecasts, climate research, and environmental analyses. They work primarily for the Czech Hydrometeorological Institute (ČHMÚ), universities, private weather services, and media. The field employs around 500 professionals, with demand driven by climate monitoring, renewable energy planning, and aviation safety. Most positions require a Master's degree in meteorology or related fields, and fluency in Czech is often essential for public-facing roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior forecaster or researcher, advancing to senior meteorologist, team leader, or specialized analyst in areas like long-range forecasting or climate modeling. Many meteorologists pursue doctoral studies for roles in academia or advanced research. Opportunities exist in the private sector, such as weather risk management or environmental consulting, offering paths to senior advisory positions. Cross-disciplinary skills in data science or GIS enhance career prospects.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of routine weather forecast text
- Compilation and preliminary statistical analysis of historical meteorological data
- Standard warning content editing based on numerical model output
- Simple fault diagnosis and standardized reporting of meteorological observation equipment
- Write descriptive reports on basic climate trends

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Interpretation and Probability Calibration of Ensemble Forecasts Using AI
- Combine deep learning to predict local extreme weather (e.g., thunderstorms, heavy rain).
- AI-assisted climate change attribution analysis and scenario simulation
- Automatically generate customized industry weather reports (agriculture, energy).
- Using natural language processing to automatically extract meteorological research progress from literature

**Human moat:**

- Physical intuition and interdisciplinary understanding of complex weather systems
- Deliver authoritative advice to the public and decision-makers during high-impact weather events
- Designing novel observation experiments and scientific methods for integrating multi-source data
- Assume legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., consequences of failing to issue early warnings)
- Collaborative decision-making with related fields (hydrology, ecology)

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch)
- Principles and applications of weather AI models (e.g., FourCastNet, GraphCast)
- Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification techniques
- Climate big data visualization and interactive dashboard development
- Cross-disciplinary Skills (Economics, Risk Management, Public Policy)
- AI ethics and social impact assessment of weather warnings

**Key growth areas:**

- climate change adaptation
- renewable energy forecasting
- data science
- early warning systems

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a meteorologist in Czechia?**
A: Entry-level meteorologists earn around 35,000-45,000 CZK/month gross, while experienced professionals can earn 55,000-80,000 CZK/month. Senior roles in the private sector may exceed 100,000 CZK/month.

**Q: Can a non-EU meteorologist get a work visa in Czechia?**
A: Yes, through the Employee Card or EU Blue Card. You need a job offer and a university degree. The Blue Card is suitable for those with a Master's degree and a salary above the threshold (approx. 60,000 CZK/month in 2024).

**Q: Do I need to speak Czech to work as a meteorologist?**
A: For public sector roles at ČHMÚ and media, Czech is essential. In private companies and research, English may suffice, but Czech is advantageous for integration.
