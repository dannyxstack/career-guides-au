# Odd-job Person — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons in Czechia perform a variety of simple manual tasks, such as cleaning, carrying goods, assisting in warehouses, or supporting construction sites. They are often employed on short-term or seasonal contracts in hospitality, retail, tourism, or agriculture. This occupation does not require formal education and serves as an entry point to the labour market for low-skilled workers, including some foreign nationals from EU/EEA countries. Demand fluctuates with economic cycles, with peak needs in summer tourism and harvest seasons.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,187. The outlook for odd-job persons in Czechia is stable but limited. Automation and digitalization reduce demand for simple manual tasks, but seasonal needs in tourism, hospitality, and agriculture persist. The post-pandemic recovery boosted temporary jobs in hotels, restaurants, and events. Labour shortages in other sectors may also trickle down to create some vacancies. However, wages remain low and job security is weak. EU/EEA migrants fill many such roles, especially from neighbouring countries like Slovakia and Poland.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for odd-job persons is very limited. With on-the-job experience, one might move into a semiskilled role (e.g., warehouse assistant, cleaner with specialized equipment) or supervisory position. Typically, this occupation is a stepping stone for those seeking their first job or temporary income. Upskilling through short courses (e.g., forklift license, hospitality certificates) can improve prospects, but long-term advancement usually requires transitioning to another field or obtaining formal qualifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal tourism
- Hospitality staffing
- Warehouse logistics
- Event support

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely but generally range from minimum wage (around 18,900 CZK monthly in 2025) to roughly 25,000 CZK, depending on employer and hours. Overtime and seasonal bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can a non-EU national become an odd-job person in Czechia?**
A: It is extremely difficult. The Employee Card requires medium-skilled work (ISCED 3+), which odd-job jobs do not meet. Non-EU nationals would need to be covered by a specific program (e.g., seasonal work permit for agriculture or tourism, but quotas are limited). EU/EEA citizens have free access.

**Q: What are the chances of getting a permanent contract?**
A: Low. Most odd-job positions are temporary, seasonal, or agency-based. Permanent contracts are rare unless you gain experience and move into a more stable role within the same company.
