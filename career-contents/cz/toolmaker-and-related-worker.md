# Toolmaker — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 7222 (ISCO08)**

Toolmakers and related workers in Czechia design, manufacture, and maintain precision tools, dies, jigs, and fixtures used in metalworking, plastics, and other manufacturing processes, particularly in the automotive and engineering sectors. They operate conventional and CNC machine tools, interpret technical drawings, and ensure high accuracy. The role is central to Czechia's strong industrial base.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship (dual system) or secondary vocational school, followed by years of on-the-job training. Experienced toolmakers can advance to master craftsman (mistr), team leader, or specialist in CNC programming or quality control. Further education allows moves into engineering or technical management. Mobility is high within the EU.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual CNC programming and tool path generation
- Simple repetitive machine operations (e.g., loading/unloading workpieces, starting machining cycles)
- Routine dimensional measurement and quality recording (manual measurement with calipers and micrometers)
- Paper-based drawing management and document archiving
- Basic troubleshooting (based on fixed diagnostic procedures)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted tool path optimization (automatic collision avoidance, improved machining efficiency)
- Machine learning-based online quality inspection (real-time prediction of dimensional deviations)
- Digital twin simulation of machining processes (pre-validate fixture designs)
- Smart production scheduling system integration (automatic assignment of machine tasks)
- Augmented reality-assisted assembly and debugging (overlaying virtual guidance)

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment in complex fixture design and debugging
- On-site diagnosis and repair of multi-process integration issues.
- Ability to communicate non-standard requirements with clients/engineers
- Safety responsibility and process innovation decisions
- Precision manual finishing and assembly touch

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX machining modules)
- Robot-assisted loading/unloading programming and integration
- Python or MATLAB for machining data analysis
- Basics of additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and smart tool management
- Quality control data visualization (e.g., Power BI)

**Key growth areas:**

- Automotive
- Precision Engineering
- CNC Machining
- Industrial Automation

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Detail-oriented individuals with strong spatial awareness and manual dexterity.
- Those who enjoy precision work and problem-solving in a workshop environment.
- People comfortable with both traditional hand tools and modern CNC equipment.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who dislike repetitive tasks or require constant variety.
- Individuals averse to working with oil, coolants, and metal dust.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for toolmakers in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region; entry-level toolmakers earn around CZK 25,000–30,000 per month, while experienced ones can reach CZK 40,000–50,000. Overtime and bonuses are common. Figures are gross.

**Q: What visa do I need to work as a toolmaker in Czechia from a non-EU country?**
A: The Employee Card is the typical route, as toolmaking is a shortage occupation. EU Blue Card is possible if the salary meets the higher threshold. Intra-Company Transfer applies if you move within a multinational firm.

**Q: Is toolmaker considered a shortage occupation in Czechia?**
A: Yes, toolmakers (CZ-ISCO 7222) are on the official shortage list. This simplifies work permit procedures and sometimes allows a higher quota for non-EU workers.
