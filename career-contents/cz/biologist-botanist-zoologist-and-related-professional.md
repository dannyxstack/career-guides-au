# Biologists, Botanists, Zoologists and Related Professionals — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Biologists, botanists, zoologists, and related professionals in Czechia are primarily employed in research institutions, universities, environmental agencies, and the pharmaceutical industry. The labour market is stable but niche, with opportunities in both public and private sectors, particularly in biodiversity conservation, biotechnology, and environmental monitoring. Competence in Czech is often required for public sector roles, while English dominates in international R&D settings.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a master's degree, followed by a PhD for advanced research roles. Junior researchers or field assistants can advance to senior scientist, lab manager, or project coordinator. In the private sector, paths lead to specialist roles in biotech or quality assurance. Public sector positions offer stable progression within state institutions, but promotion may be slower. International experience and publications are key for academic advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)
- Manual sorting of ecological monitoring data (replaced by IoT sensors + AI).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Creativity in experiment design, hypothesis formulation and validation
- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)
- Responsibility attribution and professional certification under the regulatory framework.

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning applications
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., AlphaFold, BLAST)
- Data visualization and interactive reporting (e.g., R Shiny, Tableau)
- Automated laboratory equipment operation and process optimization
- Science communication and policy advocacy skills
- AI ethics and biosecurity regulations knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- biotechnology
- environmental monitoring
- biodiversity conservation
- pharmaceutical research

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for biologists in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by sector and experience. Entry-level positions in public research start around 30,000 CZK/month, while senior roles in pharma can exceed 80,000 CZK/month. Private sector and international companies tend to offer higher pay.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to work as a biologist in Czechia?**
A: If you are an EU/EEA citizen, no visa is required. Non-EU citizens need a work permit, typically via an Employee Card or EU Blue Card if highly qualified. The employer must usually pass a labour market test.

**Q: Is a PhD necessary for career advancement in biology in Czechia?**
A: For most academic and senior research positions, a PhD is essential. In industry, a master's degree may suffice for specialist roles, but a PhD can accelerate progression to leadership positions.
