# Forestry and Related Worker — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry and related workers in Czechia perform manual and semi-skilled tasks in forest management, including planting, thinning, felling, and harvesting trees, as well as maintaining forest roads and firebreaks. Czechia has extensive forest cover (about one-third of the country), and the sector employs a stable workforce mainly in rural regions. The occupation requires physical stamina and basic knowledge of forestry practices, often combined with the operation of chainsaws and other machinery.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from general labourer to specialized roles such as harvester operator, forest technician, or foreman. Workers can pursue vocational training in forestry management or obtain certificates in chainsaw operation or pesticide application. With experience and additional qualifications, advancement to supervisory or technical advisory positions in public forest agencies or private companies is possible.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable forestry
- mechanization
- reforestation
- climate adaptation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for forestry workers in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, but entry-level workers can expect around 25,000–30,000 CZK per month before tax; experienced workers may earn up to 40,000 CZK or more, especially with specialized certifications.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as forestry workers in Czechia?**
A: Yes, but they need a valid job offer and must apply for an Employee Card. The employer must prove the vacancy cannot be filled by a Czech or EU citizen. The occupation is not on the shortage list, so the process may be more complicated.

**Q: Is a forestry degree required to become a forestry worker?**
A: No, formal education is not mandatory. Most workers learn on the job, but a vocational certificate in forestry can improve job prospects and lead to higher pay.
