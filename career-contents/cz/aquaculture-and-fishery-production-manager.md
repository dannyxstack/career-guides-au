# Aquaculture and Fisheries Production Manager — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 1312 (ISCO08)**

In Czechia, aquaculture and fisheries production managers oversee fish farming (especially carp and trout), hatcheries, and freshwater fisheries. They coordinate breeding, feeding, health monitoring, harvesting, and compliance with EU environmental and food safety standards. The role is vital for the domestic carp market and recreational fishing sector, though the industry is relatively small compared to other EU countries.

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★½☆).** National workforce approx. 390. Employment in Czech aquaculture and fisheries is stable but limited, with slow growth driven by demand for sustainable local fish and organic products. Climate change and water quality regulations pose challenges, but pond revitalization and EU subsidies support modernization. New entrants are few, and managerial turnover is low, so opportunities arise mainly through retirement or expansion of existing operations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a fish farmer or technician, advancing to production supervisor, then manager. A university degree in fisheries, aquaculture, or agricultural science is increasingly preferred. Experience in fish biology, water management, and business administration is valuable. Some managers become owners of private fish farms or move into consultancy, research, or public administration related to water management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable aquaculture
- Organic fish farming
- Pond restoration
- Local food production

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an aquaculture and fisheries production manager in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on farm size and location; entry-level managers may earn around 400,000 CZK/year, while experienced managers at larger operations can earn up to 800,000 CZK/year. Bonuses and housing may be included.

**Q: Can a non-EU aquaculture manager get a work visa for Czechia easily?**
A: It is possible via the Employee Card, but the occupation is not on the shortage list, so a labor market test is required. The employer must prove the position cannot be filled by an EU citizen. EU Blue Card is less common due to salary thresholds.

**Q: What are the main duties of an aquaculture production manager in Czechia?**
A: Duties include planning breeding cycles, monitoring fish health and water quality, managing feeding, coordinating harvest, ensuring compliance with EU regulations, and supervising staff. Financial management and marketing are also key.
