# Landscape Architect — career analysis · Czechia

**Occupation code: 2162 (ISCO08)**

Landscape architects in Czechia plan and design outdoor spaces, from public parks and urban squares to private gardens and ecological restoration projects, integrating environmental sustainability with aesthetic and functional needs. They work with municipalities, developers, and private clients, and often collaborate with architects, urban planners, and engineers. The profession requires a blend of creative design, technical knowledge of plants and construction, and understanding of local regulations. Czechia's growing focus on green infrastructure, climate adaptation, and quality of urban life feeds demand for these specialists.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with junior designer or project assistant roles, advancing to senior landscape architect, project manager, or team lead. Many Czech landscape architects eventually establish their own studios or partner in multidisciplinary design firms. Authorization (licence) from the Czech Chamber of Architects is key for independent practice and signing off on projects. Long-term, professionals may specialize in ecological restoration, urban design, or landscape management, and can move into public administration or academia.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a landscape architect in Czechia?**
A: Salaries vary widely by experience, sector, and location. Entry-level might earn around 450,000–600,000 CZK annually, while senior–licensed professionals can earn 800,000–1,200,000 CZK. For up-to-date figures, consult the Czech Statistical Office or job portals.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to work as a landscape architect in Czechia if I am from outside the EU?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals need a work permit. The most common route is the Employee Card, which requires a job offer and meets salary conditions. If you have a Master's degree and the salary is high, you may qualify for the EU Blue Card.

**Q: Is my foreign landscape architecture degree recognized in Czechia?**
A: Degrees from EU countries are generally recognized. For non-EU degrees, you may need a nostrification process (recognition by a Czech university) or the Czech Chamber of Architects may assess equivalency for authorization.
