**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary by region, experience, and season. Entry-level workers may earn around €700–€1,000 per month, while experienced fishermen can earn €1,200–€1,800. Pay is often supplemented by profit-sharing or bonuses. Many are self-employed or work on a daily wage basis, especially in seasonal coastal fishing. Income fluctuates with catch volumes and market prices.

**Q: What are the official routes for non-EU citizens to work as inland or coastal waters fishery workers in Greece?**
A: Non-EU workers need a Greek national work visa (type D), which requires a job offer and employer sponsorship. The EU Blue Card may apply if the worker holds a higher education degree or 5+ years of professional experience and the salary meets the national threshold (approx. €35,000/year). Seasonal work permits are also available for temporary fishing jobs, typically valid for up to 9 months.

**Q: What are the typical working conditions for inland and coastal waters fishery workers in Greece?**
A: Work involves long hours outdoors, often starting before dawn, in variable weather. Tasks include netting, harvesting fish, sorting, and maintaining gear. Coastal fishing uses small boats, while inland work occurs on lakes or rivers. The job is physically demanding and may include overnight trips. Safety regulations cover vessel standards and protective equipment. Licensing and basic training are required.
