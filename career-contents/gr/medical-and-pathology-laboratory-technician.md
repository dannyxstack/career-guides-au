## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automation of routine hematology, biochemistry, and other batch testing
- Microscopic cell counting and preliminary classification
- Clerical tasks such as sample coding, registration, and result entry
- Standardized quality control and calibration processes
- Preliminary rule-based result review and alerting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Identification and classification of abnormal samples (AI-assisted microscope image analysis)
- Complex result interpretation and report generation (AI integrating multi-omics data)
- Instrument fault prediction and preventive maintenance scheduling
- Inspection process optimisation and work scheduling
- Discovery of rare disease screening patterns based on big data

**Human moat:**

- Morphological interpretation of rare or complex samples
- Clinical communication and consultation with doctors and patients
- Manual processing decisions for non-standard, urgent, or contaminated samples
- Design and supervision of laboratory quality assurance systems
- Ethics and regulatory compliance (e.g., patient privacy, data security)

**Skills to build:**

- AI-Assisted Diagnostic Software Operation and Result Validation
- Advanced applications of Laboratory Information Management Systems (LIS).
- Data Analysis and Statistics (Python/R basics)
- Automated instrument maintenance and troubleshooting
- Quality assurance and regulatory compliance (ISO 15189)
- Interdisciplinary communication and clinical collaboration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for medical laboratory technicians in Greece?**
A: In Greece, entry-level medical laboratory technicians earn around €800 to €1,200 per month gross. Experienced technicians can earn between €1,500 and €2,000. Public sector salaries are often lower but include benefits, while private hospitals and diagnostic centers may offer higher pay. Salaries also vary by region and employer.

**Q: What are the official routes for non-EU citizens to work as medical laboratory technicians in Greece?**
A: Non-EU citizens can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a recognized degree and a job offer meeting the minimum salary threshold (currently around €38,000 annually). Alternatively, a national work visa may be available for shortage occupations; however, medical laboratory technicians are not always listed. Check the Greek Ministry of Migration website for updates.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for medical laboratory technicians in Greece?**
A: Medical laboratory technicians in Greece can advance by specializing in fields like clinical chemistry, microbiology, or molecular diagnostics. With experience, they may become lab supervisors or managers. Continuous professional development is encouraged. Some pursue further studies to become biomedical scientists. Demand remains stable due to the public healthcare system's needs.
