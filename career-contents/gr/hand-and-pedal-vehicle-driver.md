## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for hand and pedal vehicle drivers in Greece?**
A: Pay varies widely based on location and type of work. In Athens, food delivery cyclists often earn around the minimum wage (€830 gross monthly in 2024) plus tips, while tourist rickshaw drivers may earn more seasonally, typically €800-1,200 per month. Self-employed drivers set their own rates but face irregular income.

**Q: What work visa options are available for non-EU hand and pedal vehicle drivers to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU drivers can apply for a national work visa (type D) if they obtain a job offer and the employer proves a labor market test. The EU Blue Card is not applicable as this occupation is not considered highly skilled. Seasonal work permits for up to 6 months are possible in tourism areas. All routes require employer sponsorship and residence permit.

**Q: What are typical career prospects for hand and pedal vehicle drivers in Greece?**
A: Most drivers work as independent contractors or for delivery platforms and tourist companies. Advancement is limited; some move into dispatch or logistics roles. The job offers flexibility but low job security. Seasonal peaks in tourism provide opportunities, but off-season earnings drop. Acquiring a motorcycle license can expand options into motorized delivery jobs.
