**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Regular maintenance and simple troubleshooting (such as replacing filters, belts), automated system can monitor and alert
- Remote Monitoring and Predictive Maintenance, AI Analysis of Sensor Data for Automatic Alerts
- Spare parts inventory management and ordering, with system-optimized inventory
- Repair reports and document organization, AI-generated
- Basic parameter calibration, assisted by automation tools

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis: AI-assisted analysis of historical data and real-time parameters to provide troubleshooting suggestions
- Maintenance decision support: augmented reality (AR) displays repair steps and hidden parts
- Training and skill enhancement: AI mentors customize learning paths and simulate fault scenarios.
- Tool and equipment operation: AI optimizes and adjusts maintenance parameters, improving precision
- Customer communication: AI generates plain-language explanations and repair plans to build trust

**Human moat:**

- Creative repair of non-standard equipment, requiring hands-on modification and adaptability
- On-site safety judgment and emergency handling
- Client relationship maintenance and communication.

- Teamwork and cross-trade coordination

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an agricultural and industrial machinery mechanic in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary based on experience, region, and employer. Entry-level mechanics typically earn €800–€1,000 net per month, while experienced professionals earn €1,200–€1,500 net. Overtime and specialization in specific machinery can increase earnings. The legal minimum wage applies to entry positions.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as an agricultural or industrial machinery mechanic in Greece through the EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU workers can apply for an EU Blue Card for this occupation. Requirements include a binding job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the Greek average gross annual salary, a relevant vocational diploma or equivalent professional experience, and a valid employment contract. The employer must demonstrate the position could not be filled by a Greek or EU national.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a machinery mechanic in Greece?**
A: Typically, a vocational secondary education diploma in mechanical engineering or a related field is required. Apprenticeships or on-the-job training are common. Certification from recognized technical schools improves prospects. Continuous learning is important due to technological advances in agricultural and industrial machinery.
