**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for biologists, botanists, zoologists and related professionals in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level positions earn around €1,200 per month, while senior roles in research or industry can reach €2,500. Public sector and academic salaries are set by collective agreements, often lower than private sector. Specializations like marine biology or conservation may have different ranges.

**Q: What is the work-visa or EU Blue Card route for non-EU biologists to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU biologists can apply for an EU Blue Card if they have a job offer meeting the minimum salary threshold (set annually, typically around €2,000 gross monthly). Alternatively, a national work visa is available for employment with a valid contract. Both require recognition of qualifications by Greek authorities, often via the Hellenic NARIC.

**Q: What are the main career opportunities for biologists in Greece?**
A: Career opportunities include academic research at universities, work in environmental consulting firms, government agencies like the Hellenic Ministry of Environment, and the pharmaceutical industry. Marine biology is prominent due to Greece's coastline. Many professionals also work in conservation NGOs or teaching. The public sector offers stable positions but limited openings.
