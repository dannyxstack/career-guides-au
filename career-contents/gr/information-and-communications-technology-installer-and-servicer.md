- Using AI chatbots to handle common user installation/configuration issues

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted augmented reality (AR) tools to guide complex equipment installation and wiring

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making: adjust plans dynamically based on environment (e.g., temporary cable routing detours)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for ICT installers and servicers in Greece?**
A: In Greece, ICT installers and servicers typically earn between €15,000 and €25,000 annually, depending on experience and location. Entry-level positions start around €12,000, while senior roles or those in Athens may reach €30,000. Wages are often higher in the private sector and for specialized skills, such as fiber optics or security systems.

**Q: What are the visa routes for non-EU ICT installers to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work in Greece via the EU Blue Card (for highly qualified positions, requiring a recognized degree or equivalent experience) or a National Visa for Employment (for salaried work). The employer must obtain a work permit from the Greek authorities. Self-employment routes also exist but are more complex. No specific quotas are publicly stated.

**Q: What qualifications are important for ICT installers in Greece?**
A: Vocational training or a diploma in electronics, telecommunications, or IT is highly valued. Certifications from vendors like Cisco or Microsoft boost employability. Greek language skills are essential for communicating with clients and reading local technical manuals. Work experience in installation and maintenance of networks, security systems, or telecom equipment is often required.
