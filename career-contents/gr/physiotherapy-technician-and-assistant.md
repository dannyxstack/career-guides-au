## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Patient treatment progress recording and report writing
- Basic vital signs monitoring and data entry
- Setup and calibration of routine treatment equipment
- Appointment scheduling and patient file management.
- Simple Rehabilitation Exercise Guidance (Standardized Movements)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI to analyze patient movement data and optimize personalized treatment plans
- Balance and coordination training assisted by virtual reality (VR)
- Use wearable devices to monitor patient progress in real time and adjust intensity
- AI-assisted generation of treatment plan documents and patient education materials
- Smart voice assistant for patient communication and follow-up

**Human moat:**

- Manual therapy techniques (e.g., massage, joint mobilization)
- Empathy to assess patient mental state and pain
- Clinical judgment in adjusting treatment based on nonverbal cues
- Build trust with patients and motivate them to adhere to rehabilitation
- Handling emergencies (e.g., patient falls or discomfort)

**Skills to build:**

- Patient communication and behavior change coaching
- Wearable device data interpretation.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapy technician or assistant in Greece?**
A: Entry-level physiotherapy technicians and assistants in Greece earn around €800–€1,000 per month, while experienced professionals can expect €1,200–€1,500. Public sector salaries follow fixed pay scales based on qualifications and seniority, often slightly higher. Private practice or specialized clinics may offer additional benefits.

**Q: What is the process for a non-EU physiotherapy technician to work in Greece through a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a job offer from a Greek employer, who obtains a work permit from the Ministry of Migration. For the EU Blue Card, the role must be high-skilled, requiring a recognized university degree and a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (currently about €26,000). Both routes lead to a residence permit.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for physiotherapy technicians and assistants in Greece?**
A: With experience, assistants can specialize in fields like sports rehabilitation or neurology. Many pursue further education to become licensed physiotherapists (via university programs). Others move into supervisory roles in clinics or hospitals, or start their own private practice. Continuous professional development is encouraged.
