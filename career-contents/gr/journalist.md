## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for journalists in Greece?**
A: Journalists in Greece typically earn between €18,000 and €25,000 annually, though entry-level positions may start lower. Salaries vary by employer (e.g., major media outlets vs. freelancing) and experience. Freelancers often have fluctuating income.

**Q: Can non-EU journalists obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for Greece?**
A: Yes, non-EU journalists can apply for an EU Blue Card if they hold a university degree and a job offer meeting the minimum salary threshold set by Greece. Alternatively, a national work visa may be obtained through employer sponsorship. Both require a valid work contract and meeting regulatory requirements.

**Q: What is the career progression like for journalists in Greece?**
A: Journalists often start as junior reporters or correspondents for print, broadcast, or digital media. With experience, they can become editors, columnists, or specialize in fields like political or investigative journalism. Freelancing is common, and many pursue advanced degrees for career advancement.
