## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually enter data from paper or electronic documents into databases or spreadsheets
- Check format and basic accuracy of input data
- Repetitive batch data entry, such as invoices and forms
- Basic data cleaning and deduplication

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses AI tools to automatically extract document data, with humans handling exceptions.
- Use automated scripts to verify data consistency and improve efficiency
- Analyze data trends and generate reports through AI-assisted analysis

**Human moat:**

- Complex data judgment and anomaly handling capability
- Client communication and business understanding (e.g., explaining data deviations)
- Cross-system data integration and process optimization
- Maintain data privacy and security compliance

**Skills to build:**

- Learn Python or VBA for automated data processing
- Proficiency in advanced Excel functions (e.g., Power Query, macros)
- Familiar with OCR and RPA tools (e.g., UiPath)
- Data quality management and database basics (SQL)
- Basic data analysis and visualization (e.g., Tableau)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a data entry clerk in Greece?**
A: In Greece, data entry clerks earn an average gross monthly salary of €800 to €1,200, depending on experience and location. Entry-level positions start around €700, while senior clerks may earn up to €1,500. Salaries are lower in smaller cities compared to Athens. Overtime pay is uncommon.

**Q: How can a non-EU national work as a data entry clerk in Greece?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work visa or EU Blue Card. The employer must prove no Greek/EU candidate is available. For the EU Blue Card, a university degree and salary at least 1.5 times the national average (roughly €1,800/month) are required, which is high for data entry. A national work permit is an alternative route, but employer sponsorship is essential.

**Q: Are data entry jobs at risk due to automation in Greece?**
A: Yes, automation and AI are reducing demand for routine data entry in Greece. Many tasks are now handled by software. However, roles requiring accuracy, handling sensitive data, or bilingual skills remain. Upskilling in data analysis or digital tools can improve job security. The Greek job market is shifting toward more technical positions.
