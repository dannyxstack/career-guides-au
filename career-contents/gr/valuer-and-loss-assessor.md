## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for valuers and loss assessors in Greece?**
A: Salaries for valuers and loss assessors in Greece vary by experience and employer. Entry-level professionals earn around €15,000–€20,000 annually, while experienced assessors can earn €25,000–€35,000. Senior roles or specialization in high-value assets may exceed €40,000. Public sector pay is often lower than private insurance or banking. These figures are gross and before taxes.

**Q: What official routes exist for non-EU valuers to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU valuers can seek work in Greece through the EU Blue Card for highly qualified professionals, requiring a job offer with salary at least 1.5 times the national gross average. Alternatively, a national work visa (Type D) is possible if a Greek employer obtains a work permit via the labor ministry. Both routes require recognized qualifications and employer sponsorship.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a valuer or loss assessor in Greece?**
A: In Greece, valuers and loss assessors typically need a university degree in finance, economics, engineering, or real estate. Professional certification from the Ministry of Finance or the Hellenic Valuation Institute is often required for property valuation. Loss assessors, mainly in insurance, may need exams from the Bank of Greece. Experience and ongoing training are essential for career advancement.
