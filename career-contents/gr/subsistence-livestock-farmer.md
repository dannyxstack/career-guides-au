## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Greece?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Greece generally earn low, variable incomes primarily from small-scale sales of meat, milk, or cheese. Most produce is for personal consumption. Government subsidies under the Common Agricultural Policy may provide supplemental income. Earnings rarely exceed €10,000 annually, with many farmers relying on off-farm work to make ends meet.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for non-EU nationals to work as subsistence livestock farmers in Greece?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for a national work permit if they secure a job offer from a Greek employer. The EU Blue Card is available for highly skilled workers, but subsistence farming is unlikely to meet the salary threshold. Seasonal work permits exist for temporary agricultural labor. All routes require employer sponsorship and approval from Greek authorities.

**Q: What are the main challenges for subsistence livestock farmers in Greece?**
A: Key challenges include low and unstable incomes, high production costs, limited access to markets, and reliance on traditional methods. Climate change impacts such as droughts and heatwaves affect grazing and water availability. Bureaucratic hurdles, aging farmer populations, and lack of generational succession also pose difficulties in sustaining this occupation.
