**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate weaving/woodworking design drawings based on customer size parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI generative design to explore more forms and structural possibilities

**Human moat:**

- Cultural heritage and innovative interpretation of traditional crafts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a handicraft worker in wood, basketry, or related materials in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, skill, and region. Entry-level workers earn around €700–€900 gross monthly, while experienced artisans can earn €1,000–€1,400. Self-employed or master craftspeople may earn more. Pay is often project-based, and income can be seasonal, especially in tourism-related crafts.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a handicraft worker in Greece via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is designed for highly skilled workers with a university degree and a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross salary. For handicraft roles, this route is rarely applicable. Non-EU workers usually require a standard work permit sponsored by a Greek employer, who must prove the position cannot be filled locally.

**Q: What training or apprenticeship options exist for handicraft workers in wood and basketry in Greece?**
A: Vocational training institutes (IEK) offer programs in woodworking and traditional crafts. Apprenticeships with master craftsmen are common, especially in regions like Epirus and Crete known for basketry. The Ministry of Culture also supports workshops and certifications for preserving heritage crafts.
