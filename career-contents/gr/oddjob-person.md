## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for odd-job persons in Greece?**
A: Odd-job persons in Greece usually earn hourly wages ranging from €4 to €8, depending on the task and location. Many are paid in cash. Monthly income varies widely due to irregular work, typically between €400 and €800 gross, often below the minimum wage threshold for formal employment.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as odd-job persons in Greece via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers and does not cover odd-job roles. Non-EU citizens may obtain a work visa for seasonal or temporary work, but it requires a formal job offer and employer sponsorship. Self-employment as an odd-job person is possible only with a residence permit for independent economic activity, subject to strict criteria.

**Q: What are the career prospects for odd-job persons in Greece?**
A: Odd-job roles are typically informal and offer limited career advancement. Some may transition to skilled trades through vocational training or apprenticeships. Building a reputation and network can lead to more stable contracts, but formal employment with benefits remains uncommon. Many use this work as a stepping stone to other sectors.
