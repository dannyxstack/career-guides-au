## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated sewing machines perform standardized straight stitching and overlocking.
- AI layout systems automatically arrange embroidery patterns and generate machine instructions.
- Computer vision detects stitch defects and automatically adjusts parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates embroidery patterns and previews them.
- Smart sewing machine automatically adjusts tension based on fabric elasticity
- Using AR technology to overlay virtual samples to assist hand sewing
- Data analysis optimizes production scheduling to reduce changeover time

**Human moat:**

- Aesthetic and tactile qualities of complex techniques such as hand embroidery and seed stitch
- Expertise in three-dimensional tailoring and creative design for custom clothing.
- Exclusive handling experience with non-standard fabrics (silk, leather)
- Communicate face-to-face with clients to understand personalized needs

**Skills to build:**

- Digital embroidery design software (e.g., Wilcom, Tajima)
- Industrial sewing machine programming and automated tuning
- Fabric properties and sustainable materials knowledge
- Basic CAD pattern making and 3D garment simulation
- Customer communication and customized service skills
- Lean production and quality control methods

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for sewing and embroidery workers in Greece?**
A: In Greece, sewing and embroidery workers earn approximately €600 to €1,000 gross per month, depending on experience and location. Entry-level positions often start at the national minimum wage (€830/month in 2024). Skilled artisans in tourist areas may earn higher rates. Piecework and overtime can supplement income.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as sewing or embroidery workers in Greece via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU nationals may work in Greece through the EU Blue Card if they have a high-skilled job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary. However, sewing/embroidery roles rarely meet this threshold. Alternatively, employers can sponsor a national work permit for shortage occupations, though this occupation is not typically listed as a high-demand field.

**Q: How is technology affecting sewing and embroidery jobs in Greece?**
A: Automation is increasing in mass production, but hand embroidery and custom sewing remain in demand for tourism, luxury fashion, and cultural heritage preservation. Workers should learn digital design software and computerized embroidery machines. Traditional skills still hold value in niche markets, especially in regions with strong textile traditions.
