**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use a potter's wheel or molds for batch forming of standardized vessels
- Mechanical reproduction of basic carving and patterns
- Automated monitoring and adjustment of kiln temperature and time
- Automatic calculation and mixing of glaze formulas
- Visual inspection of dimensions and defects in product quality inspection

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Quickly design complex ceramic shapes using 3D modeling software
- Use AI to generate glaze formulas and pattern ideas
- Use AR technology to show customers virtual finished ceramic products
- Analyze market data to optimize product design and pricing

**Human moat:**

- Irreproducible personal style and artistic expression in handmade pottery
- Ability for manual adjustments to handle customized, non-standard requirements.
- Deep perception and control over clay material properties
- Inheritance and innovation of traditional craft culture

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a potter in Greece?**
A: Salaries for potters in Greece vary widely based on experience and location. Entry-level positions may start near the national minimum wage (€830/month in 2024). Experienced artisans in high-demand areas like Crete or Athens can earn between €1,200 and €1,800 per month. Many potters are self-employed, leading to income fluctuations.

**Q: Can non-EU potters work in Greece via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU potters can apply for an EU Blue Card in Greece if they hold a higher education degree or at least five years of relevant professional experience. They must have a binding job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary (approximately €1,500/month in 2024). Alternatively, a standard work permit may be pursued if the employer demonstrates a labor market need.

**Q: What training is required to become a potter in Greece?**
A: Formal training is not mandatory but is highly recommended. Greece offers vocational programs at public IEK (Vocational Training Institutes) and private workshops, focusing on ceramic techniques and kiln operation. Apprenticeships with experienced potters are common. A portfolio of work is often more important than formal qualifications for employment or self-employment.
