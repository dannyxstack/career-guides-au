## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a university lecturer in Greece?**
A: Salaries for university lecturers in Greece vary by rank: assistant professors earn about €1,500–€2,000 net monthly, while full professors can reach €3,500. Public universities follow a fixed payscale; private institutions may differ. Additional income from research or consulting is possible. Austerity measures have affected public sector pay, but recent adjustments have been made.

**Q: What are the official work visa or EU Blue Card requirements for non-EU university teachers wishing to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU university teachers can work in Greece via the EU Blue Card or a national work permit. The EU Blue Card requires a valid job offer, a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary, a recognized university degree, and health insurance. The employer must obtain a work permit from the Ministry of Migration. Alternatively, a national visa for highly qualified workers is available.

**Q: What are the typical career progression opportunities for university teachers in Greece?**
A: Career progression in Greek universities follows a hierarchical path: lecturer, assistant professor, associate professor, and full professor. Advancement requires strong publication records, teaching experience, and research output. Tenure is common after a probationary period. Competition is high due to limited positions and budget constraints. Some teachers pursue administrative roles like department head or dean.
