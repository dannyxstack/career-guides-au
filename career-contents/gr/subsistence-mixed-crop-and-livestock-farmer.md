## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Greece?**
A: Income is highly variable as subsistence farming prioritizes self-consumption. Surplus sales and EU Common Agricultural Policy subsidies may yield around €800–€1,200 per month for small-scale farmers, but many supplement with off-farm work. Official statistics are limited due to the informal nature of this occupation.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Greece via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled workers and does not apply. Non-EU citizens may obtain a national work visa if employed by a farm, but subsistence farming is typically self-employment. Self-employment visas require a detailed business plan and proof of sufficient funds. Always consult the Greek migration authority for current regulations.

**Q: What are main challenges for subsistence farmers in Greece?**
A: Challenges include small fragmented plots, water scarcity, high input costs, and low profitability. Climate change exacerbates droughts and wildfire risks. Additionally, complex EU subsidy applications and an aging farmer population hinder sustainability, as younger generations often migrate to cities.
