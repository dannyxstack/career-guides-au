## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a musical instrument maker or tuner in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on experience, specialization, and location. Entry-level tuners might earn around €800–€1,200 per month, while experienced craftspeople in high-demand niches (e.g., violin or piano restoration) can earn €1,500–€2,500. Self-employed professionals often charge €50–€100 per hour for repairs.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as musical instrument makers or tuners in Greece via the EU Blue Card or a work visa?**
A: Non-EU citizens can apply for a Greek work visa if they secure a job offer and meet the criteria, such as having a recognized degree or equivalent skills. The EU Blue Card is another option for highly qualified workers, requiring a higher-education degree and a job with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average gross salary. Both routes require employer sponsorship and labor market testing.

**Q: What are the main career opportunities for musical instrument makers and tuners in Greece?**
A: Opportunities exist in specialized workshops (e.g., Athens, Thessaloniki, Crete) focusing on traditional Greek instruments like the bouzouki and lyra, as well as classical instruments. Many professionals work independently, offering repair and restoration services. Some secure positions in music schools, orchestras, or museums. Apprenticeships and courses at vocational institutes like the IEK of Athens can also lead to careers in this field.
