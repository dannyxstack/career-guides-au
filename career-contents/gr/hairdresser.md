- Automated telephone or online booking system for handling customer appointments

- Smart mirrors or apps guide customers to self-preview hairstyles

- AI hairstyle design tool recommends haircuts based on face shape and hair texture

**Human moat:**

- Fine tactile and manual judgment in haircutting, perming, and dyeing
- Communicate directly with clients to understand their personalized needs and aesthetic preferences

- Live body language interaction to create a trusting and comfortable service atmosphere

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a hairdresser in Greece?**
A: In Greece, hairdressers typically earn between €600 and €1,200 per month, depending on experience, location, and salon type. Entry-level workers start around minimum wage, while seasoned professionals in upscale salons or with a clientele can earn higher through tips and commissions. Self-employed hairdressers may have variable income.

**Q: Can a non-EU hairdresser work in Greece via an EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card in Greece is intended for highly skilled workers with a university degree or equivalent, not typically for hairdressers. Non-EU hairdressers must instead obtain a standard Greek work permit, which requires a job offer, proof of qualifications (recognized certificate), and a labor market test. The employer sponsors the visa. No specific Blue Card route exists for this occupation.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a hairdresser in Greece?**
A: To work legally as a hairdresser in Greece, you need a recognized vocational diploma from a public or private school (e.g., IEK or OEEK). Additionally, you must register with the local professional chamber and hold a professional license. Continuing education is recommended but not mandatory. EU citizens can have their qualifications recognized; non-EU need to get their credentials evaluated.
