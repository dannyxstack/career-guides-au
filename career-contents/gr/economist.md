## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for economists in Greece?**
A: In Greece, entry-level economists earn around €18,000–€24,000 annually, while experienced economists in senior roles or financial sectors can earn €35,000–€55,000. Public sector economists follow different scales. These figures are indicative and vary by employer, location, and specialization.

**Q: What are the work visa or EU Blue Card routes for economists moving to Greece?**
A: Non-EU economists can work in Greece via the EU Blue Card, requiring a valid job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (approx. €32,000 in 2024) or a residence permit for highly qualified employment. Alternatively, a standard work permit may apply for lower salaries but requires employer sponsorship.

**Q: Which industries primarily employ economists in Greece?**
A: Economists in Greece find employment in banking and finance, consulting, government ministries, the Bank of Greece, international organizations, academia, and research institutes. The tourism and shipping sectors also hire economists for analysis. The private sector offers most opportunities, but public sector positions are competitive.
