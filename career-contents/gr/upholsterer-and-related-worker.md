## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using CAD software for standardized layout and cutting
- Sewing machine operation for simple straight stitching
- Mechanical cutting and forming of foam and filling materials
- Automated recording of inventory management and material ordering
- Repetitive filling and fastening tasks.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates and modifies soft package patterns
- AR/VR tools visualize final results, reducing rework
- Machine vision detects fabric defects, improving quality
- Digital cutting beds improve material utilization rates
- Smart supply chain systems optimize material procurement

**Human moat:**

- Customized complex patterns and hand-sewing techniques
- The ability to communicate with clients and understand personalized needs
- Experience handling special fabrics (e.g., antique textiles, leather)
- Flexibility for on-site installation, adjustment, and repair
- Comprehensive assessment of furniture structure and comfort

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/3D modeling software (e.g., Rhino, SketchUp)
- Proficient in operating digital cutting and sewing equipment
- Familiar with AR/VR presentation tools
- Develop project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and eco-design trends
- Learn basic programming to customize automated workflows

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an upholsterer in Greece?**
A: In Greece, upholsterers typically earn between €800 and €1,200 gross per month, depending on experience, location, and employer. Entry-level positions may start around €700, while highly skilled craftsmen can reach up to €1,500. Overtime and specialization in antique or luxury furniture can increase earnings.

**Q: How can a non-EU upholsterer obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU upholsterers need a job offer from a Greek employer, who must apply for a work permit through the Greek Ministry of Migration. For an EU Blue Card, a university degree and a salary at least 1.5 times the national average (currently around €1,600 gross monthly) are required. Upholstery work typically does not meet Blue Card criteria, so a standard work visa is the usual route.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to work as an upholsterer in Greece?**
A: Formal qualifications include vocational training certificates from IEK (Vocational Training Institutes) or apprenticeship programs. Many upholsterers learn through on-the-job training. Key skills include knowledge of fabrics, patterns, and tools. Professional experience is highly valued, and knowledge of traditional Greek upholstery techniques can be an advantage for jobs in restoration or furniture workshops.
