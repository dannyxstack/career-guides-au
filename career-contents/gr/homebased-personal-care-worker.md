## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for home-based personal care workers in Greece?**
A: Salaries for home-based personal care workers in Greece vary based on experience and employer. Entry-level workers earn around the national minimum wage (€830 per month in 2024, 14 payments per year). Experienced workers may earn €1,000–€1,200 monthly. Private clients may offer higher rates, but this is less common.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a home-based personal care worker in Greece via the EU Blue Card or other routes?**
A: Non-EU citizens can work in Greece as home-based personal care workers through a national work visa, not the EU Blue Card, which requires a higher-skilled job. They must secure a job offer and apply for a Type D visa for employment. The employer must prove the position cannot be filled by a Greek or EU worker. Self-employment is also possible under a business visa.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a home-based personal care worker in Greece?**
A: No formal qualification is mandatory, but employers often prefer candidates with a certificate from a vocational training institute (IEK) in elderly care or home care. Basic first aid knowledge and experience with chronic conditions are valued. Registration with the National Organization for the Certification of Qualifications (EOPPEP) is not required but can enhance job prospects.
