## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a carpenter or joiner in Greece?**
A: In Greece, carpenters and joiners typically earn between €800 and €1,500 per month, depending on experience, location, and employer. Entry-level workers may start at the lower end, while skilled professionals with several years of experience can earn higher wages. Overtime and project-based work can increase total earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU carpenter work in Greece via the EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU carpenters and joiners may qualify for the EU Blue Card for Greece if they have a valid job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary in Greece (around €30,000 in 2024). They must also hold recognized higher vocational qualifications or five years of professional experience. The employer must prove that the position could not be filled locally.

**Q: What training or qualifications are needed to become a carpenter in Greece?**
A: Formal training in carpentry is often obtained through vocational schools (IEK) or apprenticeship programs. A certificate of vocational training or a diploma in carpentry is valued. Practical experience is essential, and many carpenters learn on the job. Knowledge of modern tools, safety standards, and basic mathematics is required. Continuing education helps in advancing to more specialized joinery work.
