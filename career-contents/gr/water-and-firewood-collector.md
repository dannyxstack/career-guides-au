## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical pay for water and firewood collectors in Greece?**
A: Earnings vary significantly as work is often informal and paid per task or volume. Daily wages range from €25 to €40, depending on region and workload. Full-time collectors may earn near the national minimum wage (€830/month in 2024), but consistent income is uncertain due to seasonal demand and lack of formal contracts.

**Q: Can a foreigner get a work visa or EU Blue Card to work as a water or firewood collector in Greece?**
A: This occupation does not qualify for the EU Blue Card, which requires a higher education degree and a high-skilled position. Third-country nationals may theoretically obtain a standard work permit if a Greek employer proves no local candidate is available. However, such permits are rarely issued for this informal, low-skilled work, making legal migration for this role extremely difficult.

**Q: Is water and firewood collection a formal occupation in Greece, and what are the working conditions?**
A: It is primarily informal, especially in rural areas and islands. Workers often operate independently or for small businesses without formal contracts, insurance, or benefits. The work is physically demanding, involving long hours outdoors, carrying heavy loads, and exposure to weather. Seasonality is common, with higher demand in winter for firewood and summer for water collection in remote areas.
