## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining and quarrying labourer in Greece?**
A: The monthly minimum wage in Greece is €830 (as of 2024). Mining and quarrying labourers often earn this amount or slightly more, depending on experience, location, and employer. Overtime and shift allowances may increase earnings, but overall pay remains moderate compared to other sectors.

**Q: Can mining and quarrying labourers obtain an EU Blue Card to work in Greece?**
A: Mining and quarrying labourers typically do not qualify for the EU Blue Card, which requires a highly skilled job and a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross salary. Instead, non-EU workers can apply for a Greek work permit if an employer sponsors them and proves no local workers are available. The permit is employer-specific.

**Q: What career progression opportunities exist for mining labourers in Greece?**
A: With experience, mining labourers can advance to machine operators or team leaders. Vocational training programs in drilling or blasting can lead to supervisory roles. The Greek mining sector offers limited upward mobility, but on-the-job training and certifications help workers move into more skilled positions over time.
