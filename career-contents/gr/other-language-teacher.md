## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for other language teachers in Greece?**
A: Salaries for language teachers in Greece vary. In private language schools (frontistiria), entry-level teachers earn around €800–€1,200 per month, while experienced teachers may earn up to €1,500. Public school positions are limited and pay higher, based on collective agreements. Freelance teachers can charge €15–€30 per hour but face income instability.

**Q: Can non-EU language teachers work in Greece via an EU Blue Card?**
A: Non-EU teachers can work in Greece, but the EU Blue Card is rarely applicable to language teaching. It requires a high-skilled job with a salary at least 1.5 times the gross average wage (approx. €2,250/month in 2023), which most language teaching positions do not meet. The standard route is a national work visa sponsored by an employer, subject to labor market tests.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for language teachers in Greece?**
A: Language teachers in Greece can advance by gaining experience, specializing in exam preparation (e.g., IELTS, Goethe), or opening a private language school. Some pursue postgraduate degrees to teach at universities or in public schools through ASEP exams. Others transition to corporate training or online teaching, which offers flexibility and higher rates.
