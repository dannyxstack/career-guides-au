- Basic infection control and safe operating procedures

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for personal care workers in Greece?**
A: Personal care workers in Greek health services typically earn between €700 and €1,200 per month, depending on experience, employer, and region. Public sector positions may include allowances, while private sector rates vary. Overtime and night shifts can increase pay, but overall wages remain modest compared to other European countries.

**Q: What are the work visa or EU Blue Card routes for personal care workers coming to Greece?**
A: Non-EU personal care workers can obtain a Greek work visa if an employer proves no local candidate is available. The EU Blue Card is an option for highly qualified workers with a job offer paying at least €22,000 annually (subject to change). Applicants need recognized qualifications and must apply through the Greek consulate in their home country.

**Q: What career prospects exist for personal care workers in Greece?**
A: Due to an aging population, demand for personal care workers in Greece is strong. Career progression includes supervisory roles in home care agencies or nursing homes, specialization in elderly or disability care, and further training to become nursing assistants or social workers. Continuing education courses are widely available and enhance advancement opportunities.
