## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic measurement of room dimensions and material quantity calculation, AI can quickly generate optimal layout plans
- Automatic cutting of tiles/stone, CNC waterjet or laser cutting replaces manual cutting
- Automatic detection and adjustment solution generation for ground flatness, reducing manual trial laying
- Inventory management and material ordering processes, AI analyzes project requirements to automatically place orders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to view tiling blueprints and level indicators in real time, improving accuracy
- AI-assisted design of complex patterns and parquet, generating diverse options for clients
- Work collaboratively with automatic mortar mixing and plastering robots to improve efficiency
- AI-driven project progress prediction and scheduling to optimize multi-trade collaboration

**Human moat:**

- Manual cutting and fine placement in irregular areas and corners
- Removal of old flooring, base treatment, and leveling judgment for uneven floors.
- Communicate design preferences with clients and provide personalized advice
- Flexible problem-solving ability for on-site unexpected issues.

**Skills to build:**

- Use of augmented reality (AR) tools for construction assistance.
- Project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and environmentally friendly construction methods

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for floor layers and tile setters in Greece?**
A: Monthly salaries for floor layers and tile setters in Greece range from €1,000 to €1,800 gross, depending on experience and location. Experienced workers on large projects may earn higher rates. The sector has collective agreements setting minimum wages. Self-employed individuals charge per square meter.

**Q: What are the official work visa options for floor layers and tile setters from non-EU countries to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU floor layers can obtain a work visa if they secure a job offer and the employer proves no suitable EU worker is available (labor market test). The EU Blue Card is for highly qualified workers and requires a degree or five years' experience, which may not apply. Seasonal work permits are possible for short-term assignments.

**Q: Are there any specific certifications or licenses required to work as a floor layer in Greece?**
A: No formal license is required to work as a floor layer in Greece, but vocational training or apprenticeship is expected. Foreign qualifications may need recognition by DOATAP. Specialized tile work in heritage sites requires experience. Safety certificates, such as for epoxy resin use, are occasionally needed on larger construction sites.
