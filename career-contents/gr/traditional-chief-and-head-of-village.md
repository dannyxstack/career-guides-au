## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a traditional chief or head of a village in Greece?**
A: There is no standard salary for this role in Greece, as it is often honorary or voluntary. In some cases, local communities or municipal authorities may provide modest honorariums or expense reimbursements, but amounts vary widely and are not publicly regulated. Most individuals in this position do not rely on it as a primary income source.

**Q: Can a foreign national work as a traditional chief or head of a village in Greece through the EU Blue Card or work visa route?**
A: The EU Blue Card is designed for highly skilled employment and does not apply to this traditional, often ceremonial role. Non-EU nationals seeking to work in Greece in this capacity would need a standard work permit, but such positions are typically reserved for Greek citizens or long-term community members. No specific visa category exists for this occupation.

**Q: How does one become a traditional chief or head of a village in Greece?**
A: This role is not gained through formal education or competitive hiring. In Greece, it is typically inherited or chosen by community elders among local families, especially in remote or island communities. The position is often informal and carries prestige rather than legal authority. Individuals are expected to have deep ties to the village and knowledge of local customs.
