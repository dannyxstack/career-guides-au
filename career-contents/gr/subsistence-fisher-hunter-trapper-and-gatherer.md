**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary or income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Greece?**
A: Income is highly variable and often informal. Many engage in subsistence activities for personal consumption or local sale. Official statistics are scarce; typical earnings may range from €500 to €1,000 monthly, depending on region and season. Most practitioners supplement with other jobs or social benefits.

**Q: What are the work visa options for non-EU nationals to work as subsistence fishers in Greece?**
A: The EU Blue Card requires a university degree and a high salary, unlikely for subsistence roles. Non-EU workers may apply for a seasonal work permit (up to 6 months) for fishing or agriculture. Family reunification or a residence permit for self-employment could be options if the applicant meets investment criteria. Check with the Greek Ministry of Migration.

**Q: What are the career prospects and challenges for subsistence fishers in Greece?**
A: Career prospects are limited due to declining fish stocks, strict EU fishing quotas, and the informal nature of the work. Many practitioners are aging. Younger people often migrate to cities. However, ecotourism and small-scale sustainable fishing initiatives offer some opportunities. Government support is minimal, and the occupation is often part of a mixed livelihood strategy.
