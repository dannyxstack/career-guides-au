**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate treatment records and reports
- Preliminary diagnostic assistance based on images and data
- Patient appointment management and follow-up reminders
- Basic parameter calculation and adjustment of exercise prescriptions

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI motion capture and biomechanical analysis to optimize rehabilitation plans
- Generate clinical documentation quickly using speech recognition and NLP
- Identify complication risks early through AI predictive models
- Remotely monitor patient compliance with home-based training and intervene promptly
- Using digital twins to simulate treatment outcomes

**Human moat:**

- Fine perception in manual therapy and palpation
- Patient emotional support and therapeutic communication
- Complex clinical decision-making and individualised care planning
- Clinical judgment in interdisciplinary collaboration
- Ethics and accountability (e.g., tracing medical errors)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for paramedical practitioners in Greece?**
A: Salaries for paramedical practitioners in Greece vary by sector and experience. In the public sector, entry-level positions start around €800–€1,000 gross per month, while experienced professionals can earn up to €1,500. Private sector salaries may be slightly higher but often lack benefits. Pay is governed by collective labor agreements for public employees.

**Q: What are the official routes for non-EU paramedical practitioners to work in Greece?**
A: Non-EU paramedical practitioners can obtain a work visa or an EU Blue Card. The EU Blue Card requires a recognized university degree and a job offer with a salary of at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (approximately €21,000 in 2023). Applications are submitted to the Greek consulate in the applicant's home country.

**Q: Are paramedical qualifications from other countries recognized in Greece?**
A: Yes, but recognition requires an application to the Greek Ministry of Health or the relevant professional body. For EU qualifications, recognition is straightforward under Directive 2005/36/EC. Non-EU qualifications undergo a case-by-case evaluation, often requiring supplementary exams or training. The process can take several months.
