## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for physiotherapists in Greece?**
A: The salary for physiotherapists in Greece varies by experience and sector. Entry-level positions in private clinics may start around €800–€1,200 per month, while experienced professionals in hospitals or private practice can earn €1,500–€2,500. Public sector salaries follow national pay scales, typically €1,200–€1,800 monthly. Earnings may be supplemented by private sessions.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for physiotherapists to work in Greece?**
A: Physiotherapists from non-EU countries can work in Greece via the EU Blue Card, requiring a recognized degree and a job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary (approx. €29,000 in 2023). Alternatively, a national work permit is possible for specific sectors, with employer sponsorship and labor market test. Both routes require recognition of qualifications by the Greek authorities.

**Q: What is the career outlook for physiotherapists in Greece?**
A: The demand for physiotherapists in Greece is stable, driven by an aging population and increased awareness of rehabilitation. Opportunities exist in public hospitals, private clinics, sports centers, and home care. Specialization areas like sports physiotherapy or geriatrics offer growth. Professional registration with the Panhellenic Physiotherapists Association is mandatory. Competition is moderate, especially in urban areas.
