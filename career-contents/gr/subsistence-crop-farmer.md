**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual recording of crop growth logs and yield data, replaced by automatic sensors
- Manual pest and disease identification replaced by AI image recognition

- Paper-based bookkeeping and tax calculations are being replaced by financial software

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI tools analyze soil moisture and meteorological data to optimize irrigation timing.
- Use satellite imagery and drones to monitor crop health and provide early warning of pests and diseases

- Online platforms connecting local consumers with restaurants to expand direct sales channels
- Smart devices monitor storage conditions to reduce post-harvest loss

**Human moat:**

- Physical labour and on-site work (e.g., manual weeding, harvesting)
- Decisions based on local experience (e.g., traditional crop rotation, variety selection)
- Trust relationship with communities and consumers
- Flexible judgment in response to sudden weather and pests

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence crop farmers in Greece?**
A: Annual earnings for subsistence crop farmers in Greece are modest, typically ranging from €5,000 to €12,000, as farms are small and produce mainly for personal consumption. Income from surplus sales is supplemented by EU common agricultural policy subsidies. Many farmers also rely on off-farm work to reach the minimum wage of €830 per month.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as subsistence crop farmers in Greece via an EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is reserved for highly skilled workers with a university degree and a salary threshold of at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary in Greece. Subsistence crop farming does not meet these criteria. Non-EU nationals may instead apply for a seasonal work permit for agriculture, which has annual quotas and requires a job offer.

**Q: What are the main challenges for subsistence crop farmers in Greece?**
A: Key challenges include small fragmented landholdings, vulnerability to climate change, low profitability, an aging workforce, and limited access to modern technology and credit. Many farmers struggle to compete with imported produce. Diversification into agritourism, organic farming, or local value chains can improve viability.
