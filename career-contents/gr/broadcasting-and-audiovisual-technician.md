**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted real-time audio noise reduction and restoration

**Human moat:**

- Real-time decision-making in response to emergencies (e.g., equipment failure, severe weather)
- Communicate in real-time with directors, hosts, and musicians to understand artistic intent.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for broadcasting and audiovisual technicians in Greece?**
A: Entry-level technicians earn around €1,000–€1,300 per month, while experienced professionals in major broadcasters or production companies can earn €1,800–€2,500 monthly. Salaries vary by region, employer, and specialization (e.g., sound, lighting, camera). Overtime and freelance work often supplement income, especially during live events.

**Q: How can a non-EU national work as a broadcasting technician in Greece?**
A: Non-EU nationals need a work visa or EU Blue Card. The Blue Card is for highly qualified workers with a job offer meeting a salary threshold (currently around €38,000 gross annually). Alternatively, a national work visa requires employer sponsorship and proof that no EU/EEA candidate could fill the role. Both routes involve applying through Greek consulates.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become a broadcasting technician in Greece?**
A: A degree or diploma from a vocational school (e.g., IEK) in audiovisual technology, electronics, or media production is common. Certifications in specific equipment (e.g., cameras, sound consoles) or software (e.g., Avid, DaVinci Resolve) boost prospects. Licenses for operating certain broadcast systems may be required. Practical experience through internships is highly valued.
