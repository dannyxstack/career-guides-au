## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shopkeeper in Greece?**
A: Shopkeeper earnings vary widely by store size, location, and sector. On average, net monthly income ranges from €800 to €1,500. Many small shopkeepers earn near minimum wage, while those in tourist areas or with larger stores can earn more. Profit from the business is the primary income, so personal earnings depend on sales and expenses.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a shopkeeper in Greece via the EU Blue Card?**
A: The EU Blue Card is for highly skilled employees and requires a university degree and a job offer with a salary at least 1.5 times the national average. Shopkeepers are typically self-employed, making them ineligible. Instead, non-EU nationals can apply for a National Visa for self-employment or a work permit if employed by a retail company. Each route has specific requirements.

**Q: What are the main requirements to become a shopkeeper in Greece?**
A: No formal qualifications are mandatory, but knowledge of retail management, Greek language skills, and familiarity with local regulations are important. You must register a business with the tax authorities, obtain a business license, and comply with tax and labor laws. Many shopkeepers gain experience through apprenticeships or previous work in retail.
