**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generating preliminary diagnostic suggestions based on symptoms and tongue/pulse images.
- Automatic recommendation of commonly used herbal formulas or acupuncture prescriptions
- Organizing and basic analysis of patient health records
- Automated responses for appointment management and patient inquiries

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: Use image recognition to analyze tongue coating, complexion, etc., to assist in syndrome differentiation.
- Personalized treatment plans: recommend optimized herbal combinations or acupuncture plans based on big data.
- Knowledge retrieval: quickly obtain the latest research literature and classic cases
- Patient education: auto-generate illustrated wellness advice and rehabilitation plans

**Human moat:**

- Holistic differentiation and individualized adjustment for complex cases.

**Skills to build:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical annual salary for Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Greece?**
A: Salaries vary significantly by specialization and experience. Entry-level positions may earn around €15,000–€20,000 per year, while experienced professionals in private practice can earn €25,000–€35,000. Public sector roles are less common, with lower pay but added benefits. Self-employment income depends on client base and reputation.

**Q: What official routes exist for non-EU citizens to work as Traditional and Complementary Medicine Professionals in Greece?**
A: Non-EU professionals can apply for a national work visa sponsored by a Greek employer, or an EU Blue Card if they hold a recognized university degree and meet salary thresholds. For Blue Card, qualifications must be equivalent to Greek standards. Self-employment requires a business visa and proof of sufficient funds. Recognition of professional titles is handled by the relevant regulatory body.

**Q: Are Traditional and Complementary Medicine professions regulated in Greece?**
A: Yes, some professions are regulated. For example, acupuncturists must register with the Greek Acupuncture Society and may need certification. Herbalists face less strict regulation, but all practitioners should adhere to general health laws. The Ministry of Health oversees registration. Without official recognition, practicing legally is difficult, so verifying requirements with local authorities is essential.
