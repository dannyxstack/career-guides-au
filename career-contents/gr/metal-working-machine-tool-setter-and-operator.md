**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- CNC machine programming (G-code/M-code) automatically generated by AI
- Real-time monitoring of processing and tool life prediction
- Automatic measurement and feedback compensation of workpiece dimensions
- Optimization of batch processing paths for standard parts.
- AI-assisted equipment fault diagnosis

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Simulation and optimization of complex part machining processes using AI.
- Using augmented reality (AR) for equipment debugging and maintenance
- AI-driven quality prediction system improves yield rate
- Digital twin technology enables virtual commissioning and training
- Smart scheduling system improves efficiency in high-mix, low-volume production

**Human moat:**

- Experience in debugging non-standard, high-precision parts
- Understand drawing design intent and propose process improvement suggestions
- Hands-on ability to troubleshoot on-site sudden failures
- Programming and operating combined machining (turning, milling, drilling) on a single workpiece
- Cross-departmental collaboration and communication with designers, quality engineers, etc.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a metal working machine tool setter or operator in Greece?**
A: Entry-level operators earn around €12,000–€15,000 annually. Experienced setters can earn €18,000–€24,000 per year. Salaries vary by region and industry, with higher pay in manufacturing hubs like Athens and Thessaloniki. Overtime and shift allowances may increase total compensation.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as metal working machine tool setters in Greece via a work visa?**
A: Yes, non-EU workers can apply for a national work visa (Type D) sponsored by a Greek employer, or an EU Blue Card if they hold a university degree or five years of relevant professional experience. The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled by a local or EU candidate.

**Q: What training is required to become a metal working machine tool setter or operator in Greece?**
A: Completion of vocational training at a public IEK (Vocational Training Institute) or apprenticeship through OAED (Manpower Employment Organization) is common. Certificates in CNC operation or metalworking are valued. On-the-job training is also widely provided. Continuing education is recommended to keep up with new technologies.
