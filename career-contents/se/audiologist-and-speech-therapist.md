# Audiologist and Speech Therapist — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2266 (ISCO08)**

Audiologists and speech therapists in Sweden (ISCO-08 2266) are licensed healthcare professionals who diagnose and treat hearing, balance, and communication disorders. They work in hospitals, rehabilitation centers, schools, and private practices. The Swedish healthcare system faces a growing demand for these services due to an aging population and increased awareness of hearing and speech impairments. Public sector employment is stable, with opportunities in both clinical and preventive care. The profession is regulated, requiring a university degree and registration with the National Board of Health and Welfare (Socialstyrelsen).

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,861. The employment outlook for audiologists and speech therapists in Sweden is very positive. An aging population and higher survival rates for premature infants and stroke patients increase the need for rehabilitation services. The Swedish Public Employment Service predicts a shortage in both professions over the next five years, especially in rural areas. Digitalization, including teletherapy and advanced hearing aids, is creating new roles. However, competition is lower than in many other healthcare fields, making it a stable career choice.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with clinical work after graduation. Audiologists and speech therapists can specialize in areas such as pediatric audiology, cochlear implants, or voice disorders. Advanced roles include clinical supervision, management of rehabilitation departments, or research at universities. Some move into private practice or product development for hearing aid companies. The Swedish Association of Audiologists and the Swedish Association of Speech and Language Pathologists offer continuing education and networking opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Initial screening and automated report generation for standard hearing tests
- Preliminary analysis of voice recordings and marking of abnormal patterns
- Automatic scoring and preliminary suggestions for dysphagia screening questionnaires.
- Standardized speech recognition and feedback for speech therapy exercises
- Basic content generation and customization of patient education materials

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted diagnosis: using deep learning models to analyze audiograms, speech spectra, and images to improve diagnostic accuracy
- Enhanced remote therapy: AI-driven real-time voice feedback system improves patient self-training at home
- Personalized treatment plan design: generate customized rehabilitation plans based on patient data (e.g., voice samples, hearing loss patterns)
- Data-Driven Prognostic Assessment: Analyze large clinical datasets to predict treatment responses and optimize treatment pathways
- Multilingual support: AI real-time translation and accent adaptation expand coverage to multicultural populations

**Human moat:**

- Clinical judgment and human empathy for complex communication barriers.
- Empathy for building trust with patients and their families
- Collaborative decision-making and case management in multidisciplinary teams
- Clinical reasoning skills for rare or comorbid conditions
- Ethical decision-making and privacy protection (involving sensitive health data)

**Skills to build:**

- AI/machine learning basics (how to explain model outputs for clinical diagnosis)
- Remote health assessment and treatment technology (platform usage, data security)
- Biomedical signal processing (speech and hearing-related data analysis)
- Patient data privacy and ethics (HIPAA/PIPEDA compliance)
- Multilingual and cross-cultural communication (adapted to Canada's multicultural patient population).
- Human-machine collaborative treatment design (integrating AI tools into traditional therapies)

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Teletherapy
- Digital hearing aids
- Early intervention

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals with strong communication skills and empathy, comfortable working closely with patients of all ages
- Detail-oriented people interested in both clinical care and technology (e.g., hearing aids, speech software)
- Those seeking a stable, in-demand profession in the Swedish healthcare system with good work-life balance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the starting salary for a newly graduated audiologist or speech therapist in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by region and employer. Typically, entry-level salaries range around 30,000–35,000 SEK per month before tax, with increases for specialization or managerial roles.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA audiologist or speech therapist get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the terms of collective agreements (salary, insurance, etc.). Since the profession is on the shortage list, the process is streamlined. You must also obtain a professional license from Socialstyrelsen, which requires qualification recognition by UHR.

**Q: Is knowledge of Swedish mandatory to work as an audiologist or speech therapist in Sweden?**
A: Yes, because patient communication and documentation require proficiency in Swedish. Most employers require at least C1 level. Some degree programs in English exist, but clinical placements demand Swedish. Language tests may be part of the license process.
