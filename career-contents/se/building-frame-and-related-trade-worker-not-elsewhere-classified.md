# Building Frame and Related Trades Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7119 (ISCO08)**

Building Frame and Related Trades Workers NEC cover a range of construction roles such as carpenters, bricklayers, plasterers, and concreters. In Sweden, these skilled trades are vital to the construction sector, which is experiencing robust demand driven by urbanisation, housing shortages, and major infrastructure projects. Workers typically have vocational training or apprenticeships and work on new builds, renovations, and civil engineering sites. The occupation is heavily regulated by collective agreements regarding pay and conditions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving from apprentice to fully qualified tradesperson, then to specialist roles (e.g., in timber framing, restoration) or supervisory positions like foreman or site manager. Many experienced workers become self-employed or start their own small construction firms. Certification for specific tasks (e.g., scaffolding, working at heights) can enhance employability. Continuing professional development is valued, especially in sustainable construction methods.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using laser rangefinders and digital levels for conventional measurement and marking
- Manually calculating material lists and cutting lists based on BIM models.
- Filling in paper work logs and progress reports
- Repetitive template setup and dismantling work (partially done by robots)
- Manually inspect and adjust component alignment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use BIM and AR glasses for precise positioning and installation guidance
- Structural health monitoring via drones and sensors
- Using AI-assisted design to optimize frame structure layout
- Use automated cutting and welding equipment to improve efficiency
- Coordinate workflows for different trades through digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Flexible response and problem-solving in complex on-site environments
- Manual adjustments and customization for irregular or old structures
- Monitor automated equipment operation and handle anomalies
- Communication and safety supervision responsibilities in multi-trade coordination

**Skills to build:**

- BIM modeling and construction simulation software (such as Revit, Tekla)
- Operation of digital surveying and setting-out tools (e.g., total stations, laser scanners)
- Automated equipment maintenance and programming basics (e.g., robotic welding, CNC)
- Safety regulations and sustainable construction practices
- Data analysis and report generation tools (e.g. Excel, Power BI)
- Reading English technical documentation (for international projects)

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable building
- renovation
- infrastructure
- urban development

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building frame worker?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by region, experience, and specialization. They generally range between 25,000 and 35,000 SEK per month before tax. Always refer to the current collective agreement for accurate figures.

**Q: Can I get a work permit for this job?**
A: Yes, if you are a non-EU/EEA citizen. You must have a job offer that meets the collective-agreement salary threshold (approximately 27,000 SEK/month). The employer must also provide insurance and report the vacancy to the Public Employment Service first (for most occupations). EU/EEA citizens can work freely.

**Q: Is this occupation on the shortage list?**
A: Yes, many building trades (e.g., carpenters, bricklayers) are on Sweden's shortage occupation list, indicating high employer demand. This can facilitate work permit processing but does not waive requirements.
