# Ships' Deck Crew — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 8350 (ISCO08)**

Ships' deck crew perform manual and operational tasks on vessels, including navigation watchkeeping, mooring, cargo handling, and maintenance. In Sweden, this occupation is predominantly found in merchant shipping, ferry services, and offshore support. The Swedish maritime sector is regulated by strict safety standards and requires STCW certification. Deck crew work under the supervision of officers and are essential for safe vessel operations, with employment concentrated along major ports like Gothenburg and Stockholm.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as deckhand or ordinary seaman, advancing to able seaman, boatswain, and eventually deck officer through maritime education. Swedish seafarers often combine vocational training with onboard experience. Pathways include specialization in safety, navigation, or offshore operations. Many deck crew transition to shore-based roles in port operations or maritime administration.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic navigation and route optimization during navigation watch.
- Sensor-based collision avoidance and alert monitoring
- Automation of cargo loading and stability calculations
- AI tools for daily log and report generation
- Predictive maintenance diagnosis for equipment performance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making system enhances berthing safety under complex port weather conditions
- Augmented reality (AR) systems used for repair guidance and system inspection
- AI-driven energy management system optimizes fuel consumption
- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data fusion provides full maritime situational awareness
- Assisted hull external inspection and rust removal using drones and remote-controlled robots

**Human moat:**

- Rapid decision-making and team coordination skills in emergency situations
- Complex manual operations, such as cable mooring and maintenance
- Unstructured judgment of sea conditions and human factors
- Communication and responsibility as a team leader
- Execution of safety culture in compliance with maritime regulations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and AI tool usage (e.g., route optimization software)
- Data analysis and dashboard interpretation skills
- Skills in remotely operating drones or robots.
- Advanced maritime regulations and certification updates
- Soft skills: cross-cultural communication and crisis management
- Digital forensics and cybersecurity fundamentals

**Key growth areas:**

- Offshore wind
- Ferry transport
- Coastal shipping
- Maritime tourism

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for deck crew in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements between unions and employer associations. They vary by rank, experience, and vessel type. For current figures, check the Swedish Transport Agency or seafarer unions like Seko or Sjöbefälsföreningen.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as deck crew in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but they need a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the collective agreement terms (minimum pay and conditions) and a work permit. The employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled locally. EU/EEA citizens do not need a permit.

**Q: Is there a shortage of deck crew in Sweden?**
A: While not on the official shortage list, there is a structural demand due to retirements and a limited number of Swedish seafarers. Opportunities exist for skilled workers, especially in special segments like offshore wind.
