# Odd-job Person — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Odd-job persons in Sweden perform a variety of manual or routine tasks, often temporary or seasonal, such as cleaning, moving, gardening, or simple maintenance. This occupation requires minimal formal qualifications and serves as a flexible entry point into the labour market, particularly for students, retirees, and newly arrived immigrants. Jobs are commonly arranged through temporary staffing agencies or informal networks, with wages typically low and hours irregular. The sector is highly cyclical and sensitive to economic fluctuations, but provides essential labour in areas like hospitality, retail, and household services.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 21,439. The employment outlook for odd-job persons in Sweden is stable but not expanding significantly. Demand fluctuates with the business cycle and seasonal needs, especially in tourism and agriculture. Automation and digital platforms (gig economy) are reshaping the market, increasing short-term contract availability but reducing job security. The ongoing labour shortage in skilled occupations may indirectly boost demand for helpers to support specialists. Overall, competition for these roles is moderate, with a constant inflow of workers due to low entry barriers.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for odd-job persons is limited; most workers stay in temporary roles or move to similar low-skilled positions. With additional training, some transition into specific trades (e.g., painting, plumbing) or customer service. The lack of standardized career paths means advancement often depends on networking, gaining references, or acquiring skills like a driver's license or basic construction knowledge. Many use odd jobs as a stepping stone to more stable employment or combine multiple part-time positions for a full-time income.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Temporary staffing
- Gig economy
- Seasonal work
- Household maintenance

Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an odd-job person in Sweden?**
A: The hourly wage for odd-job persons varies widely between 120–150 SEK per hour before tax, depending on the sector and region. Annual income is irregular; most workers do not reach the median wage. Collective agreements in cleaning or construction set higher minimums, but many temporary contracts pay at the lower end.

**Q: Can I obtain a work permit as an odd-job person in Sweden?**
A: It is very difficult. Sweden's work permit system requires a job offer with salary and conditions at least matching collective agreements, typically around 13,000 SEK/month (2023). Most odd jobs pay below this threshold or are too short-term. Seasonal agricultural work may be possible under a special quota, but competition is high.

**Q: Do I need any formal education to work as an odd-job person?**
A: No formal education is required. Basic literacy and numeracy are helpful, and some tasks may demand a driver's license or safety training. Employers primarily value reliability and physical ability.
