# Plasterer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7123 (ISCO08)**

Plasterers in Sweden apply plaster, render, and other coatings to walls and ceilings in both residential and commercial construction. The occupation is part of the broader construction sector, which is sensitive to economic cycles but maintains steady demand due to ongoing renovation projects, energy-efficiency upgrades, and new urban developments. Skilled plasterers are needed for both interior and exterior finishes, and familiarity with modern materials and techniques can enhance job prospects. The work is physical and requires precision, with opportunities to specialize in decorative or sustainable applications.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for plasterers in Sweden typically involves moving from apprentice to journeyman, then to foreman or site manager. Many experienced plasterers start their own businesses, especially in the renovation niche. Specialization in decorative plasterwork or sustainable materials can lead to higher demand. Further education in construction management or building techniques can open roles in project coordination or quality control. Union membership is common, and the trade is recognized for its craftsmanship, with opportunities for lifelong learning and skill development.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive wall construction (e.g., straight walls, standard brick walls) is taken over by bricklaying robots or automatic laying equipment.
- Material handling and mortar mixing via automated conveyor systems
- Design and layout of simple masonry structures are automatically generated by BIM software
- On-site measurement and positioning using laser scanners and AI correction, reducing manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI visual inspection system assists in checking masonry verticality and mortar joint uniformity, improving quality inspection efficiency
- BIM modeling tools help bricklayers precisely lay bricks in complex curved structures
- Augmented reality (AR) glasses overlay construction drawings, reducing reading errors.
- AI brick layout algorithms optimize material usage and reduce waste

**Human moat:**

- Complex irregular masonry (curved, hollow, historical building restoration) cannot be standardized
- Flexible operation in unstructured environments such as bad weather and confined spaces
- Non-standard requirements from owners or architects for aesthetics and texture, relying on craftsman experience
- On-site safety management and emergency judgment, e.g., support, temporary fixing risks

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated bricklaying equipment and collaborating with robots
- Proficiency in BIM modeling and CAD drawing interpretation.
- Learn drone surveying and laser scanning data analysis
- Proficient in green building and energy-efficient masonry material construction
- Improve project management and multi-trade coordination skills
- Familiarity with building regulations and accessible masonry standards

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Energy Efficiency
- Decorative Plaster
- Urban Construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How does the salary for plasterers compare to other construction trades?**
A: Plasterer salaries are comparable to other skilled trades in construction, such as carpenters or bricklayers, and are negotiated through collective agreements.

**Q: What are the requirements for a non-EU plasterer to work in Sweden?**
A: A job offer from a Swedish employer, terms at least at collective agreement level, and proof of qualifications. Processing time varies.

**Q: What training is needed to become a plasterer in Sweden?**
A: A vocational upper secondary education in construction or an apprenticeship is common. Practical experience is highly valued.
