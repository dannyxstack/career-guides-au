# Other Language Teacher — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2353 (ISCO08)**

Other Language Teachers in Sweden instruct adults or teenagers in languages other than Swedish or English, typically within municipal adult education (Komvux), folk high schools, or private language institutes. They develop students' linguistic skills for integration, work, or personal development. Demand varies by language and is closely tied to immigration trends and global mobility. Non-EU/EEA teachers require a work permit, and proficiency in Swedish is often necessary for classroom management. Salaries are set by collective agreements, averaging around 420,000–480,000 SEK annually. The profession offers stable employment for less common languages like Arabic or Chinese, but competition is higher for popular languages like Spanish or French.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a teaching position in adult education or language schools. With experience, teachers can become senior instructors, curriculum coordinators, or heads of language departments. Some move into university teaching or teacher training after completing a master's degree. Others specialize in materials development or start private tutoring businesses. Continuing professional development (CPD) in digital pedagogy and specific student groups (e.g., asylum seekers) enhances prospects. Licensure (lärarlegitimation) is required for municipal adult education, but private schools may offer flexibility. A career shift to interpretation or translation is also possible.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary for an Other Language Teacher in Sweden?**
A: The average annual salary is approximately 420,000–480,000 SEK (pre-tax), based on collective agreements. Starting salaries are around 30,000–35,000 SEK/month, while experienced teachers can earn up to 40,000–45,000 SEK/month.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to teach languages in Sweden as a non-EU citizen?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens need a job offer and a work permit (Arbetstillstånd). The employer must demonstrate that the position cannot be filled within the EU/EEA (labour market test) unless the language is on a shortage list. The permit is tied to the specific employer.

**Q: Is a Swedish teaching license required for language teaching in private schools?**
A: No, it is only mandatory for municipal adult education and public schools. Private language institutes can hire teachers without a license, but a relevant degree and language proficiency are expected.
