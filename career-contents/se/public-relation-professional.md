# Public Relations Professional — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2432 (ISCO08)**

Public relations professionals in Sweden plan and manage communication between organizations and their audiences, including media, the public, and stakeholders. With a strong emphasis on digital channels and crisis communication, the role has evolved with the rise of social media and sustainability reporting. Most positions require fluency in Swedish and English, and the field is concentrated in Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö. Employment is stable, with opportunities in corporate, agency, and public sector roles.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts from a specialist role (e.g., PR coordinator) to manager (PR manager, head of communications), and eventually to director (communications director, chief communications officer). Many professionals specialise in areas like internal communications, media relations, or digital strategy. Cross-sector mobility is common, and advanced degrees or certifications in strategic communication can accelerate advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Media monitoring and information collection (AI automatic capture of public opinion)
- Drafting initial press releases (LLM-generated standard text)
- Scheduled social media posts and basic replies (automation tools)
- Data analysis report generation (AI auto-summarizes trends)

**Where AI augments the role:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a PR professional in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location. Entry-level positions range from 30,000 to 35,000 SEK/month, while experienced managers can earn 50,000-70,000 SEK/month. Specialists in crisis or digital PR may command higher pay.

**Q: Do I need to speak Swedish to get a work permit as a PR professional?**
A: While not a formal requirement for the permit, most employers require fluent Swedish due to the nature of the work. Agencies and international companies may accept English-only for certain roles, but competition is stiff.

**Q: Is there a demand for PR professionals outside Stockholm?**
A: Yes, but to a lesser extent. Gothenburg and Malmö have growing PR scenes, and public sector roles are spread across the country. Remote work is increasing, though on-site presence is often expected.
