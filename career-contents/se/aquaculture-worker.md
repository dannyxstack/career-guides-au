# Aquaculture Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6221 (ISCO08)**

Aquaculture workers in Sweden cultivate fish, shellfish, and aquatic plants in coastal waters, lakes, and recirculating systems. The industry is growing as Sweden aims to expand sustainable domestic seafood production, driven by environmental concerns and food security. Workers perform feeding, monitoring health, harvesting, and maintenance of equipment. The sector includes salmon farming, mussel cultivation, and emerging species like arctic char. Most jobs are in rural coastal regions such as Norrland, Bohuslän, and the Baltic Sea islands.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Progression typically starts as an entry-level worker, advancing to team lead, farm manager, or specialist in hatchery operations, fish health, or sustainability. With further education (e.g., bachelor's in marine biology or aquaculture engineering), workers can move into R&D, consulting, or regulatory roles. Many employers offer internal training for certifications such as feed management or water treatment.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding system replaces manual timed feeding
- Water quality monitoring sensors and AI analysis replace manual sampling and testing.
- Underwater drone inspections replace manual observation of fish school status.
- Automated harvesting equipment replaces manual picking and sorting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted disease early warning system enhances early identification and prevention efficiency.
- Data-driven precision feeding decisions to optimize feed utilization
- Digital twin aquaculture model assists in environmental parameter regulation
- AI analysis of growth curves to optimize harvest time windows

**Human moat:**

- On-site judgement and emergency response to sudden illness or abnormal environmental conditions
- Intuition and experience for the overall ecological balance of farming systems
- Manual observation and early intervention for abnormal animal behavior (AI struggles to capture subtle signs)
- Cross-disciplinary coordination and decision-making with experts such as veterinarians and nutritionists

**Skills to build:**

- Sensor and automation equipment operation and maintenance
- Data processing and basic statistics (interpreting breeding data)
- Use of AI Tools (e.g., Disease Diagnosis Applications)
- IoT system management and troubleshooting
- Biosecurity and sustainable aquaculture practices
- Basic programming and scripting (automated workflow customization)

**Key growth areas:**

- Recirculating Aquaculture Systems (RAS)
- Salmon farming
- Sustainable seafood
- Automation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for an aquaculture worker in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level workers earn around 25,000–30,000 SEK per month (300,000–360,000 SEK annually). Experienced workers and specialists can earn 30,000–38,000 SEK per month (360,000–456,000 SEK annually). Salaries vary by region, employer, and qualifications.

**Q: Can a non-EU/EEA aquaculture worker get a Swedish work permit?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement wages and terms. The employer must advertise the position in Sweden/EU first (labour market test). The permit is tied to that employer. After 4 years, you may apply for permanent residence.

**Q: What training is required to become an aquaculture worker in Sweden?**
A: No formal education is mandatory, but vocational training in aquaculture (1 year) or a bachelor's in marine biology is highly valued. Many employers prefer certified experience. Licenses for boat operation and food safety are often required.
