# Judge — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2612 (ISCO08)**

Judges in Sweden preside over courts, ensuring impartial application of law, and are typically appointed from among experienced lawyers after rigorous judicial training and assessment, reflecting the country's commitment to judicial independence and rule of law.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a law degree (juristexamen), followed by a notary period (notariegång) at a court, then appointment as deputy judge (fiskal) and later permanent judge. Advancement to appellate or supreme courts requires exceptional performance and seniority, with salaries rising significantly.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a judge in Sweden?**
A: Salaries for judges vary by court level and experience, ranging from approximately 700,000 SEK per year for a district court judge to over 1,200,000 SEK for a Supreme Court justice, but exact figures depend on collective agreements.

**Q: Can a foreigner become a judge in Sweden?**
A: Generally, judges must be Swedish citizens who have completed a Swedish legal education and judicial training. Non-citizens may work as legal advisors or prosecutors, but permanent judge appointments are restricted to citizens.

**Q: What is the typical career path to becoming a judge?**
A: After earning a juristexamen, candidates serve a notary period (notariegång) in a district court, then apply for appointment as a deputy judge (fiskal). After several years, they may become permanent judges, with further advancement through seniority and assessments.
