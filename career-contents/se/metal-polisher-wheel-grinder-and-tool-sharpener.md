# Metal Polisher and Tool Sharpener — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7224 (ISCO08)**

Metal polishers, wheel grinders and tool sharpeners in Sweden are skilled tradespeople who use abrasive wheels, belts, and hand tools to finish metal surfaces and sharpen cutting tools. They work in manufacturing, engineering, and tool maintenance sectors, often in small to medium-sized workshops or large industrial plants. The occupation requires precision, attention to detail, and knowledge of different metal types and grinding techniques. In Sweden, vocational training through upper secondary school or adult education is common, and many workers enter via apprenticeship. The role is essential for producing high-quality metal components and maintaining tooling efficiency.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational training program or apprenticeship, leading to a journeyman position. Experienced workers can advance to team leader, quality inspector, or workshop manager. Specialization in CNC grinding, precision tool sharpening, or diamond polishing opens further opportunities. Some choose to start their own business. Continuous learning in new materials and automated systems is valued. The path is linear, with experience and additional certifications improving pay and responsibility.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive metal surface polishing and grinding operations
- Use automated grinding machines for batch workpiece processing.
- Grinding wheel dressing and balancing based on preset parameters
- dimensional inspection and pass/fail determination of simple workpieces
- Automatic grinding of standard tool edges.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted process parameter optimization: automatically recommends grinding parameters based on workpiece material and precision requirements
- Predictive maintenance: AI analyzes equipment vibration and temperature data to provide early fault warnings
- Visual inspection: AI system checks surface finish in real-time, reducing manual inspection
- Collaborative robots: human-robot collaboration to move heavy workpieces, reducing labor intensity
- Digital Twin: Simulate machining processes, shorten trial cutting cycles

**Human moat:**

- Manual polishing and finishing of non-standard workpieces
- Manual adjustment and grinding of complex-shaped molds and tools
- On-site judgment and process adjustment for material defects
- Manual repair and precision restoration of aging equipment
- Communicate with clients on customized surface treatment requirements

**Skills to build:**

- CNC grinding programming and operation
- CAD/CAM software (e.g., Mastercam)
- Fundamentals of robotics and automation system integration.
- Data analysis and quality control (SPC)
- Additive manufacturing post-processing techniques
- Electrical and mechanical maintenance skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision manufacturing
- Tool maintenance
- Metal fabrication
- Automated grinding

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a metal polisher in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by experience and region. Entry-level workers earn around 24,000–27,000 SEK per month, while experienced professionals can reach 30,000–35,000 SEK. Overtime and shift allowances may add to income.

**Q: Can a non-EU metal polisher get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the collective agreement salary and terms. The permit is employer-tied and initially granted for up to two years, renewable. You must also have valid vocational qualifications or experience.
