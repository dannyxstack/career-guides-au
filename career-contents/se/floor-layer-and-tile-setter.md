# Floor Layers and Tile Setters — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7122 (ISCO08)**

Floor Layers and Tile Setters install and repair floor coverings, tiles, and stone flooring in residential and commercial buildings. In Sweden, this trade is in demand due to ongoing construction and renovation projects, particularly in urban areas. Many workers are self-employed or employed by small firms, with a significant share of foreign-born workers, especially from other EU countries. The job requires physical stamina and precision. Most workers have vocational training from upper secondary school or adult education, and the occupation is regulated with mandatory certifications for certain tasks like asbestos handling.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving from apprentice to skilled craftsman, then to supervisor or specialist in areas like stone restoration or underfloor heating. Many experienced workers start their own businesses. Further training can lead to roles in project management or teaching vocational courses. Certifications in waterproofing, fireproofing, or handling hazardous materials enhance prospects. Union membership is common, and wages are set by collective agreements.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic measurement of room dimensions and material quantity calculation, AI can quickly generate optimal layout plans
- Automatic cutting of tiles/stone, CNC waterjet or laser cutting replaces manual cutting
- Automatic detection and adjustment solution generation for ground flatness, reducing manual trial laying
- Inventory management and material ordering processes, AI analyzes project requirements to automatically place orders

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to view tiling blueprints and level indicators in real time, improving accuracy
- AI-assisted design of complex patterns and parquet, generating diverse options for clients
- Work collaboratively with automatic mortar mixing and plastering robots to improve efficiency
- AI-driven project progress prediction and scheduling to optimize multi-trade collaboration

**Human moat:**

- Manual cutting and fine placement in irregular areas and corners
- Removal of old flooring, base treatment, and leveling judgment for uneven floors.
- Communicate design preferences with clients and provide personalized advice
- Flexible problem-solving ability for on-site unexpected issues.

**Skills to build:**

- Digital Measurement Tools and BIM Software Operations
- CNC cutting machine programming and maintenance
- Use of augmented reality (AR) tools for construction assistance.
- Project management and client communication skills
- Knowledge of sustainable materials and environmentally friendly construction methods
- Basic electrical and mechanical troubleshooting

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation Projects
- Energy-Efficient Flooring
- Heated Floor Systems
- Large-Format Tiles

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a floor layer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by region and experience. As of 2024, entry-level floor layers earn around 28,000–33,000 SEK per month, while experienced specialists can earn 35,000–45,000 SEK. Overtime and project bonuses can increase earnings. Always check the current collective agreement for your sector.

**Q: Can a non-EU tile setter get a work permit in Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the prevailing wage and terms set by collective agreements. Your employer must show that the position could not be filled from within the EU/EEA. Work permits are granted for up to two years with possibility of extension and eventual permanent residence.

**Q: Is Swedish language required for this job?**
A: While not always mandatory, knowing Swedish is increasingly important for safety communication and customer interaction. Many employers prefer at least basic Swedish. Language courses are available through SFI (Swedish for Immigrants) free of charge for residents.
