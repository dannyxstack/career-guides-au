# Butcher, Fishmonger and Related Food Preparer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7511 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, butchers, fishmongers and related food preparers work in slaughterhouses, meat processing plants, fish markets, grocery stores and restaurants. The profession requires knowledge of food safety and hygiene regulations. Employment is concentrated in rural areas near livestock farms and coastal regions for fish. The sector faces challenges from automation and centralization of slaughterhouses, but demand for artisanal and locally sourced products provides niche opportunities. Many workers are employed by large companies like HKScan, Atria, and ICA.

The Swedish Public Employment Service forecasts stable demand for butchers and fishmongers over the next five years, with a slight decline in slaughterhouse jobs due to automation but growth in retail and specialty shops. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for butchers and fishmongers typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training. With experience, one can become a master butcher or specialize in charcuterie, fish filleting, or quality control. Some move into management of meat or fish departments in stores, or start their own butcher shop. Further education in food technology can lead to roles in product development or food safety inspection. The Swedish meat industry offers structured career paths through the butchers' guild (Slaktareförbundet).

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch cutting and segmentation of standard parts
- Repetitive motions in deboning and skinning processes
- Basic processing like meat grinding and sausage stuffing
- Weighing, labeling, and packaging line operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI vision systems for meat grading and defect detection
- Optimizing cutting plans through data analysis to reduce waste
- Manage meat preservation and turnover using smart inventory systems
- Using augmented reality (AR) to assist training new employees in cutting techniques

**Human moat:**

- Hand-finishing special parts and custom cutting according to client requirements
- Sensory judgment of meat freshness, texture, and abnormalities
- Flexibility in handling unexpected orders and personalized needs.
- Provide cooking advice and demonstrate cutting techniques to customers

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of automated cutting equipment
- Food quality and safety management systems (e.g., HACCP)
- Basic data analysis and inventory management software
- Customer service and communication skills
- Fine hand cutting (for high-end customisation)
- Food safety regulations and traceability knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Artisanal butchery
- Local food movement
- Sustainable seafood
- Food safety

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a butcher in Sweden?**
A: The average monthly salary for butchers in Sweden is around 27,000-32,000 SEK before tax, depending on experience and location. Senior butchers can earn up to 37,000 SEK. Overtime and shift work may increase earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a butcher in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. You need a job offer from a Swedish employer with terms that meet collective agreements and a salary of at least 29,000 SEK/month (2025 threshold). The employer must advertise the job in Sweden first (labour market test). Since butchers are not on the shortage list, the process is competitive.

**Q: Do I need formal training to work as a butcher in Sweden?**
A: No, it is not mandatory, but employers prefer vocational training or apprenticeship. A food safety certificate is legally required for handling meat. Upper secondary vocational programmes are free and widely available.
