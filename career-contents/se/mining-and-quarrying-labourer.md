# Mining and Quarrying Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9311 (ISCO08)**

Mining and quarrying labourers perform manual tasks in extracting minerals, rock, and stone in Sweden's mining and quarrying industry, primarily in the northern counties. They operate and maintain basic equipment, assist in blasting, and ensure safety. The role demands physical stamina and willingness to work in remote, often underground environments. Labourers are employed by large mining companies and smaller quarry operators.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves gaining experience and moving into more skilled roles such as drill operator, blasting supervisor, or maintenance technician. Formal training through vocational courses or apprenticeships can accelerate advancement. Some labourers transition to environmental monitoring or safety roles. The industry values on-the-job experience and safety certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Ore handling and transportation: replaced by autonomous trucks and conveyor belts
- Equipment cleaning: handled by automatic cleaning robots
- Site maintenance and inspection: performed by drones and AI vision systems
- Manual crushing and screening: completed by automated crushers
- Safety monitoring records: AI sensors and automated reporting systems replace manual work

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated equipment (e.g., remote-controlled excavators, drills).
- Quick troubleshooting of equipment issues using AI fault diagnosis systems
- Optimizing mining processes using digital twin technology
- Real-time ore positioning and quality data obtained through AR glasses

**Human moat:**

- Emergency judgment in complex geological environments.
- On-site coordination for collaborative operation of multiple devices
- Ability to quickly resolve non-routine faults
- Experience working with specialized roles such as blasters

**Skills to build:**

- Automated mining equipment operation and maintenance
- Basic data analysis and AI scheduling system usage
- Drone/robot inspection and control
- Safety protocols and digital report generation
- Mechanical maintenance basics (hydraulics, electrical)
- Introduction to collaborative robot programming

**Key growth areas:**

- automation
- safety
- mechanization
- environmental technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a mining labourer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by region, experience, and shift schedule. Labourers generally earn hourly wages that increase with experience and certifications. Entry-level positions may earn slightly above the industry minimum, while experienced labourers earn more.

**Q: Can I get a work permit for a non-EU country as a mining labourer?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. You need a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement standards. The Swedish Migration Agency assesses whether the role meets the labour market needs test; low-skilled positions often face higher scrutiny. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: What are the advancement opportunities?**
A: Labourers can progress to machine operator, foreman, or safety inspector after a few years of experience and additional training. Some pursue vocational education to become mining technicians or geologists.
