# Cook — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 5120 (ISCO08)**

Cooks prepare and cook food in restaurants, hotels, hospitals, schools, and other catering establishments. In Sweden, the occupation is in steady demand due to a strong hospitality sector and a growing focus on sustainable and locally sourced ingredients. Many cooks work in fast-paced environments, often requiring evening and weekend shifts. The profession offers opportunities for creativity and specialization, such as in pastry, plant-based cuisine, or fine dining.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 27,092. Employment for cooks in Sweden is expected to remain stable, with moderate growth driven by population increase and tourism. However, competition for positions in top restaurants and urban areas can be high. The industry faces challenges with retaining staff due to irregular hours and physical demands, creating opportunities for reliable and skilled cooks. Regional shortages exist, particularly in northern Sweden, where restaurants struggle to recruit.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from assistant cook or kitchen helper, advancing to cook, then to chef de partie, sous chef, and eventually head chef. Some cooks move into specialized roles such as pastry chef or sushi chef. Continuing education through vocational courses in gastronomy, food safety, or management can accelerate advancement. Entrepreneurship is also common, with many cooks opening their own restaurants or food trucks.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard repetitive cooking tasks such as frying, stir-frying, and other fixed-procedure dishes can be completed by AI-controlled robots.
- Recipe and ingredient management: AI systems can automatically optimise recipes, calculate ingredient ratios, and generate purchase lists.
- Inventory counting and ordering: AI vision and barcode scanning systems monitor stock in real time and place orders automatically.
- Customer ordering and order transmission: self-service kiosks and AI voice systems replace waitstaff taking and passing orders to kitchen.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Menu innovation and dish development: AI analyzes dietary trends, ingredient pairings, and nutritional data to assist chefs in designing new dishes.
- Cost control and food procurement: AI systems forecast demand, optimize purchase volumes, reduce waste, and increase profits
- Quality control and consistency: AI vision systems monitor the color, size, and plating of each dish to ensure standard output.
- Kitchen scheduling and workflow optimization: AI automatically schedules based on customer flow predictions and coordinates equipment usage to improve efficiency.

**Human moat:**

- Fine manual skills like knife work and heat control: AI cannot replicate a chef's intuition for ingredient texture and craftsmanship.
- Creativity and personalized customization: improvise flavors and plating based on guest needs, showcasing artistry and human touch
- Tasting and flavor assessment: human perception of subtle flavors (e.g., acidity, umami) far surpasses any sensor.
- Kitchen leadership and team collaboration: managing kitchen staff, handling emergencies (e.g., excessive heat, customer complaints), requiring interpersonal coordination.

**Skills to build:**

- Learn to use AI kitchen management systems: e.g., inventory, purchasing, scheduling software such as Mozza or KitchenCUT.
- Master food science and nutrition analysis: use AI tools to design healthy menus meeting special dietary needs.
- Data interpretation and cost modeling: adjusting menu pricing and supply strategies based on sales data.
- Enhance creative cooking and fusion cuisine skills: personalize services beyond standard dishes.
- Learn to operate and maintain robotic equipment: such as automatic stir-fryers, smart ovens, etc.
- Enhance leadership and communication skills: manage teams, train new chefs on AI tools.

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable cuisine
- Plant-based cooking
- Fine dining
- Food tech

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are the working hours for a cook in Sweden?**
A: Working hours typically include evenings, weekends, and holidays. Many cooks work in shifts, often totaling 40 hours per week, but schedules can be irregular, especially in restaurants.

**Q: How are salaries determined for cooks in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective bargaining agreements and vary based on experience, region, and establishment type. Starting pay for assistant cooks is often around industry minimum, while experienced chefs earn more.

**Q: Can I work as a cook in Sweden as a non-EU citizen?**
A: Yes, you can obtain a work permit if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the applicable collective agreement salary and working conditions. The employer must also prove that the position could not be filled from within the EU/EEA.
