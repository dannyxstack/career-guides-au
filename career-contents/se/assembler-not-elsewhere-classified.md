# Assembler Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 8219 (ISCO08)**

Assemblers not elsewhere classified perform manual assembly of components and parts across various industries, including automotive, electronics, and general manufacturing. In Sweden, these workers are often employed in large production facilities such as Volvo and Scania, as well as in smaller subcontractors. The role typically involves following blueprints, using hand tools, and quality checking finished products. Automation is reshaping the occupation, but demand remains stable in sectors requiring flexible, hands-on assembly.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for assemblers in Sweden often starts with on-the-job training and leads to roles such as team leader, quality inspector, or production technician. Many employers offer internal training programs, and some assemblers pursue further vocational education to become specialists in automation or industrial engineering. The path is generally linear, with opportunities to advance to supervisory roles after several years of experience.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repetitive parts assembly (e.g., electronic component insertion, screw fastening)
- Vision-Based Quality Inspection (AI Vision System Replacement)
- Simple material handling and sorting (replaced by AMR)
- Record production data (replaced by automated MES system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for real-time assembly guidance, reducing training time
- Use collaborative robots for heavy or precision assembly, with worker supervision and fine-tuning
- Use AI predictive maintenance tools to monitor equipment and replace worn parts in advance
- Optimize processes based on digital twin simulation of assembly workflows

**Human moat:**

- Handling non-standard parts or unexpected anomalies (e.g., flexible adjustments when part tolerance is out of spec)
- Hand-finishing small-batch, multi-variety custom assemblies
- Make experience-based comprehensive judgments on assembly quality
- Cross-workstation collaboration and on-site problem-solving skills

**Skills to build:**

- Collaborative robot programming and debugging.
- Operation of digital twin and AR-assisted assembly systems
- Basic data analysis (reading from MES system to improve efficiency)
- Mechatronics maintenance skills
- Lean production and continuous improvement methods
- Cross-disciplinary communication (liaising with engineers, suppliers).

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Electronics assembly
- Automotive manufacturing
- Industrial production

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for an assembler in Sweden?**
A: Salaries for assemblers vary by industry and region, but generally fall within the collective agreement levels for manufacturing, typically between 25,000 and 30,000 SEK per month. This is below the EU Blue Card threshold.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as an assembler in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is restricted. The job offer must meet the salary threshold (around 27,000 SEK/month) and collective agreement terms. Since the occupation is not on the shortage list, the employer must also show that the position could not be filled from within the EU/EEA.

**Q: What advancement opportunities exist for assemblers?**
A: Experienced assemblers can move into team leader roles, quality control, or production planning. Some pursue further vocational training to become automation technicians or industrial engineers.
