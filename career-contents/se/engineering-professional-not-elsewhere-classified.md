# Other Engineering Professional — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2149 (ISCO08)**

Other Engineering Professionals (ISCO 2149) encompass a diverse range of engineering roles in Sweden, including mechanical, chemical, and industrial engineering, working in sectors like manufacturing, energy, and consulting. The Swedish labour market for these professionals is robust, driven by digitalization, sustainability, and infrastructure investments, with strong demand in regions such as Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from a junior engineering role, advancing to senior positions, project manager, or technical specialist. Many engineers in Sweden pursue further education, such as a master's or PhD, often combined with industry certifications. There is a growing trend towards interdisciplinary skills, combining engineering with business or sustainability expertise, enabling roles in innovation management.

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for this occupation in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by specialization and experience but generally follow collective agreements. Starting salaries for new graduates are around 30,000-35,000 SEK/month, while experienced professionals can earn 50,000-70,000 SEK/month or more. Consult the relevant union for exact figures.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU engineers?**
A: Non-EU engineers can apply for an employer-tied work permit if they have a job offer meeting collective agreement pay, or an EU Blue Card for higher salary thresholds. The process requires employer sponsorship and takes 1-3 months.

**Q: Is Swedish language required for engineering jobs?**
A: While many international companies use English, knowledge of Swedish is a strong advantage, especially for roles involving clients, authorities, or Swedish documentation. Some employers may require proficiency, but it is not a universal requirement.
