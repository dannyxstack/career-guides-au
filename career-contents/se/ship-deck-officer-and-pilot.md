# Ship's Deck Officer and Pilot — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3152 (ISCO08)**

Ships' Deck Officers and Pilots in Sweden navigate and manage vessels in coastal and inland waters, ensuring safe passage and cargo operations. The profession is concentrated in shipping hubs like Gothenburg and Stockholm, as well as along the Baltic coast. Sweden's maritime sector is significant for trade and tourism, with a strong tradition of maritime education. Employment is influenced by global shipping cycles and domestic regulations. The Swedish Transport Agency oversees licensing, and there is a steady demand for experienced officers in both merchant marine and pilotage services.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically follows a hierarchy from junior officer (Third Officer) to Chief Officer and then Captain. Many start as deck cadets through the four-year nautical science programme. After obtaining a watchkeeping license, officers accumulate sea time to advance. Pilots are usually experienced captains who undergo additional training. Some move into onshore roles in shipping companies, maritime administration, or education. Swedish maritime unions play a strong role in wage bargaining. Continuous professional development is required to keep up with new navigation technology and environmental regulations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic Identification System (AIS) data integration and collision avoidance computation
- Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS) automatic route planning and updates
- Repetitive data collection and analysis in weather route optimization
- Automated recording and report generation of routine navigation logs
- Automatic interpretation of sensor data in ship performance monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Combine AI weather models and real-time ocean data to make more accurate route adjustment decisions
- Use digital twins and predictive maintenance systems to identify equipment issues early
- Optimizes workforce and fatigue management through AI-assisted crew scheduling systems
- Computer vision-based berthing and anchoring operation assistance
- AI-generated emergency scenario simulations for training and crisis decision-making rehearsals

**Human moat:**

- Take ultimate decision-making responsibility in emergencies (e.g., engine failure, man overboard)
- Statutory qualifications and experience required by international maritime regulations (SOLAS, STCW)
- Cross-cultural crew management and interpersonal communication (multi-national team collaboration on board)
- Manual control and situational judgment in complex port environments (e.g., tugboat coordination)
- Long-term experience (intuitive judgment) with hulls, machinery, and marine environments

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced configuration and troubleshooting of electronic charts and autopilot systems
- Basic data analysis (Python/R) for interpreting ship sensor data
- Digital twin and predictive maintenance software operation
- Maritime cybersecurity knowledge (to counter onboard system attacks)
- Advanced English Communication and Cross-Cultural Leadership
- Use of fatigue management and compliance software (e.g., rest time monitoring)

**Key growth areas:**

- Autonomous shipping
- Baltic Sea trade
- Cruise tourism
- Environmental regulations

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Ships' Deck Officer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and experience. A junior officer (Third Officer) earns around 35,000-45,000 SEK per month, while a Captain can earn 50,000-70,000 SEK. Pilots may have different pay structures. All jobs must meet collective agreement minimums.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as deck officers in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but they need a job offer from a Swedish employer. The employer must demonstrate that the position cannot be filled by an EU/EEA worker. A work permit is tied to the employer, and the salary must meet collective agreement levels.

**Q: Is it possible to switch from deck officer to pilot in Sweden?**
A: Yes, pilots are usually recruited from experienced captains. You need a captain's license and then undergo pilot training. The process is competitive and requires passing exams. Pilots are employed by the Swedish Maritime Administration.
