# Precision-Instrument Maker and Repairer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7311 (ISCO08)**

Precision-instrument makers and repairers in Sweden specialize in the fabrication, assembly, and maintenance of high-accuracy instruments such as watches, measuring devices, and scientific apparatus. Working mainly in specialized workshops, manufacturing firms, and research laboratories, they combine traditional handcraft skills with modern technology. The occupation is small but essential, particularly in sectors like medical technology, aerospace, and precision engineering. Sweden's strong manufacturing base and focus on quality sustain demand for these skilled professionals, though the field is niche and requires extensive training.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically involves moving from apprentice to journeyman and then to master craftsman, often with specialization in areas like watchmaking, medical instruments, or industrial gauges. Experienced professionals may become quality control specialists, workshop supervisors, or instructors at vocational schools. Some transition into sales or technical support for instrument manufacturers. Continuous learning is key as instruments become more electronic and software-driven.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Repeated part dimension measurement and recording (replaced by AI vision and automated instruments)
- Calibration process for standard instruments (taken over by AI automatic calibration algorithms)
- Remote diagnosis of common faults (AI knowledge base provides solutions)
- Inventory management and material procurement (optimised by AI prediction systems)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex fault diagnosis (AI-generated fault trees and historical cases)
- Fine-tuning decisions in precision adjustment (AI provides real-time compensation suggestions)
- Customized instrument design (AI-assisted 3D modeling and tolerance analysis)
- Repair plan optimization (AI compares efficiency and cost of multiple approaches)
- Multi-domain knowledge quick retrieval (AI integrating optics, mechanics, electronics materials)

**Human moat:**

- Manual finishing and tactile feedback (e.g., micron-level polishing, balance tuning)
- Repair of non-standard/antique instruments (no blueprints, no spare parts)
- Communicate with clients to understand custom requirements and translate them into technical solutions
- On-site emergency repairs (decision-making in uncontrollable environments)

**Skills to build:**

- Using AI-assisted fault diagnosis tools (e.g., image-based inspection systems)
- CNC machine and 3D printing operation (for manufacturing replacement parts)
- Digital measurement and reverse engineering software (e.g., CAD, laser scanning).
- IoT sensor and smart instrument debugging.
- Python scripts (automating repetitive data collection)
- Client communication and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Medical device technology
- Watchmaking
- Industrial automation
- Scientific instrument maintenance

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How are salaries determined for precision-instrument makers in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set through collective bargaining agreements and vary by experience, sector, and region. Entry-level pay is lower, while experienced specialists earn more. The general level is competitive within the manufacturing industry.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen obtain a work permit as a precision-instrument maker?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the requirements of the Migration Agency, including a salary in line with applicable collective agreements.

**Q: What skills are most important for this occupation?**
A: Manual dexterity, precision, knowledge of materials, and understanding of mechanical and electronic systems are crucial. Problem-solving and patience are also highly valued.
