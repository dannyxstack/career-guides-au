# Police Officer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 5412 (ISCO08)**

Police officers in Sweden are responsible for maintaining public order, preventing crime, and ensuring safety. They work under the Swedish Police Authority (Polismyndigheten), which is divided into seven regions. The role involves patrol duties, criminal investigations, traffic control, and community engagement. Sweden has faced police staffing shortages, leading to increased recruitment efforts and a focus on diversity. Officers undergo a rigorous two-year paid training program at the Police Academy, combining theory and practical experience. Swedish fluency is mandatory due to communication with the public and legal procedures.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 11,359. The Swedish Police Authority plans to hire thousands of new officers over the next decade to address staffing gaps and an aging workforce. Demand is driven by rising crime complexity, including cybercrime and organized crime. However, budget constraints and high attrition rates pose challenges. The government has invested in expanding police education capacity, but competition for qualified candidates remains fierce. Job prospects are excellent for those meeting language and fitness requirements, especially in Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a patrol officer, then to specialist roles like criminal investigator, crime scene technician, or traffic police. With experience, officers can become team leaders, detectives, or enter management at the local or national level. Some pursue further education in law or criminology for senior positions. The police force offers lateral moves to units such as border police, national task force, or cybercrime. Promotion is merit-based, but seniority and additional training play a role.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a police officer in Sweden?**
A: Starting salary for a police officer trainee is around 24,000 SEK per month during training, rising to approximately 36,000 SEK after graduation. Experienced officers can earn 45,000–55,000 SEK, with senior roles reaching 60,000+ SEK.

**Q: Can a non-EU national become a police officer in Sweden?**
A: Generally, no. Swedish police require EU/EEA citizenship or a permanent residence permit. Only in exceptional cases and with specific expertise might a work permit be considered, but it is very rare.

**Q: What is the career advancement timeline?**
A: After 1–2 years as a patrol officer, you can apply for specialist roles. Detective positions typically require 3–5 years of experience. Management roles (e.g., group leader, regional chief) often require 10+ years or additional academic qualifications.
