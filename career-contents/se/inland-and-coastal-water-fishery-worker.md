# Inland and Coastal Waters Fishery Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6222 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, inland and coastal waters fishery workers engage in catching fish and other aquatic organisms in lakes, rivers, and coastal areas. The occupation is part of the traditional fishing industry but has declined due to consolidation and competition from aquaculture. Despite this, there is a niche demand for sustainable and local seafood, especially in coastal communities. Workers may be employed by small-scale fisheries, cooperatives, or larger companies, and tasks include netting, trapping, sorting, and maintaining gear. The role requires physical stamina and knowledge of local ecosystems.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★☆☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression in this field is often flat; workers may advance to skipper or vessel owner, but this requires capital and additional licensing. Many transition to related roles in aquaculture, fish processing, or marine tourism. Formal vocational training through gymnasium or adult education can improve prospects. The Swedish Board of Agriculture and the Swedish Agency for Marine and Water Management oversee regulations. Networking and reputation are key for independent fishers.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual net casting and retrieval operations: automatic nets and robotic arms can handle this
- Manual sorting and weighing of catch: machine vision and automatic grading systems
- Manual navigation and obstacle avoidance for fishing vessels: GPS and autopilot systems
- Visual monitoring of fishery resources: drones and remote sensing technology
- Hand repair of fishing gear: automatic repair equipment

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using drone and satellite data to identify fish locations, improving fishing efficiency
- Real-time monitoring of environmental parameters such as water quality and temperature via IoT sensors
- Use data analysis to optimize navigation routes and working hours
- Using machine vision to assist in catch quality grading and inventory management
- Using digital platforms for sustainable fishery certification and traceability

**Human moat:**

- Ability to repair and make emergency decisions for sudden mechanical failures
- Safe judgment and operations under complex weather and sea conditions
- Local ecological knowledge and traditional fishing experience
- Compliance communication with regulatory authorities and environmental organizations
- Manual processing of high-value catch (e.g., rare fish)

**Skills to build:**

- Automated fishing gear and drone inspection operations
- Fisheries Data Collection and Analysis (Python/R)
- Applications of GIS and remote sensing technology in fisheries
- Fisheries machinery repair and basic electrical skills
- Knowledge of sustainable fisheries management and regulations
- Aquaculture and Resource Monitoring Technology

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable fishing
- aquaculture
- coastal tourism
- environmental monitoring

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy working outdoors and on or near water.
- Individuals who are physically fit and comfortable with manual labor in varying weather conditions.
- Those who appreciate a traditional, hands-on lifestyle and are self-motivated.

**Who is not suited**
- Those who dislike cold, wet environments or have seasickness.
- Individuals seeking a predictable, indoor desk job with regular hours.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an inland and coastal waters fishery worker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on employer, experience, and catch volume. For employed workers, monthly pay is often around 25,000–30,000 SEK before tax, but many are paid per catch or work seasonally. Self-employed fishers' income fluctuates.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a fishery worker in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. You need a job offer from an employer who advertises the position in Sweden and the EU first (labor market test). The job must meet collective agreement standards. Since it is not on the shortage list, approval is not guaranteed.

**Q: Do I need a university degree to become a fishery worker in Sweden?**
A: No, a university degree is not required. Vocational training at upper secondary level or adult education is common. Practical experience and a fishing license are more important.
