# Shotfirer and Blaster — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7542 (ISCO08)**

Shotfirers and blasters are skilled workers responsible for handling and detonating explosives in mining, quarrying, construction, and demolition projects across Sweden. They ensure safe and precise blasting operations, often in remote areas like the Norrbotten mining region or urban infrastructure projects. The role requires strict adherence to safety regulations and a mandatory blasting license from the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an assistant or apprentice, advancing to licensed blaster after certification. Experienced shotfirers can move into supervisory roles, safety inspection, or blasting design. Some transition into mining engineering or technical sales for explosives suppliers. Additional training in risk management or environmental blasting can open paths to management or consulting, though formal higher education is rarely required.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Blasting design calculations: AI can automatically calculate charge weight, hole spacing, and other parameters to generate blasting plans.
- Safety monitoring and early warning: real-time detection of hazardous gases, vibrations, and alerts using sensors and visual AI
- Blast records and reports: automatically generate blasting logs and compliance reports
- Inventory management: AI optimizes inventory and usage records of explosives and detonators

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Geological analysis and blast optimization: AI-assisted analysis of rock structure to improve blast efficiency
- Remote monitoring and drone inspection: use drones and AI to identify unexploded ordnance and slope stability
- Safety training and simulations: VR/AI simulated blasting accidents to improve emergency response
- Equipment maintenance prediction: AI predicts failures of drilling machines and other equipment, reducing downtime

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making and adaptability in hazardous on-site environments
- Fine motor skills such as handheld charging and wiring
- Intuitive judgment of dynamic field conditions, such as geology and weather
- Statutory licensing responsibilities and enforcement of safety regulations
- Team coordination and leadership in emergency situations

**Skills to build:**

- Basic Programming and Data Management: Python/Excel for handling blasting data
- Drone operation and image analysis: used for site inspection before and after blasting
- Digital geological modeling: learn software such as Surpac, Datamine
- Basics of Sensors and IoT: Understanding deployment and troubleshooting
- Safety regulation updates: continuously learn automated blasting safety standards
- Collaboration software: such as BIM, project management tools

**Key growth areas:**

- Mining expansion
- Infrastructure projects
- Urban construction
- Environmental remediation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a shotfirer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level around 25,000-30,000 SEK/month, experienced up to 40,000 SEK/month. Mining sector often pays higher. Always check collective agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a shotfirer in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but you need a job offer from a Swedish employer who can show the position meets collective agreement pay. Additionally, you must obtain a blasting license from MSB, which may require language skills and training in Sweden.

**Q: What training is required to become a shotfirer in Sweden?**
A: Typically you need an upper secondary education in construction or mining, followed by a blasting course and apprenticeship. The blasting license from MSB is mandatory. Many adults change careers via vocational college (KYH) programs.
