# Crop Farm Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9211 (ISCO08)**

Crop farm labourers perform manual tasks in agricultural production, including planting, harvesting, weeding, and irrigation. In Sweden, they are essential for seasonal work, often employed on a temporary basis. The labour market is influenced by EU/EEA free movement, with many workers from other EU countries. The work is physically demanding and typically does not require formal education. Mechanisation is increasing, but demand for manual labour persists in certain niches such as organic farming and greenhouse cultivation.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 3,151. Employment outlook for crop farm labourers in Sweden is stable but seasonal. Demand peaks during planting and harvest periods. Automation is reducing some manual tasks, but specialist crops like organic and greenhouse produce sustain demand. Many positions remain temporary or part-time. The sector relies heavily on migrant labour from within the EU/EEA, and Brexit has tightened some earlier labour sources. Overall, the occupation is not expected to grow significantly, but replacement needs will create openings.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: very high (★★★★½).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for crop farm labourers is limited but possible. Experienced workers may advance to farm supervisor, machinery operator, or team leader roles. Some transition into self-employment as own-account farmers or into agricultural consultancy. Further training in agricultural science, farm management, or specialised crop production can open doors to higher-skilled or managerial positions. The path is not strongly structured, but ambition and additional qualifications can lead to more stable and better-paid roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual seeding and transplanting: replaced by automated seeders and transplanting robots
- Weed control and pest monitoring: AI visual recognition combined with precision spraying robots.
- Routine irrigation operations: sensors and smart irrigation systems automatically regulate
- Crop maturity detection and harvesting: computer vision and robotic arms automatically assess and pick
- Field data recording: automatic collection and upload by IoT sensors

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operating and maintaining complex agricultural machinery: AI-assisted diagnostics and remote guidance improve efficiency
- Precision agriculture data analysis: using AI to optimize planting density and fertilization timing
- Crop health monitoring: AI-assisted early disease detection and intervention suggestions
- Quality grading and packaging: AI vision systems assist manual sorting with high speed and precision

**Human moat:**

- Decision-making ability for sudden anomalies (e.g., extreme weather, equipment failure)
- Flexible operation in complex terrain and multi-crop mixed planting environments
- Interpersonal collaboration and communication with farmers and technology suppliers
- Quality assessment based on local experience (e.g., optimal harvest window)

**Skills to build:**

- Agricultural machinery operation and basic maintenance.
- Use of precision agriculture tools (e.g., GPS navigation, drones)
- Data analysis and agricultural software application
- Basic programming and automation system configuration
- Read technical documentation in English or Italian
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices

**Key growth areas:**

- Organic farming
- Precision agriculture
- Greenhouse technology
- Seasonal labour demand

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a crop farm labourer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by region and experience. They are generally above the minimum set by the collective agreement for agricultural workers.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a crop farm labourer in Sweden?**
A: Non-EU nationals can apply for a seasonal work permit for up to 6 months, but it is competitive and requires a job offer meeting collective agreement standards. EU/EEA citizens have free movement and can work without a permit.

**Q: What education is required?**
A: No formal education is required. Most skills are learned on the job, but vocational training in agriculture can be beneficial.
