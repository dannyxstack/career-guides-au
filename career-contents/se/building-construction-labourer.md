# Building Construction Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9313 (ISCO08)**

Building construction labourers in Sweden perform manual tasks such as carrying materials, digging, and cleaning on construction sites, typically under the guidance of skilled tradespeople. The occupation is vital for the country's robust construction sector, which includes residential, commercial, and infrastructure projects. Labourers are employed by construction companies, subcontractors, and temporary staffing agencies, with demand fluctuating with the economic cycle. Entry-level positions are common, but ongoing safety training and certifications are required.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Progression from building construction labourer typically involves moving into specialised trades such as carpentry, bricklaying, or concrete work after completing vocational training. Many labourers also become machine operators or site supervisors. In Sweden, the Byggnads trade union offers training programs. With experience, some start their own small contracting businesses. Further education can lead to roles in construction management or technical supervision.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Moving construction materials (replaced by AGVs/drones).
- Basic excavation and earthworks (replaced by unmanned excavators)
- On-site cleaning and debris sorting (replaced by cleaning robots)
- Simple bricklaying and plastering (replaced by automatic brick-laying machines/spraying machines)
- Repetitive rebar tying (replaced by tying robots)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to read construction drawings, improving accuracy.
- Operate semi-automated tools (e.g., electric screwdrivers, laser levels)
- Maintenance and debugging of automated equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Report progress and receive instructions via digital platforms
- Using BIM software for collaborative work and problem-solving

**Human moat:**

- Judgment in unstructured environments (e.g., complex terrain, unexpected situations)
- Fine hand-eye coordination (e.g., installing irregular components).
- Team on-site communication and emergency coordination
- Experience in safety supervision and accident prevention
- Mastery of traditional crafts (e.g., stonemasonry, special plastering)

**Skills to build:**

- Operating automated construction equipment (e.g., drones, robots)
- Reading digital drawings and BIM basics
- Basic programming or equipment debugging skills
- Enhanced safety and quality management knowledge
- Knowledge of electrical/hydraulic system maintenance
- English or digital tools usage

**Key growth areas:**

- Renovation
- Infrastructure
- Sustainable building
- Urban development

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a building construction labourer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements between trade unions and employers' associations. They vary based on experience, region, and specific employer. The Swedish Public Employment Service provides detailed wage statistics.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a building construction labourer in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is difficult due to the low-skilled classification. A job offer with collective-agreement pay is required, and the Migration Agency typically prioritises high-skilled occupations. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: What is the typical career progression for a building construction labourer?**
A: Many labourers progress to skilled trades like carpentry or become foremen after gaining experience and additional certifications.
