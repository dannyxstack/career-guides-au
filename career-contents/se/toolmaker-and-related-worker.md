# Toolmaker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7222 (ISCO08)**

Toolmakers produce and repair precision tools, dies, jigs, and molds used in manufacturing. In Sweden, this occupation is critical to the automotive, engineering, and plastics industries. The labour market faces a shortage of skilled toolmakers due to an aging workforce and limited new entrants. Many positions are in small and medium-sized enterprises, particularly in industrial regions like Västsverige and Mälardalen. Apprenticeships and vocational training are common entry paths, and the work requires technical precision and problem-solving skills.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 2,743. The employment outlook for toolmakers in Sweden is positive but constrained by a persistent shortage of qualified workers. While automation reduces demand for basic tasks, the need for complex and custom tooling remains steady. The retirement of experienced toolmakers over the next decade is expected to create vacancies. Regional demand is highest in manufacturing hubs. Overall, the profession offers stable employment with moderate growth, but candidates with CNC and CAD skills will have an advantage.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Toolmakers can advance to specialist roles in mold design, CNC programming, or quality assurance. With experience, they may become workshop supervisors or production managers. Further education at vocational colleges can lead to roles as production technicians or industrial engineers. Self-employment is an option for highly skilled individuals. The career path typically involves starting as an apprentice or after upper-secondary vocational training, with opportunities for continuous skill development in precision manufacturing and automation.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual CNC programming and tool path generation
- Simple repetitive machine operations (e.g., loading/unloading workpieces, starting machining cycles)
- Routine dimensional measurement and quality recording (manual measurement with calipers and micrometers)
- Paper-based drawing management and document archiving
- Basic troubleshooting (based on fixed diagnostic procedures)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted tool path optimization (automatic collision avoidance, improved machining efficiency)
- Machine learning-based online quality inspection (real-time prediction of dimensional deviations)
- Digital twin simulation of machining processes (pre-validate fixture designs)
- Smart production scheduling system integration (automatic assignment of machine tasks)
- Augmented reality-assisted assembly and debugging (overlaying virtual guidance)

**Human moat:**

- Intuitive judgment in complex fixture design and debugging
- On-site diagnosis and repair of multi-process integration issues.
- Ability to communicate non-standard requirements with clients/engineers
- Safety responsibility and process innovation decisions
- Precision manual finishing and assembly touch

**Skills to build:**

- Advanced CAD/CAM applications (e.g., Mastercam, NX machining modules)
- Robot-assisted loading/unloading programming and integration
- Python or MATLAB for machining data analysis
- Basics of additive manufacturing (3D printing molds)
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) and smart tool management
- Quality control data visualization (e.g., Power BI)

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision Manufacturing
- Automation
- 3D Printing
- Mold Design

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a toolmaker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, but generally range from 30,000 to 40,000 SEK per month before tax. Overtime and shift allowances can increase earnings. Always check the applicable collective agreement.

**Q: Do I need a work visa to work as a toolmaker in Sweden?**
A: EU/EEA citizens can work freely. Non-EU/EEA citizens require a job offer and a work permit, which is typically granted if the occupation is in shortage and meets salary thresholds. The EU Blue Card may be an option for highly specialized roles.

**Q: What education is required to become a toolmaker in Sweden?**
A: A vocational diploma in toolmaking from upper secondary school or a vocational college (KY/YH) is common. Apprenticeships are also highly valued. Many employers prefer certified training in CNC operation and CAD.
