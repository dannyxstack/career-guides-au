# Jewellery and Precious Metal Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7313 (ISCO08)**

Jewellery and precious metal workers (guldsmeder) in Sweden design, manufacture, and repair jewellery and objects made of precious metals such as gold, silver, and platinum. This craft occupation is relatively small, with most practitioners employed in small workshops, retail jewellery stores, or self-employed. The industry faces competition from imported mass-produced items, but there is a stable demand for custom-made and bespoke jewellery, as well as repair and restoration services. Key locations include Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö. The Swedish Public Employment Service categorizes the occupation as having balanced recruitment prospects, with moderate opportunities for skilled artisans.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a vocational training programme in goldsmithing or silversmithing, followed by an apprenticeship under a master craftsperson. Many workers become specialists in areas like gem setting, engraving, or jewellery design. After gaining experience, some pursue a master craftsperson certificate (mästarbrev) and open their own workshops or retail stores. Further education in gemology or jewellery design can lead to roles in upper management or teaching. The career path is often entrepreneurial, with many eventually working as independent artists or studio owners.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated polishing and grinding: AI-controlled robotic arms can efficiently perform basic grinding and polishing, replacing repetitive manual labor.
- CAD-assisted design modeling: AI generates multiple design variants, reducing the need for manual drafting and wax pattern carving.
- Quality inspection: computer vision systems automatically detect gem flaws and metal defects, replacing visual inspection.
- Inventory and order management: AI automatically optimises material procurement and order scheduling, reducing administrative positions.
- Basic repairs: standardized ring resizing, chain repair, etc., can be done by automated equipment.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Complex custom design: AI generates design sketches, which craftsmen refine, boosting creative efficiency.
- Customer preference analysis: AI analyzes sales data to assist designers in creating styles that better align with target markets.
- Material simulation: AI simulates the appearance of different metal and gemstone combinations, reducing the cost of producing physical samples.
- Education training: AR/VR combined with AI teaching to accelerate beginners' learning of carving and inlay techniques.
- Repair solution recommendations: AI scans damaged jewelry and suggests optimal repair paths to assist craftsmen in decision-making.

**Human moat:**

- Handcrafted carving and artistic creation: unique aesthetic sense and manual dexterity that AI cannot replicate.
- Customer trust and personalized service: Jewelry purchases involve high emotional value, making human communication irreplaceable.
- Complex setting and restoration: high technical difficulty gem setting and antique restoration rely on artisan experience.
- Security and compliance responsibility: authenticity and origin of precious metals and gems (e.g., conflict-free diamonds) require human endorsement.

**Skills to build:**

- AI design tools: Master AI plugins for CAD software such as Rhino 3D and MatrixGold.
- 3D printing and modeling: learn digital manufacturing processes, such as 3D printing wax patterns and lost-wax casting.
- Gemology certification: Obtain professional qualifications such as GIA to enhance appraisal authority.
- Customer experience design: learn personalized marketing and AR virtual try-on technology.
- Sustainable supply chain knowledge: understanding blockchain traceability and fair trade certification.
- Data analysis: using AI tools to analyze market trends and guide design strategies.

**Key growth areas:**

- custom jewellery
- repair services
- 3D printing
- gemstone setting

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a jewellery worker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries for jewellery and precious metal workers vary by experience, region, and employer. Entry-level positions may start around the collective agreement minimum, while experienced craftspersons with a master certificate can earn significantly more. Many are self-employed and their income depends on commissions and client base.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as a jewellery maker in Sweden?**
A: Yes, non-EU nationals can obtain a work permit for this occupation if they have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the salary threshold and collective agreement standards. The employer must prove that the position could not be filled locally. The occupation is not on a specific shortage list, so the application is assessed on its merits.

**Q: What training is required to become a jewellery maker in Sweden?**
A: The most common path is completing a three-year upper secondary vocational programme in Crafts with a goldsmith/silversmith specialisation. Alternatively, an apprenticeship with a master craftsperson can lead to a journeyman certificate. Further education in gemology or design can enhance career prospects.
