# Social Work and Counselling Professionals — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2635 (ISCO08)**

Social work and counselling professionals, known as socionomer in Sweden, work in municipal social services, healthcare, schools, and private practice. The profession requires a Bachelor's degree in Social Work (socionomexamen) and is regulated by Socialstyrelsen. Sweden faces a shortage of social workers, especially in rural areas and specialized fields like child welfare and mental health, making it a stable career with high demand.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 28,087. The employment outlook for socionomer is positive, driven by an aging population, increasing mental health awareness, and ongoing integration efforts. Demand is particularly high in elder care, child protection, and healthcare social work. However, budget constraints in municipalities may affect hiring rates. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression often begins with general social work in municipal offices, then specialization through further education (e.g., master's in social work or psychotherapy). Experienced professionals can become supervisors, team leaders, or clinic managers. Some move into private practice or research. Continuing education is common and often employer-funded.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate case reports and progress records
- Initial psychological assessment based on standardized questionnaires
- Crisis hotline initial screening and information triage
- Scheduling appointments and case management system operation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze social media data for early warning of community mental health risks
- Using virtual reality therapy to assist in PTSD treatment
- AI-supported multilingual automatic transcription and sentiment analysis improve consultation efficiency.
- Data-driven case management optimizes resource allocation.

**Human moat:**

- Establishes deep trust relationships and empathetic interaction
- Handling Ethical Dilemmas and Complex Legal Cases
- Flexible communication strategies in multicultural contexts

**Skills to build:**

- Digital literacy and use of AI tools (e.g., data analysis, VR therapy)
- Trauma-Informed Care and Crisis Intervention in Practice
- Cross-disciplinary collaboration skills (with healthcare and education sectors)
- Research evaluation and evidence-based practice methods
- Self-care and burnout management
- Multicultural competence and interpretation of Italian local policies

**Key growth areas:**

- Child Welfare
- Elder Care
- Mental Health
- Integration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a social work professional in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. According to Statistics Sweden, median monthly salary for social workers is around SEK 35,000, with entry-level around SEK 30,000 and experienced professionals up to SEK 45,000. Public sector positions follow collective agreements.

**Q: Can non-EU social workers easily get a work visa for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if they have a degree recognized as equivalent to Swedish socionomexamen and a job offer from a Swedish employer. The employer must show that the position cannot be filled locally. Swedish language proficiency is often required, and licensure may take time.

**Q: How do I get my foreign social work degree recognized in Sweden?**
A: You must apply to the Swedish Council for Higher Education (UHR) or Socialstyrelsen for assessment. Additional courses or internships in Swedish welfare systems may be required.
