# Garden and Horticultural Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9214 (ISCO08)**

Garden and horticultural labourers perform manual tasks such as planting, weeding, harvesting, and maintaining gardens, parks, and agricultural fields. In Sweden, this occupation is highly seasonal, with peak demand in spring and summer. Many workers are employed on temporary contracts, often from EU/EEA countries. The role requires physical stamina and basic plant knowledge. For non-EU/EEA citizens, work permits are rarely granted due to low skill level and salary thresholds.

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Many transition into self-employment or start landscaping businesses. Formal vocational education in horticulture can lead to higher-skilled positions with better pay and stability.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a garden labourer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by region and employer, typically around the collective agreement level for the industry. Entry-level wages are at the lower end of the scale, with experienced workers earning more.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a garden labourer in Sweden?**
A: It is difficult. Non-EU citizens generally need an employer-tied work permit, which requires the job to meet salary thresholds (around 27,360 SEK/month in 2024) and labour shortage criteria. These conditions are rarely met for this low-skilled occupation, making it nearly impossible for third-country nationals.

**Q: What are the main duties of a garden labourer?**
A: Duties include soil preparation, planting, watering, weeding, pruning, harvesting, and maintaining garden tools and equipment. They may also assist in landscaping projects and apply fertilizers or pesticides under supervision.
