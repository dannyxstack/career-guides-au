# Biologist — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2131 (ISCO08)**

Biologists, botanists, zoologists and related professionals study living organisms and their interactions with the environment. In Sweden, they work in research institutes, universities, government agencies (e.g., Naturvårdsverket, Havs- och vattenmyndigheten), environmental consultancies, pharmaceutical and biotech companies. The job market is steady with demand for specialists in environmental monitoring, conservation, and life sciences driven by sustainability goals and the bioeconomy. EU/EEA citizens have free access; non-EU/EEA professionals need a work permit with a job offer meeting collective agreement pay.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a master's degree (many pursue PhDs) leading to roles like project manager, senior researcher, or environmental specialist. In the private sector, biologists can advance into R&D management, regulatory affairs, or business development. Public sector paths include roles as nature conservation officer or environmental coordinator. Specialization in molecular biology, ecology, or bioinformatics opens further opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Species identification and classification under a traditional microscope (replaced by computer vision)
- Manual literature search and data extraction (replaced by natural language processing)
- Basic statistical analysis and report generation (replaced by automated analysis tools).
- Repetitive laboratory sample handling and recording (replaced by robotic process automation)
- Manual sorting of ecological monitoring data (replaced by IoT sensors + AI).

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI accelerates genomic sequence analysis and pattern discovery
- AI simulates ecosystem dynamics to optimize conservation strategies
- AI-assisted drug molecular design (computational biology)
- AI-driven automated biodiversity image monitoring
- AI integrates multi-source data to predict biological invasion risks

**Human moat:**

- Creativity in experiment design, hypothesis formulation and validation
- Field sampling and conservation decision-making for rare species.
- Judgment and public communication of ethical dilemmas (e.g., gene editing)
- Systems thinking for interdisciplinary integration (ecological, social, economic)
- Responsibility attribution and professional certification under the regulatory framework.

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning applications
- Bioinformatics tools (e.g., AlphaFold, BLAST)
- Data visualization and interactive reporting (e.g., R Shiny, Tableau)
- Automated laboratory equipment operation and process optimization
- Science communication and policy advocacy skills
- AI ethics and biosecurity regulations knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Environmental Monitoring
- Biotechnology
- Pharmaceutical Research
- Marine Biology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a biologist in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by sector and experience. Entry-level positions start around 28,000–32,000 SEK per month, while senior specialists or managers can earn 40,000–55,000 SEK per month. Private sector and pharmaceutical roles often pay above public sector averages.

**Q: How do I get a work visa as a non-EU biologist in Sweden?**
A: You need a confirmed job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement pay and terms. The employer must advertise the position in the EU/EEA first. After securing a work permit, you can apply for a residence permit. The process typically takes 1–6 months.

**Q: Is a PhD required to work as a biologist in Sweden?**
A: Not always. Many positions in industry (biotech, pharma) and government agencies accept a master's degree. However, academic research and senior R&D roles often require a PhD.
