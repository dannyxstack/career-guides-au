# Hairdresser — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 5141 (ISCO08)**

Hairdressers in Sweden work in salons or as self-employed professionals, providing haircuts, styling, coloring, and treatments. The profession requires strong customer service skills and creativity, with most practitioners completing a vocational education program. Employment is widespread across the country, though competition exists in major cities. The industry is characterized by a mix of chain salons, independent studios, and mobile services, with an increasing focus on specialized treatments and sustainable products.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 5,216. Employment demand for hairdressers is stable but not high-growth. While the population continues to need grooming services, increased productivity and the popularity of self-employment limit job creation. The Swedish Public Employment Service expects moderate recruitment need over the next five years, with replacements for retiring workers being the main driver. New opportunities may arise in organic and specialist salons, though overall numbers remain steady.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a salon assistant or junior stylist, advancing to senior stylist or specialising in areas like colouring or bridal. Experienced hairdressers may become salon managers, open their own business, or teach at vocational schools. Some move into product sales, event styling, or media work. Continuing education is common to keep up with trends and techniques.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated telephone or online booking system for handling customer appointments
- AI inventory management system monitors supplies such as shampoo and hair dye
- Automated billing and payment processing based on customer historical data
- Smart mirrors or apps guide customers to self-preview hairstyles

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI hairstyle design tool recommends haircuts based on face shape and hair texture
- virtual trial system lets clients preview hair dye results on mobile devices
- AI analyzes customer preferences and fashion trends to provide personalized styling advice.
- smart temperature-controlled hair dryers and electric clippers improve operation precision and efficiency

**Human moat:**

- Fine tactile and manual judgment in haircutting, perming, and dyeing
- Communicate directly with clients to understand their personalized needs and aesthetic preferences
- Creative styling ability, designing novel hairstyles suitable for specific clients
- Live body language interaction to create a trusting and comfortable service atmosphere

**Skills to build:**

- Master the use of virtual hair styling and intelligent hairstyle recommendation tools
- Learn basic data analysis, interpret customer preferences and trend reports
- Enhance social media marketing and personal branding skills
- Obtain an Italian Advanced Hairdresser certification to increase professional barriers
- Learn scalp health and hair care science to provide value-added services
- Participate in training courses on AI-assisted design software

**Key growth areas:**

- Ethnic salons
- Bridal styling
- Colour specialists
- Men's grooming

**Who is suited**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a hairdresser in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and location; beginners earn around 22,000–25,000 SEK per month, while skilled stylists in high-end salons can earn 30,000 SEK or more. Self-employed income depends on client base.

**Q: Can a non-EU hairdresser get a work permit in Sweden?**
A: It is possible but difficult. The employer must show that the position cannot be filled locally and offer a salary that meets collective agreements (generally above 25,000 SEK/month). The profession is not on the shortage list, so permits are rare.

**Q: Do I need a formal education to work as a hairdresser in Sweden?**
A: No, it is not legally required, but most employers prefer a vocational diploma. Having certification (e.g., Frisörlicens) can improve job prospects and is often expected in high-end salons.
