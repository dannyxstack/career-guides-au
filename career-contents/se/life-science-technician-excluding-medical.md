# Life Science Technician — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3141 (ISCO08)**

Life Science Technicians in Sweden perform laboratory experiments, operate analytical instruments, and handle samples in research, pharmaceutical, environmental, and food testing settings. They are essential to Sweden's strong life science sector, with major employment hubs in Stockholm, Uppsala, Lund, and Gothenburg. Work involves strict quality control, safety protocols, and documentation. Common employers include universities, biotech firms, hospitals (non-medical labs), and government agencies.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a laboratory technician, advancing to senior specialist, lab manager, or quality assurance roles. Further specialization in genomics, bioanalysis, or regulatory affairs opens doors. Many pursue a master's degree to move into research or management. Technical sales and product support are alternative paths. The industry values continuous learning and certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Routine sample preparation and processing (e.g., pipetting, centrifugation)
- Standard biochemical tests (e.g., ELISA, PCR result interpretation)
- Record and organize experimental data
- Reagent inventory management and ordering
- Repetitive animal husbandry and observation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- High-throughput sequencing data analysis (AI-assisted variant identification)
- Experimental protocol optimization (machine learning predicts optimal conditions)
- Literature mining and hypothesis generation (Natural Language Processing)
- Automatic analysis of microscopic images (cell counting, phenotype recognition)
- Multi-omics data integration (transcriptome and proteome correlation analysis)

**Human moat:**

- Unstructured experimental troubleshooting (e.g., investigating unexpected contamination)
- Ethical Decision-Making and Animal Welfare Assessment
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and communication (with clinicians, engineers)
- Experimental innovation design and hypothesis generation.
- Regulatory compliance and quality control responsibilities

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and bioinformatics tools
- Machine Learning Fundamentals and Biomedical Applications
- Operation of laboratory automation systems (e.g., liquid handling workstations).
- Data visualization and reporting (Tableau, Power BI)
- Laboratory Information Management System (LIMS) Configuration
- Professional English and scientific writing

**Key growth areas:**

- Biotech
- Pharmaceutical R&D
- Environmental Analysis
- AgriFood

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a life science technician in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and increase with experience. Starting salaries are around 28,000–33,000 SEK per month before tax, with higher pay for specialists and in the private sector.

**Q: Can a non-EU life science technician get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, with a job offer that meets collective agreement pay and conditions. The employer must advertise the position in Sweden/EU first. The occupation is on the shortage list, which may facilitate the process. The permit is employer-tied.

**Q: Is further education necessary for career advancement?**
A: While not strictly required, a master's degree or specialized certifications can lead to senior roles like lab manager or quality assurance. Many technicians pursue part-time studies while working.
