# Tree and Shrub Crop Grower — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6112 (ISCO08)**

Tree and shrub crop growers in Sweden cultivate fruit, berries, nuts, and ornamental trees and shrubs. The sector is dominated by small to medium-sized farms, with a strong emphasis on organic and locally sourced produce. Seasonal demand drives labour needs, particularly during planting and harvest. Many growers are self-employed or run family businesses, and the industry faces challenges from climate change, pests, and market competition. Skilled workers are valued for their knowledge of horticulture, sustainable practices, and machinery operation.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts from farm worker or trainee, advancing to specialized grower, farm manager, or starting an independent farm. Further education in horticulture or agricultural science can lead to roles in research, advisory services, or quality control. Many successful growers diversify into agrotourism or direct sales (farm shops, farmers' markets). The path requires hands-on experience and business acumen, with lifelong learning in sustainable practices being increasingly important.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated fruit picking using AI vision and robotic arms
- Automated irrigation and fertilization scheduling based on sensor data
- Automatically monitor crop health and pests using drones and satellite imagery
- Automated pruning and shaping operations (e.g., grapevines)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to analyze soil and meteorological data to optimize planting plans and variety selection
- Precisely identify early diseases through computer vision and recommend localized pesticide application.
- Uses predictive models to optimize harvest timing and market sales strategies
- Use digital twin technology to simulate the impact of different management strategies on production yields

**Human moat:**

- Ability to flexibly adjust operations in complex terrain or extreme weather
- Holistic management intuition and experience for multi-year tree growth cycles
- On-site coordination and negotiation with seasonal workers, cooperatives, and buyers.
- Emergency decision-making for handling unexpected events (e.g., frost, pest outbreaks)

**Skills to build:**

- Precision agriculture technologies (e.g., variable rate irrigation, drone operation and image analysis)
- Agricultural data analysis and basic machine learning applications
- Smart sensor network maintenance and troubleshooting
- Sustainable agriculture practices (carbon accounting, soil health management)
- Digital tool integration (farm management software, ERP systems)
- Knowledge of crop physiology and new breeding techniques

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- berry production
- sustainable horticulture
- local food systems

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Individuals who enjoy outdoor, hands-on work in nature and are comfortable with physical labour.
- People interested in sustainable agriculture, organic farming, and local food production.
- Entrepreneurial self-starters who may want to run their own farm or agritourism business.

**Who is not suited**
- Those seeking stable, year-round indoor employment with fixed hours.
- People who dislike seasonal variations in workload or working in unpredictable weather.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tree and shrub crop grower in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region, but a skilled employee can expect 25,000–35,000 SEK per month before tax. Self-employed farmers' income depends on crop yields and market prices, often ranging from 20,000–40,000 SEK/month after expenses.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a tree and shrub crop grower in Sweden?**
A: Yes, with a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement standards (salary, insurance). However, this occupation is not on the shortage list, so the employer may need to advertise within EU/EEA first. Processing can take 1–4 months.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities for growers?**
A: Progression from farm worker to team leader, farm manager, or starting your own farm. With further education, roles in agricultural advisory, quality inspection, or research are possible. Some growers specialize in organic certification or niche crops.
