# Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Mechanic — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7127 (ISCO08)**

Air conditioning and refrigeration mechanics install, maintain, and repair cooling systems in residential, commercial, and industrial settings. In Sweden, demand is driven by the green transition, with a surge in heat pump installations and energy-efficient refrigeration. The occupation requires vocational training and certification, and it is on the shortage list, offering stable employment. Most workers are employed by specialized contractors or facilities management firms, with opportunities across the country.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with an apprenticeship or vocational program, leading to a journey-level technician. With experience, one can specialize in industrial refrigeration, heat pumps, or automation. Further education may lead to project management or supervisory roles. Some technicians become self-employed. Continuous learning is essential due to evolving refrigerants and technology. Certification for handling refrigerants is mandatory and required for career advancement.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an air conditioning and refrigeration mechanic in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and region. For precise data, refer to Statistics Sweden (SCB) or your union's collective agreement. Generally, entry-level mechanics earn around 28,000 SEK/month, while experienced ones can earn 38,000 SEK/month or more.

**Q: Can non-EU workers get a visa for this occupation?**
A: Yes, Sweden offers employer-tied work permits for non-EU nationals if the job meets collective agreement pay and terms. The occupation is on the shortage list, which facilitates approval. EU Blue Cards are possible for higher-paid roles.

**Q: Do I need Swedish certification if I am qualified abroad?**
A: Yes, a Swedish refrigerant handling certificate (Kylcertifikat) is mandatory regardless of foreign qualifications. Your vocational training will be assessed by the Swedish Council for Higher Education (UHR) for equivalence. Practical knowledge may need to be supplemented.
