# Supply, Distribution and Related Managers — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 1324 (ISCO08)**

Supply, Distribution and Related Managers in Sweden oversee procurement, logistics, and supply chain operations, ensuring efficient flow of goods and services. With Sweden's strong export-oriented economy and growing e-commerce sector, these managers are critical for maintaining competitiveness. They work in industries such as retail, manufacturing, and third-party logistics. The role requires strategic planning, supplier relationship management, and cost control. Swedish companies increasingly focus on sustainable and digitalized supply chains, creating demand for leaders with expertise in green logistics and automation.

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 8,175. The employment outlook for Supply, Distribution and Related Managers in Sweden is positive, driven by ongoing digitalization of supply chains, expansion of e-commerce, and emphasis on sustainability. The Swedish Public Employment Service reports a shortage of experienced logistics managers, particularly in sectors like food, pharmaceuticals, and construction. While automation may reduce some lower-skilled jobs, demand for strategic leaders remains high. Swedish companies actively recruit internationally for these roles, though proficiency in Swedish is often preferred for client-facing positions. Regional variations exist, with strong demand in Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for Supply, Distribution and Related Managers in Sweden often starts from roles such as logistics coordinator, buyer, or warehouse supervisor. With experience, professionals move to managerial positions like supply chain manager or logistics director. Many pursue further education in logistics management or accreditation from organizations like CIPS or SOLE. Swedish employers value international experience and language skills. The career path offers opportunities to specialize in areas such as sustainable supply chains, digital procurement, or global logistics, with potential advancement to executive roles like VP of Supply Chain.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Daily inventory data entry and updates
- Manual planning and optimization of transport routes
- Order tracking and status updates
- Automatic generation of supplier performance reports
- Basic statistical calculations in demand forecasting

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI for demand forecasting and inventory optimization
- Optimizing transport routes through real-time data analysis
- Use automated tools to monitor supply chain risks and issue warnings
- Utilize natural language processing to analyze supplier communications and contracts
- Use digital twin simulation of supply chain scenarios for decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Cross-departmental coordination and negotiation skills
- Emergency decision-making ability to handle sudden disruptions
- Maintenance of supplier relationships and long-term strategy
- Understand business objectives and set supply chain strategies.
- Ethical and compliance judgement (e.g., labour law, environmental protection)

**Skills to build:**

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for Supply, Distribution and Related Managers in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector. Entry-level managers earn around 45,000 SEK/month, while experienced managers in large companies can earn up to 65,000 SEK/month or more. Senior directors may exceed 80,000 SEK/month. Collective agreements set minimum levels, but actual pay is negotiated.

**Q: What visa options are available for non-EU/EEA supply chain professionals?**
A: The most common route is the employer-tied work permit (arbetstillstånd). You need a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement pay and terms. The EU Blue Card is an option if the salary is above about 53,000 SEK/month. Intra-corporate transfers are possible for multinational firms.

**Q: Is fluency in Swedish required for these manager positions?**
A: Not always, but it is highly valued. In multinational companies and logistics hubs, English may suffice. However, for roles involving domestic suppliers or customer contact, Swedish is often required. Many employers prefer at least a working knowledge of Swedish.
