# Livestock Farm Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9212 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, livestock farm labourers perform daily care and feeding of animals such as cattle, pigs, sheep, and poultry. The role is physically demanding, often involving early mornings and seasonal peaks like calving or lambing. Sweden's agriculture sector is highly mechanized yet still relies on a skilled workforce. EU/EEA nationals fill many positions via free movement, while third-country workers face restrictions due to low skill classification. Labour demand is stable but declining slowly due to automation and structural change. Organic farming and animal welfare focus create niches. The occupation is not on the shortage list and offers limited upward mobility without further education.

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Formal vocational education (e.g., agricultural high school or adult education) can lead to higher-skilled roles like animal health worker or farm manager. Some move into agri-business, sales, or self-employment. The occupation often serves as an entry point for immigrant workers seeking to establish residency, though long-term career advancement typically requires further study.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a livestock farm labourer in Sweden?**
A: The pre-tax annual salary typically ranges from 250,000 to 320,000 SEK, depending on region, experience, and employer. Collective bargaining agreements set minimum rates; for example, the agricultural sector's minimum is around 23,000 SEK per month (2025). Overtime and seasonal bonuses may apply.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as livestock farm labourers in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is not a shortage occupation. Employers must prove that no suitable EU/EEA worker is available through a labour market test. The job must meet collective agreement pay and conditions. Work permits are employer-tied and generally allow a maximum stay of 2 years, with options to extend. Seasonal permits (up to 6 months) are also possible.

**Q: What are the career advancement opportunities?**
A: Without formal education, progression is limited. Common paths include becoming a specialised animal caretaker, herd manager, or farm supervisor. Completing upper secondary agricultural education or a vocational degree can open doors to higher-skilled roles like farm manager or animal production consultant. Some workers transition to self-employment or agri-business.
