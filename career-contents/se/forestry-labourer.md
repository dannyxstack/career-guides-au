# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers in Sweden perform manual tasks such as planting, thinning, and harvesting in forests, supporting the nation's large forestry industry, which is a key economic sector, but the work is increasingly mechanized and seasonal, requiring physical stamina and a tolerance for outdoor work in all weather conditions, and often involves working in remote areas of the country.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 3,151. Employment for forestry labourers in Sweden is projected to remain relatively stable in the near term, though long-term trends show a gradual decline due to increased mechanization and efficiency in logging operations. However, there is consistent demand for manual labour in forest management activities such as planting and pre-commercial thinning, particularly in northern Sweden where the forestry industry is concentrated. Additionally, growing focus on bioenergy and carbon sequestration may sustain some demand for labourers in reforestation projects.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression for forestry labourers in Sweden typically involves gaining experience and then moving into machine operator roles handling harvesters and forwarders, which offer higher pay. With additional training, workers can become forest technicians or foremen overseeing operations. Specialized courses in sustainable forestry, forest damage assessment, or bioenergy open up opportunities in environmental consulting or government agencies. Some choose to start their own forestry services company.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable forestry
- Bioenergy
- Reforestation
- Climate adaptation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a forestry labourer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements, typically around 25,000-30,000 SEK per month before tax.

**Q: Can I get a work permit as a forestry labourer?**
A: It is possible but difficult; the occupation is not on shortage lists, and employers must demonstrate that no EU/EEA workers are available.

**Q: What career advancement is possible?**
A: With experience, you can become a machine operator or a foreman, or pursue further education to become a forest technician.
