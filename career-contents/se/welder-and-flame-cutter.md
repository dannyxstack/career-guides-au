# Welder and Flame Cutter — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7212 (ISCO08)**

Welders and flame cutters join and cut metal parts using various welding techniques. In Sweden, they are vital in manufacturing, construction, and shipbuilding industries. The profession requires precision and technical skill, with opportunities in both large industrial firms and smaller workshops. Demand is steady due to ongoing infrastructure projects and a need for skilled tradespeople in the Swedish labour market.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for welders often involves specialization in high-demand techniques like underwater welding or robotic welding operations. Experienced welders may become welding inspectors (certified by IW or European standards) or workshop supervisors. Formal vocational education (e.g., from Swedish gymnasium or adult education) plus on-the-job training is typical. Many pursue further certifications to increase employability and salary.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Batch standardised metal parts automatic welding (e.g., car body welding, pipe joints)
- Robot programming and path planning based on CAD models replace manual teaching
- Online weld quality inspection (visual inspection + AI defect detection)
- Automatic adjustment of welding parameters and welding process monitoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted welding parameter optimization (real-time recommendation of current/voltage based on material thickness and ambient temperature)
- Augmented reality (AR) welding guidance system improves novice training efficiency
- Drones/crawler robots equipped with welding modules for high-altitude or hazardous welding
- Digital twin welding simulation reduces material waste from test pieces
- AI-driven multi-robot collaborative welding group control

**Human moat:**

- On-site manual welding of non-standard workpieces and confined spaces (e.g., on ships, inside pipes)
- Welding of pressure vessels and load-bearing structures with high stress and fatigue life requirements
- Welding process development for dissimilar metals and special alloys (titanium, aluminum-magnesium alloys)
- Welding quality responsibility determination and repair decisions

**Skills to build:**

- Programming and debugging of welding robots (e.g., FANUC, KUKA, ABB)
- Welding process simulation software (e.g., Simufact Welding, SYSWELD)
- Automated welding quality control and testing methods
- Digital manufacturing basics (CAD/CAM, BOM management)
- Advanced certifications such as International Welding Engineer (IWE)
- Operation of AR/VR welding simulation training systems

**Key growth areas:**

- Structural steel welding
- Pipeline welding
- Robotic welding
- Maintenance welding

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a welder in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level welders can expect around 300,000 SEK/year, while experienced welders with certification can earn up to 450,000 SEK/year. Salaries vary by sector and region.

**Q: Do I need a job offer before applying for a work permit as a welder?**
A: Yes, the Swedish work permit requires a confirmed job offer meeting collective agreement pay and terms. You cannot search for a job on a visitor visa.

**Q: Is welding education in Sweden free?**
A: Public vocational programmes at upper secondary level and higher vocational education (YH) are free for EU/EEA citizens. Some costs for materials may apply; non-EU students may need to pay tuition fees.
