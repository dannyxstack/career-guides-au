# Field Crop And Vegetable Grower — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6111 (ISCO08)**

Field crop and vegetable growers in Sweden manage the cultivation of grains, potatoes, vegetables, and other crops. The sector is highly mechanized and faces an aging workforce, relying on seasonal foreign labor. Many farms are family-owned and transitioning to organic or precision agriculture. The Swedish climate allows for a short growing season, making greenhouse cultivation increasingly popular.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker, advancing to farm manager or self-employed farmer. Many growers also diversify into agritourism or direct sales. Formal education in agriculture improves prospects, especially for management roles. The aging farmer population opens opportunities for young entrants, but access to land and capital remains a barrier.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using traditional tractors for repetitive field operations such as sowing and tillage
- Apply fertilizers and pesticides manually or on a fixed schedule
- Visually inspecting crop health and pests
- Manually record data such as yield and soil moisture
- Simple sorting and basic processing such as bagging grain

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using satellite or drone imagery to analyze crop growth conditions, optimizing fertilization and irrigation timing
- Develop precise irrigation and pesticide plans using AI prediction models
- Use autonomous farming equipment to improve operational accuracy and efficiency, reducing resource waste
- Use IoT sensors to monitor soil and weather data in real time to aid decision-making

**Human moat:**

- Dealing with complex mechanical breakdowns and on-site emergencies in the field
- In-depth experiential knowledge of local climate, soil, and crop varieties
- Coordinate team operations, train new workers
- Communicate with farm owners and agronomists to develop flexible production plans
- Quickly respond to bad weather or equipment anomalies

**Skills to build:**

- Skills in operating and maintaining autonomous agricultural vehicles.
- Use drones and remote sensing equipment for field monitoring
- Use data analysis and IoT platforms (e.g., AgriWebb)
- Application of precision agriculture software (e.g., Climate FieldView)
- Basic programming or debugging skills for calibrating automation equipment
- Knowledge of sustainable agricultural practices such as crop rotation and organic management

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- precision agriculture
- sustainable farming
- greenhouse cultivation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a field crop and vegetable grower in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level workers earn around 25,000–30,000 SEK per month, while experienced farm managers can earn 35,000–45,000 SEK. Seasonal workers often receive hourly wages; the collective agreement stipulates minimum rates.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as field crop and vegetable growers in Sweden?**
A: Yes, via a seasonal work permit (säsongsarbete) for up to 6 months, or a standard work permit (arbetstillstånd) if the salary meets the threshold (currently 29,000 SEK/month). The occupation is not on the shortage list, but permits are possible with a job offer.

**Q: Is formal education required to become a field crop and vegetable grower in Sweden?**
A: No formal education is mandatory, but upper secondary vocational training in agriculture is common and improves job prospects. For management or own farming, a university degree in agricultural science is advantageous.
