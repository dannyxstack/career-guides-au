# Gardener — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6113 (ISCO08)**

Gardeners in Sweden cultivate and maintain gardens, parks, and green spaces, including planting, pruning, and landscape upkeep. With a growing focus on sustainability and urban greening, demand is steady, particularly in municipalities and private landscaping firms. The profession ranges from general maintenance to specialized horticulture, and many gardeners work seasonally or full-time. The Swedish labour market values practical skills, and formal education is often combined with on-the-job training.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 13,447. ## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a general gardener, advancing to specialized roles in arboriculture, landscape design, or park management. Further education in horticulture or landscape architecture can lead to supervisory or consulting positions. Many gardeners eventually start their own businesses, especially in landscaping services. Continuing professional development is encouraged through industry courses and certifications.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use automatic lawnmowers for grass cutting.
- Use robots for precision planting and fertilization
- Automatically detect pests and diseases using AI image recognition
- Scheduling and maintenance of automated irrigation systems
- Use drones for large-area spraying operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Analyze soil data using AI tools to optimize fertilization plans
- Use plant identification apps to quickly diagnose problems and provide care suggestions
- Simulate landscape effects through digital twin technology to assist design
- Use smart devices to monitor environmental conditions and predict plant growth needs.
- Client communication and proposal demonstration using VR/AR

**Human moat:**

- Creative design and aesthetic judgment for complex landscapes
- Empirical diagnosis of atypical plant problems
- Communicate with clients to understand needs and provide personalized services
- Ability to respond to emergencies such as sudden weather and diseases.
- Fine pruning and styling handcraft skills.

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of smart gardening equipment (e.g., automatic pruning robots)
- Data analysis and soil/plant monitoring software usage
- Drone operation and aerial image analysis
- Landscape design software (e.g., AutoCAD, SketchUp)
- Customer relationship management and communication skills
- Basic programming and automation scripting (such as Python)

**Key growth areas:**

- urban farming
- park maintenance
- sustainable landscaping
- green roof installation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: Can non-EU gardeners get a work permit in Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer meeting Sweden's salary threshold (at least 13,000 SEK/month as of 2025) and the employer provides terms in line with collective agreements. However, competition is strong and employers must show they couldn't find suitable EU/EEA workers first.

**Q: What is the typical salary for a gardener in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary by region, experience, and sector. Entry-level positions start around 22,000-25,000 SEK per month, while experienced specialists can earn 30,000 SEK or more. Always refer to current union statistics.

**Q: What are the best cities for gardening jobs?**
A: Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö have the highest demand due to urban development projects, while countryside areas need gardeners for estate maintenance and horticulture farms.
