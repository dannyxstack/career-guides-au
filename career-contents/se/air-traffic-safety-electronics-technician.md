# Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3155 (ISCO08)**

Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technicians install, maintain, and repair electronic systems used for air traffic control, navigation aids, and airport safety in Sweden. They work for the Swedish Transport Agency (Transportstyrelsen), LFV, Swedavia, or private contractors. The role requires precision, technical knowledge, and adherence to strict aviation safety regulations.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a trainee period, advancing to fully qualified technician. Experienced technicians may become shift supervisors, team leaders, or specialists in areas like radar or ground-to-air communications. Further formal education can lead to engineer roles in system design or air traffic management. The career path is structured and linear within the aviation sector.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic comparison and recording of routine maintenance checklists (e.g., post-flight checks)
- AI image recognition initial screening for structural fatigue cracks
- Automated diagnostic report generation from engine vibration data
- Maintenance Schedule Optimization (Leveraging Fleet Usage Data)
- Predictive replenishment and automatic ordering of parts inventory

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses to overlay repair diagrams and real-time data to improve troubleshooting efficiency
- AI assists in analyzing historical repair case databases to quickly locate rare faults
- Digital twin simulates maintenance processes to optimize disassembly and assembly steps
- Use natural language processing to automatically generate maintenance logs and compliance documents
- Remote expert system collaboration for complex modifications/repairs

**Human moat:**

- Manual craftsmanship and experiential intuition in high-risk repair operations (e.g., engine disassembly, line repair)
- Creative troubleshooting for unexpected failures (involving cross-system logical reasoning)
- Legal responsibility and signing authority for airworthiness release (individual license holder bears liability)
- Communication with and compliance assessment of the regulator (CASA)
- On-site resource coordination and decision-making during emergency repairs

**Skills to build:**

- Digital twin platform operation and virtual maintenance drills
- Output verification and bias correction of AI diagnostic tools
- Aviation data analysis basics (Python/R, focusing on engine margin trends)
- Using augmented reality (AR) maintenance assistance systems
- Digital auditing of airworthiness safety management systems (SMS)
- Remote repair support tools for cross-border collaboration (e.g., Teamcenter)

**Key growth areas:**

- Air traffic management
- Radar systems
- Communication systems
- Maintenance technology

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician in Sweden?**
A: Salaries for Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technicians are generally above the national average, reflecting the specialized skills and responsibility. Entry-level salaries typically range from 35,000 to 45,000 SEK per month, while experienced technicians can earn 50,000 SEK or more. Exact figures depend on employer, location, and collective agreements.

**Q: Can I work as an Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician in Sweden as a non-EU citizen?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens can work in Sweden via an employer-tied work permit or an EU Blue Card. However, the profession is regulated: you must have your foreign qualifications assessed by Transportstyrelsen and possibly complete supplementary training. Swedish language proficiency is often required, as safety documentation and communication are in Swedish.

**Q: What education is needed to become an Air Traffic Safety Electronics Technician in Sweden?**
A: The most common path is a 2-year vocational programme (Yrkeshögskola) in aviation electronics or a 3-year upper secondary technical programme followed by on-the-job training. A bachelor's degree in electrical engineering can also be a starting point. Certification from Transportstyrelsen is mandatory for independent work.
