# Civil Engineering Labourer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9312 (ISCO08)**

Civil engineering labourers perform manual tasks at construction sites, roads, dams, railways, and other large-scale infrastructure projects. In Sweden, this occupation is part of the broader construction labour force, with demand closely tied to public infrastructure investment and housing development. Work is often seasonal and physically demanding, requiring outdoor work in varying weather conditions. Many workers are employed through temporary agencies or on project-by-project contracts. The role typically does not require formal education, but on-the-job training and safety certifications are common.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for civil engineering labourers in Sweden often leads to specialized roles such as paving operator, concrete worker, or driller. With experience and additional training, some move into supervisory positions like foreman or site supervisor. Others may transition to related trades, such as carpentry or plumbing. On-the-job training and vocational courses through municipal adult education (Komvux) or labour market training (Arbetsförmedlingen) are common pathways. Few advance to higher engineering roles without formal education, but the occupation can serve as an entry point to the construction sector.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use total stations, laser rangefinders and other instruments for manual measurement and marking
- Manual labour of carrying and stacking building materials (e.g., steel bars, cement bags)
- Manually recording work logs, material lists, and progress reports
- Performs quality checks through visual inspection or simple tools
- Operate traditional hand tools (e.g., shovels, picks) for digging and leveling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using BIM models and AR glasses for visual construction guidance
- Monitor equipment operation and material inventory through real-time sensors
- Using drones and AI image recognition for construction site safety inspections
- Use data analysis to optimize construction processes and material usage
- Use digital platforms for remote collaboration and reporting with teams

**Human moat:**

- Adjust construction plans in complex terrain and adverse weather conditions.
- On-site judgment and handling of emergencies (e.g., underground pipeline conflicts)
- Team coordination and verbal communication skills (especially cross-trade collaboration)
- Quick response to abnormal tool failures and material defects
- Creatively solve non-standard construction problems (e.g., reinforcing old structures)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital tool usage (tablets, construction management apps)
- Reading and basic operation of BIM models
- Digital operation of measuring instruments (e.g., digital levels)
- Safety awareness and use of AI-assisted safety systems
- Basic data analysis (material consumption, progress records)
- Communication and teamwork skills (multilingual construction site environment)

**Key growth areas:**

- infrastructure investment
- road maintenance
- rail expansion
- green energy construction

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a civil engineering labourer in Sweden?**
A: The typical monthly salary for civil engineering labourers in Sweden is around 28,000–32,000 SEK pre-tax, depending on experience, region, and employer. Annual salaries range from 336,000 to 384,000 SEK. Collective agreements set minimums, and overtime or night work can increase pay.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a civil engineering labourer?**
A: It is possible but challenging. Sweden's work permit system requires the occupation to be on the shortage list or the employer to demonstrate recruitment efforts within EU/EEA. As of 2025, civil engineering labourer is not on Sweden's shortage list, so permit approval may be unlikely unless the job involves specialized skills (e.g., machine operation) or the employer can prove a genuine shortage.

**Q: What career advancement opportunities exist for civil engineering labourers in Sweden?**
A: Common paths include becoming a machine operator (e.g., excavator, bulldozer) through additional training, or moving into supervision (foreman). Some use the role as a stepping stone to other construction trades (e.g., carpenter, concrete worker). Vocational education can lead to higher-skilled positions.
