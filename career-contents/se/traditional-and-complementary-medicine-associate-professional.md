# Traditional and Complementary Medicine Associate Professional — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3230 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, traditional and complementary medicine associate professionals include naprapaths, osteopaths, chiropractors, and acupuncturists. They provide non-pharmacological treatments often in private clinics or integrated healthcare settings. The profession is regulated for some titles, requiring licensing from the National Board of Health and Welfare, while others operate under voluntary certification. Demand is growing due to increased interest in holistic and preventive care, but competition is moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a Bachelor's degree in naprapathy, osteopathy, or chiropractic (3 years), followed by licensing and private practice. Many professionals eventually open their own clinics or specialize in areas like sports medicine or geriatrics. Continuing education is important for maintaining certification. Some transition into teaching or research at universities, but the majority remain in clinical roles.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a complementary medicine associate professional in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, specialization, and sector. Private practitioners' income depends on client volume, while employed roles in clinics or sports teams follow collective agreements. No specific figures are provided here, but typical annual pay ranges from 300,000 to 500,000 SEK for employees.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a naprapath in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but requires a work permit sponsored by an employer. The job must meet the occupation's standard salary and benefits as per collective agreements. Licensure from Socialstyrelsen is mandatory, which involves a lengthy recognition process of foreign qualifications.

**Q: Is a Swedish degree necessary to practice?**
A: For regulated titles like naprapath, osteopath, and chiropractor, a degree from an approved program (often from Sweden or other EU/EEA) is required. International qualifications must be evaluated by Socialstyrelsen and may require supplementary training.
