# Street and Related Services Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9510 (ISCO08)**

Street and related services workers in Sweden include street vendors, news sellers, and other individuals providing direct services in public spaces. These roles often involve selling goods, offering services, or engaging in informal economic activities. The labour market for such workers is limited, with most positions being part-time or seasonal, and competition is high due to low entry barriers. Employment opportunities are primarily in larger cities and tourist areas, but overall demand is weak.

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 22,115. Sweden's employment outlook for street and related services workers is negative. The occupation is not expected to see significant recovery post-pandemic, with few new job openings. Most workers in this category are self-employed or in precarious employment.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression for street and related services workers in Sweden is very limited. Many individuals use these roles as temporary income sources while seeking more stable employment. Transitioning to retail, hospitality, or other service sectors is common, but formal career paths are rare. The occupation offers little job security or advancement, and most workers remain at the same level throughout their tenure.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

**Key growth areas:**

- Urban tourism
- Food trucks
- Digital payments
- Seasonal events

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are typical earnings for street vendors in Sweden?**
A: Earnings vary widely by location and season, but average monthly income is low, often around 20,000–25,000 SEK before tax. Many vendors are self-employed and must manage their own taxes and social fees.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen get a work permit as a street vendor in Sweden?**
A: Highly unlikely. The occupation does not meet the salary threshold or skill level required for a Work Permit. Non-EU citizens should explore other, more qualified occupations.
