# Sewing, Embroidery and Related Workers — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7533 (ISCO08)**

Sewing, embroidery and related workers in Sweden typically work in textile manufacturing, tailoring, or repair shops, using industrial or domestic sewing machines to produce or alter garments, home textiles, and other fabric items. The occupation is concentrated in small businesses and the declining garment industry, with most workers employed in the regions of Västsverige and Östergötland. Automation and offshoring have reduced demand, but niche markets in high-end tailoring, costume making, and textile restoration offer some opportunities.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Career progression is limited, often moving from operator to supervisor or self-employed specialist. Formal training through vocational adult education (yrkesvux) or upper secondary school can lead to certification. Many workers transition to roles in fashion design, textile technology, or retail, while others advance by mastering high-skill niches like hand embroidery or haute couture. Self-employment via ateljé or freelance work is common for experienced tailors.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated sewing machines perform standardized straight stitching and overlocking.
- AI layout systems automatically arrange embroidery patterns and generate machine instructions.
- Computer vision detects stitch defects and automatically adjusts parameters

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software quickly generates embroidery patterns and previews them.
- Smart sewing machine automatically adjusts tension based on fabric elasticity
- Using AR technology to overlay virtual samples to assist hand sewing
- Data analysis optimizes production scheduling to reduce changeover time

**Human moat:**

- Aesthetic and tactile qualities of complex techniques such as hand embroidery and seed stitch
- Expertise in three-dimensional tailoring and creative design for custom clothing.
- Exclusive handling experience with non-standard fabrics (silk, leather)
- Communicate face-to-face with clients to understand personalized needs

**Skills to build:**

- Digital embroidery design software (e.g., Wilcom, Tajima)
- Industrial sewing machine programming and automated tuning
- Fabric properties and sustainable materials knowledge
- Basic CAD pattern making and 3D garment simulation
- Customer communication and customized service skills
- Lean production and quality control methods

**Key growth areas:**

- Bespoke tailoring
- Sustainable fashion
- Textile restoration
- Costume making

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a sewing worker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries range from approximately 22,000 to 28,000 SEK per month for full-time employment, depending on experience and region. This is below the average Swedish wage and often near the minimum for work permit eligibility.

**Q: Can a non-EU tailor move to Sweden to work as a sewing worker?**
A: It is very difficult, as the occupation is not on the shortage list and typical salaries may not meet the work permit threshold. Self-employment or intra-company transfer are alternative but challenging routes.

**Q: What advancement options exist beyond sewing?**
A: Skilled workers can become pattern makers, textile technicians, or start their own tailoring business. Further education in fashion design or textile engineering opens doors to higher-paying roles.
