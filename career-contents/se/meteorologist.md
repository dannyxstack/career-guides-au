# Meteorologist — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2112 (ISCO08)**

Meteorologists in Sweden study atmospheric processes to provide weather forecasts, climate analysis, and environmental monitoring. They are primarily employed by the Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute (SMHI), a government agency, but also work in the private sector for energy companies, consulting firms, and media. The role requires strong analytical skills and often involves shift work for real-time forecasting. Sweden's focus on climate change adaptation and renewable energy, particularly wind power, adds demand for specialized meteorological expertise. Entry typically requires a Master's degree, and career progression can lead to senior forecaster, research, or management positions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression for meteorologists in Sweden typically starts as a forecaster or research assistant at SMHI or a university. With experience, one can move into senior forecaster roles, project management, or specialized areas such as climate modeling, air quality monitoring, or renewable energy assessment. An advanced degree (PhD) opens pathways to research and academic positions. Some meteorologists transition into data science or environmental consulting. Continuous learning and adaptation to new technology, such as advanced satellite data and AI-driven models, are key for advancement.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic generation of routine weather forecast text
- Compilation and preliminary statistical analysis of historical meteorological data
- Standard warning content editing based on numerical model output
- Simple fault diagnosis and standardized reporting of meteorological observation equipment
- Write descriptive reports on basic climate trends

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Interpretation and Probability Calibration of Ensemble Forecasts Using AI
- Combine deep learning to predict local extreme weather (e.g., thunderstorms, heavy rain).
- AI-assisted climate change attribution analysis and scenario simulation
- Automatically generate customized industry weather reports (agriculture, energy).
- Using natural language processing to automatically extract meteorological research progress from literature

**Human moat:**

- Physical intuition and interdisciplinary understanding of complex weather systems
- Deliver authoritative advice to the public and decision-makers during high-impact weather events
- Designing novel observation experiments and scientific methods for integrating multi-source data
- Assume legal and ethical responsibilities (e.g., consequences of failing to issue early warnings)
- Collaborative decision-making with related fields (hydrology, ecology)

**Skills to build:**

- Python/R programming and machine learning frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch)
- Principles and applications of weather AI models (e.g., FourCastNet, GraphCast)
- Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification techniques
- Climate big data visualization and interactive dashboard development
- Cross-disciplinary Skills (Economics, Risk Management, Public Policy)
- AI ethics and social impact assessment of weather warnings

**Key growth areas:**

- climate change
- weather forecasting
- environmental monitoring
- data analysis

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for meteorologists in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level meteorologists at SMHI typically earn around 35,000–40,000 SEK per month, while experienced meteorologists can earn 45,000–55,000 SEK. Senior roles or specialized positions may exceed 60,000 SEK.

**Q: Can non-EU meteorologists get a work visa for Sweden?**
A: Yes, non-EU meteorologists can obtain an employer-tied work permit if they have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets collective agreement salary and work conditions. A EU Blue Card is also possible for roles with a higher salary threshold (typically above 50,000 SEK/month).
