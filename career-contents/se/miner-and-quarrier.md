# Miner and Quarrier — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 8111 (ISCO08)**

Miners and quarriers in Sweden extract minerals, ore, and rock from underground mines and surface quarries. This occupation is concentrated in northern Sweden (e.g., Kiruna, Gällivare) and Bergslagen, working for large mining companies like LKAB, Boliden, and Zinkgruvan. Work involves drilling, blasting, loading, and transporting materials using heavy machinery. Modern Swedish mines emphasize automation, safety, and environmental standards. Employment is typically full-time, shift-based, and often requires relocation to remote communities. The occupation demands physical fitness, technical skill, and adherence to strict safety protocols.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as an underground or quarry helper, advancing to machine operator, then to senior operator or supervisor. Specialization options include drilling and blasting, mine transport, or processing plant work. Further education can lead to roles as mining technician or shift foreman. The occupation offers opportunities to work with advanced autonomous equipment, with paths toward mining engineering for those who pursue higher education.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Operating mining equipment such as drills and excavators (automated remote control replaces manual driving)
- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)
- Daily equipment inspection and simple maintenance (predictive maintenance system)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)
- Production scheduling and resource allocation (AI optimization algorithm)

**Human moat:**

- On-site assessment and handling of emergencies (e.g., collapses, gas leaks)
- Adaptive operation for abnormal geological conditions (unstructured environment)
- Team safety management and communication coordination
- Mining regulation compliance and government reporting responsibilities
- Complex repairs and equipment modifications (requiring physical hands-on skills)

**Skills to build:**

- Operate automated mining systems and remote-control equipment
- Data analysis and use of mining software (e.g., Surpac, Datamine)
- Skills in drone and sensor maintenance
- Basic programming and automation scripting (Python)
- Safety regulations and emergency response training
- Hazardous environment work skills (e.g., confined space entry)

**Key growth areas:**

- Automation
- Green transition
- Electric mine vehicles
- Remote operation

## 8. FAQ

**Q: How are salaries determined for miners and quarriers in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements between trade unions (e.g., IF Metall) and employer organizations. Pay varies by experience, shift work, and location. Underground work typically pays more than surface. Bonuses may apply for remote sites. There is no official published minimum, but entry-level pay often exceeds the work permit threshold.

**Q: Can a non-EU miner get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish mining company that meets the collective agreement salary (at least SEK 26,560/month) and the employer has advertised the position in Sweden and EEA. The permit is tied to that employer for the first two years. After four years, you may qualify for permanent residence.

**Q: What are the working conditions like for miners in Sweden?**
A: Most work is full-time on rotating shifts (day, evening, night). Safety standards are high, with mandatory training and protective equipment. Work may involve exposure to dust, noise, and vibration. Modern mines are increasingly automated, reducing heavy manual labour. Remote mine sites often provide housing and transportation.
