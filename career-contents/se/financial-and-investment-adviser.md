# Financial and Investment Adviser — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2412 (ISCO08)**

Financial and investment advisers in Sweden guide clients on savings, investments, pensions, and insurance, working primarily for banks, insurance companies, and independent advisory firms. The profession is regulated by Finansinspektionen, requiring a license (Swedsec) for advising on financial instruments. Demand remains stable due to increasing financial complexity and an aging population seeking retirement planning, though competition is moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a junior adviser, advancing to senior adviser, then team leader or branch manager. Many move into independent advisory or specialise in areas like corporate finance, wealth management, or sustainable investing. Professional development through certifications (e.g., CEFA, CFA) is common and enhances career opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standardized portfolio reports and risk assessment documents
- Automated market trend analysis and preliminary investment recommendations based on historical data
- Manage client account information and send automated investment reminders and performance updates
- Handling common customer inquiries and basic financial issues via AI chatbot

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Leverage AI tools to quickly extract key insights from massive data, supporting personalized investment strategies
- Multi-dimensional financial planning (e.g., retirement, tax optimization) through AI-driven scenario simulations.
- Use natural language generation tools to draft high-quality customer communication materials
- Using AI to monitor market anomalies and adjust portfolio risk exposure in real time
- Assisting with compliance reviews and anti-money laundering screening through algorithms

**Human moat:**

- Empathy and communication skills to deeply understand clients' personalized goals and risk preferences
- Complex judgment and decision-making in legal, tax, and ethical scenarios
- Build and maintain long-term client trust through personal relationships
- Cross-disciplinary knowledge integration (e.g., macroeconomics, industry analysis, behavioral finance)

**Skills to build:**

- Data processing and analysis with Python/R and SQL
- Application of AI/machine learning in financial modeling (e.g., asset pricing, risk prediction)
- Proficient in natural language processing tools (e.g., ChatGPT) for customer interactions and report generation
- Operation and configuration of digital investment platforms (e.g., WealthTech)
- Data visualization (Tableau/Power BI) and KPI dashboard design
- Strengthen critical thinking and ethical judgment in complex decision-making and compliance areas

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable investing
- Wealth management
- Digital financial advice
- Pension planning

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary range for financial advisers in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and employer, but are competitive within the banking sector. Entry-level positions start around 35,000–40,000 SEK per month, while experienced advisers earn 50,000–70,000 SEK or more, plus bonuses.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as financial advisers in Sweden?**
A: Yes, with a job offer meeting collective agreement salary and typically a relevant degree. A Swedsec license may be required for certain roles, which can be obtained after employment. The employer must obtain a work permit decision from the Swedish Migration Agency.

**Q: What education is needed to become a financial adviser in Sweden?**
A: A bachelor's degree in finance, economics, or business is standard. Additional certifications like Swedsec (mandatory for securities advice) and CEFA/CFA are advantageous. For investment advice, a master's degree may be preferred.
