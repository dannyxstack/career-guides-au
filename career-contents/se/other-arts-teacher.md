# Other Arts Teacher — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2355 (ISCO08)**

Other arts teachers in Sweden provide instruction in music, dance, drama, fine arts, and other creative disciplines outside the formal school system, often in municipal cultural schools, adult education associations, or private studios. This occupation is part of Sweden's rich tradition of folkbildning (popular education) and community arts, with many practitioners working part-time or freelance. The role demands both artistic skill and pedagogical ability, and can lead to diverse opportunities in cultural institutions, community projects, or private teaching.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 14,145. Employment for arts teachers is stable but modest in growth, closely tied to public funding for culture and adult education. Municipal culture schools (kulturskolor) are a key employer, but budgets vary by region. Digitalization has opened new avenues for online teaching, while integration initiatives create demand for arts as a tool for inclusion. Overall, opportunities are best in larger cities and for those with combined competencies in multiple art forms.

**Competition: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**Work intensity: low (★½☆☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression often involves moving from part-time teaching to more senior or specialized roles, such as head of a department in a kulturskola, or becoming a freelance artist-educator. Many combine teaching with own artistic practice. Further education in pedagogy or arts management can lead to positions in cultural policy, museum education, or adult education administration. Networking and a strong portfolio are crucial for advancement.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an arts teacher in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by employer and experience. Full-time positions typically offer a monthly salary in the range of 30,000–40,000 SEK, but many positions are part-time, so actual income may be lower.

**Q: Is it easy to get a work permit as an arts teacher in Sweden?**
A: It is challenging because the occupation is not on the shortage list. Non-EU applicants need a job offer meeting collective-agreement pay and the employer must demonstrate recruitment efforts within Sweden and the EU. EU/EEA citizens do not need a permit.

**Q: Do I need a Swedish teaching license to work as an arts teacher?**
A: No, a formal teaching license is not mandatory for arts teachers in non-formal settings like kulturskolor or adult education associations. However, a relevant degree and experience are expected.
