# Customs and Border Inspector — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3351 (ISCO08)**

Customs and border inspectors in Sweden, employed primarily by the Swedish Customs (Tullverket) and the Police Authority, control the flow of goods and people across Sweden's borders. They enforce customs and immigration laws, collect duties and taxes, prevent smuggling, and ensure compliance with EU and national regulations. The role requires vigilance, integrity, and knowledge of trade and travel rules. With Sweden's position as an EU external border and increased security focus, demand remains steady. Inspectors work at airports, seaports, land borders, and postal terminals, often in shifts. The job offers societal impact and a structured career path within public service.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically begins with a trainee inspector role, followed by permanent positions as a customs inspector or border control officer. With experience, one can advance to senior inspector, team leader, or specialist roles in areas such as risk analysis, criminal investigation, customs intelligence, or trade facilitation. The Swedish Customs offers internal training and career development programs. Further advancement may lead to management positions within the agency. The profession values continuous learning in regulations and technology. Retraining possibilities exist, but the career path is linear within the public sector.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically scan passports and verify visa authenticity

**Where AI augments the role:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a customs inspector in Sweden?**
A: Starting monthly salary for a customs inspector is around 30,000–35,000 SEK pre-tax, increasing with experience and specialization. Senior inspectors may earn 40,000–48,000 SEK.

**Q: Can non-EU/EEA citizens become customs inspectors in Sweden?**
A: Generally no, because the position requires Swedish citizenship or, in rare cases, EU/EEA citizenship plus fluent Swedish. Non-EU citizens cannot obtain a work permit for this occupation due to the citizenship requirement.

**Q: How long does it take to become a customs inspector?**
A: After being hired, the Swedish Customs training program lasts about 1.5 years, after which you become a certified inspector. Prior education in law or logistics may speed up recruitment but is not mandatory.
