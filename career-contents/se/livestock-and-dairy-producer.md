# Livestock and Dairy Producer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6121 (ISCO08)**

Livestock and dairy producers in Sweden manage herds of cattle, sheep, or goats for milk, meat, or breeding, often on family-owned farms. The sector is concentrated in southern and central Sweden, with a strong emphasis on animal welfare, sustainability, and productivity. Structural changes and an aging workforce create steady demand for skilled professionals, though competition for positions on larger, modern farms is moderate.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts as a farm worker or assistant, advancing to herd manager or farm manager. Many eventually become self-employed farm owners. Specialization in niche areas (e.g., organic dairy, rare breeds) or additional education in agricultural engineering or veterinary science can open roles in agri-tech, advisory services, or quality control. Continuing education in animal welfare and environmental regulations is valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic feeding systems replace manual feeding and water management.
- Smart sensors automatically monitor animal health data and send alerts
- Milking robots replace manual milking operations.
- Automation of data recording and analysis replaces manual ledgers
- Driverless tractors replace some field labor

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted early disease warning system improves veterinary diagnostic accuracy.
- IoT optimizes pasture environment control and feed ratios on farms
- Drone inspection of large-scale pastures to improve management efficiency
- Data analysis tools assist breeding plans and yield prediction
- Blockchain technology tracks dairy supply chains to enhance trust

**Human moat:**

- On-site judgment of animal welfare and emergency health conditions
- Unstructured problem-solving ability in complex environments
- Interpersonal communication and team management with veterinarians, staff, and others
- Trade-off decisions on macro factors such as ecology, market, and policy.
- Intuition and feel gained from long-term farming experience

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and sensor technology application
- Automated equipment operation and maintenance
- Use of AI diagnostic tools for animal health
- Sustainable agriculture and precision farming practices
- Digital marketing and supply chain tracking
- Change management and team digital leadership

**Key growth areas:**

- organic farming
- animal welfare
- precision livestock farming
- sustainability

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- People who enjoy working outdoors and with animals in a rural setting.
- Individuals with a strong work ethic and willingness to handle physical tasks in varying weather.
- Those interested in sustainable agriculture and modern livestock management.

**Who is not suited**
- People who prefer an urban, desk-based, or highly structured work environment.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary range for a livestock and dairy producer in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary with farm size, region, and experience. Entry-level farm workers earn around 24,000–28,000 SEK/month, while experienced herd managers can earn 30,000–40,000 SEK/month. Self-employed farmers' income depends on production and market prices.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a livestock producer in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but they need an employer-tied work permit. The employer must offer terms equivalent to Swedish collective agreements (salary, insurance, etc.) and demonstrate that the position could not be filled by an EU/EEA candidate. Work permits are typically granted for up to two years and can be renewed.

**Q: Is there demand for livestock producers in Sweden?**
A: Demand is moderate. While the total number of farms is declining, many existing producers are retiring, creating replacement demand. Opportunities are best for those with skills in organic farming, animal health, and modern technology. Competition for jobs on large, efficient farms is higher.
