# Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2132 (ISCO08)**

Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Advisers in Sweden provide expert guidance to landowners, companies, and government agencies on sustainable agricultural practices, forest management, and aquaculture. They work within a framework of EU and national regulations, advising on crop rotation, pest control, soil conservation, timber harvesting, fish stock management, and environmental compliance. The role often involves fieldwork, data analysis, and extension services to improve productivity while minimizing ecological impact. With Sweden's strong emphasis on green transition and bioeconomy, these advisers are key players in the shift toward climate-smart land use and renewable resources.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★½☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a Bachelor's or Master's degree in agricultural science, forestry, or related fields. Entry-level roles often involve fieldwork and junior advisory tasks under supervision. With experience, advisers can specialize (e.g., organic farming, forest certification, aquaculture) or advance to senior consultant, project manager, or regional coordinator positions. Some move into policy development at the Swedish Board of Agriculture or EU institutions. Self-employment as a private consultant is also common. Continuing education through courses from the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU) or industry bodies is valued.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Generate standardized soil test reports and crop growth cycle analyses
- Automated fertiliser recommendation based on historical data and satellite imagery
- Draft basic sustainable forest management plans
- Automatically aggregate fishing catch data and generate compliance reports
- Answer common pest and disease control questions (knowledge base chatbot)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimize precision agriculture decision-making recommendations using AI remote sensing analysis and big data
- Simulate long-term environmental impacts of different planting/breeding strategies using AI models
- Real-time monitoring of weather and market data, dynamically adjusting advisor recommendations
- Automates data collection and initial analysis, allowing consultants to focus on farmer communication and customized solutions
- AI-assisted generation of visual reports to improve communication efficiency with farmers

**Human moat:**

- Ability to build trust and long-term relationships with farmers
- On-site problem diagnosis based on local experience and intuition
- Handling complex, unique sustainability conflicts involving multiple stakeholders
- Adaptability to unstructured, low-information-density environments (e.g., remote farms)
- Cross-disciplinary integration (agriculture, ecology, economics) providing customized solutions

**Skills to build:**

- Application of AI tools (e.g., remote sensing analysis platforms, agricultural decision support systems)
- Data science fundamentals (Python/R, statistical modeling, data visualization).
- Precision agriculture technology (drones, sensors, IoT data analysis)
- Environmental and social impact assessment methodology
- Communication and change management skills (to promote adoption of new technologies among farmers)
- Continuous learning and industry knowledge updates (policy, market, biotechnology).

**Key growth areas:**

- Precision agriculture
- Sustainable forestry
- Aquaculture
- Digital advisory tools

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a Farming, Forestry and Fisheries Adviser in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, employer, and region. Entry-level advisers typically earn around 30,000–35,000 SEK per month, while senior consultants can earn 40,000–50,000 SEK. Public sector roles often follow collective agreements, and private consultancies may offer performance-based bonuses.

**Q: Can a non-EU national work as an agricultural adviser in Sweden?**
A: Yes, through a work permit tied to a specific employer. The job must meet collective agreement pay and conditions. An EU Blue Card is also possible if the salary exceeds the high-skilled threshold. The occupation is not on the shortage list, so labour market testing (advertising in Sweden/EU) is required.

**Q: What are the most in-demand specializations for agricultural advisers in Sweden?**
A: Precision agriculture, organic farming, sustainable forestry, and aquaculture are growing fields. Advisors with expertise in EU subsidy applications (CAP) and climate adaptation are also highly sought.
