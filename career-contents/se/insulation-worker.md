# Insulation Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7124 (ISCO08)**

Insulation workers install and maintain thermal, acoustic, and fire-resistant insulation in buildings and industrial facilities. In Sweden, demand is driven by strict energy efficiency building codes, a booming construction sector, and extensive renovation of older housing stock. Workers apply materials such as mineral wool, foam, and fiberglass in walls, roofs, and pipes. The role requires physical stamina, attention to detail, and comfort working in confined spaces or at heights. Employment is typically with construction firms or specialized insulation contractors, and many workers are unionized under collective agreements.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically follows a path from apprentice to skilled journeyman, then to foreman or site supervisor. Experienced workers may specialize in industrial insulation, fireproofing, or sustainable materials such as cellulose or sheep's wool. Self-employment is common, with many starting their own subcontracting firms. Continuing education through adult vocational courses (Komvux) can lead to further certifications in advanced techniques or project management. The trade also offers mobility to related construction roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated material cutting and layout based on architectural drawings
- Heat loss calculation and insulation thickness optimization
- Automated inventory management and material ordering
- Digital tracking and report generation for construction progress

**Where AI augments the role:**

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an insulation worker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by experience, region, and employer. Entry-level workers can expect a monthly salary around 25,000–30,000 SEK, with higher pay for experienced journeymen. Overtime and travel allowances may add to earnings.

**Q: Can a non-EU insulation worker get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the collective agreement wage and conditions. The employer must advertise the job in Sweden and the EU first. The permit is tied to that employer. After four years, you may qualify for permanent residence.

**Q: Is there demand for insulation workers in Sweden?**
A: Yes, due to ongoing construction and energy renovation projects, especially in larger cities. The occupation is on Sweden's shortage list for some regions, meaning good opportunities for skilled workers.
