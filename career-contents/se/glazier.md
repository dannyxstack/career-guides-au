# Glazier — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7125 (ISCO08)**

Glaziers install, replace, and repair glass in windows, doors, facades, and interior partitions. In Sweden, demand is driven by construction, renovation, and energy-efficiency upgrades, with a steady need for skilled tradespeople, especially in urban areas. The occupation requires precision and safety awareness, with opportunities in both residential and commercial sectors. Glaziers often work on scaffolding or lifts, handling large glass panels and using specialized tools. The job is physically demanding but offers stable employment and potential for specialization in areas like insulated glass units or safety glass.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 1,765. The employment outlook for glaziers in Sweden is favorable due to ongoing construction activity and a growing focus on energy-efficient buildings. Renovation of older properties and new green building projects sustain demand. However, the industry faces competition from cheaper imports and automation in some segments. Skilled glaziers are in short supply, leading to good job prospects for qualified workers. The trend toward larger glass surfaces in architecture also creates niche opportunities.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with a vocational education (gymnasieskola or vuxenutbildning) and apprenticeship. Glaziers can advance to master glazier status (mästarbrev) or become site supervisors. Many choose self-employment or start their own glass company. Specializations include energy-efficient glazing, soundproofing, and historical restoration. Continuous learning in new materials and techniques (e.g., smart glass) is valued. With experience, glaziers may move into sales, project management, or teaching.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic optimization calculation of glass dimensions and layout
- BIM-based glass installation sequence planning
- Automated processing of inventory management and material procurement.
- Automatic generation and archiving of installation reports

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AR glasses for installation positioning and verification
- AI-assisted glass cutting optimization to reduce waste
- Drone or Robot-Assisted Glass Installation on High-Rise Buildings
- Smart tools for real-time monitoring of installation quality

**Human moat:**

- Physical tasks: on-site glass handling, positioning, and fixing
- Handling custom installation of non-standard and shaped glass
- Ability to handle emergencies such as glass breakage or structural deviations.
- Coordinate on-site with architects, contractors, etc.

**Skills to build:**

- Reading BIM models and collaboration
- Drone/robot-assisted installation operations
- Use of AR/VR assisted construction tools
- Basic Python for automated reporting
- Green energy-efficient glass performance evaluation
- Project management and communication coordination skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Energy-efficient glazing
- Sustainable construction
- Renovation
- Safety glass

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a glazier in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience, region, and sector, but are generally competitive and meet the minimum threshold required for work permits. Entry-level positions may earn around 25,000-30,000 SEK per month, with experienced glaziers earning 35,000-45,000 SEK or more. Self-employed glaziers can earn higher but incur business costs.

**Q: Can a non-EU glazier get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if there is a job offer from a Swedish employer with terms meeting collective agreements. The occupation is often on the shortage list, which facilitates the process. The employer must advertise the job in Sweden/EU first. The permit is tied to that employer and job.

**Q: Do I need formal education to work as a glazier in Sweden?**
A: While not strictly mandatory, most employers prefer candidates with a vocational certificate in glass working. Upper secondary or adult education programs provide necessary skills. On-the-job training is possible, but certification improves job prospects.
