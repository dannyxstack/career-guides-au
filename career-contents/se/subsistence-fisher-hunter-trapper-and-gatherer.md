# Subsistence Fisher, Hunter, Trapper and Gatherer — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 6340 (ISCO08)**

This occupation covers individuals who fish, hunt, trap, or gather wild products primarily for their own subsistence, with limited commercial sales. In Sweden, it is a very small and traditional group, mainly in rural and remote areas such as the far north or archipelago. Practitioners often combine these activities with other income sources or are part of indigenous Sámi communities. The work depends strongly on natural resource availability and seasonal cycles. Formal employment is rare, as most are self-employed or operate within family or cultural traditions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression is almost nonexistent; individuals typically remain in the same role for life. Some may expand their operations to include guiding services, selling handicrafts, or processing and selling meat or fish commercially. Knowledge is passed down informally, and there are no formal advancement paths. Most practitioners are self-employed or engage in part-time activities alongside other occupations. The work offers high autonomy but low and unpredictable income.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using satellite images and weather forecasts to plan collection routes (AI replaces manual experience-based judgment)
- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)
- Record and count catch volume (automated data entry replaces manual recording)
- Basic tool maintenance reminders (IoT sensors replacing manual checks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work
- Skills in manually handling catch and performing simple processing

**Skills to build:**

- Basic data analysis (capture records, weather patterns)

**Key growth areas:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What are typical earnings for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers and gatherers in Sweden?**
A: Earnings are modest and highly variable, often supplemented by other work. Most practitioners earn below the median Swedish income, with annual income typically ranging between 100,000 and 250,000 SEK depending on season and local conditions.

**Q: What visa options exist for non-EU subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers and gatherers?**
A: This occupation does not qualify for Swedish work permits. Only EU/EEA citizens can freely engage in this activity. Non-EU nationals would need to apply based on other grounds, such as family ties or refugee status.

**Q: Is there any formal training available in Sweden for this occupation?**
A: No formal degree exists, but courses in hunting safety, fishing techniques, and game processing are offered by organizations like the Swedish Hunters' Association and folk high schools.
