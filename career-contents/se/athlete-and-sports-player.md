# Athletes and Sports Players — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 3421 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, athletes and sports players compete in professional or semi-professional sports while often balancing part-time jobs or studies. The labour market is niche, with opportunities concentrated in football, ice hockey, handball, and increasingly e-sports. Most athletes are employed by clubs or associations, and career longevity is limited. Sweden's strong sports culture and public funding for elite athletes support a small but stable sector.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for an athlete in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary enormously: top football or ice hockey players can earn millions, but most athletes in lower leagues or niche sports earn around 20,000–30,000 SEK per month, often with part-time complementary jobs. Living expenses are high, so financial planning is essential.

**Q: Can a non-EU athlete get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, but only for elite-level positions. The club must offer a salary in line with collective agreements (often high, e.g., 250,000 SEK/year or more) and prove that the athlete is among the best. It's a restricted route; most professional athletes come from EU/EEA.

**Q: What are the long-term career prospects after retiring from sport?**
A: Many become coaches, personal trainers, or enter sports management. Some retrain in other fields via university or vocational education. The Swedish Sports Confederation offers transition support programs.
