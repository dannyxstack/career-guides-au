# Secondary Education Teacher — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2330 (ISCO08)**

Secondary education teachers in Sweden instruct students in grades 7–9 (grundskola) and upper secondary school (gymnasium), covering a range of subjects. They hold a teaching license (lärarlegitimation) from Skolverket, typically requiring a university degree of 4–5 years. The profession is regulated and offers stable employment with strong union representation. Demand is high in STEM, languages, and special education, particularly in rural areas. Salaries are set by collective agreements, with a starting annual salary around 320,000–360,000 SEK.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 28,020. The Swedish Public Employment Service forecasts continued shortages of certified secondary teachers through 2030, especially in mathematics, natural sciences, technology, and modern languages. An aging workforce and increasing student numbers drive demand. Urban areas are more competitive, while rural and northern regions face acute deficits. Digitalisation and bilingual education (e.g., English-taught programmes) are emerging needs.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

Career progression includes becoming a lead teacher (förstelärare) or senior teacher (lektor) with higher pay and responsibilities. Some move into school leadership (principal, rektor) or subject development roles. Continuing professional development is encouraged. Mobility between schools and municipalities is common, but licensed teachers are highly sought after. Many transition to adult education (Komvux) or vocational training.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated grading of standardized assignments and multiple-choice questions
- Generate basic lesson plans and course draft materials
- handling student attendance and grade records
- Provide standardized FAQ services

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Analyze student learning data with AI to achieve personalized teaching
- Using AI to generate differentiated practice problems and extension materials
- Enhance classroom interaction and lab simulation with AI virtual assistants
- Using natural language processing tools to quickly search teaching materials
- Use AI to monitor student participation and adjust teaching strategies in a timely manner.

**Human moat:**

- Classroom management and student behavior guidance
- Emotional support and psychological counseling
- Complex teaching decisions and curriculum design
- In-depth communication with parents and the community
- Cultivate students' critical thinking and creativity

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and learning situation diagnosis.
- AI teaching tool applications (e.g., Knewton, Carnegie Learning)
- Digital content creation and multimedia teaching
- Programming and STEM education basics
- Online teaching and blended learning design
- AI ethics and data privacy protection

**Key growth areas:**

- STEM
- Digital Pedagogy
- Special Education
- Bilingual Teaching

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a secondary education teacher in Sweden?**
A: Starting annual salary is about 320,000–360,000 SEK (around 27,000–30,000 SEK/month). Experienced teachers with lead teacher status can earn 500,000–600,000 SEK/year. Salaries are set by collective agreements and vary by municipality and subject.

**Q: Do I need a work permit to teach in Sweden as a non-EU/EEA citizen?**
A: Yes, you need an employer-tied work permit. You must have a job offer that meets collective agreement pay, and the employer must have advertised the position in the EU first. Additionally, you must obtain a Swedish teaching license (lärarlegitimation) from Skolverket, which may require supplementing your education and passing Swedish proficiency exams.

**Q: Can I teach in Sweden with a foreign teaching degree?**
A: Yes, but you must have your qualifications assessed by Skolverket. You may need to complete additional courses (e.g., in Swedish education system, didactics) and prove Swedish language skills at CEFR B2/C1. Once approved, you receive a temporary license and complete a probationary year.
