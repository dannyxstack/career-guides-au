# Glass Maker, Cutter, Grinder and Finisher — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7315 (ISCO08)**

Glass makers, cutters, grinders and finishers in Sweden work with glass production, shaping, cutting, grinding, and finishing glass products ranging from art glass and windows to industrial and automotive glass. The occupation requires precision, manual dexterity, and knowledge of glass properties. Employment is concentrated in small specialized workshops and larger manufacturing plants, especially in regions with a history of glassmaking like Småland. The role is niche but essential for both artistic and functional glassware.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium (★★½☆☆).** National workforce approx. 473. The Swedish labour market for glass makers is stable but limited in size. Demand is driven by construction (insulated glass units), automotive (windshields), and artistic glass sectors. Automation is reducing need for manual cutting and grinding, but skilled craftspeople are still required for custom work. The outlook is moderately positive for those with expertise in art glass or specialized industrial processes. Geographic mobility can improve prospects, as production is concentrated in certain regions.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression typically starts with apprenticeship or vocational training, advancing to independent glass maker or specialist in techniques like glassblowing or engraving. Experienced workers may become supervisors, quality controllers, or entrepreneurs running their own studios. Further training in design or material science can lead to higher-value art glass or technical consulting roles. The path is traditional and experience-driven.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automated cutting and grinding: CNC machines and robots perform precise glass cutting and polishing, reducing manual work
- Quality inspection: AI vision systems can quickly detect defects such as bubbles and scratches, replacing manual inspection
- Dimensional measurement and calibration: laser scanners automatically complete workpiece measurement and parameter adjustment
- Batch polishing: robotic arms with grinding heads can perform standard polishing tasks continuously
- Material handling and sorting: Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and robotic arms handle and classify glass panels

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Customized design and processing: AI-aided design software (CAD/CAM) helps workers quickly create complex geometric patterns
- Equipment maintenance and programming: workers can acquire robot programming and preventive maintenance skills to improve production efficiency
- Process optimization: AI analyzes historical data to optimize parameters such as furnace temperature and cutting path, reducing scrap
- Virtual training: AR/VR simulation training systems allow novices to quickly master high-risk operations
- Collaborative work: Workers and cobots cooperate to handle and position irregular glass

**Human moat:**

- Art carving and manual finishing: unique patterns, engraving, etc. rely on creativity and manual dexterity, difficult to automate
- On-site installation and repair: architectural glass must adapt to site conditions, requiring flexible adjustments and judgment
- Non-standard parts handling: glass with irregular shapes or special sizes requires manual intervention and decision-making
- Quality anomaly handling: identification and manual correction of complex defects (e.g. internal stress).
- Customer communication and custom solutions: understand client needs and translate them into feasible process plans

**Skills to build:**

- CNC programming and operation (G-code)
- Robot maintenance and collaborative robot application basics
- AI vision system debugging and maintenance
- CAD/CAM software applications (e.g., SolidWorks, AutoCAD)
- Data analysis and process optimization (Python/R)
- Safety regulations and equipment operation certifications (e.g., ISO standards)

**Key growth areas:**

- art glass
- insulated glass
- automotive glass
- restoration glass

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a glass maker in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary by experience and sector, but are generally based on collective agreements. Entry-level positions may start around 25,000 SEK/month; experienced craftspeople can earn 30,000-35,000 SEK/month.

**Q: Can a non-EU glass maker get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, if you receive a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the salary threshold and other conditions. The occupation is not on the shortage list, but permits are still possible if the employer demonstrates recruitment efforts.
