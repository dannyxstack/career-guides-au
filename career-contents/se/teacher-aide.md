# Teachers’ Aide — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 5312 (ISCO08)**

Teachers’ aides support classroom teachers in Swedish compulsory and upper secondary schools, assisting with lesson preparation, student supervision, and individual learning support. They often work with students who have special needs or language difficulties, helping to create an inclusive educational environment. Employment is primarily in municipal schools, with growing demand in after-school programs and special education settings.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 24,590. Sweden faces a teacher shortage, increasing the need for teachers’ aides to assume support roles. Demographic trends and rising student numbers in areas with high immigration sustain demand. However, many positions are part-time or temporary, and competition can be strong in larger cities. The occupation is not typically on shortage lists, but local variations exist.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career progression often involves moving into specialized support roles, such as special education assistant or study counselor assistant. Some teachers’ aides pursue further education to become licensed teachers or preschool teachers. In-service training is common, and experience can lead to higher responsibility in after-school programs or administrative support within schools.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using AI to generate class notes, student attendance reports, and transcripts
- Automated scheduling and classroom resource allocation
- Answer common parent questions (e.g., school calendar, policies) via chatbot
- Automatically Organize Student Files and IEP (Individualized Education Program) Data
- Recording meeting minutes using voice-to-text tools

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to Analyze Student Academic Data to Precisely Identify Students Needing Extra Tutoring
- Assist non-English speaking students and parents using AI translation tools
- Generate personalized learning materials via AI (e.g., differentiated exercises)
- Using AI to simulate classroom management scenarios to enhance behavioral intervention strategies
- Using AI to optimize teacher workflows, freeing up more time for instructional support

**Human moat:**

- Empathy and real-time adaptability in handling student emotional and behavioral issues
- One-on-one physical assistance and emotional connection with students with special needs
- Interpersonal communication in unstructured situations (e.g., sudden conflicts, parent meetings)
- Coordination work requiring flexible judgment in cross-departmental collaboration
- Recognition and response to non-verbal signals in classroom management

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and data interpretation of AI education tools (e.g., Classcraft, Knewton)
- Basic data analysis (intermediate+ Excel/Google Sheets)
- Digital content creation (Canva/Adobe Express to produce learning materials)
- AI-assisted communication tools (e.g., ChatGPT generating parent letter templates)
- Classroom behavior management techniques (positive discipline, trauma-informed practices)
- Project management tools (Asana/Trello) for tracking student support plans

**Key growth areas:**

- Special Education
- After-School Programs
- Multilingual Support
- Inclusive Education

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a teachers’ aide in Sweden?**
A: Salaries typically range from 24,000 to 30,000 SEK per month, depending on experience, location, and employer. Municipal workers are covered by collective agreements.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen become a teachers’ aide in Sweden via a work permit?**
A: It is difficult because the occupation is not on the shortage list and salaries often fall below the work permit minimum threshold (around 27,360 SEK/month in 2025). Exceptions may exist for highly qualified candidates or if the job includes extra duties.

**Q: Is further education necessary to advance as a teachers’ aide?**
A: While not required, many aides study to become licensed teachers or specialize in special education to increase responsibilities and salary.
