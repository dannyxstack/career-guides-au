# Dietitian and Nutritionist — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2265 (ISCO08)**

In Sweden, dietitians and nutritionists work primarily in healthcare settings such as hospitals, primary care, and elderly care, where they provide medical nutrition therapy and dietary counseling. The profession is regulated, requiring a license from the National Board of Health and Welfare (Socialstyrelsen). With an aging population and increasing focus on preventive health, demand for qualified dietitians is growing, particularly in clinical roles and public health initiatives.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Career progression typically begins with clinical work in hospitals or primary care, with opportunities to specialize in areas such as pediatrics, oncology, or renal nutrition. Experienced dietitians may move into management roles, research, teaching, or open private practices. Continuous professional development is encouraged, and a master's degree can open doors to advanced clinical or academic positions.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard Meal Formula Generation and Nutrient Calculation
- Automated dietary record analysis (generate reports from diaries)
- Preliminary dietary advice for common chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes)
- Online nutrition education and general dietary advice
- Database query and literature review assistance

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted clinical diagnosis (e.g., identifying malnutrition risk)
- Generate personalized nutrition plans (combined with genetic and microbiome data)
- Real-time nutrition monitoring and feedback via wearable devices
- Patient data management (automatic sorting of medical records and dietary history)
- Automation of online booking and follow-ups

**Human moat:**

- Complex nutritional interventions for multiple comorbidities (e.g., kidney disease with diabetes)
- Enteral/parenteral nutrition formulation and critical care management
- Behaviour change counselling (psychological support, overcoming eating habits)
- Medical team coordination (communicating with doctors, nurses, and pharmacists)
- Ethical judgment and patient privacy protection

**Skills to build:**

- Clinical nutrition assessment and disease management (e.g., ICU nutritional support)
- Hands-on AI tools (e.g., IBM Watson Nutrition Advisor)
- Health data analysis (basic Python/R, electronic medical record systems)
- Telemedicine communication and patient education
- Culturally sensitive nutrition counselling (diverse clients)
- Research and evidence-based practice updates

**Key growth areas:**

- Clinical Dietetics
- Public Health Nutrition
- Food Industry
- Sports Nutrition

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a dietitian in Sweden?**
A: The average monthly salary for a dietitian in Sweden is around 32,000–35,000 SEK (pre-tax). Salaries vary by experience, region, and sector; higher for specialists or those in private practice.

**Q: Can a non-EU dietitian work in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but a work permit is required. You need a job offer that meets the salary threshold (about 35,000 SEK/month in 2024) and you must obtain a license from Socialstyrelsen. The employer must advertise the job in Sweden/EU first.

**Q: Do I need to be licensed in Sweden?**
A: Yes, to work clinically as a dietitian you must have a license (legitimation) from the National Board of Health and Welfare. This requires a recognized degree and supervised internship.
