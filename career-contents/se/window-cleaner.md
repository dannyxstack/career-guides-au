# Window Cleaner — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 9123 (ISCO08)**

Window cleaners in Sweden perform professional cleaning of windows, glass facades, and other glass surfaces in residential, commercial, and public buildings. The occupation is part of the broader cleaning services sector, with workers often employed by specialized cleaning companies or working as self-employed contractors. Demand is steady, driven by property maintenance standards and seasonal peaks in spring and autumn. The work can be physically demanding and may involve working at heights, requiring good physical fitness and attention to safety. While formal education is not required, safety training for working at heights is highly recommended.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: very low (½☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for window cleaners often involves moving into supervisory roles, starting one's own cleaning business, or specializing in high-rise or industrial cleaning. Additional certifications in working at heights or using specialized equipment (e.g., water-fed poles) can enhance prospects. Some may transition to other facility maintenance roles. Membership in trade associations and obtaining a professional qualification can support career advancement and credibility.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a window cleaner in Sweden?**
A: Salaries generally range from 25,000 to 30,000 SEK per month before tax, depending on experience and region. Starting salaries may be lower.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen obtain a work permit as a window cleaner in Sweden?**
A: It is very unlikely. Window cleaning is not considered skilled work, and work permits for non-EU citizens are generally reserved for shortage occupations or highly skilled positions. EU/EEA citizens have free movement.

**Q: What qualifications do I need to become a window cleaner in Sweden?**
A: No formal education is required, but employers often prefer candidates with safety training for working at heights. A driving license (B) is advantageous for mobile jobs. Voluntary professional certification is available through the industry association.
