# Protective Services Worker — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 5419 (ISCO08)**

Protective services workers not elsewhere classified include security guards, patrolmen, and surveillance officers who protect property, people, and information. In Sweden, they are employed by private security firms, airports, ports, shopping centers, and government agencies. The role often requires a license (väktarkort) and involves monitoring alarms, controlling access, and responding to incidents. The sector is regulated by the Swedish Police Authority, and demand is steady due to ongoing needs in retail, events, and critical infrastructure.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 2,590. The employment outlook for protective services workers in Sweden is stable. Demand is driven by increased security needs in public spaces, airports, and large events. However, automation and AI surveillance may reduce some entry-level jobs. The Swedish Public Employment Service notes moderate recruitment difficulties in certain regions, especially for experienced guards. Overall, the occupation is not expected to grow significantly but will maintain a baseline demand due to turnover and retirements.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

Career progression in Sweden typically begins as a security guard (väktare) with basic training. After experience, workers can specialize in areas like airport security, cash-in-transit, or personal protection. Advanced roles include supervisory positions or security managers, which may require further education in security management or risk analysis. Some workers transition to related fields like law enforcement or emergency services. Continuous professional development is available through industry courses.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- AI video surveillance automatically detects abnormal events and alerts
- Automated visitor registration and identity verification systems replace manual registration
- AI-driven patrol robots replacing fixed-route patrols

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI video analysis assisting real-time threat assessment and decision-making
- AI report generation tools quickly summarize event logs
- AI voice assistant supports emergency response communication and coordination
- AI training simulators improve exercise effectiveness

**Human moat:**

- On-site emergency judgment and crisis intervention ability
- Interpersonal communication and persuasion with clients, visitors, and law enforcement
- Legal compliance and licensing liability (e.g., use-of-force boundaries)
- Risk assessment intuition in complex physical environments

**Skills to build:**

- AI monitoring system operation and data analysis
- Basic cybersecurity knowledge (integration of physical security and IT)
- Emergency communication and coordination software (e.g., Duress system).
- Advanced training in conflict resolution and counter-terrorism awareness.
- Data privacy regulations and compliance (Australian Privacy Act)
- Digital reporting and visualization tools (e.g., Tableau)

**Key growth areas:**

- security technology

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Alert and detail-oriented individuals who can monitor surroundings for extended periods.
- Physically fit people comfortable with standing, walking, and responding to incidents.
- Those who can work night shifts, weekends, and irregular hours without issue.

**Who is not suited**
- People easily distracted or who dislike routine and monotony.
- Individuals uncomfortable with confrontation or working alone in isolated settings.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a protective services worker in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level security guards earn around 24,000-26,000 SEK per month before tax. With experience and specializations (e.g., airport security), salaries can reach 30,000 SEK or more. Pay is typically governed by collective agreements.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens work as a security guard in Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it is challenging. They need a work permit with a concrete job offer meeting collective agreement wages, and the employer must demonstrate that the position could not be filled locally. Obtaining a väktarkort also requires a clean criminal record and passing a background check.
