# Tailor — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7531 (ISCO08)**

Tailors, dressmakers, furriers and hatters in Sweden are skilled artisans who create, alter and repair garments and headwear, often working in small ateliers or as freelancers. The occupation is small but valued for custom-made clothing, theatrical costumes and textile restoration. Demand is niche, driven by local craftsmanship trends and a growing interest in sustainable fashion. Many tailors are self-employed or work in specialized shops. The sector faces competition from ready-to-wear and fast fashion, but expert tailoring remains sought after for bespoke suits, formalwear and traditional Swedish folk costumes ('folkdräkter').

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).** National workforce approx. 638. Employment outlook for tailors in Sweden is stable but limited. The occupation is not on any shortage list, and job openings are few. However, an aging workforce and a rising appreciation for quality craftsmanship may create replacement demand. Digitalization and mass production reduce need for basic alteration skills, but high-end tailoring and repair services for luxury or historical garments offer opportunities. The market is concentrated in major cities like Stockholm, Gothenburg and Malmö. Self-employment and niche specialisation (e.g., bridalwear, leather work) are common paths.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression for tailors in Sweden often begins with a vocational education followed by apprenticeship. Many move from employee to self-employed or open their own atelier. Experienced tailors may become master craftsmen (mäster) or specialize in haute couture, costume design for theatre/film, or textile conservation. Continuous learning in pattern making, draping and digital tools (CAD) enhances prospects. Some transition into fashion design or teaching at vocational schools. Union membership (e.g., Unionen or GS) is common for employees.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Uses AI clothing design software to automatically generate basic patterns
- Replace traditional tailoring with 3D body scanning and virtual fitting
- Automated sewing robot completes standard sewing tasks
- AI fabric cutting optimization algorithm reduces manual calculations
- Big data-based trend prediction replacing manual research

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use AI design tools to quickly generate personalized patterns and styles
- Use 3D modeling software for virtual sample display and client communication
- AI fabric identifier to assist material selection and inventory management
- Semi-automated precision sewing using intelligent sewing machines.
- Optimizing pricing and marketing strategies through data analysis

**Human moat:**

- Creative design and impromptu modification skills in handmade haute couture
- Fine handling and restoration of complex fabrics (e.g., leather, fur)
- Build trust with clients and understand personalized needs
- Artistry of traditional handicraft techniques (e.g., embroidery, beadwork)
- Deep understanding and ability to reconstruct clothing history and culture.

**Skills to build:**

- 3D garment modeling and rendering software (e.g., CLO 3D)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g., Adobe Firefly)
- Digital pattern-making and cutting software
- Fabric knowledge and supply chain management
- Customer relationship management and experience design
- Knowledge of sustainable fashion and eco-friendly materials

**Key growth areas:**

- Bespoke tailoring
- Sustainable fashion
- Costume design
- Textile restoration

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a tailor in Sweden?**
A: Entry-level tailors earn around 25,000–30,000 SEK per month. Experienced tailors or master craftsmen can earn 35,000–45,000 SEK or more, especially in high-end bespoke or costume work. Self-employed tailors' income varies greatly depending on client base.

**Q: Can a non-EU tailor get a work permit for Sweden?**
A: Yes, but it requires a job offer from a Swedish employer with wages meeting collective agreements (minimum ~28,000 SEK/month). Since tailors are not on the shortage list, the employer must advertise the job in Sweden/EU first. The permit is tied to that employer for the first two years.

**Q: Is tailoring considered a skilled migration route to Sweden?**
A: No, tailoring is not on any shortage or positive list. While work permits are possible, demand is low and competition from local/EU tailors is high. It is not a recommended pathway for migration.
