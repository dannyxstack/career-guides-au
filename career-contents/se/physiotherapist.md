# Physiotherapist — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 2264 (ISCO08)**

Physiotherapists in Sweden assess, diagnose, and treat movement dysfunction and pain, promoting health and functional ability. They work in hospitals, primary care, sports medicine, and private clinics. The profession is regulated by the National Board of Health and Welfare (Socialstyrelsen), requiring a license (legitimation) to practice. With an aging population and increased focus on physical activity and rehabilitation, demand is high. The Swedish labour market offers stable employment, with most positions in the public sector under municipal or regional healthcare. EU/EEA nationals benefit from free movement, while non-EU/EEA workers need a work permit and license recognition.

Career progression in physiotherapy in Sweden often involves specialization in areas such as orthopaedics, neurology, or paediatrics, typically through postgraduate courses or a Master's degree. Experienced physiotherapists may move into managerial roles, clinical leadership, or private practice. Research and teaching positions are available in universities. ## 8. FAQ

**Q: Do I need a Swedish license to work as a physiotherapist?**
A: Yes, you must obtain a license (legitimation) from Socialstyrelsen. For EU/EEA-trained physiotherapists, recognition is straightforward under the professional qualifications directive. Non-EU/EEA applicants face a longer process including language tests and possible bridging courses.

**Q: What is the typical salary for a physiotherapist in Sweden?**
A: Salaries are determined by collective agreements and vary with experience, sector, and region. Starting salaries for newly licensed physiotherapists are around 27,000–30,000 SEK per month in the public sector, with increases over time. Private practice may offer higher earnings.

**Q: Is it easy to find a job as a physiotherapist in Sweden?**
A: Yes, demand is high due to an aging population and increased focus on rehabilitation. There are shortages in many regions, especially in rural areas. However, fluency in Swedish is essential for most clinical positions.
