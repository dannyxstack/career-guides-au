# Craft and Related Workers Not Elsewhere Classified — career analysis · Sweden

**Occupation code: 7549 (ISCO08)**

This catch-all occupation in Sweden includes a diverse range of skilled craft workers such as musical instrument makers, glassblowers, woodworkers, and textile artisans. Employment is typically found in small workshops, cultural heritage sectors, and niche manufacturing. The role requires manual dexterity, creativity, and often specialized training. The Swedish labour market values craftsmanship, particularly in restoration and custom production, but the sector is small and highly specialized.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 68,731. Employment outlook for craft workers is stable but limited. Automation and industrial production reduce demand for mass-market crafts, while niche areas like restoration, artisanal food, and sustainable design see moderate growth. Job openings arise mainly from retirements and replacement needs. Geographic concentration in urban and tourist regions offers more opportunities. Overall, the occupation is not expected to expand significantly.

**Competition: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Career progression often starts with an apprenticeship or vocational training, leading to journeyman status. Experienced workers may become master craftsmen, workshop managers, or self-employed artisans. Continuing education in new techniques or materials can enhance prospects. Some transition into teaching or conservation roles. The path is highly individual and depends on the specific craft.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standardised jewellery grinding and polishing
- Basic assembly and tuning of musical instruments
- Repeating pattern reproduction in hand carving
- Traditional mold making and casting processes
- Simple wood/stone cutting and shaping

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Conceive personalized jewelry/musical instrument designs using AI design software
- Accelerated prototyping through 3D scanning and modeling
- Using AI quality inspection system to improve product precision
- Using AR/VR for complex assembly guidance
- Customized client communication and quoting via digital platforms

**Human moat:**

- Highly customized artistic creation and aesthetic judgment
- Experience and tactile feel of handling rare materials
- On-site repair and adaptation of complex non-standard parts
- Cultural story interpretation through in-depth communication with clients
- Overall process control through multi-process integration

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM and 3D modeling (e.g., Rhino, Fusion 360)
- Digital fabrication equipment operator (3D printing, CNC engraving)
- AI-assisted design tools (e.g. Midjourney, DALL-E for concept design)
- Materials science and sustainable process knowledge
- Digital customer management and custom platform operations
- Cross-sector collaboration and project management skills

**Key growth areas:**

- restoration
- custom furniture
- artisan food production
- traditional crafts

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for this occupation in Sweden?**
A: Salaries vary widely by craft, experience, and region. Most are covered by collective bargaining agreements, with entry-level around 23,000–27,000 SEK per month and experienced masters earning up to 35,000 SEK or more.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens get a work permit for this occupation?**
A: Yes, if you have a job offer from a Swedish employer that meets the collective agreement salary and terms. A labor market test may be required since the occupation is not on the shortage list.

**Q: Do I need formal qualifications to work as a craft worker in Sweden?**
A: Not strictly mandatory, but vocational training or apprenticeship is highly valued. Many employers prefer certified craftspeople for quality assurance.
