# Container Filler — career analysis · Australia

**Occupation code: 832112 (ANZSCO)**

Container fillers are responsible for safely and efficiently loading goods into containers, ensuring cargo is secured and weight is distributed properly for transport.

> **This is a non-skilled-migration occupation.** It is generally not on the skilled occupation lists and cannot be used for independent skilled migration.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

Entry-level workers can gain experience and advance to loading supervisor or warehouse team leader, or further learn forklift operation or logistics management, progressing to transport coordinator or supply chain roles.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using forklifts to load goods into containers in a preset pattern; now can be done by automated guided vehicles and robotic arms.
- Manual calculation and adjustment of cargo weight distribution for optimal center of gravity is now optimized by smart stowage software.
- Manual inspection of cargo securing status and safety; alternative is real-time monitoring via sensors and vision systems.
- Packing lists and labels are automatically generated by the system integrated with the enterprise resource planning system.
- Move goods within a dock or warehouse to the packing area; automated handling systems are widely deployed.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Operate automated systems: workers can monitor and dispatch multiple automated guided vehicles via tablet or voice interface to improve efficiency.
- Exception handling and quality inspection: workers intervene and repair when sensors detect damaged or abnormally secured goods.
- Data analysis and optimization: using AI to analyze historical loading data and propose more efficient stowage plans.
- Remote operation and multi-device collaboration: manage a dozen automated devices from a remote console, one person operating multiple machines.
- Precision handling: For fragile or irregularly shaped goods, perform manual fine operations with AR guidance.

**Human moat:**

- Flexibility and judgment when handling non-standard goods (e.g., live animals, dangerous goods, irregular oversized items).
- Quickly respond to emergencies in complex on-site environments (e.g., crowded container terminals, night operations).
- Ability to communicate and coordinate in real time with multiple parties such as port dispatchers, truck drivers, and customs.
- Execute manual emergency plans when automated systems fail to ensure operational continuity.
- Design custom packaging and securing solutions for special goods such as precision instruments and artwork.

**Skills to build:**

- Basic programming and automated system operation (e.g., PLC, SCADA interfaces).
- Data analysis and visualization (using Excel, Power BI to analyze packing efficiency).
- Basics of forklift and automated equipment maintenance.
- Principles of logistics and supply chain management.
- Safety and compliance knowledge (e.g., dangerous goods loading regulations).
- Foreign languages and communication coordination (multilingual port environment).

**Key growth areas:**

- Logistics Growth
- Port Activity
- Trade Volume
- Automation Impact

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for a container filler?**
A: Entry-level annual salary approx. A$45,000–55,000, mid-level A$55,000–65,000, senior supervisor approx. A$65,000–75,000. Overtime and night shifts can increase earnings.

**Q: Is there a skilled migration pathway for container fillers?**
A: Almost no skilled migration pathway. This occupation is not on the skilled occupation list, generally unable to apply for visas 189, 190, 482, etc. Unless switching to warehousing supervisor or higher-level role.

**Q: Will this occupation be replaced by automation?**
A: Moderate risk. Automated container handling and robotics are developing, but many complex operations still require humans; some positions may decrease in the next 10 years.
