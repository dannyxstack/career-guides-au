# Vineyard Worker — career analysis · Australia

**Occupation code: 842222 (ANZSCO)**

Vineyard workers are responsible for planting, pruning, irrigation, pest control and grape harvesting, forming an important grassroots workforce in the Australian wine industry.

> **This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: high (★★★★☆).**

Vineyard workers can advance to team leader or farm manager, or specialize in wine grape cultivation techniques. Through experience and training (such as TAFE courses), they can also transition to winemaking or winery operations.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually recording temperature, rainfall, and pest data
- Routine grapevine pruning pattern recognition and decision-making
- Calculation and adjustment of irrigation schedules
- Yield prediction and harvest time estimation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI vision systems to quickly identify pests, diseases, and nutrient deficiency symptoms
- Optimize irrigation and fertilization plans using sensors and algorithms
- Use drones or robots for large vineyard inspection
- Predict yield, disease risks, and optimal harvest time based on historical data
- Automated recording and generation of compliance reports

**Human moat:**

- Intuition and hands-on experience with soil and plant physiology
- Handle complex environmental variables (e.g., microclimate, soil heterogeneity)
- Fine manual tasks such as pruning and harvesting
- On-site communication and coordination with farm teams and suppliers
- Response to emergencies (e.g., equipment failure, extreme weather)

**Skills to build:**

- Operation and maintenance of agricultural IoT devices
- Basic data analysis (Excel, Python, or specialized agricultural software)
- Drone operation and image analysis
- Sustainable agriculture management practices
- Basic mechanical maintenance and troubleshooting of automation systems
- Digital record keeping and compliance report generation

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal Workforce
- Wine Industry
- Automation
- Irrigation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

**This is a restricted skilled-migration occupation (employer-sponsored / regional agreements only).** It is not on the independent skilled lists (e.g. 189/190).

| Visa | Description |
|---|---|
| Subclass 482 (Temporary Skill Shortage Visa) | Requires employer sponsorship, occupation must be on the short-term list (e.g., agricultural technician); vineyard workers as entry-level roles typically do not qualify. |

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average hourly wage for vineyard workers?**
A: Typically AUD 23-28, depending on experience, region, and whether holding specific licenses. Seasonal temporary workers may earn higher.

**Q: Can vineyard workers stay in Australia through skilled migration?**
A: Unlikely, as this occupation is usually not on the core skills list. A few remote DAMA programs may cover it, but require employer sponsorship and typically a temporary visa.

**Q: Are there advancement opportunities for vineyard workers?**
A: Yes, can advance to team leader, farm manager, or wine grape cultivation specialist. Completing TAFE courses (e.g., Certificate III in Agriculture) boosts competitiveness.
