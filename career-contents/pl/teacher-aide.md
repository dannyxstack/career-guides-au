**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using AI to generate class notes, student attendance reports, and transcripts
- Automated scheduling and classroom resource allocation
- Answer common parent questions (e.g., school calendar, policies) via chatbot
- Automatically Organize Student Files and IEP (Individualized Education Program) Data

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using AI to Analyze Student Academic Data to Precisely Identify Students Needing Extra Tutoring
- Assist non-English speaking students and parents using AI translation tools
- Generate personalized learning materials via AI (e.g., differentiated exercises)
- Using AI to simulate classroom management scenarios to enhance behavioral intervention strategies
- Using AI to optimize teacher workflows, freeing up more time for instructional support

**Human moat:**

- Empathy and real-time adaptability in handling student emotional and behavioral issues
- One-on-one physical assistance and emotional connection with students with special needs
- Interpersonal communication in unstructured situations (e.g., sudden conflicts, parent meetings)
- Coordination work requiring flexible judgment in cross-departmental collaboration
- Recognition and response to non-verbal signals in classroom management

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a teachers’ aide in Poland?**
A: Teachers’ aides in Poland earn between 3,000 and 4,500 PLN gross per month, depending on experience, school type, and region. Public schools generally offer lower pay, while private or international schools may pay higher. Additional benefits like bonuses or 13th salary may apply in some institutions.

**Q: Can a non-EU citizen work as a teachers’ aide in Poland via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, non-EU citizens can work as teachers’ aides in Poland through a standard work permit (type A), sponsored by an employer. The EU Blue Card route is less common for this role, as it requires a higher salary threshold typically not met by teachers’ aides. The employer must prove no suitable local candidate is available.

**Q: What qualifications are required to become a teachers’ aide in Poland?**
A: Typically, you need at least a secondary education diploma. Many employers prefer or require a pedagogical preparation course (e.g., 150-hour course) or related experience. Certification in first aid and a clean criminal record are also often mandatory. Schools may provide on-the-job training.
