## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★½☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manually record farming data (water temperature, feeding amounts, etc.)
- Generate daily production reports and compliance documents
- Image recognition of initial disease symptoms
- Inventory counting and feed order calculation

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Uses sensors and AI to predict optimal feeding times and amounts
- Monitor fish health and abnormal behavior through machine vision.
- Simulate the impact of different stocking densities and feed plans on yield
- Real-time water quality monitoring and automatic adjustment of oxygenation and filtration equipment

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making for responding to sudden disease outbreaks or environmental disasters
- Experience-based biosecurity management and overall disease prevention planning.
- Negotiations and relationship management with regulators, suppliers, and sales channels
- Assess and implement new sustainable aquaculture technologies

**Skills to build:**

- Data analysis and statistical software (Python/R)
- Internet of Things (IoT) and sensor system management
- Operation and troubleshooting of automated control systems
- Knowledge of environmental regulations and sustainability certification standards
- Inspection skills for drones or underwater robots

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for aquaculture and fisheries production managers in Poland?**
A: Salaries for aquaculture and fisheries production managers in Poland typically range from 7,000 to 14,000 PLN gross per month, depending on experience, company size, and location. Senior managers or those in large-scale operations may earn higher, while entry-level positions start at the lower end. Benefits such as bonuses or housing may also be included.

**Q: What are the official visa routes for non-EU nationals to work as an aquaculture and fisheries production manager in Poland?**
A: Non-EU nationals can work via an EU Blue Card if they hold a relevant higher education degree and a job offer with a gross annual salary exceeding the threshold (currently around PLN 120,000). Alternatively, a standard Type A work permit is available for qualified professionals, regardless of salary level, provided the employer demonstrates labour market need.

**Q: What qualifications are needed to become an aquaculture and fisheries production manager in Poland?**
A: Typically, a bachelor’s or master’s degree in aquaculture, fisheries, marine biology, or a related field is required. Several years of practical experience in fish farming or hatchery management are essential. Knowledge of Polish regulations, business management skills, and often proficiency in Polish or English are also important for this role.
