- Ore transport and loading (autonomous trucks and conveyor belt systems).
- Mine site environmental monitoring and safety inspection (drones and sensor networks)
- Basic ore crushing and sorting (AI vision system automatic classification)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Drilling path planning and blasting scheme optimization (assisted by AI geological modeling)
- Decision support under complex geological conditions (real-time data analysis)
- Equipment fault diagnosis and remote maintenance guidance (AR collaboration)
- Safety training and accident simulation (VR immersive training)

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for miners and quarriers in Poland?**
A: The average monthly gross salary for miners in Poland is around 8,000–12,000 PLN, with underground miners earning higher due to hazardous conditions. Quarriers typically earn 5,000–7,000 PLN. Salaries vary by region, with Silesian coal mines offering higher pay, and depend on experience, company, and specific role.

**Q: What work visa options are available for foreign miners and quarriers to work in Poland?**
A: Foreign workers can apply for a national work visa (type D) based on a work permit (type A, employer-specific). Highly qualified workers may qualify for the EU Blue Card if they meet salary and education requirements. No specific quotas exist for this occupation. All applications require a valid job offer from a Polish employer and approval by the voivodeship office.

**Q: What are the main employment sectors for miners and quarriers in Poland?**
A: The primary sector is hard coal mining in Upper Silesia, with major companies like PGG and JSW. Lignite mining operates in central and southwest regions. Quarrying of limestone, granite, and sand/gravel occurs nationwide, supplying construction materials. Employment is also found in copper and zinc mining (e.g., KGHM). Industry restructuring is reducing coal jobs, while quarrying remains stable.
