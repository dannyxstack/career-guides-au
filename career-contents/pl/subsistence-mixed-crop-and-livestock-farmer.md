## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Poland?**
A: Income varies widely but is generally low, often below the national minimum wage (currently about 3,490 PLN gross per month in 2024). Many farmers rely on self-consumption and direct sales. According to GUS, average farm income for small mixed farms can be around 2,000–4,000 PLN net monthly, but subsistence farmers may earn less.

**Q: Can non-EU citizens obtain a work visa to become subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Poland?**
A: The EU Blue Card is not available as it requires a higher education degree and a high salary. Non-EU citizens may apply for a national (D) work visa if an employer offers a job and passes a labour market test. However, subsistence farming typically does not generate enough income to meet visa requirements, and such roles are rarely offered to foreigners. Seasonal work permits are possible for temporary employment.

**Q: What are the main duties of a subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmer in Poland?**
A: Subsistence mixed crop and livestock farmers in Poland manage small plots to grow crops (e.g., grains, potatoes, vegetables) and raise animals (e.g., cows, pigs, chickens) primarily for their own household needs. Tasks include planting, harvesting, feeding livestock, milking, and maintaining equipment. Surplus produce may be sold at local markets. The work is physically demanding and follows seasonal cycles.
