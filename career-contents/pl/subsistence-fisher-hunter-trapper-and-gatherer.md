- Identifying edible plants and animal tracks (computer vision replacing some identification tasks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Optimizes fishing and hunting time windows by analyzing historical climate data through AI.
- Use drones or remote sensing images to quickly locate fish schools or animal migration paths.
- AI assists in predicting resource distribution, reducing ineffective exploration time
- Use a mobile app to record and analyze capture efficiency, continuously optimizing strategies

**Human moat:**

- On-site decision-making and immediate adaptability in harsh environments
- Deep experiential knowledge of local ecosystems and animal behavior
- Physical and mental resilience for high-risk outdoor work

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers in Poland?**
A: Income is highly variable and generally low due to the subsistence nature of the work. Most practitioners rely on self-consumption and small local sales. Official statistics are scarce, but earnings rarely exceed the national minimum wage. Many engage in this work as a supplement to other income sources, often in rural or forested areas.

**Q: Can foreign nationals obtain a work visa or EU Blue Card for subsistence fishing, hunting, trapping, or gathering in Poland?**
A: These occupations are typically self-employed or informal, making them ineligible for standard work visas requiring employer sponsorship. The EU Blue Card targets highly skilled workers, which does not apply here. Poland offers no specific visa category for subsistence activities. Non-EU nationals interested in this work would need alternative grounds, such as family reunification or permanent residence.

**Q: What are the prospects for subsistence fishers, hunters, trappers, and gatherers as a career in Poland?**
A: This is a traditional livelihood with limited prospects for growth or formal employment. It is often practiced in remote areas like the Białowieża Forest or Masurian lakes. Modern regulations, declining wildlife populations, and competition from commercial fishing/hunting reduce opportunities. It may be pursued part-time or for cultural preservation, but is not recommended as a primary career for long-term stability.
