## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence livestock farmers in Poland?**
A: Subsistence livestock farmers in Poland primarily produce for their own consumption, so they do not receive a regular salary. Their income derives from occasional sale of surplus livestock or products, often supplemented by other jobs or EU agricultural subsidies. Average earnings vary widely, but such farmers typically earn below the national average wage, with many relying on mixed income sources.

**Q: What visa routes are available for non-EU citizens to work as subsistence livestock farmers in Poland?**
A: The EU Blue Card is not applicable for subsistence livestock farming as it requires highly skilled work with a university degree and high salary. Non-EU citizens may apply for a seasonal work permit (type S) for up to 9 months per year, or a temporary work permit (type A) if employed by a Polish farm. Self-employment as a farmer requires a business visa or residence permit for economic activity, subject to meeting legal requirements.

**Q: What skills are important for subsistence livestock farmers in Poland?**
A: Key skills include animal husbandry, veterinary knowledge, pasture management, and basic farm maintenance. Practical experience with livestock (e.g., cattle, pigs, poultry) is essential. Familiarity with Polish agricultural regulations and EU subsidy programs is beneficial. Given the subsistence nature, self-sufficiency in feeding and breeding animals is crucial.
