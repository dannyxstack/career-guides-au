## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Standard conference interpreting (e.g., business negotiations)
- Simple telephone interpreting or community interpreting
- Basic subtitling/transcription proofreading
- Standardized oral exam scoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time captioning assisting interpretation (AI providing background information)
- Term base management (AI automatically extracts terms)
- AI speech-to-text note-taking assistance during simultaneous interpretation
- Remote interpreting platforms (AI optimizes audio/video).
- Quality self-check (AI playback comparison against standard translation)

**Human moat:**

- Cultural context understanding and emotional conveyance
- Rapid decision-making under high pressure
- Confidentiality and ethical judgment
- Interpretation of Non-Verbal Signals (Tone, Body Language)
- Transcreation and rhetorical refinement

**Skills to build:**

- Specialized domain knowledge (legal, medical, financial)
- AI-assisted translation tools (e.g., Otter.ai, SDL Trados)
- Remote interpreting platform operations
- Terminology management and data annotation
- On-site equipment commissioning and troubleshooting
- Advanced cross-cultural communication skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for translators and interpreters in Poland?**
A: Salaries vary widely based on language pair, specialization, and experience. On average, full-time in-house translators earn around 4,000-7,000 PLN gross per month. Freelance rates range from 30-80 PLN per standard page. Specialized fields like legal or medical translation command higher rates. Conference interpreters can earn 500-1500 PLN per day.

**Q: What are the official routes for non-EU translators to work in Poland?**
A: Non-EU citizens can work in Poland via a national visa (type D) for employment or an EU Blue Card for highly qualified workers. For translators, a valid job offer is required. The Blue Card demands a higher education degree (or 5 years' experience) and a salary at least 150% of the average national wage. The employer must obtain a work permit first.

**Q: What is the job market like for translators and interpreters in Poland?**
A: Poland has a solid demand for translators and interpreters, especially in English-Polish pairs. The market favors specialists in legal, medical, technical, and IT fields. Conference interpreting is competitive, requiring accreditation. Many work as freelancers or for agencies. Knowledge of German, French, or Russian is a plus. The rise of machine translation has impacted lower-skilled roles but increased demand for post-editing.
