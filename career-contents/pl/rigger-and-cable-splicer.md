**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Image recognition-based automatic detection of cable wear
- Use drones for overhead cable inspection

- Installation path planning using digital twin simulation
- Automated report generation and compliance document filling.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AR headset displays complex rigging connection steps and safety annotations
- AI real-time analysis of tension data and early warning of potential breakage risks
- Smart wearable devices monitor worker posture and fatigue to prevent occupational injuries
- machine learning-based defect identification and repair guidance for cable joints

**Human moat:**

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for riggers and cable splicers in Poland?**
A: In Poland, riggers and cable splicers typically earn between 4,500 and 7,500 PLN gross per month, depending on experience, certifications, and location. Senior specialists or those working on large infrastructure projects may earn up to 10,000 PLN. Overtime and hazard pay are common.

**Q: What are the official work visa routes for riggers and cable splicers to work in Poland?**
A: Non-EU riggers can work in Poland via a work permit (Type A) or the EU Blue Card. The work permit requires a job offer and labour market test. The Blue Card requires a higher education degree (or equivalent experience) and a salary at least 150% of the average national wage. Both routes lead to temporary residence.

**Q: What are typical career prospects for riggers and cable splicers in Poland?**
A: Riggers and cable splicers in Poland find employment in construction, telecommunications, energy, and maritime sectors. Career advancement includes becoming a team leader, supervisor, or specialist in high-voltage or offshore rigging. Certification from the Office of Technical Inspection (UDT) boosts prospects. Demand is steady due to infrastructure projects.
