## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★★☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Parking meter collection (replaced by mobile payments and automatic billing systems)
- Street road cleaning (replaced by autonomous cleaning robots)
- Simple customer inquiries (replaced by AI voice assistants and self-service kiosks)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile payment data analysis to analyze foot traffic and sales timing.
- Post real-time service locations and promotional information through social platforms
- Using voice translation tools to serve multilingual tourists

**Human moat:**

- On-site incident handling and de-escalation in emergencies (e.g., traffic accidents, disputes)
- Ability to build trust and emotional connection with regular customers
- In-depth understanding and flexible application of local regulations and community norms

**Skills to build:**

- Basic digital literacy (using mobile payment terminals, managing online reviews)
- Basic English or foreign language communication
- Customer service and conflict resolution skills
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (e.g., trolleys, cleaning equipment)

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for street and related services workers in Poland?**
A: Salaries vary widely depending on location, hours, and type of goods or services sold. Most earn minimum wage or slightly above, which as of 2023 is about 3,490 PLN gross per month. Tips or commissions may supplement income but are not guaranteed. Wage theft is illegal; workers should have a written contract.

**Q: Can non-EU nationals work as street services workers in Poland via a work visa or EU Blue Card?**
A: Yes, but the EU Blue Card is unlikely as it requires a highly skilled job with at least 1.5x average wage. For lower-skilled street work, a national work visa (type D) is possible if an employer obtains a work permit. The process involves proving no local candidate is available. Self-employment is another route but requires a separate visa.

**Q: What are the main duties and working conditions for street services workers in Poland?**
A: Duties include selling goods (e.g., food, newspapers, souvenirs) or offering services (e.g., shoe shining, street performances) in public spaces. Work is often seasonal, outdoors, and involves irregular hours. Workers need a permit from the local municipality to trade. Health and safety regulations apply, and workers must be registered for tax and social insurance.
