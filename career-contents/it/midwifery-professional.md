# Midwifery Professionals — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 2222 (ISCO08)**

Midwives provide prenatal, intrapartum, and postpartum care, assist with natural childbirth, and take necessary medical measures in emergencies. This profession is a regulated healthcare profession in Italy, with work environments including hospitals, clinics, and community health centers.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 25,000. Italy faces a midwife shortage, especially in the south and remote areas. With declining birth rates and an aging population, demand for community midwifery services is increasing. The government encourages young people to enter the field, and employment prospects are stable.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: medium-high (★★★½☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Midwives can advance from entry-level to senior midwife, midwifery manager, or clinical educator. They can also pursue further training to become obstetric nurses or midwifery consultants. Some midwives choose to open private practices or engage in academic research.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic entry and organization of routine prenatal electronic records
- Guideline-based routine pregnancy risk assessment report generation
- Automatic Analysis of Birth Monitoring Data and Anomaly Alerts
- AI voice automated outbound calls and basic screening for postpartum follow-up
- Automatic Filling and Coding of Standardized Descriptions in Medical Records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI assisted fetal monitoring interpretation to improve anomaly recognition accuracy
- AI-assisted measurement and preliminary diagnosis of prenatal ultrasound images
- AI recommendation for personalized birth plans based on historical data
- AI-driven continuous obstetric education simulation training and skill assessment
- Real-time AI translation and information integration in telemedicine consultations

**Human moat:**

- Manual assistance maneuvers and emergency judgment in complex deliveries
- Emotional communication, psychological comfort, and education with mothers and families
- Clinical Leadership and Decision-Making in Cross-Disciplinary Team Collaboration
- Rapid clinical reasoning in unpredictable obstetric emergencies
- Sensitive understanding of maternal cultural background and personal preferences

**Skills to build:**

- Operation of AI-assisted diagnostic tools in obstetrics (e.g., smart monitors)
- Clinical data analysis and information system management
- Telehealth consultation and digital health education skills
- Interventional prenatal examination techniques (e.g., ultrasound-guided)
- Cross-cultural communication and psychological support skills
- Simulation training for maternal and infant emergencies and team collaboration

**Key growth areas:**

- aging population
- rural healthcare demand
- midwifery-led care
- community health expansion

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level for midwives in Italy?**
A: Annual salary for junior midwives is about 22,000-28,000 euros, while senior midwives can earn up to 50,000 euros. Salaries are stable in public hospitals, and may be higher in private ones.

**Q: How can foreign midwives immigrate to Italy?**
A: Can apply via EU Blue Card or Decreto Flussi work visa; requires Italian B2 level, degree authentication, and professional qualification recognition. Demand is higher in rural areas.

**Q: What qualifications do Italian midwives need?**
A: Must complete a three-year undergraduate midwifery program and pass the national exam to obtain practice qualifications. Non-EU qualifications require assessment.
