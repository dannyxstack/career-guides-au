# Forestry Worker — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 6210 (ISCO08)**

Forestry workers are responsible for tree planting, logging, forest maintenance, and fire prevention, forming the foundational workforce of Italy's forestry sector. This occupation is mostly in mountainous and rural areas, requiring high physical fitness and outdoor work capability.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 25,000. Italy's forest cover accounts for about 36% of its land area, and forestry employment is relatively stable but affected by seasonality and policies. With the development of sustainable forestry and bioeconomy, demand for skilled forestry workers is increasing, but overall employment growth is slow.

**Competition: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**Work intensity: high (★★★★☆).**

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★½☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Junior forestry workers can advance to team leaders or forest technicians; some become forestry engineers or natural resource managers through training. Government-funded reforestation projects also provide temporary advancement opportunities.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using GPS and drones for forest mapping and boundary marking
- Early detection of pests and diseases based on AI image recognition
- Automated felling machinery operation and pruning
- Operation of forest fire monitoring and early warning systems
- data recording (e.g., tree growth, harvest volume)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Develop precision harvesting plans using satellite imagery and AI analysis.
- Using VR simulation for safety training and emergency drills
- AI-assisted Forest Carbon Sequestration Measurement and Reporting
- Real-time monitoring of soil moisture and wind speed via IoT sensors
- Mobile apps to track tools and resource locations

**Human moat:**

- Fine control of manually operating tools such as chainsaws in complex terrain.
- Intuition and experience-based judgment of forest ecosystems
- On-the-spot decision-making in emergencies (e.g., bushfires)
- Manual thinning and specific species management within nature reserves
- Social skills for collaborating and communicating with landowners and communities

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and maintenance
- GIS and remote sensing data analysis
- Foundational programming (Python/R) for forestry data analysis
- Use of digital maps and mobile applications
- Operation of mechanized harvesting equipment
- Ecological Restoration and Biodiversity Knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Sustainable Forestry
- Bioeconomy
- Fire Prevention
- Mountain Conservation

## 6. Migration pathways / PR

As an EU member state, Italy admits skilled foreign professionals mainly through the EU Blue Card and national work-permit routes. Eligibility depends on your qualifications, a qualifying job offer and salary threshold — not on any single occupation. Rules and salary thresholds change yearly, so always check the official source before making plans.

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average annual salary of forestry workers in Italy?**
A: Entry-level workers earn a pre-tax annual salary of approximately €18,000-22,000, while experienced workers can earn €28,000-35,000.

**Q: How can non-EU citizens become forestry workers in Italy?**
A: Mainly via Decreto Flussi quota work visa, requires employer sponsorship. EU citizens do not need a visa.
