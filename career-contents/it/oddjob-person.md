# Odd-job Person — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 9622 (ISCO08)**

Gig workers engage in non-permanent temporary jobs such as cleaning, moving, gardening, etc., common in households, hotels, or small businesses, offering flexible work but unstable income.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: low (★½☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Gig workers face high risk of automation; tasks like cleaning and moving are easily replaced by robots and AI, but the need for interpersonal interaction provides some buffer, overall employment space is shrinking.

Career development paths are limited, typically remaining at entry-level positions; a few may accumulate clients and become self-employed contractors, entering the domestic or maintenance services industry.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatic cleaning robots and drones replace sweeping and window cleaning
- AI scheduling system replaces manual allocation of gig tasks
- Autonomous mobile robots replace manual handling of heavy objects.
- Online platforms replace intermediaries in matching temporary work
- Smart gardening robots replace pruning and weeding

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Smart tools enhance cleaning efficiency (e.g., automatic stain detection)
- AI-assisted optimal route planning
- Online platforms expand opportunities for receiving orders
- Wearable devices to monitor health and safety
- AI training system quickly learns new skills

**Human moat:**

- Handle unexpected and complex cleaning tasks (e.g., special stains)
- Requires interpersonal communication and trust (e.g., family services)
- Handling unstructured environments (e.g., rooms cluttered with items).
- Flexibly adapt to ad hoc client needs
- High-risk or sensitive scenarios (e.g., medical waste disposal)

**Skills to build:**

- Basic equipment operation (e.g., intelligent cleaning robots)
- Use of digital platforms (e.g., order-taking apps)
- Customer Service and Communication
- Time management and multitasking
- Basic repair skills (handling simple faults)
- Health and safety knowledge

**Key growth areas:**

- Seasonal demand
- Platform economy
- Household services
- Tourism support

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the approximate annual income of gig workers in Italy?**
A: entry-level casual workers earn around 12,000–18,000 euros per year, while senior workers can earn up to 26,000 euros, but income is unstable.

**Q: How Non-EU Citizens Can Obtain an Italian Gig Work Visa?**
A: Primarily through the Decreto Flussi quota work visa, requires employer sponsorship and meeting the annual quota, highly competitive.
