# Forestry Labourer — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 9215 (ISCO08)**

Forestry labourers perform physical tasks such as planting, harvesting, fire prevention, and maintenance in forests, typically working outdoors, forming the foundational workforce of Italy's forestry industry chain.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: medium-high (★★★☆☆).** National workforce approx. 15,000. Italy has abundant forest resources, but its forestry workforce is severely aging, with a shortage of young labor. Driven by sustainable forestry and bioeconomy, demand for forestry workers is expected to remain stable, but employment opportunities are significantly affected by seasonality and regional differences.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★☆☆☆☆).**

Limited career advancement paths, can progress from general labourer to team leader or forest ranger. Some experienced workers may receive training to become forestry technicians or forest managers, but additional qualifications are required.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Using drones and satellite imagery for forest health inspections and fire monitoring
- Operate automated feller bunchers for felling, delimbing, and skidding operations
- Monitor soil moisture and meteorological data online via IoT sensors
- Use AI systems to plan optimal planting spacing and tree species combinations
- Using autonomous tractors to perform fertilisation and pesticide spraying tasks

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Use mobile terminals to receive and execute daily tasks assigned by intelligent planning systems
- Identify pests and diseases through augmented reality glasses and receive immediate management guidance.
- Use data analysis tools to assess harvesting priority for different plots.
- Using collaborative robots to assist in transporting heavy tools and saplings
- Real-time resolution of on-site issues via remote expert systems and AI chatbots

**Human moat:**

- Emergency manual operation and maintenance of equipment on steep slopes or complex terrain.
- Assess risks in unstructured environments (e.g., unstable dead trees) based on long-term local experience.
- Complex collaboration requiring on-site decision-making, such as wildlife management and fire rescue
- Holistic understanding of forest ecosystems and long-term cultivation strategy development
- Independently perform physical rescue tasks during extreme weather or disasters

**Skills to build:**

- Drone operation and basic maintenance
- Agricultural/forestry automation equipment operation (e.g., automatic harvester)
- IoT device debugging and data interpretation
- GIS basics and digital map usage
- Basic programming or scripting skills (for log processing)
- Reading and Communication of English Technical Documents

**Key growth areas:**

- sustainable forestry
- young worker recruitment
- seasonal employment
- biomass demand

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the income of forestry laborers in Italy?**
A: Pre-tax annual salary of approximately 16,000 to 30,000 euros, with lower pay for entry-level roles and higher for senior or supervisory positions. Income is significantly affected by seasonality and region.

**Q: How can non-EU citizens obtain a work visa for forestry labor in Italy?**
A: Mainly via Decreto Flussi quota work visa or seasonal work visa, requiring employer sponsorship, with limited annual quotas. EU citizens can move freely.

**Q: What is the future outlook for forestry laborers in Italy?**
A: Driven by sustainable forestry and bioeconomy, demand is stable. However, the workforce is aging and needs to attract young people. Career advancement opportunities are limited, and it is mostly outdoor physical work.
