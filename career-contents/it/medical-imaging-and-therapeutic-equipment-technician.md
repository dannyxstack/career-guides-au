# Medical Imaging and Therapeutic Equipment Technician — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 3211 (ISCO08)**

Responsible for operating and maintaining medical imaging equipment (such as X-ray, CT, MRI) and radiation therapy equipment, ensuring safe and effective diagnosis and treatment, and working closely with radiologists and the medical team.

## 3. Job demand / competition / intensity

**Job demand: high (★★★★☆).** National workforce approx. 12,500. Italy faces a shortage of radiographers, especially in the northern region; an aging population increases demand for imaging diagnostics, with an estimated 10% job growth over the next decade. Both public hospitals and private clinics need to recruit.

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: high (★★★★☆).**
**AI replacement risk: low (★½☆☆☆).**

Can advance from junior technician to senior technician, department head, or imaging center manager; through continuing education, specialize in MRI or radiation therapy, or transition to medical physicist or radiation protection specialist.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Use CAD software for standard orthotic design
- 3D scan patient limbs to obtain basic measurements.
- Automated sanding and finishing of prosthetic components
- Generate standardized production reports and inventory records

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-driven biomechanical analysis optimizes orthotic design.
- Machine learning-assisted prediction of prosthetic fit outcomes
- Using AR/VR to simulate patient walking for plan adjustment
- Intelligent material parameter recommendations improve component durability
- Remotely monitor patient wearable data to guide maintenance

**Human moat:**

- Manual fine-tuning of prosthetic alignment to suit individual gait
- In-depth communication with patients to obtain comfort feedback
- Handle irregular shapes of complex amputations or deformities
- Field testing and real-time adjustment of adaptation plans
- Creative repairs for sudden mechanical failures

**Skills to build:**

- 3D modeling and scanning software (e.g., Meshmixer, Blender)
- Basic programming and data processing (Python, R)
- Use of AI biomechanical analysis tools.
- Materials science knowledge (smart materials, composites)
- Telemedicine and digital health record management
- Customer relationship management and communication skills

**Key growth areas:**

- Aging population
- Technology advancement
- Diagnostic imaging demand
- Pandemic preparedness

## 7. Who is / isn't suited

**Who is suited**
- Detail-oriented, organized, and interested in technology
- people who enjoy interacting with patients and have communication skills
- People willing to develop long-term in the healthcare industry

**Who is not suited**
- People sensitive or fearful of radiation
- People with poor stress tolerance and weak teamwork skills

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for a radiographer in Italy?**
A: Junior approx. €24,000-30,000, mid-level €32,000-40,000, senior up to €52,000 gross annual salary.

**Q: How can non-EU citizens become radiographers in Italy?**
A: Must obtain an Italian-recognised qualification, pass a national exam for registration, and apply for a work visa such as an EU Blue Card or quota-based work visa.
