# Journalist — career analysis · Italy

**Occupation code: 2642 (ISCO08)**

In Italy, journalists collect, investigate, and report news for newspapers, magazines, radio, television, and online media. This occupation covers freelancers and journalists employed by news organizations.

| Qualification | Issuer | Required | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journalist qualification (Elenco dei giornalisti) | Italian Order of Journalists (Ordine dei Giornalisti) | Required | Must pass a national exam and register; divided into professional journalists and public journalists |

## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-low (★★☆☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**

Career paths typically start at local media or internships, progressing to senior reporter, editor, or columnist. Also possible to move into PR, corporate communications, or digital content management.

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Automatically generate standard news briefs (e.g., financial reports, sports scores)
- Speech-to-text and automatic subtitle generation
- Fact-checking and data reconciliation (e.g., verifying statistical data)

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted data analysis and visualization (quickly identify trends)
- AI-recommended topics and audience preference prediction
- AI compiles multilingual information summaries

**Human moat:**

- Exclusive interviews and in-depth investigations (interpersonal insight and trust)
- Ethical judgment and sensitive issue gatekeeping
- Creative storytelling (features, commentary)

**Skills to build:**

- Data journalism (Python/R/data visualization)
- Multimedia production (video/podcast editing)
- AI tool applications (e.g., ChatGPT-assisted writing)
- Fact-checking and information verification
- Audience Analysis and Digital Marketing

**Key growth areas:**

- Digital media
- Content creation
- Freelance journalism
- Multimedia reporting

## 8. FAQ

**Q: What is the salary level of Italian journalists?**
A: Junior journalists earn approximately €18,000-28,000 per year, senior journalists can reach €40,000-60,000, but freelancers have unstable and lower income.

**Q: How can non-EU citizens become journalists in Italy?**
A: Need to obtain a journalist accreditation, but non-EU citizens usually need to first enter via a work visa, and since journalist is not on the shortage list, visa prospects are difficult.

**Q: What qualifications are required to work as a journalist in Italy?**
A: Must pass a national exam and register with the Italian Journalists Association (Ordine dei Giornalisti), requiring completion of an 18-month internship.
