- Combine AI weather models and real-time ocean data to make more accurate route adjustment decisions
- Use digital twins and predictive maintenance systems to identify equipment issues early
- Optimizes workforce and fatigue management through AI-assisted crew scheduling systems
- Computer vision-based berthing and anchoring operation assistance

**Human moat:**

- Take ultimate decision-making responsibility in emergencies (e.g., engine failure, man overboard)

- Cross-cultural crew management and interpersonal communication (multi-national team collaboration on board)

- Long-term experience (intuitive judgment) with hulls, machinery, and marine environments

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for ships’ deck officers and pilots in Chile?**
A: Salaries vary by rank and vessel type. A junior deck officer earns around CLP 1.5–2.5 million monthly, while a captain can make CLP 3–6 million. Pilots in major ports like Valparaíso or San Antonio may earn higher, often above CLP 5 million, due to specialized skills and demand.

**Q: What qualifications and skills are required to work as a deck officer in Chile?**
A: You need a Chilean nautical license (e.g., from Escuela Naval or Instituto Marítimo), STCW endorsements, and sea service hours. Key skills include navigation, ship handling, cargo operations, radio communication, and English. Knowledge of local maritime regulations and weather patterns is essential.

**Q: How is AI impacting the role of deck officers and pilots in Chile?**
A: AI is enhancing navigation, collision avoidance, and route optimization systems, reducing manual workload. However, human oversight remains critical, especially in congested Chilean ports and Patagonian channels. Automation may change job profiles, but demand for skilled officers stays strong due to regulatory and safety requirements.
