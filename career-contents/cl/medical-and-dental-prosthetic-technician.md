## 5. Future outlook / AI exposure

**Future prospect: medium-high (★★★☆☆).**
**AI replacement risk: medium (★★★☆☆).**

**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Digital design of standard dentures and crowns using CAD software
- Automated layout and grinding of restorations (e.g. CAM machine operation)
- Standardized model generation based on scan data
- Inventory management and order tracking.

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted design software improves the efficiency and accuracy of complex restoration design
- Smart scanning and diagnostic analysis help technicians quickly obtain patient oral data.
- AI-driven material performance simulation optimization solution selection
- Enhanced communication with dentists and prosthetists via remote collaboration platforms

**Human moat:**

- Personalised aesthetic judgment and manual fine adjustment (e.g., biomimetic colouring)
- Handling complex maxillofacial defects, biomechanical adaptation
- Ability to communicate clinical needs directly with dentists/doctors
- Innovative Restoration Design (New Solutions Without AI Database Support)

**Skills to build:**

- CAD/CAM software operation (e.g., 3Shape, exocad)
- Knowledge of 3D printing and CNC precision machining
- Digital oral scanning and data analysis
- Materials science (bioceramics, composite resins, etc.)
- Interpersonal communication and clinical collaboration skills
- Basic programming and AI tool application

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical salary for a medical and dental prosthetic technician in Chile?**
A: In Chile, the average monthly salary for a medical and dental prosthetic technician ranges from 800,000 to 1,200,000 CLP (approximately USD 950–1,400), depending on experience, certification, and region. Entry-level positions may start at around 600,000 CLP, while experienced professionals in private clinics can earn up to 1,500,000 CLP.

**Q: What skills are essential for a prosthetic technician in Chile?**
A: Key skills include manual dexterity, precision, and knowledge of dental anatomy and materials. Proficiency in CAD/CAM software and 3D printing is increasingly important. Technicians must also understand Chilean health regulations, particularly ISP (Instituto de Salud Pública) standards for prosthetics. Certification from a recognized technical institute and good communication with dentists are also crucial.

**Q: How is AI impacting medical and dental prosthetic technicians in Chile?**
A: AI is streamlining digital design and manufacturing of prosthetics, reducing manual labor in scanning and modeling. However, technicians remain vital for custom adjustments, quality control, and ensuring prosthetic fit. In Chile, adoption is growing in larger labs, but smaller workshops still rely on traditional methods. Overall, AI complements rather than replaces the technician’s expertise.
