**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Drawing patterns and layouts using CAD software
- Use automated cutting machines or laser cutters to cut materials
- Color matching and pattern design using AI color selection tools
- Manage raw material inventory and order tracking
- Perform repetitive grinding, polishing, and other finishing operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Using generative AI (e.g., Midjourney) to quickly generate design inspiration and pattern sketches
- Using AI vision inspection systems to identify material defects and stitching flaws in real time
- Optimize material usage and cutting paths using AI simulation tools.
- Using AI marketing platforms to analyse customer preferences and personalise products
- Enhancing multi-order coordination efficiency through AI-driven workflow management systems

**Human moat:**

- Non-replicable artistic skills like high-end hand embroidery and carving
- Intuitive judgment and aesthetic sense for material texture and color perception
- Unique skills for restoring antiques and customizing high-end products
- Deep trust-based communication and customized services with clients
- Cross-disciplinary ability to blend traditional craftsmanship with innovative design

**Skills to build:**

- Learn CAD/CAM software and CNC cutting equipment operation
- Enhance materials science knowledge, understand new fabrics and eco-friendly leather properties
- Train AI vision models for quality inspection and defect identification
- Learn digital marketing and social media brand operation
- Participate in traditional crafts and intangible heritage training to strengthen irreplaceable skills

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average monthly salary for a handicraft worker in textile, leather, and related materials in Chile?**
A: In Chile, the average monthly salary for handicraft workers in textile and leather ranges from 450,000 to 700,000 CLP, depending on experience, specialization, and region. Artisans in high-demand areas like Santiago or tourist destinations may earn more, while those in rural areas earn closer to the lower end. Income can vary based on piecework or self-employment.

**Q: What skills are essential for a career as a handicraft worker in textile and leather in Chile?**
A: Essential skills include proficiency in traditional hand techniques such as weaving, dyeing, embroidery, leather carving, and sewing. Attention to detail, creativity, and knowledge of local materials like alpaca wool or Chilean leather are crucial. Additionally, business skills for selling products at fairs or online are increasingly important. Apprenticeships or courses at artisan schools can provide training.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence affecting handicraft work in textile and leather in Chile?**
A: AI has limited direct impact on traditional handicraft in Chile, as the work relies on manual skill and cultural heritage. However, digital tools like 3D modeling and online marketplaces are used for design and sales. AI-powered automation may affect mass-produced goods but not artisan crafts. The demand for handmade, unique products helps preserve these roles against technological replacement.
