**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Underwater visual inspection and video recording: AI image recognition automatically labels defects.
- Routine structural measurement: ROV with sensors automatically collects data
- Underwater environment monitoring: AI analyzes parameters such as water temperature and turbidity
- Simple salvage operation: remote-controlled robotic arm performs standard operations

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI-assisted decision-making: real-time analysis of diver physiological data to warn of decompression sickness risk
- Augmented reality headset: overlays structural diagrams and repair instructions to improve work accuracy
- Predictive maintenance: AI analyzes historical data to optimize maintenance schedules.
- Remote specialist collaboration: AI translation and 3D reconstruction help land-based experts guide

**Human moat:**

- Manual operation and quick judgment in emergency situations
- Fine tactile sensation and force feedback (e.g., valve operation)
- Adaptability in harsh environments (current, visibility)
- Legal and safety responsibility attribution (divers must sign on-site)

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for underwater divers in Chile?**
A: Entry-level divers earn around CLP 800,000–1,200,000 per month, while experienced commercial divers can make CLP 1,500,000–2,500,000. Offshore oil and gas projects, plus salmon farming in southern Chile, offer higher pay. Rates vary by depth, risk, and employer. Bonuses may apply for deep or hazardous dives.

**Q: What certifications and skills are needed to work as an underwater diver in Chile?**
A: Chile requires a Professional Diver certificate from a recognized school, plus HSE or IMCA qualifications for commercial diving. Additional certifications for welding, cutting, or hyperbaric chamber operation are valued. Physical fitness, medical clearance, and advanced first aid training are mandatory. Knowledge of Spanish and local maritime regulations helps.

**Q: What is the career outlook for underwater divers in Chile, considering AI and automation?**
A: AI and ROVs handle inspection tasks but cannot replace human divers for complex repairs, salvage, or aquaculture net cleaning. Demand persists in salmon farming, offshore energy, and port maintenance. Automation may reduce routine work, requiring divers to upskill in robotics-assisted tasks and data interpretation. Overall, stable demand with emphasis on specialized skills.
