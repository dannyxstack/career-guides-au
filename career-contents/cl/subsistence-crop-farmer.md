**Tasks most exposed to AI:**

- Manual recording of crop growth logs and yield data, replaced by automatic sensors
- Manual pest and disease identification replaced by AI image recognition
- Manual calculation of fertilizer amounts replaced by precision agriculture algorithms
- Paper-based bookkeeping and tax calculations are being replaced by financial software

**Where AI augments the role:**

- AI tools analyze soil moisture and meteorological data to optimize irrigation timing.
- Use satellite imagery and drones to monitor crop health and provide early warning of pests and diseases
- AI-assisted planting decisions based on historical data and market price forecasts
- Online platforms connecting local consumers with restaurants to expand direct sales channels
- Smart devices monitor storage conditions to reduce post-harvest loss

**Human moat:**

- Physical labour and on-site work (e.g., manual weeding, harvesting)
- Decisions based on local experience (e.g., traditional crop rotation, variety selection)

- Flexible judgment in response to sudden weather and pests

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the typical income for subsistence crop farmers in Chile?**
A: Subsistence crop farmers in Chile generally earn low and variable incomes, often below the national minimum wage. Earnings depend on crop yields, market prices, and access to subsidies. Most rely on their harvest for personal consumption, with surplus sold locally. Monthly income may range from 150,000 to 300,000 CLP, but it fluctuates significantly.

**Q: What key skills are needed to be a subsistence crop farmer in Chile?**
A: Essential skills include knowledge of traditional farming techniques, crop rotation, pest management, and soil conservation. Practical abilities in planting, harvesting, and basic tool maintenance are crucial. Familiarity with local climate patterns and water management is important due to Chile's diverse geography. Financial literacy for budgeting and selling surplus also helps sustain livelihoods.

**Q: What is the career outlook for subsistence crop farmers in Chile?**
A: The outlook is challenging due to climate change impacts like droughts in central Chile and water scarcity. Urbanization and migration to cities reduce rural populations. Government programs offer technical support and subsidies, but profitability remains low. Younger generations often seek non-agricultural jobs, leading to an aging farmer demographic. Sustainable practices and niche markets (e.g., organic) offer limited opportunities.
