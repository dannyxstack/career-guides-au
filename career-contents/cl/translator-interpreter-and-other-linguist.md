- Standard conference interpreting (e.g., business negotiations)
- Simple telephone interpreting or community interpreting
- Basic subtitling/transcription proofreading
- Standardized oral exam scoring

**Where AI augments the role:**

- Real-time captioning assisting interpretation (AI providing background information)
- Term base management (AI automatically extracts terms)
- AI speech-to-text note-taking assistance during simultaneous interpretation
- Remote interpreting platforms (AI optimizes audio/video).
- Quality self-check (AI playback comparison against standard translation)

**Human moat:**

## 7. FAQ

**Q: What is the average salary for translators and interpreters in Chile?**
A: In Chile, translators and interpreters earn between CLP 700,000 and CLP 1,500,000 per month, depending on experience and specialization. Freelancers may charge hourly rates of CLP 15,000–40,000. Certified translators and those working with specialized fields like legal or medical translation typically earn at the higher end of the range.

**Q: What skills are most important for linguists in Chile?**
A: Key skills include native-level proficiency in Spanish and source languages (e.g., English, French, or Mapudungun), cultural competency, and specialized knowledge in sectors such as mining, law, or healthcare. Technical skills like CAT tools (e.g., SDL Trados) and subtitling software are increasingly valued. Strong research and writing abilities are essential.

**Q: How is artificial intelligence affecting translation and interpretation jobs in Chile?**
A: AI tools like neural machine translation handle routine texts, but human oversight remains crucial for accuracy, especially in legal and medical documents. Interpreters face less direct AI threat due to need for real-time cultural nuance. However, linguists must adapt by focusing on post-editing, specialized domains, and roles requiring emotional intelligence.
